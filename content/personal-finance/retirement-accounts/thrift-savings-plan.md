*The **Thrift Savings Plan** (TSP) is a defined-contribution retirement account available exclusively to federal civilian employees, members of the uniformed services, and former spouses of covered participants. Operating since 1986, it functions as the federal government's equivalent to the private-sector [401(k)](/401k-plan/), offering low-cost index funds and automatic payroll deductions.*

## The federal alternative to the 401(k)

The TSP exists because federal employees were historically excluded from Social Security and needed dedicated retirement savings infrastructure. Today, about 5 million federal and military workers use TSP accounts, which collectively hold hundreds of billions of dollars. Unlike many private-sector plans, the TSP charges remarkably low investment fees—among the cheapest in the nation—and provides an administrative cost-sharing model where all participants benefit from economies of scale.

Eligibility is strictly limited. Federal civilian employees covered by the Federal Employees Retirement System (FERS) or Civil Service Retirement System (CSRS) can participate. Military members, including those on active duty and reserve, gain access through separate provisions. Once a federal employee or service member leaves government work, they may maintain their TSP balance and continue contributing if they remain eligible, though new contributions cease upon separation.

## Contribution limits and employer matching

Like [401(k)](/401k-plan/) plans, TSP has annual contribution limits ($23,500 for 2024, with an additional $7,500 catch-up for those 50 and older). The real advantage lies in employer matching generosity. FERS employees receive an automatic 1% contribution from their employing agency, regardless of whether the employee contributes. They also receive matching contributions dollar-for-dollar up to 3% of salary, and 50 cents on the dollar for contributions between 3% and 5%. This 5% match is more generous than typical private-sector matching.

CSRS employees and military members operate under different rules. CSRS participants receive no automatic contribution but can participate voluntarily. Military service members receive slightly lower matching (4% automatic match plus dollar-for-dollar matching on the first 2% of contributions), but the TSP remains one of the most valuable military retirement benefits.

Contributions are made through automatic payroll deduction, simplifying the enrollment process. The TSP plan acts as trustee and administrator, eliminating the private plan sponsor middleman found in many corporate arrangements.

## Fund options and investment strategy

TSP offers a lean but sufficient investment menu. The core options consist of five individual funds: the Government Securities Fund (G fund, backed by Treasury bonds), the Fixed Income Fund (F fund), the Common Stock Index Fund (C fund, tracking the S&P 500), the International Stock Index Fund (I fund), and the Small Capitalization Stock Index Fund (S fund). A Lifecycle Fund range (L funds) provides target-date portfolios that automatically shift from stocks to bonds as retirement nears—a useful option for hands-off investors.

This stripped-down lineup contrasts sharply with private 401(k) plans, which often offer dozens of mutual funds and proprietary products. The TSP's simplicity is largely intentional: fewer choices reduce decision paralysis and lower operating costs. For investors seeking more sophisticated options, the Roth TSP variant allows after-tax contributions, analogous to Roth IRA accounts.

## Withdrawal and loan rules

TSP follows standard defined-contribution withdrawal rules. Distributions before age 59½ generally incur a 10% penalty plus income tax, though certain hardship exceptions exist. Once distributions begin, the plan requires minimum distributions starting at age 73 (as of the 2023 change to RMDs).

A distinctive feature is the TSP loan option, allowing participants to borrow against their balance. Loans must be repaid with interest, and the TSP imposes strict repayment schedules. This provides a safety valve for emergencies, though borrowing carries risks—loan defaults trigger income tax and penalties on the withdrawn amount.

## Portability and rollover considerations

Separated federal employees can maintain their TSP account indefinitely, making it one of the most durable employer plans. The TSP also accepts rollovers from [401(k)](/401k-plan/) plans, [traditional IRA](/traditional-ira/) accounts, and other qualified plans, consolidating retirement savings into a single, low-cost vehicle. Some retirees deliberately transfer outside balances into TSP to benefit from its investment cost advantages.

Rollovers from TSP to other plans are permitted but rare, given the TSP's cost structure. Advisors often recommend keeping money in TSP if the employee maintains eligibility, as few outside plans match its fee efficiency.

## Vesting and survivor benefits

Unlike some private plans, TSP contributions vest immediately—employees own all contributions from day one. This eliminates the cliff vesting or graded vesting schedules common in corporate retirement plans. Employer-matching contributions also vest immediately for FERS participants, further strengthening the plan's employee-friendly structure.

Death benefits allow designated beneficiaries to inherit the account and either roll it into their own [traditional IRA](/traditional-ira/) or take distributions. This portability ensures the account remains an effective wealth transfer mechanism.

## Why TSP matters in the broader federal compensation package

The TSP's prominence in federal employment reflects a deliberate policy choice: government agencies compete for talent partly through retirement benefits. Combined with federal employee health insurance and pension systems (for FERS employees), the TSP ensures federal workers can accumulate meaningful retirement savings. The low-cost structure means more of each contribution compounds over decades, compared to private plans where investment fees erode returns.

Federal employees considering private-sector careers should recognize TSP's advantages when evaluating job offers. Few private employers match the combination of automatic contributions, generous matching, and sub-0.05% expense ratios that the TSP routinely delivers.
