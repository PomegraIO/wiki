*The **Schedule D** (Capital Gains and Losses) is the IRS form where you report [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) and losses from sales of securities, real estate, and other assets. The form is completed after you fill out [Form 8949](/form-8949/) (detailed transactions) and summarizes your net short-term and net long-term gains or losses. The net result affects your total [taxable income](/tax-bracket-investor/) and [tax liability](/marginal-tax-rate-investor/).*

For detailed transaction reporting, see [Form 8949](/form-8949/). For [dividend](/dividend/) and [interest](/interest-rate/) income, see [Schedule B](/estate-tax-investor/).

## Structure of Schedule D

The form is divided into two parts:

**Part I: Short-term [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) and losses**. These are assets held one year or less. Short-term gains are taxed at ordinary [income tax](/tax-bracket-investor/) rates.

**Part II: Long-term [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) and losses**. These are assets held longer than one year. Long-term gains are taxed at preferential rates (0%, 15%, or 20%).

Each part has lines for gains and losses, and a line for the net (gains minus losses).

## How to fill out Schedule D

1. **Start with [Form 8949](/form-8949/)**. List each transaction (sale) with proceeds, [cost basis](/cost-basis/), and gain or loss.
2. **Subtotal [Form 8949](/form-8949/)**. Sum all short-term and long-term gains and losses.
3. **Transfer to Schedule D**. Copy the subtotals from [Form 8949](/form-8949/) to Schedule D.
4. **Add [1099-B](/1099-b/) transactions** (if not detailed on [Form 8949](/form-8949/)).
5. **Calculate net totals**. Sum short-term gains and losses (Part I), then long-term (Part II).

## Short-term vs. long-term netting

First, net short-term gains against short-term losses. If you have $50,000 in short-term gains and $30,000 in short-term losses, your net short-term [capital gain](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) is $20,000.

Then, net long-term gains against long-term losses. If you have $100,000 in long-term gains and $20,000 in long-term losses, your net long-term [capital gain](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) is $80,000.

## Netting across holding periods

If you have a net short-term loss and a net long-term gain, they can partially offset. Example:
- Short-term gain: $10,000
- Long-term loss: $30,000
- Net: $20,000 long-term loss

Conversely, if you have a net short-term gain and long-term loss:
- Short-term gain: $50,000
- Long-term loss: $40,000
- Net: $10,000 short-term gain

The character (short-term vs. long-term) of the net determines the tax rate applied.

## Capital loss deduction limit

If your net loss exceeds your net gains, you can deduct up to **$3,000** of capital losses against ordinary income in a single tax year. Any excess loss is **carried forward** to future years indefinitely until it is used up.

Example: You have a net $30,000 capital loss. You can deduct $3,000 against wages or other income this year. The remaining $27,000 carries forward to next year.

## Carryforward of losses

Capital losses are carried forward to the next tax year (and beyond) until used. You do not need to file a special form; tax software automatically tracks carryforwards. However, you should keep records of loss carryforwards.

## Interaction with [alternative minimum tax](/alternative-minimum-tax-investor/)

Large capital losses (and certain deductions) can trigger [alternative minimum tax](/alternative-minimum-tax-investor/) for some high-income investors. If you have large losses, check whether AMT applies.

## Distributions from [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/)

[Capital gain distributions](/1099-div/) from [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/) (or other pass-through entities) are reported on [Schedule D](/schedule-d/). These are treated as if you sold the underlying securities, and they are [long-term capital gains](/long-term-capital-gain-tax/) (even if you held the [fund](/mutual-fund/) for less than one year).

## [Qualified small business stock](/qualified-small-business-stock/) and exclusions

If you sold [qualified small business stock](/qualified-small-business-stock/) held over five years, you may be eligible to exclude part of the gain. This is reported on [Schedule D](/schedule-d/) with an adjustment.

## [Depreciation recapture](/depreciation-recapture-investor/)

If you sold real estate or equipment subject to [depreciation recapture](/depreciation-recapture-investor/), the recapture portion is reported on [Schedule D](/schedule-d/) (or [Form 4797](/estate-tax-investor/) for business property) and taxed at ordinary rates or the 25% [Section 1250](/section-1250-recapture/) rate.
