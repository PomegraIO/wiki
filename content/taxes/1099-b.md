*The **Form 1099-B** is a tax form issued by [brokers](/broker/) reporting sales and redemptions of [stocks](/stock/), [bonds](/bond/), [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/), and other securities during the year. The form shows the proceeds from each sale and the [cost basis](/cost-basis/) (which your [broker](/broker/) calculates based on your [basis method](/cost-basis/)). You use this information to calculate [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) and report them on [Schedule D](/schedule-d/) or [Form 8949](/form-8949/).*

## What goes on 1099-B

The form lists each sale transaction with:
- **Description**: Security sold (e.g., "Apple Inc. Class A Common Stock")
- **Proceeds**: Total sale amount (price × shares)
- **Cost basis**: [Broker](/broker/)-calculated purchase cost
- **Gain or loss**: Proceeds minus [cost basis](/cost-basis/)
- **Holding period**: Whether the holding period is short-term (under 1 year) or long-term (over 1 year)

## Cost basis reporting

For securities acquired after 2011, brokers are required to report [cost basis](/cost-basis/) on 1099-B. This is calculated using your [basis method](/cost-basis/):
- If you specified [specific identification](/specific-identification-basis/), the [broker](/broker/) uses that
- If you did not specify, the [broker](/broker/) defaults to [FIFO](/fifo-tax/)
- For [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/), [average cost](/average-cost-basis/) is often the default

The [broker](/broker/)'s calculation should match your records. If it does not, you may need to file an amended 1099-B or report the correction on your return.

## Wash-sale adjustments

If your [broker](/broker/) detects a [wash-sale](/wash-sale/) (sale and repurchase within 30 days), the form will show the disallowed loss and the adjustment to [cost basis](/cost-basis/). The [wash-sale](/wash-sale/) loss is added to the new [cost basis](/cost-basis/), deferring the deduction.

## Multiple 1099-Bs

If you have [securities](/stock/) accounts at multiple [brokers](/broker/), you will receive multiple 1099-B forms. You must aggregate all gains and losses on a single [Schedule D](/schedule-d/) or [Form 8949](/form-8949/).

## Reporting on your tax return

You use the 1099-B information to complete [Form 8949](/form-8949/) (if you have [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) and losses) and [Schedule D](/schedule-d/) (summary of [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) and losses). Your tax software typically reads 1099-B data electronically and auto-populates [Form 8949](/form-8949/).

## Non-covered securities

Securities acquired before 2012 are often "non-covered" securities. Your [broker](/broker/) may not have [cost basis](/cost-basis/) information for these. You may need to provide your own [cost basis](/cost-basis/) documentation (or reconstruct it from old confirmations).

For non-covered securities, 1099-B may show only proceeds, not [cost basis](/cost-basis/). You enter the [cost basis](/cost-basis/) manually on [Form 8949](/form-8949/).

## "B" transactions and options

If you sold [call options](/option/) or [put options](/option/), these may also appear on 1099-B. Reporting options is complex; the [gain or loss](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) depends on whether you closed the position, exercised, or let it expire. Consult a [tax professional](/schedule-d/) if you have option transactions.

## ETF in-kind redemptions

Some [ETFs](/etf/) allow in-kind redemptions (redemption in securities instead of cash). These may not generate a 1099-B because there was no sale. You may still have a [capital gain](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) on the redeemed securities, but you need to calculate it yourself.

## Timing: February 15 deadline

Your [broker](/broker/) must issue 1099-B by February 15. You then have time to gather all information before filing by April 15 (or later with an extension). If your [broker](/broker/) is late, you can file with an estimate and amend later.

## Corrections and amended forms

If your [broker](/broker/) issued a 1099-B with errors, they will issue a corrected form (usually labeled "Corrected Form 1099-B"). You should use the corrected version when filing your return.

## Reconciling 1099-B with your records

Best practice: reconcile the 1099-B against your [broker](/broker/) statements and trade confirmations. Check:
- Proceeds amounts match
- [Cost basis](/cost-basis/) is correct (or matches your [basis method](/cost-basis/) election)
- Holding period is correctly designated
- [Wash-sale](/wash-sale/) adjustments are accurate
