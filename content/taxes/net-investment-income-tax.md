*The **Net Investment Income Tax** (NIIT), also called the **3.8% tax**, is an additional federal tax on [investment income](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) for high earners. If your income exceeds $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married filing jointly), you must pay a 3.8% tax on the lesser of (a) your [net investment income](/dividend/) or (b) the excess of your total income over the threshold. The tax applies to [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/), [dividends](/dividend/), [interest](/interest-rate/), and [passive income](/hedge-fund/), but not wages.*

## How NIIT is calculated

You have income of $250,000 (single): $180,000 wages + $70,000 [investment income](/dividend/).

Threshold for single: $200,000.
Excess over threshold: $250,000 - $200,000 = $50,000.

Your [net investment income](/dividend/) is $70,000. NIIT applies to the lesser of (a) $70,000 or (b) $50,000 = **$50,000**.

NIIT = $50,000 × 3.8% = **$1,900**.

## What counts as "net investment income"

**Includes**:
- [Long-term and short-term capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/)
- [Dividends](/dividend/) ([qualified](/qualified-dividend/) and [ordinary](/ordinary-dividend/))
- [Interest](/interest-rate/)
- [Passive income](/hedge-fund/) (rental real estate, [passive partnerships](/k-1-investor/))
- [Annuity](/interest-rate/) income
- [K-1 income](/k-1-investor/) from [passive activities](/wash-sale/)

**Excludes**:
- Wages and salary
- Self-employment income (even if you are self-employed investor)
- [Social Security](/interest-rate/)
- [Tax-exempt interest](/interest-rate/) (municipal [bonds](/bond/))
- Health savings account distributions

## Why self-employment income is excluded

An oddity: NIIT does not apply to self-employment income. A software entrepreneur with $1 million in [business income](/tax-bracket-investor/) is not subject to NIIT, even if wealthy. But an investor with $1 million in [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) or [dividend](/dividend/) income is.

This creates a planning incentive: some wealthy individuals structure investments as "business" to avoid NIIT.

## Threshold adjustments

The thresholds ($200,000/$250,000) are **not indexed** for inflation. As inflation rises, more taxpayers cross the threshold, expanding the tax base. Congress set these thresholds in 2010; they have not moved.

## Combined effective rate on investment income

For a wealthy investor (37% federal bracket) in a high-tax state (say, 13.3% California), NIIT adds significantly:

- 37% federal [marginal rate](/marginal-tax-rate-investor/)
- 13.3% California [income tax](/tax-bracket-investor/)
- 3.8% NIIT
- **Total: 54.1%** on [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/)

This is one of the highest tax rates in the US on any form of income.

## Reported on Form 8960

If you are subject to NIIT, you calculate it on Form 8960 and attach to your 1040. The NIIT is paid with your regular tax return.

If your income is below the threshold, you do not file Form 8960 (it is not required).

## Strategy: timing and income sources

High earners sometimes use strategies to avoid NIIT:

1. **Harvest losses**: Realize [capital losses](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) to offset [gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/), reducing [net investment income](/dividend/).
2. **Use tax-deferred accounts**: Income in [IRAs](/hedge-fund/) and 401(k)s is not subject to NIIT.
3. **Hold for [long-term gains](/long-term-capital-gain-tax/)**: Though NIIT applies to both short- and long-term, long-term gains are taxed at lower regular rates, reducing absolute tax.
4. **Defer [realization](/holding-period/)**: Do not sell appreciated assets; defer taxation via [1031 exchange](/1031-like-kind-exchange/) or other mechanisms.

## Medicare funding connection

NIIT was enacted as part of the Affordable Care Act to help fund Medicare. It is sometimes called the "Medicare surtax" for that reason.

## State NIIT

A few states (Vermont, New Jersey, Illinois) have enacted their own versions of NIIT. These apply on top of the federal 3.8%.

## Interplay with other taxes

NIIT is calculated *after* regular [income tax](/tax-bracket-investor/). It increases total tax burden but does not trigger [alternative minimum tax](/alternative-minimum-tax-investor/) (NIIT is not an [AMT](/alternative-minimum-tax-investor/) preference).
