*A **tax bracket** is a range of [taxable income](/tax-bracket-investor/) taxed at a single federal rate. The US has seven brackets ranging from 10% to 37%. Your bracket determines your [marginal tax rate](/marginal-tax-rate-investor/)—the rate applied to [short-term capital gains](/short-term-capital-gain-tax/) and [ordinary dividends](/ordinary-dividend/). Brackets are adjusted annually for inflation. Investors often overestimate their bracket; understand yours to make [tax-efficient investing](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) decisions.*

For your average rate across all brackets, see [effective tax rate investor](/effective-tax-rate-investor/). For the rate on your next dollar, see [marginal tax rate investor](/marginal-tax-rate-investor/).

## 2024 federal brackets (single filer)

- **10%**: $0–$11,600
- **12%**: $11,600–$47,150
- **22%**: $47,150–$100,525
- **24%**: $100,525–$191,950
- **32%**: $191,950–$243,725
- **35%**: $243,725–$609,350
- **37%**: $609,350+

Example: Single filer with $150,000 [taxable income](/tax-bracket-investor/). The income is taxed as:
- First $11,600 at 10%
- Next $35,550 at 12%
- Next $53,375 at 22%
- Remaining $49,475 at 24%

Total brackets climbed = your [marginal rate](/marginal-tax-rate-investor/) is 24%.

## Married filing jointly (2024)

- **10%**: $0–$23,200
- **12%**: $23,200–$94,300
- **22%**: $94,300–$201,050
- **24%**: $201,050–$383,900
- **32%**: $383,900–$487,450
- **35%**: $487,450–$731,200
- **37%**: $731,200+

Married brackets are roughly double single brackets (they used to be exactly double, but are now slightly higher).

## Head of household (2024)

Head of household brackets fall between single and married (for taxpayers with dependents):

- **10%**: $0–$17,400
- **12%**: $17,400–$66,550
- And so on...

## How your bracket affects investment decisions

Knowing your bracket is critical for [investment decisions](/capital-gains-tax-investor/). A [short-term capital gain](/short-term-capital-gain-tax/) is taxed at your bracket rate. If you are in the 24% bracket, realizing a $50,000 short-term gain costs $12,000 in tax.

A [long-term capital gain](/long-term-capital-gain-tax/) is taxed at preferential rates (0%, 15%, or 20%), regardless of your bracket. So the same $50,000 long-term gain costs $0-$10,000 in tax.

The bracket distinction drives much of [tax-efficient investing](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) strategy.

## Bracket creep from [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/)

Adding [income](/tax-bracket-investor/) can push you into a higher bracket. A $50,000 [short-term gain](/short-term-capital-gain-tax/) added to $150,000 income makes total [taxable income](/tax-bracket-investor/) $200,000. You are now in the 32% bracket, and the last portion of the gain is taxed at 32%, not your original 24%.

This "bracket creep" is a tax cost of realizing gains.

## Married filing separately

Married couples can choose to file separately, which typically results in higher total tax (because the brackets are narrower). This is rarely optimal except in special cases (like isolating [passive loss](/wash-sale/) limitations). Consult a [tax professional](/schedule-d/) before filing separately.

## Annual adjustments for inflation

Each year, the IRS adjusts bracket thresholds for inflation. In 2023, the 24% bracket for single filers was $100,525; in 2024, it is $100,525 (the bump varies by year based on inflation). These adjustments are announced each October for the next tax year.

## Effective vs. marginal rate in brackets

Your [marginal rate](/marginal-tax-rate-investor/) is the rate of your highest bracket (the bracket your last dollar of income falls into).

Your [effective rate](/effective-tax-rate-investor/) is your total tax divided by total income—a weighted average across all brackets, always lower than marginal.

Example: Taxable income $150,000 places you in the 24% bracket (marginal), but your [effective rate](/effective-tax-rate-investor/) is roughly 17% because lower income is taxed at lower rates.

## Ordinary income vs. capital gains brackets

The seven brackets apply to ordinary income ([wages](/interest-rate/), [interest](/interest-rate/), [ordinary dividends](/ordinary-dividend/), [short-term capital gains](/short-term-capital-gain-tax/)).

[Long-term capital gains](/long-term-capital-gain-tax/) and [qualified dividends](/qualified-dividend/) have separate, preferential brackets (0%-20%), not shown here.

Example: You are in the 24% bracket for ordinary income. Your [long-term capital gains](/long-term-capital-gain-tax/) rate is 15% (based on a different set of thresholds for long-term income).
