*The **Section 179 deduction** is a tax benefit that allows businesses (and investor-owners) to immediately deduct the full cost of eligible property and equipment purchased in a tax year, rather than depreciating the cost over years. The [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) limit is $1.22 million for 2024 (adjusted annually for inflation). This provision accelerates tax deductions and improves cash flow for businesses investing in equipment, though it triggers full [Section 1245 recapture](/section-1245-recapture/) on eventual sale.*

## How [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) works

Without [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/), a business buys equipment for $100,000 and depreciates it over 5-7 years, deducting $14,000-$20,000 annually.

With [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/), the business can immediately deduct the entire $100,000 in the year of purchase, reducing taxable income by $100,000 in that year.

This front-loads the tax benefit: instead of spreading deductions over years, you get the full deduction at once. This is valuable for small businesses or startups with significant equipment purchases and positive income to offset.

## The $1.22 million limit (2024)

[Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) is capped at $1.22 million per year. If a business buys $1.5 million in equipment, it can deduct $1.22 million under [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) and must depreciate the remaining $280,000.

The limit is indexed annually for inflation. In years of significant capital expenditure, the limit may be a constraint.

## The phase-out threshold

If total property placed in service exceeds $4.88 million (2024), the [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) deduction is reduced dollar-for-dollar. Buy $5.88 million in equipment, and the deduction is reduced by $1 million, leaving $220,000 available (instead of $1.22 million).

This phase-out is meant to limit the benefit to small and mid-sized businesses. Large corporations with multi-million-dollar annual capital budgets may phase out entirely.

## Eligible vs. ineligible property

**Eligible for [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/):**
- Equipment and machinery
- Vehicles (for business use, with limits)
- Computers and technology
- Furniture and fixtures
- Qualified real property improvements (since 2018)

**Not eligible:**
- Real estate buildings (though [qualified real property improvements](/section-179-deduction/) may qualify)
- Land
- Intangible property (patents, software, etc.)
- Property held for investment

## The recapture cost

[Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) deductions trigger full [Section 1245 recapture](/section-1245-recapture/) on sale. If you deduct $100,000 under [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) and sell the equipment five years later for $50,000, the entire $50,000 gain is taxed at ordinary rates (up to 37% federally), not [long-term capital gains](/long-term-capital-gain-tax/) rates.

This is a trade-off: immediate tax savings versus recapture tax on sale. For equipment with short useful lives (vehicles, computers), [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) is usually optimal. For longer-lived assets, the math is more complex.

## [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) vs. [bonus depreciation](/bonus-depreciation/)

Both [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) and [bonus depreciation](/bonus-depreciation/) accelerate deductions, but they work differently:

- **[Section 179](/section-179-deduction/)**: Immediate deduction up to $1.22 million; must elect it; limited by passive income
- **[Bonus depreciation](/bonus-depreciation/)**: 100% deduction for most property (through 2025, then phasing down); automatic; no election needed
- **Combined**: You can use both, subject to the [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) limit

For most businesses, [bonus depreciation](/bonus-depreciation/) is now more favorable because it is automatic and not capped.

## Election and documentation

To claim [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/), you must file [Form 4797](/estate-tax-investor/) (Sales of Business Property). You must elect [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) on your tax return; it is not automatic.

Documentation required: description of the property, date placed in service, cost, and [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) deduction claimed. Keep records for IRS substantiation.

## Income limitations

[Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) deductions cannot exceed your business income. If a business has $50,000 in income, it can deduct at most $50,000 under [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/), even if it bought $500,000 in equipment. Excess deductions carry forward to the next year.

## For investors, not just businesses

Self-employed investors and [partnership](/k-1-investor/) members can claim [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) on their share of business property. Investors in real estate partnerships may have limited opportunities (real estate is generally not [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/)-eligible), but business equipment purchases are common.
