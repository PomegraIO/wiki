*The **HIFO** method (Highest In, First Out) is a [cost basis](/cost-basis/) approach where you sell the highest-cost [tax lot](/tax-lot/) first, minimising your [capital gain](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) and tax bill. HIFO is effectively the same as choosing [specific identification](/specific-identification-basis/) by always selecting the highest-cost [lot](/tax-lot/). It offers excellent tax efficiency and is increasingly popular with tax-conscious investors.*

## How HIFO works

Suppose you own 300 [shares](/stock/) in three [lots](/tax-lot/):
- **Lot 1** (2015): 100 [shares](/stock/) at $50
- **Lot 2** (2020): 100 [shares](/stock/) at $120
- **Lot 3** (2024): 100 [shares](/stock/) at $180

Current price: $200 per share. You want to sell 100 [shares](/stock/).

**HIFO approach**: Sell Lot 3 (highest cost at $180). Gain = $200 - $180 = $20 × 100 = **$2,000 gain**.

**FIFO approach** (default): Sell Lot 1. Gain = $200 - $50 = $150 × 100 = **$15,000 gain**.

By choosing HIFO, you save $13,000 in taxable gain. At a 15% long-term rate, that is roughly $1,950 in federal tax saved on a single transaction.

## Why HIFO is powerful

HIFO is mathematically the most tax-efficient method available. By always selling the highest-cost [lot](/tax-lot/), you minimise every single gain, maximising the amount you can reinvest tax-free.

Over decades, especially with [dollar-cost averaging](/holding-period/) into positions, HIFO can preserve hundreds of thousands of dollars that would otherwise go to tax.

## How to use HIFO

HIFO is not a pre-packaged election like [FIFO](/fifo-tax/), [LIFO](/lifo-tax/), or [average cost](/average-cost-basis/). Instead, you implement it through [specific identification](/specific-identification-basis/):

1. At the time of sale, write to your [broker](/broker/) with the specific [lot](/tax-lot/) you want to sell.
2. Identify the [lot](/tax-lot/) with the highest cost basis.
3. Request that the [broker](/broker/) sell that [lot](/tax-lot/).
4. Keep documentation of your instruction.

Modern brokers like Fidelity, Vanguard, and Schwab have made this easier. Some now offer "HIFO" as a named option in their trading interfaces, automatically calculating the highest-cost [lot](/tax-lot/) for you.

## Brokers supporting HIFO

**Fidelity**: Supports HIFO selection in the trade ticket. You can set a default to "always use HIFO" for a given security.

**Vanguard**: Allows [specific identification](/specific-identification-basis/) of any [lot](/tax-lot/); you must identify the high-cost [lot](/tax-lot/) manually.

**Schwab**: Supports [specific identification](/specific-identification-basis/) and has streamlined the process.

**Smaller brokers**: Check their documentation. If they support [specific identification](/specific-identification-basis/), you can implement HIFO by hand.

## Advantages over [FIFO](/fifo-tax/)

The difference between HIFO and [FIFO](/fifo-tax/) is enormous for long-term investors:
- **FIFO** sells your oldest, cheapest [lots](/tax-lot/) first—maximising gains.
- **HIFO** sells your newest, most expensive [lots](/tax-lot/) first—minimising gains.

The gap widens the longer you hold and the more volatile your purchase prices are.

## Disadvantages and limitations

**Not automatic**. You must remember to instruct your [broker](/broker/) at each sale. One forgotten instruction and it defaults to [FIFO](/fifo-tax/).

**Requires discipline**. If you trade frequently, specifying lots repeatedly becomes tedious.

**No availability for [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/)**. Most [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/) platforms do not allow [specific identification](/specific-identification-basis/); they default to [average cost](/average-cost-basis/) or [FIFO](/fifo-tax/).
