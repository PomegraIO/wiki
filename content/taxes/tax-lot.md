*A **tax lot** is a single purchase of a [stock](/stock/), [bond](/bond/), or other security at a specific price on a specific date. When you own multiple [tax lots](/tax-lot/) of the same holding—bought at different prices or times—you can choose which [lot](/tax-lot/) to sell when you liquidate, controlling the size of your [capital gain](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) and your tax bill. This choice is the foundation of tax-efficient investing.*

## Example: multiple lots, one security

Suppose you buy Apple [stock](/stock/) three times:
- January 2022: 100 shares at $150 = $15,000 (Lot A)
- June 2023: 100 shares at $175 = $17,500 (Lot B)
- March 2024: 100 shares at $200 = $20,000 (Lot C)

You now own 300 shares and have three [tax lots](/tax-lot/). If you decide to sell 100 shares at $250, you have a choice: which [lot](/tax-lot/) do you sell?

- Sell Lot A (cost $150): gain of $100 per share = $10,000 gain.
- Sell Lot B (cost $175): gain of $75 per share = $7,500 gain.
- Sell Lot C (cost $200): gain of $50 per share = $5,000 gain.

The tax on that sale depends entirely on which [lot](/tax-lot/) you choose. If you sell Lot A and owe 15% on the gain, you pay $1,500 in tax. If you sell Lot C, you pay only $750. That is a difference of $750 on a single transaction—magnified across a portfolio over years, the difference can be tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars.

## Lot identification methods

The IRS allows several ways to designate which [lot](/tax-lot/) you are selling:

**[Specific identification](/specific-identification-basis/)**. You explicitly tell your [broker](/broker/) which [lot](/tax-lot/) to sell—usually the highest-cost one to minimize gain. This requires written instruction (email or broker form) at the time of sale. It is the most tax-efficient method but requires discipline.

**[FIFO (First In, First Out)](/fifo-tax/)**. Without instruction, you are assumed to sell the oldest [lot](/tax-lot/) first. This is the IRS default and also the default for many brokers. It is often the worst choice if you have old [lots](/tax-lot/) with very low bases.

**[LIFO (Last In, First Out)](/lifo-tax/)**. You sell the newest [lot](/tax-lot/) first. Useful if recent purchases are at higher prices. Requires explicit election.

**[Average cost](/average-cost-basis/)**. All your [lots](/tax-lot/) are averaged together; you sell at the average price. Simple but rarely optimal. Often used for [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/).

Once you elect a method for a given security, you must use it consistently. Switching requires IRS approval.

## Wash-sale and lot identification

The [wash-sale](/wash-sale/) rule complicates [lot](/tax-lot/) management. If you sell a [lot](/tax-lot/) at a loss and repurchase within 30 days, the loss is disallowed and added to the cost basis of the new [lot](/tax-lot/). This can make a [loss-harvesting](/wash-sale/) trade more complicated: plan carefully to avoid accidental wash-sales.

## Inherited lots: step-up

When you inherit [stock](/stock/), each [lot](/tax-lot/) you inherit receives a [step-up in basis](/step-up-in-basis/)—the cost basis becomes the fair market value at the date of death, not the decedent's original purchase price. This means you can sell the [entire position](/stock/) immediately with zero tax. This is often called the "step-up loophole," a major tax advantage of inherited assets.

## Tracking and documentation

Your [broker](/broker/) is required (for securities acquired after 2011) to track your [lots](/tax-lot/) and report your cost basis on [Form 8949](/form-8949/). For older securities, you may need to track your cost basis yourself. Keep purchase confirmations, [dividend](/dividend/) reinvestment records, and [stock](/stock/) split notices. If you transfer to a new [broker](/broker/), confirm that all [lot](/tax-lot/) information transfers accurately.
