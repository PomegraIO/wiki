*The **Form 8949** (Sales of Securities) is where you detail every [stock](/stock/), [bond](/bond/), and [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/) transaction you sold during the year. For each sale, you list the proceeds, [cost basis](/cost-basis/), and [capital gain](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) or loss. The totals from [Form 8949](/form-8949/) then transfer to [Schedule D](/schedule-d/), which summarizes your net [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) and losses. Any investor with taxable transactions must file [Form 8949](/form-8949/).*

For the summary, see [Schedule D](/schedule-d/). For [broker](/broker/) reported transactions, see [Form 1099-B](/1099-b/).

## Why [Form 8949](/form-8949/) exists

When the IRS redesigned capital gains reporting (around 2011), it created [Form 8949](/form-8949/) to capture detailed transaction data. This allows the IRS to cross-check your reported gains against [broker](/broker/) [1099-B](/1099-b/) data electronically. Without [Form 8949](/form-8949/), tax enforcement of [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) would be difficult.

## What goes on [Form 8949](/form-8949/)

Each line reports one transaction:

**Description of property** (e.g., "Apple Inc. Class A Common Stock, 100 shares")
**Date acquired** (purchase date)
**Date sold** (sale date)
**Proceeds** (sale price × shares)
**[Cost basis](/cost-basis/)** ([broker](/broker/) or your calculation)
**Gain or loss** (proceeds minus [cost basis](/cost-basis/))

## Two parts: short-term and long-term

Part I lists sales of [securities](/stock/) held one year or less (short-term). Part II lists sales held over one year (long-term). Holding period is determined by comparing the sale date to the acquisition date.

## Matching with [1099-B](/1099-b/)

Your [1099-B](/1099-b/) from your [broker](/broker/) should contain most or all of your transactions. For securities acquired after 2011, your [broker](/broker/) must report [cost basis](/cost-basis/). You can use the [1099-B](/1099-b/) to populate [Form 8949](/form-8949/), or tax software can import it automatically.

For older securities (acquired before 2011), you provide your own [cost basis](/cost-basis/), usually from old confirmations or statements.

## Unreported transactions

If you have transactions not on [1099-B](/1099-b/)—perhaps from an old [broker](/broker/) or inherited assets—you must manually enter them on [Form 8949](/form-8949/).

## Wash-sales

If your [broker](/broker/) identified a [wash-sale](/wash-sale/) on your [1099-B](/1099-b/), the disallowed loss is shown separately. You must report the disallowed loss on [Form 8949](/form-8949/) and note that it is a [wash-sale](/wash-sale/).

## Cost basis adjustments

Sometimes you may need to adjust [cost basis](/cost-basis/) on [Form 8949](/form-8949/):
- [Wash-sale](/wash-sale/) loss added to new basis
- [Stock splits](/holding-period/) (rare on modern [Form 8949](/form-8949/), usually handled by [brokers](/broker/))
- [Return of capital distributions](/ordinary-dividend/) reducing basis

These adjustments can make [Form 8949](/form-8949/) complex; consider professional help if your situation is complicated.

## Multi-year reconciliation

If you are reconciling multiple years (e.g., an audit), you may need to file amended [Form 8949](/form-8949/) for prior years. Amendments require filing an amended Form 1040 and amended [Schedule D](/schedule-d/) with corrected [Form 8949](/form-8949/).

## Totaling and transfer to Schedule D

You sum all short-term gains and losses on Part I, then all long-term gains and losses on Part II. These subtotals transfer to [Schedule D](/schedule-d/), which nets them together and calculates your overall net [capital gain](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) or loss.

## Order of transactions

[Form 8949](/form-8949/) transactions can be listed in any order, though many software programs sort by holding period or date sold. The order does not affect your tax liability.

## IRS cross-checking

The IRS electronically compares your [Form 8949](/form-8949/) totals against [1099-B](/1099-b/) totals reported by your [broker](/broker/). Large discrepancies can trigger an IRS inquiry or audit. If your reported [cost basis](/cost-basis/) differs significantly from what the [broker](/broker/) reported, be prepared to explain.

## Special situations

**[Mutual fund](/mutual-fund/) distributions**: If a [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/) made a [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) distribution (reported on [1099-DIV](/1099-div/)), you report this on [Form 8949](/form-8949/) as if you sold the securities. The distribution is always [long-term capital gain](/long-term-capital-gain-tax/).

**[Depreciation recapture](/depreciation-recapture-investor/)**: Sales of real estate and equipment may have [depreciation recapture](/depreciation-recapture-investor/) components. These may be reported on [Form 8949](/form-8949/) or [Form 4797](/estate-tax-investor/).

**Options**: Sales of [call](/option/) and [put options](/option/) are also reported on [Form 8949](/form-8949/), though the treatment can be complex.
