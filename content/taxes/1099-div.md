*The **Form 1099-DIV** is a tax form issued by your [broker](/broker/) or investment company reporting [dividend](/dividend/) income you received during the year. The form breaks down [dividends](/dividend/) into [qualified](/qualified-dividend/) and [ordinary](/ordinary-dividend/), reports capital gain distributions from [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/), and includes foreign tax paid. You must report this income on your tax return; your [broker](/broker/) also reports it to the IRS.*

For [interest](/), see [Form 1099-INT](/1099-int/). For brokerage sales, see [Form 1099-B](/1099-b/).

## What goes on 1099-DIV

The form reports several categories:

**Box 1a: Ordinary [dividends](/dividend/)**. Cash [dividends](/dividend/) that do not qualify for preferential tax treatment, taxed at ordinary [income tax](/tax-bracket-investor/) rates. Common on REITs and foreign [stocks](/stock/).

**Box 1b: [Qualified dividends](/qualified-dividend/)**. Cash [dividends](/dividend/) from US [stocks](/stock/) (or qualifying foreign [stocks](/stock/)) that you held for 60+ days, taxed at preferential [long-term capital gains](/long-term-capital-gain-tax/) rates (0%, 15%, or 20%).

**Box 2a: Long-term [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) distributions**. Gains from [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/) sales of securities held over one year, distributed to you. Taxed at preferential rates.

**Box 2b: Unrecaptured Section 1250 gains**. [Depreciation recapture](/depreciation-recapture-investor/) from [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/) real estate holdings, taxed at 25%.

**Box 2c: Section 1202 gains**. Gains on [small business stock](/qualified-small-business-stock/), potentially with an exclusion.

**Box 2d: Collectibles gains**. Gains on metals, art, etc., taxed at 28%.

**Box 2e: Short-term [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) distributions**. Gains from [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/) sales of securities held under one year, taxed at ordinary rates.

**Box 7: Foreign [dividend](/dividend/) taxes paid**. Taxes you paid to foreign governments on [dividend](/dividend/) income; used to claim [foreign tax credit](/1099-div/).

## How to read and use 1099-DIV

The form lists box numbers in the left column and amounts in the right. You use this information to fill out your tax return:

1. **If you received [dividends](/dividend/)**: Copy the 1a and 1b amounts from the 1099-DIV to [Schedule B](/estate-tax-investor/) (interest and [dividends](/dividend/)).
2. **If you received [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) distributions**: Copy the amounts from boxes 2a-2e to [Schedule D](/schedule-d/) (capital gains and losses) or [Form 8949](/form-8949/).
3. **If you paid foreign tax**: Use the 1099-DIV amount to calculate your [foreign tax credit](/1099-div/).

Tax software typically auto-populates these fields if you upload the 1099-DIV.

## Multiple 1099-DIVs

If you own [stocks](/stock/) or [funds](/mutual-fund/) at multiple [brokers](/broker/), you will receive multiple 1099-DIVs. You must aggregate all [dividends](/dividend/) and [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) from all 1099-DIVs on a single [Schedule B](/estate-tax-investor/) and [Schedule D](/schedule-d/).

## 1099-DIV vs. actual distributions

The 1099-DIV reports [dividends](/dividend/) and distributions *declared* during the year, not necessarily *paid*. A [dividend](/dividend/) declared on December 15 and paid January 15 (of the next year) is reported on the 1099-DIV for the year it was declared.

For [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/) distributions (both [dividend](/dividend/) and [capital gains](/capital-gains-tax-investor/)), this is a common source of confusion: the distribution is taxable in the year it is paid (or ex-dividend date), not the year you receive it.

## Timing: January 31 deadline

Your [broker](/broker/) must issue the 1099-DIV by January 31. You then use it to file your tax return by April 15 (or later with an extension). If your [broker](/broker/) is late issuing the 1099-DIV, you can file with an estimate and amend later.

## Reconciliation with your [broker](/broker/) statement

The totals on your 1099-DIV should match your [broker](/broker/) statement. If they do not, contact your [broker](/broker/) to clarify. Mismatches can occur if [dividends](/dividend/) are reinvested, credited in error, or if ex-dividend dates are miscalculated.

## [Qualified dividend](/qualified-dividend/) rules

The [qualified dividend](/qualified-dividend/) amount depends on your holding period (60+ days) and the security type. Your [broker](/broker/) calculates this; it should be correct on the 1099-DIV. Verify by checking the securities you held at the ex-dividend dates during the year.

If your [broker](/broker/) incorrectly reports all [dividends](/dividend/) as qualified (when some do not meet the holding period requirement), you may need to report the correction on your return.
