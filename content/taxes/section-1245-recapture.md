*The **Section 1245 recapture** rule requires that gains on the sale of depreciable personal property—equipment, machinery, vehicles, and furniture—be taxed at ordinary [income tax](/tax-bracket-investor/) rates (up to 37% federally) to the extent of depreciation deductions taken. This contrasts with [Section 1250](/section-1250-recapture/) property (real estate) which has a preferential 25% rate. For business owners and investors in equipment-heavy operations, [1245](/section-1245-recapture/) recapture can be the largest tax on a sale.*

For real estate recapture (lower rates), see [Section 1250 recapture](/section-1250-recapture/). For the broader framework, see [depreciation recapture for investors](/depreciation-recapture-investor/).

## What is [Section 1245](/section-1245-recapture/) property?

[1245](/section-1245-recapture/) property includes most tangible personal property used in business:
- Equipment and machinery
- Vehicles (commercial)
- Furniture and fixtures
- Computers and technology
- Tools and implements

Real estate buildings are not [1245](/section-1245-recapture/) property; they are [Section 1250](/section-1250-recapture/).

## Depreciation schedules for [1245](/section-1245-recapture/) property

[1245](/section-1245-recapture/) property has much shorter useful lives than real estate:
- **5-year property**: Computers, office equipment, autos
- **7-year property**: Most machinery and equipment
- **10-year property**: Certain equipment and vessels
- **15-20-year property**: Specialized equipment

Because the depreciable life is shorter, you write off the cost faster. This means larger depreciation deductions, larger adjustments to basis, and larger recapture amounts when you sell.

## How [1245](/section-1245-recapture/) recapture works

Suppose you buy equipment for $100,000 and depreciate it over 5 years using [straight-line depreciation](/section-179-deduction/), taking $20,000 per year in deductions. After 5 years, your adjusted basis is $0.

You sell the equipment for $50,000. Your gain is $50,000 (sale price $50,000 minus adjusted basis $0). The entire $50,000 gain is [1245](/section-1245-recapture/) recapture and is taxed at ordinary rates.

If you are in the 32% [bracket](/tax-bracket-investor/), you owe $16,000 in federal tax on a $50,000 gain. This is far harsher than [long-term capital gains](/long-term-capital-gain-tax/) treatment.

## Bonus depreciation and accelerated depreciation

[Bonus depreciation](/bonus-depreciation/) (and [Section 179 expensing](/section-179-deduction/)) allows you to write off the entire cost of [1245](/section-1245-recapture/) property in the year of purchase. This creates an immediate tax deduction but also guarantees a full-amount [1245](/section-1245-recapture/) recapture when you sell.

Example: Buy $100,000 equipment, deduct the full $100,000 under [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/). Basis is now $0. Sell it ten years later for $80,000. The entire $80,000 gain is [1245](/section-1245-recapture/) recapture, taxed at ordinary rates.

## Recapture beyond depreciation

[1245](/section-1245-recapture/) recapture applies only to the extent of depreciation deductions taken. If your gain exceeds the depreciation you deducted, the excess is treated as a [long-term capital gain](/long-term-capital-gain-tax/) (if held over one year) or [short-term gain](/short-term-capital-gain-tax/) (if under one year).

Example: Buy equipment for $100,000; depreciate $50,000; sell for $120,000. Gain is $20,000. [1245](/section-1245-recapture/) recapture is $20,000 (the full depreciation taken), taxed at ordinary rates. No excess gain.

If you sell for $150,000, gain is $50,000. [1245](/section-1245-recapture/) recapture is $50,000 (depreciation deducted), taxed at ordinary rates. No additional gain.

If you sell for $160,000, gain is $60,000. [1245](/section-1245-recapture/) recapture is $50,000 (depreciation deducted), taxed at ordinary rates. Excess gain of $10,000 is a [long-term capital gain](/long-term-capital-gain-tax/) (if held over one year), taxed at preferential rates.

## Interaction with holding period

If you hold [1245](/section-1245-recapture/) property for over one year, the excess gain (beyond depreciation) is treated as a [long-term capital gain](/long-term-capital-gain-tax/). The recapture portion, however, is always at ordinary rates regardless of holding period.

## Planning: [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) and recapture

[Section 179 expensing](/section-179-deduction/) allows you to immediately deduct the full cost of equipment. This saves tax in the year of purchase but triggers full recapture when you sell.

Whether to use [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) is a planning decision: front-load the tax benefit, or spread deductions over time? For short-lived equipment, [Section 179](/section-179-deduction/) is usually optimal. For longer-held assets, the math is more complex.
