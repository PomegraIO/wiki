*An **expense ratio** is the annual percentage cost of owning a [mutual fund](/mutual-fund) or [ETF](/etf). It covers [management fees](/management-fee), administrative costs, custody fees, legal fees, and other operating expenses. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio) is deducted from the fund's returns before calculating the return you see. An [expense ratio](/expense-ratio) of 0.10% on a $100,000 investment costs $100 per year.*

## What the expense ratio covers

An expense ratio includes:

**[Management fee](/management-fee).** The cost paid to the portfolio manager and investment team. For a passive [index fund](/index-fund), this is small (0.01–0.10%); for an [active fund](/active-etf), larger (0.30–0.75%).

**Administrative costs.** Custody, accounting, legal, compliance, and other overhead.

**Distribution expense (12b-1 fee).** For [mutual funds](/mutual-fund), a fee to cover distribution and marketing costs. [ETFs](/etf) typically do not charge this.

**Other fees.** Portfolio trading costs (commissions, market impact), index licensing, etc.

The total is expressed as a percentage of average assets under management.

## How expense ratios compound

Expense ratios seem small (0.10%) but compound dramatically over decades:

Suppose you invest $100,000 in:
- **Fund A:** 0.10% expense ratio
- **Fund B:** 0.50% expense ratio

Both return 8% annually (before expenses). After 30 years:

```
Fund A: $100,000 × 1.0790^30 = $1,002,257
Fund B: $100,000 × 1.0750^30 = $946,000
Difference: $56,257 (5.6% of final value)
```

The 0.40% annual difference in expense ratio results in losing $56,257 over 30 years. This demonstrates why low [expense ratios](/expense-ratio) matter.

| Broad [bond ETF](/bond-etf) | 0.03%–0.10% |
| [Equity](/equity-etf) [mutual fund](/mutual-fund) (active) | 0.40%–0.80% |
| [Bond](/bond) [mutual fund](/mutual-fund) (active) | 0.20%–0.50% |
| [Target-date fund](/target-date-fund) | 0.10%–0.20% |
| [Balanced fund](/balanced-fund) | 0.10%–0.30% |
| [Sector ETF](/sector-etf) | 0.05%–0.15% |
| [Commodity ETF](/commodity-etf) | 0.30%–0.70% |
| [Closed-end fund](/closed-end-fund) | 0.50%–1.50% |
| [Hedge fund](/hedge-fund) | 1.5%–2.5% + 20% performance fee |

The variation is enormous. A broad [equity ETF](/equity-etf) costs 0.03%; an [active equity fund](/active-etf) costs 0.60%. Over 30 years, this 0.57% annual difference compounds into hundreds of thousands of dollars of foregone returns.

## Why expense ratios matter

**Headwind to returns.** The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio) is subtracted from gross returns before you see your actual return. A fund with 10% gross return and 0.50% [expense ratio](/expense-ratio) delivers 9.50% net return.

**Difficulty outperforming.** An [active fund](/active-etf) manager must beat the index by more than the [expense ratio](/expense-ratio) just to match the market. A 0.75% [expense ratio](/expense-ratio) means the manager must beat the index by 0.75% to deliver market returns.

**Compounding over time.** Because you are paying the ratio annually, the loss compounds. A 0.50% [expense ratio](/expense-ratio) paid for 30 years is far more expensive than it seems.

## How to minimize expense ratios

1. **Use low-cost [index ETFs](/index-fund).** Broad [equity](/equity-etf) and [bond ETFs](/bond-etf) from Vanguard, Schwab, or iShares cost 0.03–0.10%.

2. **Avoid [active funds](/active-etf).** The odds that an active manager will beat their fees are low. [Index funds](/index-fund) have historically outperformed [active funds](/active-etf) net of fees.

3. **Check the prospectus.** Always compare [expense ratios](/expense-ratio) when choosing between similar funds.

4. **Avoid [fund of funds](/fund-of-funds).** They layer fees, often resulting in total [expense ratios](/expense-ratio) of 1.5%–3.0%.

5. **Avoid [closed-end funds](/closed-end-fund) unless you have a specific reason.** [Closed-end funds](/closed-end-fund) typically charge 0.50–1.50% [expense ratios](/expense-ratio), well above [ETFs](/etf).

## The trend: expense ratios are declining

Competition from low-cost providers (Vanguard, Schwab, iShares) has driven expense ratios down across the industry:

- 20 years ago, a typical [equity mutual fund](/mutual-fund) cost 0.75–1.00%.
- Today, similar funds cost 0.40–0.60%.
- Low-cost [index ETFs](/index-fund) cost 0.03–0.10%.

This democratization of low-cost investing has been one of the most significant positive developments for retail investors.
