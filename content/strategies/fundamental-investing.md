*Fundamental investing is an approach to selecting [stocks](/stock/) rooted in deep analysis of financial statements, competitive dynamics, and management quality to estimate a company's true economic value, then buying when the price is attractive relative to that estimate.*

## The fundamental approach

A fundamental investor believes that a [stock](/stock/)'s price should eventually reflect the underlying business's economic reality. By analyzing financial statements, competitive positioning, and management quality, an investor can estimate intrinsic value. When price diverges from value, an opportunity exists.

The process typically involves:

1. **Financial statement analysis.** Reading 10-Ks, 10-Qs, and shareholder letters to understand the business's profitability, cash generation, and balance sheet strength.
2. **Competitive analysis.** Assessing the company's market position, moats, and risks relative to competitors.
3. **Valuation modeling.** Building a discounted cash flow (DCF) model or comparable-company analysis to estimate intrinsic value.
4. **Margin of safety assessment.** Determining how far below intrinsic value the stock must trade to justify purchase.
5. **Monitoring.** Tracking actual results versus expectations to know when to sell.

## Key financial metrics

Fundamental investors examine:

- **[Earnings per share](/earnings-per-share/) and growth.** Profit per share and its trajectory.
- **Free cash flow.** Cash available after reinvestment; often more honest than accounting profit.
- **Return on equity and capital.** How much profit the business generates per dollar of shareholder or total capital.
- **Debt levels.** Leverage and financial flexibility.
- **Asset quality.** The balance sheet — are assets valuable or impaired?
- **[Dividend sustainability.](/dividend/)** Can the company afford its dividend, and will it grow?

## Advantages

1. **Deep understanding.** An investor who truly understands a business often spots risks and opportunities that market consensus misses.
2. **Conviction.** Deep analysis builds confidence to hold through market noise and drawdowns.
3. **Edge.** If the investor has genuine research advantage (private channels, superior analysis), fundamental investing can be highly profitable.
4. **Long-term focus.** Fundamental analysis naturally leads to patient, long-term holding, which compounds better than short-term trading.

## Disadvantages

1. **Time-intensive.** Thorough fundamental analysis of 100 stocks requires enormous effort. Most investors can only monitor 10–20 positions.
2. **Skill-dependent.** Fundamental analysis is an art; results depend heavily on analyst skill and judgment.
3. **Information disadvantage.** Professional investors and corporate insiders have better information than retail analysts.
4. **Wrong timing.** Even if your analysis is correct, you can be early and suffer opportunity cost.
