*A core-satellite portfolio combines a large passive index-fund core (70–90% of assets) with smaller active-management satellite positions (10–30%), balancing the stability and low costs of passive [index investing](/index-fund/) with the upside potential and active [stock-picking](/fundamental-investing/) of active management.*

## Core-satellite structure

**The core (75% example):**

- 45% total US stock market [index fund](/index-fund/).
- 15% total international stock [index fund](/index-fund/).
- 15% bond [index fund](/index-fund/).

**The satellites (25% example):**

- 10% individual high-conviction stock picks.
- 10% small-cap [value](/value-investing/) [stocks](/stock/) or sector tilt.
- 5% emerging markets or other [thematic](/top-down-investing/) positions.

The core provides stability, diversification, and low costs. The satellites are where the investor's stock-picking or sector-rotation conviction goes.

## Advantages

1. **Balanced risk-reward.** The core prevents catastrophic losses; the satellites offer upside potential.
2. **Cost control.** Most money (75%+) is in low-cost index funds. Only satellite money incurs active fees or trading costs.
3. **Psychological benefit.** For those who believe they can pick stocks, satellites provide an outlet without jeopardizing the entire portfolio.
4. **Flexibility.** The core can shift (rebalance) mechanically; satellites can be adjusted actively.
5. **Behavioral hedge.** If satellites crash or underperform, the core keeps the portfolio afloat.

## Disadvantages

1. **Complexity.** More complex than pure passive; requires monitoring both core and satellites.
2. **Satellite drag.** If satellite picks underperform (as they often do), the satellite drag reduces overall returns.
3. **Benchmark confusion.** With both core and satellites, it is harder to compare against a single benchmark.
4. **Rebalancing friction.** Rebalancing between core and satellites can be tax-inefficient and costly.

## Common satellite strategies

- **Individual [stock](/stock/) picks.** The investor's highest-conviction picks (value, growth, or dividend stocks).
- **Sector tilts.** Overweighting growth, energy, healthcare, or financials based on macro views.
- **Factor tilts.** Overweighting [value-factor](/value-factor/) or [dividend-growth](/dividend-growth-investing/) stocks within the satellite.
- **Thematic bets.** Emerging markets, tech disruption, green energy, or other themes.
- **Contrarian picks.** Cheap, out-of-favor sectors or industries.
