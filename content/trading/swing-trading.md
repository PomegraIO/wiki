*Swing trading is a trading style where positions are held for multiple days to a few weeks (anywhere from 2 days to 4 weeks), aiming to profit from price swings or temporary reversals within a longer trend. Swing traders use technical analysis, support and resistance levels, and often employ [limit orders](/limit-order) and [trailing stops](/trailing-stop-order) to manage positions. It sits between [day trading](/day-order) (hours to minutes) and longer-term [position trading](/position-trading) (months to years).*

## How swing trading works

A swing trader identifies a price pattern and enters a position betting on a short-term move. Example:

**Stock:** Microsoft (MSFT), trading in an uptrend but showing a pullback.

**Setup:**
- Price falls from $350 to $340 (a pullback).
- Swing trader identifies support at $340 and buys.
- Target: catch the rebound back to $350–$355.

**Execution:**
- **Monday:** Buy 100 shares at $341 (limit order).
- **Wednesday:** Price rises to $352; sell at $352.
- **Profit:** $11 per share × 100 = $1,100 (before commissions).

The swing trader held for only 2 days, captured a $11 move, and is now looking for the next setup. Over 10 such trades per month, if successful, the gains compound.

## Swing trading strategies

**Support and resistance:** Buy at support (expected bounce), sell at resistance (expected reversal). See support and resistance.

**Moving average crossovers:** Buy when a fast moving average crosses above a slow one (momentum); sell on the reverse cross.

**Pullback in a trend:** In an uptrend, wait for a pullback to a moving average, then buy the rebound.

**Channel trading:** Price oscillates between a high and low. Buy near the low, sell near the high. Repeat.

**Reversal patterns:** Identify chart patterns (double bottoms, head-and-shoulders, etc.) that signal reversals; trade the reversal.

**Breakout trading:** Wait for a break above resistance or below support; trade in the direction of the break.

## Advantages of swing trading

**Less time:** 1–3 hours of monitoring per day (vs. 6+ hours for day trading). You can maintain a day job (though this is risky).

**Lower stress:** Positions are held overnight and over days; no panic from rapid intraday moves.

**Lower commissions:** Fewer trades per day means lower per-day commission costs (though still relevant over time).

**Emotions more manageable:** Multi-day holds allow time to think and adjust, vs. second-to-second decision-making in scalping.

**Possible to be profitable:** Studies suggest more swing traders are profitable than day traders or scalpers (though still a minority).

## Challenges of swing trading

**Overnight risk:** Holding overnight means you are exposed to gap risk. News overnight can gap the stock down, hitting your stop-loss and locking in losses.

**Technical analysis is subjective:** What looks like support to one trader is not to another. False breakouts and fakeouts are common.

**Trend identification is hard:** You can pick the wrong trend and miss the move, or catch a reversal that continues against you.

**Still requires discipline:** Even 2–3 trades per week require daily monitoring and adherence to your plan. One bad trade can erase gains from several good ones.

**PDT rule impacts:** If you swing-trade and accidentally execute 4+ day trades in a 5-day window, you become a PDT and face restrictions. Careful tracking is required.

## Order types for swing traders

**Limit orders:** Swing traders often use [limit orders](/limit-order) to enter at support or exit at resistance, rather than [market orders](/market-order). This allows them to set and forget.

**Trailing stops:** [Trailing stop orders](/trailing-stop-order) are popular with swing traders to lock in gains as the price rises while protecting against reversals.

**GTC orders:** [Good-til-canceled](/gtc-order) orders allow swing traders to place orders today and let them sit for days, waiting for price targets.

**Brackets:** [Bracket orders](/bracket-order) combine entry with profit target and stop-loss, automating the entire trade.

## Capital requirements

Swing trading can work with less capital than day trading (which requires $25,000 minimum for PDT compliance). A swing trader could:
- Start with $3,000–$5,000 in a cash account (no borrowing, no PDT rule).
- Execute 1–2 swing trades per week without hitting the 4-day-trade PDT threshold.
- Aim for 2–5% gains per trade.

Over a year, $5,000 × 1.05^50 (50 trades, 5% each) ≈ $57,600, if all trades were successful (unrealistic, but the point is growth potential).

## Risk management for swing traders

**Position sizing:** Risking only 1–2% of account on each trade limits drawdowns.

**Stop-losses:** Always have a stop-loss (or a trailing stop). Never hold a losing trade in hopes of a reversal.

**Reward-to-risk ratio:** Aim for at least 1:2 (risk $1 to make $2). This ensures that even if you win only 40% of trades, you are still profitable over time.

**Avoiding the "swing trap":** Holding a swing trade too long in hopes of a bigger move can turn it into a long-term hold with losses. Be willing to exit if the setup breaks.

## Swing trading and taxes

Short-term capital gains (held <1 year) are taxed at ordinary income rates (up to 37%). A swing trader holding for 2 weeks has short-term gains taxable at these rates.

This significantly reduces after-tax returns. A swing trader making 5% per trade still loses 1.85% to taxes (37% × 5%), leaving ~3.15% after-tax.

For comparison, long-term capital gains (held >1 year) are taxed at 15–20%, much more favorable.
