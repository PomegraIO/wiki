*Scalping is an extremely short-term trading style where a trader holds positions for seconds to minutes, aiming to profit on small price moves ($0.01–$0.10 per share). A scalper might execute 50–200 trades per day, each with minimal profit, and rely on volume and consistency to build returns. Scalping requires speed, focus, discipline, and low commissions. It is exhausting and typically only profitable for a small percentage of traders.*

## How scalping works

A scalper watches a stock closely, looking for the smallest price moves. Example:

**Stock:** Apple (AAPL), trading around $150.

**Trade 1:**
- 9:31 a.m.: Buy 100 shares at $150.00 (using a [limit order](/limit-order) or [market order](/market-order)).
- 9:31:30 a.m.: Sell 100 shares at $150.02.
- **Profit:** $0.02 × 100 = $2 (gross; minus commissions/fees).

**Trade 2:**
- 9:32 a.m.: Buy 100 shares at $150.00.
- 9:32:45 a.m.: Sell at $150.03.
- **Profit:** $0.03 × 100 = $3.

(Repeat 50–100 times throughout the day.)

A scalper executing 100 trades with an average profit of $2 per trade = $200 profit for the day. Over 250 trading days, that is $50,000 per year (gross profit). After commissions, equipment costs, taxes, it may not be enough to justify the stress.

## Scalping strategies

**Bid-ask bounce:** Buy just inside the bid, sell just inside the ask, capturing the spread. Requires speed and tight risk control.

**Order book reading:** Watch the order book for large orders that might be about to move the price. Execute just before the move.

**Technical setups:** Scalpers often use very short-term charts (1-minute, 5-minute) and trade support/resistance levels.

**Algorithmic:** Some scalpers use algorithms to detect patterns and execute automatically (though this blurs into [high-frequency trading](/high-frequency-trading)).

**Momentum fading:** Detect a rapid move in one direction and bet it reverses slightly; profit on the reversion.

## Challenges of scalping

**Commission costs:** With 100 trades per day, commission adds up quickly. If you pay $1 per trade, that is $100 per day = $25,000 per year (assuming 250 trading days). You need substantial per-trade profits to overcome this.

**Slippage:** The gap between your intended and actual fill price. Buy at $150.00 but execute at $150.01? That eats into your thin profit margin.

**Stress and fatigue:** Watching a screen for 6.5 hours, making hundreds of split-second decisions, is mentally exhausting. Mistakes increase as the day goes on.

**Taxes:** Short-term trades are taxed at ordinary income rates (up to 37%), not long-term capital gains rates (up to 20%). This significantly reduces after-tax returns.

**Regulatory:** The [PDT rule](/pattern-day-trader) requires $25,000 minimum and caps buying power at 4×. This limits the number of shares a scalper can hold simultaneously.

## Scalping in different markets

**Equities:** Scalping is possible but challenged by commissions and spreads. Institutional-grade systems and very low commissions are needed.

**Futures:** Many scalpers trade futures because:
- Commissions per contract are low (often $3–$10 round-trip).
- Leverage is built in (you control contracts with far less capital).
- Spreads are often tighter (large market, high liquidity).
- Leverage can be 50:1 or more (vs. 4:1 for stocks).

**Forex and crypto:** Scalpers also trade foreign exchange and cryptocurrencies for similar reasons (low commissions, high leverage, continuous trading).

## Requirements for successful scalping

**Fast reflexes and focus:** You need to identify trades, execute, and exit within seconds. Daydreaming for 10 seconds costs trades.

**Good equipment:** You need:
- A fast internet connection (latency matters).
- Powerful computers (multiple monitors for different strategies/stocks).
- Professional trading software (not just a broker's web terminal).

**Low commissions:** Ideally $0.01–$0.05 per share (or fixed fees like $1–$2 per round-trip trade).

**Emotional discipline:** After losses (which are frequent in scalping), you must stick to your plan and not over-trade to "make up" losses.

**Capital:** $25,000+ to avoid PDT rule restrictions, though some scalpers trade futures or use accounts held overseas to bypass this.

## Scalping success rates

Studies and anecdotal evidence suggest:
- **Most scalpers lose money** (estimated 80–90% of retail scalpers are net negative).
- **A small percentage are profitable** (estimates 10–20%), but many of those only break even after commissions and taxes.
- **The profitable few** often have:
  - Very low commissions (institutional fees or proprietary firm backing).
  - Strong discipline and emotional control.
  - Excellent technical analysis or algorithmic edge.
  - Significant start-up capital to survive a difficult learning period.

## Scalping vs. HFT

[High-frequency traders](/high-frequency-trading) are often called "extreme scalpers." The mechanics are similar (hold for microseconds to milliseconds, profit on tiny moves, scale with volume), but HFT has:
- **Speed advantage:** Microsecond latency; scalpers can only achieve millisecond speed.
- **Capital:** Millions in infrastructure; scalpers operate with thousands to millions.
- **Algorithmic sophistication:** HFTs use machine learning; scalpers use manual trading or simpler algos.
- **Regulatory advantage:** HFTs exploit market microstructure in ways retail scalpers cannot.

A retail scalper is essentially attempting to do manually what HFT firms do algorithmically, at a huge disadvantage.
