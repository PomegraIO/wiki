*An **exposure limit** is a ceiling on the notional value or [leverage](/wiki/leverage-ratio-forex/) of a single [currency pair](/wiki/currency-pair/) or asset that a [forex broker](/wiki/broker/) allows a [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) to hold. A broker might, for example, cap [EUR/USD](/wiki/eur-gbp-euro-sterling/) positions at $5 million notional per account, or restrict [leverage](/wiki/leverage-ratio-forex/) to 50:1 on [emerging market currencies](/wiki/emerging-market-currency-pairs/). These limits protect both the broker (from [counterparty risk](/wiki/counterparty-risk/) if a [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) defaults) and the [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) (from catastrophic losses on a single bet).*

For broader [risk management](/wiki/market-risk/) frameworks, see [Position Limits](/wiki/position-limit-regulations/). For leverage mechanics, see [Forex Leverage](/wiki/forex-leverage/).

| Category | Typical Limit | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Per-pair notional** | $1M–$50M depending on [pair liquidity](/wiki/liquidity-risk/) | Prevents [concentration risk](/wiki/concentration-risk/) |
| **Max leverage** | 50:1 (retail), 500:1 (professional) | Prevents [margin calls](/wiki/margin-call-forex/) cascading into default |
| **Portfolio notional** | 2–5x account [equity](/wiki/equity-financing/) | Caps total [market risk](/wiki/market-risk/) |
| **Emerging market pairs** | Lower limits | Higher [volatility](/wiki/volatility-smile/) justifies stricter caps |
| **[Exotic currencies](/wiki/exotic-currency-pair/)** | Lowest limits | [Bid-ask spreads](/wiki/bid-ask-spread-forex/) and [liquidity](/wiki/liquidity-risk/) constraints |

## Why brokers impose exposure limits

A [forex broker](/wiki/broker/) is a [counterparty](/wiki/counterparty-risk/) to every [trade](/wiki/trade-reporting/). If a [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) has a $10 million [long EUR/USD](/wiki/long-call-ladder/) position and the [euro](/wiki/euro/) falls 20%, the [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) loses $2 million—but the broker loses it first, on its [balance sheet](/wiki/balance-sheet/). The [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) then faces a [margin call](/wiki/margin-call-forex/) and either deposits more cash or liquidates the position. But if the [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) cannot meet the [margin call](/wiki/margin-call-forex/) and defaults, the broker absorbs the loss.

To manage this [counterparty risk](/wiki/counterparty-risk/), brokers impose **exposure limits** on a per-[trader](/wiki/trading-halts/), per-pair basis. These limits serve multiple purposes:

1. **[Concentration risk](/wiki/concentration-risk/) prevention**: A [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) betting the farm on one [currency pair](/wiki/currency-pair/) is a black swan [event](/wiki/black-swan/) waiting to happen. If every [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) at the broker took [leveraged](/wiki/forex-leverage/) [positions](/wiki/position-trading/) in [GBP/JPY](/wiki/currency-pair/), a surprise [Bank of England](/wiki/bank-of-england/) policy shift could trigger simultaneous [margin calls](/wiki/margin-call-forex/) and a wave of [liquidations](/wiki/liquidation/) that bankrupts multiple [traders](/wiki/trading-halts/) at once.

2. **[Liquidity](/wiki/liquidity-risk/) management**: Some [currency pairs](/wiki/currency-pair/) are less [liquid](/wiki/liquidity-pool/) than others. A broker might allow a $10 million [position](/wiki/position-limit-regulations/) in [EUR/USD](/wiki/eur-gbp-euro-sterling/) (the most [liquid](/wiki/liquidity-pool/) [pair](/wiki/currency-pair/)) but only $500,000 in [USD/TRY](/wiki/currency-pair/) (Turkish lira, far less [liquid](/wiki/liquidity-pool/)). If the [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) suddenly tries to [liquidate](/wiki/liquidation/) the [TRY position](/wiki/position-trading/), the broker might not find [buyers](/wiki/buyer-inspection-period/) and suffer a loss.

3. **Systemic risk control**: During [financial crisis](/wiki/lehman-brothers-collapse/) episodes (e.g., the [2008 crisis](/wiki/bear-stearns-collapse/)), [volatility](/wiki/volatility-smile/) spikes and [correlation](/wiki/correlation-coefficient/) across [currency pairs](/wiki/currency-pair/) rises. A broker with many [traders](/wiki/trading-halts/) holding [leveraged](/wiki/forex-leverage/) positions in multiple [pairs](/wiki/currency-pair/) could face [margin calls](/wiki/margin-call-forex/) cascading across its entire client base simultaneously. Exposure limits cap the broker's aggregate notional risk.

## How exposure limits work in practice

Most brokers publicly post exposure limit policies. A typical regime for a retail [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) might state:
- Maximum notional per [major currency pair](/wiki/major-currency-pairs/) ([EUR/USD](/wiki/eur-gbp-euro-sterling/), [GBP/USD](/wiki/gbp-usd-sterling/)): $5 million
- Maximum notional per [minor currency pair](/wiki/minor-currency-pair/) ([EUR/GBP](/wiki/eur-gbp-euro-sterling/)): $2 million
- Maximum notional per [exotic currency pair](/wiki/exotic-currency-pair/): $500,000
- Maximum total [portfolio notional](/wiki/asset-allocation/) (all [positions](/wiki/position-trading/) combined): $20 million
- Maximum [leverage](/wiki/leverage-ratio-forex/): 50:1 (this implies that with a $100,000 account, you can control up to $5 million in total notional)

If a [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) already has a $5 million [long EUR/USD](/wiki/long-call-ladder/) position, the broker's [risk management](/wiki/market-risk/) system will reject a new [buy order](/wiki/buy-and-hold-strategy/) for [EUR/USD](/wiki/eur-gbp-euro-sterling/). The [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) must either reduce the existing [position](/wiki/position-trading/) or move capital to another [pair](/wiki/currency-pair/).

## Exposure limits vs. position limits

"Exposure limit" and "[position limit](/wiki/position-limit-regulations/)" are often used interchangeably, but they have subtle differences:

- **Exposure limit**: A broker-level cap on how much notional risk a [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) may take in a single [currency pair](/wiki/currency-pair/).
- **Position limit**: A [regulatory](/wiki/regulation-best-interest/) or [exchange](/wiki/stock-exchange/)-level cap on how many [contracts](/wiki/futures-contract/) or [shares](/wiki/common-stock/) a [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) (or a [firm](/wiki/broker/)) may hold in a single instrument, designed to prevent [market manipulation](/wiki/market-surveillance/) and [flash crashes](/wiki/flash-crash-2010/).

[Forex brokers](/wiki/broker/) impose exposure limits unilaterally to protect themselves. [Regulatory position limits](/wiki/position-limit-regulations/) are imposed by [exchanges](/wiki/stock-exchange/) or [central regulators](/wiki/securities-and-exchange-commission/) to protect market integrity.

## Exposure limits and leverage caps

Exposure limits and [leverage](/wiki/forex-leverage/) caps are tightly linked. If a broker caps [leverage](/wiki/leverage-ratio-forex/) at 50:1 and a [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) has a $100,000 account, the [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) can control up to $5 million in notional value. The exposure limit on each [pair](/wiki/currency-pair/) then further subdivides this total. A [leverage](/wiki/leverage-ratio-forex/) cap of 50:1 and a per-pair exposure limit of $5 million together ensure that no single [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) can control an excessive notional amount.

Regulatory changes in 2018 reduced [leverage](/wiki/forex-leverage/) for retail [traders](/wiki/trading-halts/) in the US to 50:1 and in Europe to 30:1. These caps directly reduce exposure limits because they shrink the total notional a [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) can hold. A [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) with a $10,000 account can control only $500,000 notional under 50:1 [leverage](/wiki/forex-leverage/).

## Exposure limits and [margin requirements](/wiki/initial-margin/)

Brokers also impose **[margin requirements](/wiki/initial-margin/)**, which interact with exposure limits. A [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) must maintain a certain percentage of the notional position value as [margin](/wiki/forex-margin/) (collateral) in the account. A 2% [margin requirement](/wiki/initial-margin/) on a $5 million [position](/wiki/position-trading/) requires $100,000 of [margin](/wiki/forex-margin/). If the account shrinks below this amount due to losses, a [margin call](/wiki/margin-call-forex/) is triggered and the broker may [liquidate](/wiki/liquidation/) the [position](/wiki/position-trading/).

This interplay ensures that exposure limits are enforced: a [trader](/wiki/trading-halts/) cannot accumulate a [position](/wiki/position-trading/) that would exceed account [equity](/wiki/equity-financing/) because [margin requirements](/wiki/initial-margin/) will block further [leverage](/wiki/forex-leverage/).

## Exposure limits and [liquidity](/wiki/liquidity-risk/)

Exposure limits are also calibrated to [liquidity](/wiki/liquidity-risk/). [Major currency pairs](/wiki/major-currency-pairs/) like [EUR/USD](/wiki/eur-gbp-euro-sterling/) trade $500+ billion daily; a $10 million [position](/wiki/position-trading/) is microscopic. A broker can safely allow large exposure limits on these [pairs](/wiki/currency-pair/). But [emerging market currencies](/wiki/emerging-market-currency-pairs/) like [USD/TRY](/wiki/currency-pair/) (Turkish lira) or [USD/RUB](/wiki/currency-pair/) (Russian ruble) trade only billions daily and have wide [bid-ask spreads](/wiki/bid-ask-spread-forex/). A $5 million [position](/wiki/position-trading/) in [USD/TRY](/wiki/currency-pair/) could exhaust [liquidity](/wiki/liquidity-pool/) and lead to [slippage](/wiki/slippage-management/) and [execution](/wiki/order-execution-speed/) problems.
