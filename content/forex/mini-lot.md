*A **mini lot** is 10,000 units of the base currency in a [currency pair](/currency-pair). It is one-tenth the size of a [standard lot](/standard-lot), producing $1 of profit or loss per [pip](/pip) for most pairs. Mini lots are the default position size for retail FX traders with accounts from $5,000 to $100,000.*

For even smaller sizes, see [micro lot](/micro-lot); for the institutional default, see [standard lot](/standard-lot).

## The math of a mini lot

On EUR/USD at 1.0850, a mini lot is 10,000 euros worth $10,850. Each pip move is $1. A 50-pip winning trade is $50; a 100-pip losing trade is $100. The $1-per-pip figure is easy to remember and easy to calculate: 50 pips × $1 = $50. Many retail traders choose mini lots specifically because the math is so clean.

[Margin](/forex-margin) requirements for a mini lot are also manageable. At 50:1 leverage, a mini lot on EUR/USD requires about $217 in [margin](/forex-margin) (10,850 / 50). At 100:1 leverage, it requires about $108. This puts mini-lot trading within reach of someone with $5,000–$10,000 in their trading account.

## Who trades mini lots?

Retail traders make up the bulk of mini-lot volume. A trader with a $10,000 account might trade 1–3 mini lots per position, keeping total exposure to a manageable fraction of capital. A trader with $50,000 might trade 5–10 mini lots. The leverage is the same as with standard lots, but the capital requirement is much lower.

Some small hedge funds and professional traders use mini lots in tandem with other positions — not because they need the small size, but because it allows flexibility. If a strategy calls for 350,000 units of exposure, they might trade 3.5 standard lots or 35 mini lots, depending on the market conditions and whether fractional sizing is useful.

## Mini lots and risk management

The $1-per-pip structure makes risk management clean. If you decide to risk $50 on a trade, you know immediately that you can tolerate a 50-pip loss. If you place a 10-mini-lot position and it moves 50 pips against you, you lose exactly $500. This certainty is valuable for discipline.

For comparison, a trader using standard lots must mentally divide by 10, and small errors compound into larger mistakes.

## Execution and spreads

Mini lots execute with the same [spreads](/forex-spread) as standard lots on [major pairs](/major-currency-pair). A broker offering 2-pip spreads on EUR/USD offers them to mini-lot traders as well. This is a huge advantage over [micro lots](/micro-lot), which sometimes face wider spreads on some brokers.

On [exotic pairs](/exotic-currency-pair), spreads on mini lots are the same as on standard lots — both are tight by exotic standards. You do not pay extra for using mini size.

## Psychological weight

Many retail traders find that mini lots remove the paralyzing fear of larger positions without sacrificing meaningful profit. On $10,000 capital, a 50-pip win on a mini lot is $50 — 0.5% of your account. That is real profit, enough to compound into gains over time, but small enough to be psychologically tolerable. Many traders report that this reduces emotional decision-making compared to positions they feel are "too big."
