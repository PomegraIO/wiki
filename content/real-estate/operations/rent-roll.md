*A **rent roll** is the foundational operational record of a [commercial](/commercial-real-estate/) or multifamily property: a line-by-line inventory of every active lease, showing tenant name, lease commencement and expiration dates, rentable square footage, monthly and annual rent, and any special terms. It is simultaneously a snapshot of current occupancy and the skeleton of cash-flow forecasts.*

## The property manager's master list

A rent roll is to real estate what a [balance sheet](/balance-sheet/) is to accounting: the authoritative statement of the business at a point in time. When a property manager logs into the property management system each morning, the rent roll shows which units are occupied, which are vacant, how much rent is due from each tenant, and which leases are expiring soon. For a 50-unit apartment complex, the rent roll has 50 rows (or fewer, depending on [vacancy](/vacancy-rate-property/)). For a 200,000 square foot office building with 30 tenants, it might have 30 rows if each tenant occupies contiguous space, or hundreds if suites are subdivided among multiple occupants.

The rent roll is the backbone of a property's [net operating income](/net-operating-income/) model. Start with total annual contracted rent, subtract vacancy loss, subtract concessions (free-rent periods, temporary reductions), and add ancillary income (parking, storage, amenity fees). The result is effective gross income, which is then reduced by operating expenses to arrive at [net operating income](/net-operating-income/).

## Standardized format

Most institutional property managers and [REITs](/real-estate-investment-trust/) use a standardized rent-roll format:

| Unit | Tenant | Sq. Ft. | Move-In | Expires | Monthly Rent | Annual Rent | Notes |
|------|--------|--------|---------|---------|--------------|-------------|-------|
| 101 | Acme Corp | 2,000 | 2022-01-15 | 2027-01-14 | $4,000 | $48,000 | Renewal option |
| 102 | Vacant | 1,500 | — | — | — | — | Available |
| 103 | Beta LLC | 1,500 | 2023-06-01 | 2025-05-31 | $2,250 | $27,000 | Triple-net |
| 201 | Gamma Inc | 3,000 | 2020-11-01 | 2030-10-31 | $7,200 | $86,400 | Expansion option |

This format reveals occupancy at a glance and highlights maturity. A cluster of expirations in a single year signals concentrated re-leasing risk; a staggered pattern of maturities suggests more stable, diversified cash flow.

## Uses in financial reporting and analysis

Buyers conduct exhaustive rent-roll due diligence when acquiring a property. The seller provides a certified rent roll, and the buyer's team reconciles it against actual signed leases, rent-payment records, and tenant communications. Discrepancies—a tenant listed on the roll but no executed lease, or a lease indicating higher rent than the roll shows—must be resolved. A missing or misrepresented lease can materially affect [valuation](/discounted-cash-flow-valuation/).

Lenders similarly require current, certified rent rolls before advancing mortgage funds. The rent roll is used to calculate debt service coverage ratios and to model cash-flow stability. A lender will scrutinize the quality of tenants, the concentration of rent among a few large occupants, and the lease-maturity schedule to assess credit risk.

Accountants use rent rolls to confirm recorded rental revenue and to reconcile accounts receivable. If the rent roll shows a tenant owing three months of back rent, that obligation must appear on the [balance sheet](/balance-sheet/) as either accounts receivable or bad-debt expense.

## Lease abstraction and the rent roll

A rent roll is typically derived from [lease abstraction](/lease-abstraction/). Each lease is reviewed, key terms are extracted, and the results are consolidated into the rent roll. For a small property with five leases, a single spreadsheet might suffice. For a large [REIT](/real-estate-investment-trust/) with thousands of properties and hundreds of thousands of leases, the rent roll is maintained in a centralized database that feeds analytics and forecasting tools.

Updates to the rent roll occur when:
- A new lease is executed (new tenant or unit added)
- A lease expires and the space is vacated
- A lease is renewed, extended, or amended (rent changed, term extended)
- A tenant is evicted or surrenders early
- A [vacancy](/vacancy-rate-property/) is filled

Institutional investors require rigorous controls to keep the rent roll current and accurate. Monthly or quarterly reconciliations identify discrepancies between the rent roll and property management records. Stale or inaccurate rent rolls lead to miscalculated [net operating income](/net-operating-income/) and mispriced property valuations.

## Occupancy and rent growth

The rent roll reveals the property's occupancy rate—the percentage of rentable space under active lease. A property with 48 of 50 units leased has a 96% occupancy rate. The roll also shows rent growth by comparing new leases to rolling expirations: if expiring tenants paid $1,500 per month and new leases for the same space are $1,650, the property has achieved 10% rent growth during the turnover.

Analysts use historical rent-roll comparisons to estimate future rent trajectories. A property in a strong market might see consistent 3–4% annual rent growth as tenants churn; a weak market might see flat or negative growth. Over a long holding period, rent growth is a material driver of value; the rent roll is the granular data behind such projections.

## Special terms and obligations

Rent rolls often include columns for special terms that affect actual cash receipt:

- **Tenant improvement allowance** – a one-time upfront payment to the tenant to offset build-out costs; reduces cash collected during the lease's first months
- **Free-rent periods** – common in downturns or for high-value tenants; contractually zero rent for an initial period
- **Rent abatement** – temporary rent relief, often due to disruption or remediation
- **Options** – renewal, expansion, or termination rights that create contingencies in future cash flows
- **Pass-throughs** – in triple-net structures, the tenant's share of real estate taxes and common-area maintenance, invoiced separately from base rent

A complete rent roll documents these provisions, because they affect the timing and certainty of cash flow.

## Portfolio-level aggregation

For a multi-property owner or [REIT](/real-estate-investment-trust/), individual property rent rolls are consolidated into a portfolio-level summary. This portfolio rent roll might show total portfolio occupancy, average rent per square foot by property type or geography, and lease-maturity schedules that highlight concentration risk and reinvestment opportunities.

## Auditing and certification

Large institutional properties require formal rent-roll certification, often by an independent third party (auditor, appraiser, or property manager) who verifies each lease against signed documents and confirms the accuracy of rent figures, expiration dates, and occupancy status. This certification is especially critical in real estate securitizations, where investors rely on the accuracy of the underlying rent roll to model cash flows and credit risk.
