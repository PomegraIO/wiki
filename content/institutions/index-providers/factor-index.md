*A **factor index** is a systematic, rules-based index that selects and weights stocks according to quantitative factors — valuation metrics, profitability, size, momentum — rather than market [capitalization](/wiki/market-capitalization/). A [cap-weighted index](/wiki/cap-weighted-index/) like the S&P 500 sizes positions by market cap; a factor index sizes them by [dividend yield](/wiki/dividend-yield/), earnings growth, or [price-to-book ratio](/wiki/price-to-book-ratio/), aiming to tilt the portfolio toward economically undervalued or fundamentally strong firms.*

## From passive to smart beta

Traditional [index funds](/wiki/index-fund/) track [market-cap-weighted indexes](/wiki/cap-weighted-index/) like the S&P 500 or MSCI World, giving larger companies proportionally larger weight. This is "passive" — minimal human judgment, low cost, broad diversification.

Factor indexes introduced a middle ground called "[smart beta](/wiki/smart-beta/)" — still rule-based and transparent (no active manager discretion), but the rules select or weight stocks by fundamental traits rather than price alone. A [dividend factor](/wiki/dividend-factor-investing/) index overweights high-dividend-yield stocks. A [value index](/wiki/value-investing/) overweights cheap stocks (low [P/E](/wiki/price-to-earnings-ratio/), high [dividend yield](/wiki/dividend-yield/)). A [momentum index](/wiki/momentum-factor/) overweights recent winners.

## Construction: the factor definition

The key step is defining the factor. A hypothetical **Quality Factor Index** might:

1. **Compute quality scores** for each stock: [return on equity](/wiki/return-on-equity/), [operating margin](/wiki/operating-margin/), [cash flow](/wiki/free-cash-flow/) generation, [debt](/wiki/debt-to-equity-ratio/) levels.
2. **Rank all stocks** in the universe by quality score (highest to lowest).
3. **Select top 200 stocks** or include all with score > median.
4. **Equal-weight or optimize weights** by quality score. A stock with ROE = 25% gets larger weight than one with ROE = 10%.

A [value index](/wiki/value-fund/) might screen for [P/B ratio](/wiki/price-to-book-ratio/) < 1.0, [dividend yield](/wiki/dividend-yield/) > 3%, [P/E ratio](/wiki/price-to-earnings-ratio/) < 15x.

## Major factor families

**[Value](/wiki/value-investing/):** overweights cheap stocks (low [earnings yield](/wiki/earnings-yield/), high [dividend yield](/wiki/dividend-yield/), low [P/B](/wiki/price-to-book-ratio/)). Rebalances annually or quarterly, locking in gains from winners and buying underperformers.

**[Momentum](/wiki/momentum-factor/):** overweights stocks with strong recent performance (12-month return in top quintile). Rebalances monthly or quarterly.

**[Quality](/wiki/quality-factor/):** high [profitability](/wiki/profit-margin-ratio/), low [leverage](/wiki/debt-to-equity-ratio/), stable [earnings](/wiki/earnings-per-share/). Aims to own the "best" companies.

**[Size](/wiki/size-factor/):** small-cap stocks tend to outperform large-cap over long periods (the "size premium"). A [small-cap factor index](/wiki/size-factor-small-cap/) tilts toward companies with market cap in the $1–10 billion range.

**Dividend:** overweights high-dividend-payers ([dividend aristocrats](/wiki/dividend-aristocrats/), [dividend growth investing](/wiki/dividend-growth-investing/)).

## Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths:**
- Transparency: rules are published and fixed. No hidden manager bias.
- Lower cost than [actively managed funds](/wiki/actively-managed-fund/) — less research staff, more mechanical rebalancing.
- [Factor premiums](/wiki/factor-investing/) are documented: value, momentum, quality, and size have positive long-term expected returns across decades.
- Customization: investors can choose or combine factors (e.g., "quality + dividend" index).

**Weaknesses:**
- [Factor crowding](/wiki/crowded-trade/): if many investors own the same factor index, prices of high-quality or high-momentum stocks inflate, and factor returns compress.
- Factor crashes: [momentum](/wiki/momentum-factor/) performs terribly in downturns (when risk-off reversals occur); [value](/wiki/value-investing/) underperforms in growth rallies.
- [Rebalancing costs](/wiki/asset-rebalancing/): mechanical selling of winners and buying of losers locks in losses when trends persist.
- Factor definitions evolve: a "quality" stock today may fall out of grace tomorrow if accounting changes or competitive position shifts.

## Implementation vehicles

**[ETFs](/wiki/etf/):** Most factor indexes are offered as [factor ETFs](/wiki/factor-etf/) — tradable, liquid, low-cost vehicles ([expense ratios](/wiki/expense-ratio/) of 0.3–0.6% annually). [Ishares](/wiki/factor-etf/), [Vanguard](/wiki/value-fund-strategy/), and [Schwab](/wiki/charles-schwab/) offer [factor-based ETFs](/wiki/factor-etf/).

**Index licenses:** Index providers (MSCI, S&P, Russell) license factor indexes to fund managers. A fund family might create multiple factor-tilted funds on the same underlying index.

**Direct indexing:** Wealthy investors can construct a custom [factor index](/wiki/factor-index/) via [direct indexing](/wiki/direct-indexing-vehicle/) — buying individual stocks that meet factor criteria, minimizing tax drag via [tax-loss harvesting](/wiki/tax-loss-harvesting/).

## Academic foundations

The [Fama-French three-factor model](/wiki/fama-french-three-factor-model/) (market, size, value) and later [five-factor model](/wiki/fama-french-five-factor-model/) (adding profitability and investment) form the intellectual foundation. These factors have statistically significant, economically meaningful long-term risk premiums. [Smart-beta ETFs](/wiki/smart-beta-etf/) operationalize this academic insight at scale.

The [Carhart four-factor model](/wiki/carhart-four-factor-model/) adds momentum, further documenting a factor premium.

## Comparison to active management

A factor index is *more passive* than a traditional active fund (rules, no discretion, lower cost) but *less passive* than market-cap indexing (intentionally tilts away from market weights toward specific characteristics). For investors skeptical of active manager skill but confident in factor premiums, factor indexes offer a disciplined middle ground.
