*[Fitch Ratings](/wiki/fitch-ratings/) is one of the "Big Three" [credit rating agencies](/wiki/credit-rating/), alongside Moody's and Standard & Poor's, providing independent assessments of default risk on [bonds](/wiki/bond/), loans, and financial obligations. Founded in 1914, Fitch rates corporate debt, sovereign bonds, and [financial institutions](/wiki/credit-rating/) globally, influencing [borrowing costs](/wiki/cost-of-debt/) and investor demand.*

## Rating scale and default probability mapping

Fitch grades range from **AAA** (highest quality, minimal default risk) to **D** (in default). Debt rated **BBB-** or higher is [investment-grade](/wiki/investment-grade-bond/); below that is [speculative-grade](/wiki/speculative-grade/) or [junk](/wiki/high-yield-bond/). Each notch carries implied default probability: AAA companies default less than 0.1% over 10 years; B-rated firms default 5–10%. Investors use these mappings to set required returns; a company downgraded from BBB to BB sees [borrowing costs](/wiki/cost-of-debt/) jump 300+ basis points, raising [weighted average cost of capital](/wiki/weighted-average-cost-of-capital/) and hurting [valuation](/wiki/enterprise-value/).

## Methodology: financial metrics and qualitative factors

Fitch analyzes [leverage](/wiki/debt-to-ebitda-ratio/), [profitability](/wiki/return-on-equity/), [liquidity](/wiki/liquidity-risk/), and [interest coverage](/wiki/interest-coverage-ratio/) to assess default risk. Beyond numbers, it evaluates [management quality](/wiki/board-of-directors/), competitive position, industry cyclicality, and regulatory environment. A telecom firm with stable [cash flows](/wiki/free-cash-flow/) but facing tech disruption may receive a lower rating than financial metrics suggest. Sovereigns are assessed on debt sustainability, [GDP growth](/wiki/gross-domestic-product/), and institutional quality.

## Sovereign and corporate distinctions

Sovereign ratings cap corporate ratings in most cases—a country's companies cannot be safer than the country itself. Fitch's sovereign rating reflects debt sustainability, foreign exchange [reserves](/wiki/central-bank-independence/), and political stability. During the [Greek debt crisis](/wiki/greek-debt-crisis/), Fitch downgraded Greece multiple times, forcing high yields on [government bonds](/wiki/government-bond-auction/). Corporate ratings remain independent if businesses have diversified revenue streams or are foreign-owned with hard-currency assets.

## Rating revisions and outlooks

Fitch issues [rating outlooks](/wiki/rating-outlook/) (positive, stable, negative) signaling likely direction within 12 months, and [rating watches](/wiki/rating-watch/) for imminent changes. A negative outlook often precedes a downgrade; a positive outlook suggests possible upgrade. Companies announce major transactions, earnings, or regulatory changes, and Fitch places them on watch. [Material adverse changes](/wiki/material-adverse-change/) (e.g., loss of major customers) can trigger immediate downgrades, spiking [credit spreads](/wiki/credit-spread/) and [bond yields](/wiki/bond-yield-curve-risk/).

## Fitch Revisions and industry-specific drivers

[Fitch revisions](/wiki/fitch-revision/) follow sector shifts: healthcare consolidation, energy transitions, or tech disruption reshape competitive moats. During [stagflation](/wiki/stagflation/) cycles, firms with pricing power keep ratings; those with margin-squeezed models face downgrades. [Rating migration](/wiki/rating-migration/) analyses track the probability that a BBB firm drifts to BB within 3 years; [rating drift](/wiki/rating-drift/) has historically accelerated during recessions.

## Structured finance and CLO/CDO ratings

Fitch rates [collateralized loan obligations (CLOs)](/wiki/collateralized-loan-obligation/) and [collateralized debt obligations (CDOs)](/wiki/collateralized-debt-obligation/), assigning [tranches](/wiki/tranche/) from AAA senior to equity. The 2008 crisis exposed rating inflation: agencies gave AAA ratings to risky [mortgage-backed securities](/wiki/mortgage-backed-security/), leading to widespread defaults. Post-crisis reforms required more transparent [stress testing](/wiki/stress-testing/) and higher [overcollateralization](/wiki/overcollateralization/) levels, but debate continues over agency conflicts of interest.

## ESG considerations and methodology evolution

Modern Fitch ratings increasingly incorporate environmental, social, and governance factors. A utility facing coal-to-renewables transition may receive a negative outlook due to [stranded asset](/wiki/asset-impairment/) risk. Banks with weak [compliance](/wiki/compliance-testing-regime/) cultures face downgrades. Fitch publishes ESG scores alongside traditional metrics. This reflects investor demand for forward-looking risk assessment and potential regulatory changes.

## Regulatory framework and anti-manipulation rules

Rating agencies operate under [SEC regulation](/wiki/securities-and-exchange-commission/), with rules against conflicts of interest. However, issuers pay Fitch to rate their debt, creating an inherent incentive to rate favorably. The SEC monitors for [rating inflation](/wiki/rating-methodology/), but agencies remain powerful gatekeepers. A downgrade by all three major agencies can trigger [covenant violations](/wiki/debt-covenant-type/) in loan agreements, forcing refinancing at punitive rates.
