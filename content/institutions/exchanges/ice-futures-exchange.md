*ICE Futures (part of the [Intercontinental Exchange](/wiki/ice-intercontinental-exchange/)) is one of the world's largest futures exchanges, trading [energy futures](/wiki/crude-oil/) ([crude oil](/wiki/crude-oil/), natural gas), [metals](/wiki/copper/), agricultural [commodities](/wiki/commodity-futures-trading-commission/), and financial derivatives. It is the global benchmark for many commodity markets.*

## History and structure

ICE was founded in 1997 as an electronic platform for energy derivatives trading, an alternative to floor-based exchanges. It acquired the [Intercontinental Exchange](/wiki/ice-intercontinental-exchange/) (hence the name) and in 2013 merged with NYSE Euronext, becoming a parent holding company that operates multiple venues. ICE Futures operates several exchanges:

- **ICE Futures U.S.** (in New York)—primarily energy and agriculture
- **ICE Futures Europe** (in London)—Brent crude, soft commodities, and financials
- **ICE Futures Singapore**—Asia-Pacific energy and commodity trading

The exchange is global in scope, with trading in U.S., European, and Asian time zones. This allows [Brent crude](/wiki/brent-crude/) futures to trade nearly 24/5.

## Major markets and contracts

**Energy:** ICE is the world's largest energy futures exchange. [Crude oil](/wiki/crude-oil/) futures (both [WTI](/wiki/crude-oil-wti-brent/) and [Brent](/wiki/brent-crude/)) trade in massive volumes. [Natural gas](/wiki/natural-gas/) futures, [heating oil](/wiki/heating-oil/), and other refined products also trade. These are global benchmark prices—when OPEC announces production cuts, the impact flows through ICE [futures](/wiki/futures-contract/) prices within minutes.

**Metals:** [Copper](/wiki/copper/), [gold](/wiki/gold/), [silver](/wiki/silver/), [aluminum](/wiki/aluminum/), and other metals trade as futures. These serve industrial users (construction, electronics) and speculators/traders.

**Agriculture (Softs):** [Coffee](/wiki/coffee/), [sugar](/wiki/sugar/), [cocoa](/wiki/cocoa/) are major agricultural [commodities](/wiki/commodity-futures-trading-commission/) traded on ICE Futures Europe. These prices are often used as global benchmarks for farmers, exporters, and food companies.

**Financial derivatives:** Interest rate futures, [currency futures](/wiki/currency-futures/), and equity index futures also trade, though ICE competes with CME on financials.

## Trading volume and liquidity

ICE Futures is consistently one of the top three derivatives exchanges globally by volume (along with CME Group and Eurex). The energy complex is most liquid—[WTI crude](/wiki/crude-oil-wti-brent/) futures often trade 1–2 million contracts daily, making it the world's most-traded commodity contract.

Liquidity is critical for [price discovery](/wiki/price-discovery/). The volume at ICE Futures ensures that [bid-ask spreads](/wiki/bid-ask-spread/) are tight, allowing large trades to execute without massive slippage. A trader wanting to buy 10,000 barrels of [crude oil](/wiki/crude-oil/) can exit the position in seconds without moving the market significantly.

## Clearing and settlement

ICE Futures contracts are cleared through [ICE Clear Credit](/wiki/ice-clear-credit/) and ICE Clear Europe, which provide [central counterparty](/wiki/central-counterparty-clearing/) clearing (CCP). This means every buyer and seller faces the clearinghouse as the counterparty, removing [counterparty risk](/wiki/counterparty-risk/). Daily marking-to-market and [margin](/wiki/initial-margin/) requirements ensure that losses are realized and collected daily, reducing [default risk](/wiki/default-rate/).

## Hedging and speculation

ICE Futures serves two main customer types:

**Hedgers** (producers, end-users) reduce [commodity risk](/wiki/commodity-price-hedging/). An airline hedges [fuel costs](/wiki/crude-oil/) by selling [futures](/wiki/futures-contract/) contracts. A coffee roaster hedges [coffee](/wiki/coffee/) prices by buying [futures](/wiki/futures-contract/). A mining company hedges [copper](/wiki/copper/) prices. These hedges protect against price swings.

**Speculators and traders** profit from price movements. They use technical analysis, [momentum](/wiki/momentum-trading/), and [carry trades](/wiki/carry-trade-pairs/) to trade the [futures](/wiki/futures-contract/) market. High [leverage](/wiki/leverage-ratio-forex/) is common—traders can control $1 million of [crude oil](/wiki/crude-oil/) with $50,000 of [margin](/wiki/initial-margin/).

## Price discovery and macro markets

ICE Futures prices influence global commodity markets in real-time. When [crude oil](/wiki/crude-oil/) futures spike on geopolitical news, refineries and retailers immediately adjust plans. Agricultural [futures](/wiki/futures-contract/) prices signal expectations about harvests and inflation, influencing [central bank](/wiki/central-bank/) policy discussions.

The exchange also supports [commodity index funds](/wiki/commodity-index-fund/) and [commodity ETFs](/wiki/commodity-etf/), which track ICE (or [CME](/wiki/cme-group/)) futures prices. A passive investor in a [commodity ETF](/wiki/commodity-etf/) is ultimately exposed to ICE Futures prices.

## Regulation and oversight

ICE Futures is regulated by the [CFTC](/wiki/commodity-futures-trading-commission/) (Commodity Futures Trading Commission) in the U.S. and by the [Financial Conduct Authority](/wiki/financial-conduct-authority/) (FCA) in the UK. The exchange must maintain financial resources, manage [operational risk](/wiki/operational-risk/), and enforce [market surveillance](/wiki/market-surveillance/) to prevent manipulation.

In recent years, the CFTC has increased scrutiny of [algorithmic trading](/wiki/algorithmic-trading/) and [flash crashes](/wiki/flash-crash-2010/) in futures markets. ICE has invested in technology to prevent disruptive trading and market manipulation.

## Competition and integration

ICE Futures competes primarily with [CME Group](/wiki/cme-group/), which operates the NYMEX (energy) and COMEX (metals) futures exchanges. Each has advantages: ICE dominates Brent crude and agricultural softs; CME dominates U.S. interest rate futures and equity indices. They often trade each other's markets indirectly through price correlation and [spread trading](/wiki/spread-trading-futures/).

As a holding company, ICE also owns major data businesses (acquired Thomson Reuters' market data unit in 2016) and other exchanges and venues. This vertical integration gives ICE leverage to cross-sell data and trading services.

## Electronic trading and 24-hour markets

Unlike traditional floor exchanges, ICE Futures operates electronic-only trading (since 2000 for energy, later for other products). This allows overnight and weekend trading when global markets overlap time zones. [WTI crude](/wiki/crude-oil-wti-brent/) trades nearly continuously, with lower volume in off-peak hours but still liquid enough for traders to execute large positions.

This 24-hour capability has driven adoption: funds and corporations can hedge at any time, not just during the exchange's opening hours.
