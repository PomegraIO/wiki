The VictoryShares Core Intermediate [Bond ETF](/bond-etf/) (UITB) is a passively managed exchange-traded fund that holds a broad basket of intermediate-maturity [bonds](/bond/) rated investment grade by major rating agencies. Sponsored by Invesco and VictoryShares, the fund aims to provide simple exposure to the broad middle of the fixed-income market — bonds with maturities typically between three and ten years that pay steady coupons and carry the credit quality that distinguishes investment-grade debt from speculative borrowers.

The fund tracks an index that spans U.S. Treasury securities, [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/) from large and mid-size corporations across sectors, and agency mortgage-backed securities issued or guaranteed by [Fannie Mae](/fannie-mae/), [Freddie Mac](/freddie-mac/), and Ginnie Mae. This [diversification](/diversification/) across issuer types and credit profiles is the core appeal: a single holding delivers exposure to the government's borrowing, the debt of thousands of profitable firms, and the U.S. housing market's embedded financing — all wrapped in the relative safety of intermediate maturity and investment-grade quality. There is no active manager making directional bets; instead the fund simply holds the basket the index defines and rebalances periodically to maintain its target exposure.

The passive approach keeps costs low. The fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is measured in basis points — a fraction of what an actively managed bond fund would charge — because the fund requires no research team or portfolio manager engaged in security selection. Liquidity is straightforward: as an exchange-traded product, UITB trades during market hours at real-time prices, allowing investors to buy and sell shares flexibly without the daily-redemption constraints of a traditional [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/).

The diversification across these three broad categories — government, corporate, and mortgage-backed — provides meaningful stability. Treasury prices move on interest-rate expectations and Fed policy; corporate bonds also reflect earnings outlook and credit conditions; mortgage-backed securities respond to both rates and the health of the housing market. These forces do not always move in lockstep, so holding all three dampens volatility compared to focusing on just one category. The intermediate maturity bucket is the sweet spot for many investors: it offers better yield than short-term bonds but avoids the sharp price swings that long-term bonds experience when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) rise unexpectedly.

The fund is designed for conservative investors seeking a stable, diversified source of current income from bonds without the complexity of building a [bond ladder](/bond-ladder/) themselves. It fits naturally into a portfolio as the fixed-income core holding — providing ballast against [stock market](/stock-market/) volatility while generating a yield that varies with prevailing interest rates. Because it is intermediate in maturity, it is less sensitive to interest-rate changes than long-term bond funds but still captures more yield than short-term money-market alternatives. The trade-off is clear: in a rising-rate environment, the market value of the bonds held will fall, and investors who sell before maturity lock in losses; in a falling-rate environment, the bonds gain value and offer capital appreciation on top of their coupons.

[Credit risk](/credit-risk/) is minimal given the investment-grade requirement, though economic downturns that trigger rating downgrades or defaults in the broader corporate market will eventually erode fund holdings. Mortgage-backed securities carry additional [prepayment risk](/prepayment-risk/) — in a falling-rate environment, homeowners refinance their mortgages faster than expected, forcing reinvestment of principal at lower yields. [Interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/) dominates for most holders: UITB's intermediate duration means its value is inversely related to broad bond-market yields. Investors should view the fund as a core holding meant to be held through [market cycles](/market-cycle/), not as a trading vehicle or a substitute for cash. For those building a diversified portfolio, UITB provides foundational fixed-income exposure with minimal friction and genuine breadth.
