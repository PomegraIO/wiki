United Health Products, Inc. (ticker UEEC) is a medical device company focused on a single product category: hemostatic gauze — absorbable pads and strips designed to stop bleeding in surgical and wound-care settings. The company develops, manufactures, and markets its flagship product, CelluSTAT (formerly branded as HemoStyp), a gauze made from neutralized regenerated cellulose that conforms to wounds and forms a gel upon contact with blood and other body fluids, accelerating the clotting cascade. The company was incorporated in 1997 and is based in Mount Laurel, New Jersey. Its shares trade on the [over-the-counter markets](/over-the-counter-market/) under the ticker UEEC.

CelluSTAT is an all-natural, absorbable hemostatic agent intended to control mild to moderate bleeding. It is not a drug but a medical device — a physical material applied topically to a bleeding site. Unlike prescription anticoagulants or systemic hemostatics, CelluSTAT works locally at the wound and does not require systemic absorption, minimizing drug-drug interactions and systemic effects. This makes it attractive for use across diverse settings: operating rooms for general surgery, vascular surgery, and orthopedics; emergency departments; trauma centers; burn units; dialysis centers; and veterinary hospitals.

## Product development and FDA status

CelluSTAT is currently under review by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for Premarket Approval (PMA) — the most stringent regulatory pathway for novel medical devices. The device is not yet available for sale in the United States pending this approval. United Health Products has been working to satisfy FDA requirements for clinical evidence and manufacturing quality. In November 2024, the company submitted an Investigational Device Exemption (IDE) application proposing a clinical trial of approximately 30 subjects across three to five surgical facilities, with study objectives of demonstrating non-inferiority and superiority of CelluSTAT compared to standard surgical care, as well as confirming safety, in open abdominal surgical procedures.

This clinical trial is a critical milestone. The FDA's request for additional clinical data suggests the agency had questions about efficacy or safety based on the initial submission. Successful completion of the trial — demonstrating that CelluSTAT performs as well or better than existing hemostatic approaches with an acceptable safety profile — is the prerequisite for eventual PMA approval and market launch. The timeline for such studies is typically 12–24 months from enrollment through data analysis, placing a potential PMA decision in 2026 or 2027.

## Market opportunity

The hemostatic gauze market serves multiple customer segments: hospitals and surgery centers are the largest, using hemostatic agents during procedures; clinics and physician offices use them in minor procedures and wound care; military medical care providers rely on them in field settings; hemodialysis centers use them to control bleeding at access sites; nursing homes and assisted living facilities use them for wound management; veterinary hospitals use them for animal surgery; emergency services (EMS, fire departments, emergency rooms) use them for trauma; and dental and oral surgery offices use them for oral bleeding control.

The installed base of hemostatic products is mature. Surgeons and nurses are familiar with conventional approaches (sutures, cautery, pressure) and with existing hemostatic agents (thrombin-based products, chitosan-based gauzes, gelatin sponges). For CelluSTAT to gain market share, it must demonstrate clear advantages in ease of use, efficacy, cost, or safety compared to incumbents. The gauze format is simple and familiar — an advantage — but the hemostatic ingredient must prove its value in head-to-head comparison.

## Competitive environment and differentiation

CelluSTAT's proprietary ingredient is neutralized regenerated cellulose, derived from cotton. The claim is that this material is all-natural, absorbable, and effective at promoting clotting without systemic side effects. Competitors include thrombin-based products (which require careful handling and storage), chitosan-based hemostatic agents (derived from shellfish, raising allergy concerns), and mechanical approaches like gelatin sponges and collagen-based products. Each has established clinical use and reimbursement codes. CelluSTAT must establish sufficient differentiation to justify adoption in an already-served market.

The cellulose-based approach is not new — cellulose has been used in medical applications for decades — but United Health Products' formulation and application to hemostasis is proprietary. If the clinical trial confirms superior hemostasis, easier application, or better safety, the company has a pathway to differentiation. If results are merely equivalent, adoption will be slower and limited to cost-conscious buyers or those seeking a non-thrombin alternative.

## Business model and revenue drivers

Pre-approval, the company has no product revenue from CelluSTAT in the United States. Post-approval, revenue will depend on reimbursement codes, hospital and surgeon adoption, pricing, and volume. The business model is straightforward: manufacture the gauze, sell to distributors and directly to hospitals, and retain the margin between manufacturing cost and selling price. Hemostatic agents are consumables, implying recurring revenue as long as hospitals continue purchasing.

Reimbursement is a critical variable. If CelluSTAT receives a specific procedural code and insurance reimburses at a favorable rate, adoption can accelerate. If reimbursement is lumped into general surgical supplies codes or if payment is below manufacturing cost, the product remains a niche item. United Health Products' commercial success hinges significantly on favorable reimbursement decisions by Medicare, private insurers, and international health systems.

International markets represent additional opportunity. CelluSTAT may be available in some countries before or after U.S. approval, depending on regulatory processes. Emerging markets with growing surgical infrastructure but limited access to expensive thrombin-based products represent a potential customer base.

## Capital and execution risks

United Health Products is a development-stage medical device company with minimal current revenue. Its cash runway depends on its ability to raise capital and manage burn rate. The clinical trial will consume capital and resources; if enrollment is slow or trial results are equivocal, timelines extend and capital requirements grow. A trial failure — unexpected safety signals or failure to meet efficacy endpoints — would set the company back significantly and likely require a return to the drawing board.

Manufacturing and supply chain risks are also present. Even with FDA approval, scaling production to serve a national or global market requires capital investment in manufacturing, quality assurance, and supply-chain management. Manufacturing delays or quality issues could jeopardize the product launch or market adoption.

Adoption risk is perhaps the most subtle. Hemostasis is a commodity concern in surgery — most surgeons and operating rooms have established habits and trusted products. Convincing them to switch to a new cellulose-based product requires clinical evidence, surgeon education, and often hands-on experience. If adoption is slower than expected, revenue ramps slowly and the path to profitability extends.

## How to research United Health Products

The company's 10-K filing (SEC CIK 0001096938) discloses the product development timeline, the clinical trial protocol, capital raised and capital deployed, burn rate, and any partnerships or out-licensing agreements. Watch for updates on the clinical trial enrollment and any interim results or early safety signals. Once approval is granted, monitor quarterly results for early adoption trends, reimbursement rates achieved, and gross margins.

Key questions to track: Is the clinical trial enrolling on pace? Are interim results supportive of eventual approval? Has the company secured any partnerships or distribution agreements in preparation for launch? What is the expected manufacturing cost and the target selling price? Are there early reimbursement discussions with Medicare, Medicaid, or private insurers? How much capital runway does the company have before it must raise additional funding or achieve revenue? Any adverse events or manufacturing issues in the trial or in any international markets where the product may already be available?

The quarterly earnings calls, when held, will discuss trial progress, capital position, and management's timeline to approval and commercial launch. The [balance sheet](/balance-sheet/) is critical — a thinning cash balance raises the risk of dilutive equity raises or forced delays in development.
