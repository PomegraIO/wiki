### What is UGE, and how does it amplify returns?

ProShares Ultra Consumer Staples (UGE) is a leveraged exchange-traded fund that seeks to deliver twice the daily percentage gain or loss of the Consumer Staples Select Sector Index. When the underlying sector rises 1%, UGE aims to rise 2%. When it falls 1%, UGE aims to fall 2%. This amplification is achieved through the use of derivatives, primarily [equity swaps](/equity-swap/) and short-term [futures contracts](/futures-contract/), which allow the fund to control a large portfolio of Consumer Staples companies without buying them all outright. The fund is issued by ProShares, the largest issuer of leveraged and [inverse ETFs](/inverse-etf/), and trades on an exchange like any stock.

### Who issues UGE and what Consumer Staples companies does it track?

ProShares, a subsidiary of Direxion, manages UGE and hundreds of other specialized ETFs. The fund tracks the Consumer Staples Select Sector Index, which is a subset of the S&P 500 containing about 30 companies that sell non-discretionary goods — food, beverages, household products, personal care, and tobacco. These are the businesses people buy regardless of economic conditions: soap, cereal, diapers, shampoo, toothpaste. Unlike discretionary goods (luxury items, dining out), staples are relatively insensitive to [recession](/recession/), which is why the sector is often called "defensive." UGE's holdings therefore include [Procter & Gamble](/pg-stock/), Coca-Cola, [Walmart](/wmt-stock/), Costco, Mondelēz, Campbell Soup, Kellogg, and other household names.

### How does daily reset work, and why does it matter?

UGE resets every trading day. It is designed to deliver 2x returns over any single day, not over weeks or months or years. This daily reset is critical and is often misunderstood by retail investors. If the Consumer Staples index rises 5% on a Monday and then falls 3% on a Tuesday, UGE will rise approximately 10% on Monday and fall approximately 6% on Tuesday. But the two-day return of UGE will not be exactly 2x the two-day return of the index, because leverage compounds.

Here is the problem: suppose the underlying index ends a week flat (up 10%, then down 10%, ending at the start). A 2x leveraged fund will often end the week down, not flat, because it magnifies daily moves and then compounds. Each day it resets means each day it has the opportunity to amplify losses if markets swing up and down. This is called volatility decay or compounding drag. The longer an investor holds UGE without constant market movement in one direction, the more likely actual returns will lag 2x the index's total return.

### When would an investor actually use UGE?

UGE is not designed for [buy-and-hold investing](/buy-and-hold/). It is a tactical trading vehicle for investors with a short-term bullish view on the Consumer Staples sector and either the conviction to exit before volatility decay erodes returns or the risk tolerance to accept that decay as the price of amplified upside. Day traders and swing traders who believe the sector will rise over the next few days or weeks might use UGE to amplify their exposure without deploying as much capital. 

Some investors use [leveraged ETFs](/leveraged-etf/) as hedge tools, shorting them against long positions to adjust portfolio sensitivity. Others use them to express a view on momentum without fully sizing a position in the underlying sector.

### What are the real costs and risks?

UGE has two main costs. The first is the [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) — typically higher than the underlying [index fund](/index-fund/) would charge because derivatives and daily rebalancing require active management. The second is the silent cost of volatility decay, which eats into returns in any choppy market, regardless of the fund's performance relative to its daily target.

Beyond costs, the risks are leverage-specific. On a volatile day, UGE can swing more sharply than a single stock. If a large news event moves the Consumer Staples sector suddenly, UGE amplifies that move both ways. Investors can lose more money faster than they could with an unlevered fund. The fund can also trade at a premium or discount to its underlying [net asset value](/net-asset-value/), meaning the price you pay may not reflect the actual value of the holdings.

### How do researchers evaluate UGE?

Start with the prospectus and the fund's fact sheet, which lay out the index it tracks, the expense ratio, and the leverage mechanism. Check the [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) — if it is wide, trading costs will eat into returns. Watch whether the fund is tracking its daily target. If over a month the fund is consistently delivering less than 2x returns, that is a sign of drag. Finally, consider the Consumer Staples sector itself: is the outlook truly bullish for the next few weeks or months, or is a longer time horizon more appropriate? For longer time horizons, the volatility decay makes leveraged ETFs poor vehicles compared to direct holdings or unleveraged [sector ETFs](/sector-etf/).
