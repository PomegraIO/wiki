Harrison Street Infrastructure [Income Fund](/income-fund/) is a closed-end investment fund that invests in digital infrastructure and mission-critical real assets—principally data centers, communications towers, and the facilities that support the digital economy. Shares trade on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) under ticker VCRDX. The fund is managed by Harrison Street Capital, an investment firm focused on infrastructure and hard assets. It targets investors seeking regular income (through quarterly distributions) and long-term capital appreciation from owning stakes in the essential infrastructure that powers the internet, cloud computing, and global communications.

## What is digital infrastructure and why does it matter?

Digital infrastructure refers to the physical plants and equipment that underpin the internet and cloud computing: data centers (large buildings filled with servers and cooling systems), fiber-optic cables, cell towers, and telecommunications facilities. When you stream a video, send an email, or access a cloud service, your data travels through and is processed by these physical assets. Unlike some financial assets that have no tangible backing, digital infrastructure is concrete—it is buildings, power systems, and networks that occupy real space and generate real revenue by providing essential services.

Data centers have become critical economic assets. Every company that offers online services—from startups to megacap tech firms—relies on third-party data-center operators to host their equipment or rent computing capacity. Hyperscalers like Amazon, Google, [Microsoft](/msft-stock/), and Meta own their own massive data centers, but thousands of smaller companies and organizations depend on independent operators who build and manage facilities for hire. The business is lucrative because demand is structural and growing: artificial intelligence, cloud migration, streaming, and the broader digitalization of business all require more server capacity. Once a data center is built, it generates predictable revenue—companies sign multi-year contracts, pay monthly, and those contracts renew repeatedly.

## What does the fund own?

Harrison Street Infrastructure Income Fund invests in ownership stakes and long-term debt instruments related to digital infrastructure assets. The portfolio typically includes minority or controlling interests in data-center operators, recurring-revenue contracts with tower operators, and sometimes direct ownership of fiber-optic capacity or specialized telecom infrastructure. The fund seeks to balance current income (through distributions to shareholders) with capital appreciation (as the underlying assets grow in value or are refinanced at better terms).

The specific holdings vary—the fund's strategy is to evaluate opportunities across the digital-infrastructure ecosystem and allocate capital where management believes risk-adjusted returns are most attractive. This might mean backing a data-center operator that is expanding, acquiring stakes in established tower operators with long-term tenant contracts, or investing in emerging technologies like edge computing (smaller data centers located closer to end users for lower latency).

## How does income flow to shareholders?

Most of the fund's distributions come from the cash income generated by its underlying holdings. A data center that leases space to dozens of tenants collects monthly rent; the fund's ownership stake in that data center entitles it to a share of that cash flow. A tower operator with long-term contracts to carry cell signals for various carriers collects rent from those contracts; fund shareholders receive distributions of that income. Unlike a growth-focused fund that retains earnings and reinvests them, an income-focused fund like VCRDX prioritizes distributing cash to shareholders quarterly.

The distributions are not guaranteed—they depend on the cash generation of underlying investments and on management's decision to return capital rather than reinvest it. In strong years or when assets appreciate, distributions may be supplemented by realized gains. In weak years, distributions might decline. Shareholders should expect quarterly payouts and view them as part of the overall return alongside any appreciation or [depreciation](/depreciation/) in the share price.

## The interval fund structure

VCRDX is an [interval fund](/interval-fund/), a structure less common than open-end [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/) or traditional [closed-end funds](/closed-end-fund/). Interval funds allow redemptions only at specific intervals—typically quarterly—not daily like mutual funds. This illiquidity allows the fund to invest in less-liquid assets like minority stakes in private infrastructure companies, which would be difficult to value or sell on a moment's notice. The trade-off is that shareholders commit to holding for at least three months before they can redeem shares, and redemption windows are limited.

This structure suits long-term infrastructure investors well. Digital infrastructure assets are not meant to be traded frequently; they are purchased for stable, multi-decade cash flows. An interval fund matches that mindset by discouraging short-term trading and allowing management to invest in long-term, real-world assets without worrying about daily redemptions.

## What drives the fund's value and income?

Three factors shape the fund's performance. First, the underlying infrastructure assets—their growth, their tenancy rates (for towers), their utilization (for data centers), and their capacity to generate revenue and cash. When cloud demand booms, data-center occupancy rises and rents increase, benefiting the fund's returns. Second, [interest rates](/interest-rate/): infrastructure assets are often valued like [bonds](/bond/) (based on their cash flows discounted at prevailing rates). When rates rise sharply, valuations compress; when rates fall, they expand. Third, competition and supply: if too many data centers or towers are built in a region, oversupply can depress rents and returns. Management's skill lies in picking assets and operators with durable competitive advantages and strong contracts.
