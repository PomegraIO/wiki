Vanguard Target Maturity 2036 Corporate Bond ETF (VBCJ) is an exchange-traded fund that holds a portfolio of investment-grade corporate bonds scheduled to mature in approximately 2036. Unlike traditional bond funds that constantly rebalance to maintain a specific duration or yield, VBCJ follows a simpler discipline: buy bonds maturing around a single date, hold them, and return principal and accrued interest to shareholders when they mature. This target-maturity design appeals to investors with a known long-term cash need — someone funding a child's education, planning a retirement date, or earmarking money for a major purchase — who want to reduce uncertainty about when and at what price they can recover their capital.

## Design and mechanics

The fund is part of Vanguard's Target Maturity Bond ETF series, each designed for a different maturity year (the family includes funds maturing in 2025, 2027, 2029, and others extending into the 2050s). VBCJ specifically targets bonds maturing in 2036, typically holding securities within a narrow range around that date. As the fund ages and its bonds approach 2036, the portfolio shrinks naturally. When 2036 arrives, the remaining bonds pay off, the fund liquidates or terminates, and shareholders receive their final principal and accrued interest.

This design removes two common sources of investor anxiety. First, there is no need to time the bond market. A traditional bond fund must be sold whenever you need cash, and market prices fluctuate with interest rates — if rates have risen since you bought, you may sell at a loss. VBCJ, held to its maturity date, returns your full principal by definition (barring defaults). Second, there is no reinvestment decision. A callable bond fund pays coupons and matures bonds that you must reinvest elsewhere; VBCJ handles that automatically by simply holding to maturity.

## Holdings and credit quality

VBCJ holds investment-grade corporate bonds, meaning debt issued by companies with sound credit ratings — typically BBB or higher. These include debt from financial institutions, manufacturers, utilities, telecommunications firms, and other sectors. The portfolio is diversified across hundreds of issuers and industries, reducing the impact of any single company's failure. Vanguard discloses the fund's holdings daily, so investors can see exactly which bonds they own.

As the fund matures and individual bonds approach their 2036 maturity date, the number of holdings gradually shrinks. Bonds that were originally 20 or 30 years out are now just 10 years away, and bonds originally 10 years out are just a few years from repayment. This shortening duration also means the portfolio's sensitivity to interest-rate moves gradually declines.

## Costs and expense ratio

Vanguard's expense ratio for VBCJ is low by industry standards, typically around 0.12% annually — meaning investors pay roughly $12 per year on a $10,000 position. This reflects Vanguard's scale and its commitment to cost-focused management. The fund charges no sales loads or transaction fees to buy or sell on an exchange.

## Interest-rate risk before maturity

Although the fund is designed to be held to maturity, VBCJ trades on stock exchanges every day at prices set by supply and demand. If you need to sell before 2036, the price you receive depends on how interest rates have moved since you bought.

When interest rates rise after purchase, the bonds in VBCJ become less attractive to new buyers (because new bonds issued pay higher coupons), so their market prices fall. Selling at that point realizes a loss. Conversely, if rates fall, prices rise and you could sell at a gain. Early in the fund's life, when bonds have a decade or more until maturity, interest-rate movements can move the fund's price by several percentage points. As 2036 approaches, price volatility shrinks because there is less time for rates to matter.

## Credit risk and economic cycles

Investment-grade corporate bonds default rarely, but the risk is real. Most defaults occur during or just after recessions, when corporate profitability declines and companies struggle to service debt. If multiple issuers in VBCJ default before 2036, the fund's value falls and shareholders recover less than they put in.

Because VBCJ matures in 2036, it carries economic-cycle risk across a meaningful time horizon. A severe recession hitting in 2031 could trigger credit losses with only five years of recovery left before maturity. A downturn in 2034, very close to the maturity date, leaves no time for the portfolio to recover through credit spread tightening or refinancing.

## Who VBCJ is designed for

VBCJ works for investors with a specific cash need around 2036 — saving for a college graduation, a down payment, or a planned retirement. It suits those who want predictable income and certain capital return without ongoing portfolio management or the need to time a market sale. It is also appropriate for conservative investors who view bonds as a ballast in a diversified portfolio and appreciate knowing exactly when their capital will come back.

VBCJ is not appropriate for those seeking growth or total return over many decades. The fund is engineered to shrink to zero, not to compound. Its yield is modest, suited to income needs and capital preservation rather than capital appreciation. And for taxable investors, coupon payments are taxed as ordinary income, not at the lower capital-gains rates, making the fund more efficient in tax-deferred accounts such as IRAs and 401(k)s.

## Researching VBCJ

Start with Vanguard's fund factsheet, which lists the current holdings, weighted average maturity, average credit quality by rating, and yield to maturity. Read the prospectus to understand the investment policy, any restrictions on the types of bonds it can hold, and the fund's termination date or process. Check the daily holdings file to understand which companies' bonds you own and whether any represent concentrated positions.

Track the fund's trading price against its net asset value. A small discount or premium is normal and reflects supply-and-demand imbalances, but a persistent large gap might signal a liquidity issue worth investigating before committing significant money. As the fund approaches 2036, watch for announcements about liquidation plans or merger into a money-market fund.
