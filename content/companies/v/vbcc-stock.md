Vanguard Target Maturity 2029 Corporate Bond ETF (VBCC) is an exchange-traded fund designed to simplify bond investing for people with a specific time horizon. It holds a diversified portfolio of investment-grade corporate bonds, all scheduled to mature around 2029. When those bonds mature, the fund returns the principal and accrued interest to shareholders and winds down.

## What makes VBCC different from a traditional bond fund?

Most bond ETFs and mutual funds are "perpetual" — they are designed to exist indefinitely, holding a rotating portfolio of securities as managers buy and sell to maintain a target duration or interest-rate sensitivity. VBCC takes the opposite approach. It holds only bonds maturing in a narrow window around 2029, and as those bonds age and mature, the fund's assets shrink. Shareholders are not expected to trade in and out; they hold it until the end. This is useful for someone who knows they will need cash in 2029 and wants a fixed-income allocation that naturally delivers that capital at the right time.

## What does VBCC actually hold?

The fund owns investment-grade corporate bonds — debt issued by publicly traded companies with strong credit ratings. These bonds are typically rated BBB or higher, making them safer than high-yield (junk) bonds but riskier than government debt. The portfolio is diversified across many companies and industries, so no single issuer dominates. Vanguard's holdings documentation is updated daily, allowing investors to see the exact bonds in the fund at any moment.

As time passes and some bonds mature or are called early, VBCC trims those from the portfolio and may add new bonds that have drifted into the 2029 maturity window. This keeps the portfolio weighted toward the target maturity year.

## How much price volatility should I expect?

VBCC trades on the stock exchange at a market price, which means it can fluctuate daily based on changes in interest rates and credit sentiment. If interest rates rise after you buy, the market value of VBCC's bonds falls — because new bonds issued would pay higher coupons, making the existing lower-paying bonds less valuable. If you must sell VBCC at that moment, you take a loss. Interest rates falling would do the opposite, creating gains.

However, this interest-rate risk shrinks over time. Early in VBCC's life, with several years until 2029, a significant move in rates can swing the fund's price by several percentage points. As maturity approaches and the fund ages, price movements become smaller and more predictable. In the final months before maturity, VBCC's share price should closely track its underlying principal value with minimal volatility.

## What happens if a company in VBCC defaults?

Investment-grade companies default rarely, but it happens. A default or credit event would cause VBCC's net asset value to drop. The fund is diversified enough that a single default usually does not materially harm overall returns, but a severe economic downturn that triggers multiple credit events — especially near the 2029 maturity date when there is little time for recovery — could materially reduce what shareholders receive.

## What are the costs?

Vanguard's expense ratio for VBCC is low, reflecting the firm's cost-conscious approach. Investors also encounter the bid-ask spread when buying or selling on the exchange, which is typically tight for VBCC given its reasonable trading volume. There are no transaction fees to buy or sell, and no recurring advisory fees.

All income VBCC pays comes as ordinary coupon interest, taxed at your regular income-tax rate — not at the preferential capital-gains rates that some stock dividends enjoy. In a taxable account, this is a meaningful consideration. In a tax-sheltered vehicle like an IRA or 401(k), the ordinary-income treatment is irrelevant.

## Who should buy VBCC, and who should not?

VBCC suits conservative investors with a financial deadline in or near 2029. Someone funding a home renovation, paying off a loan, or covering education costs in that timeframe might appreciate the certainty that VBCC provides. It also works for investors who want fixed-income exposure without having to pick individual bonds or repeatedly trade to manage duration.

VBCC is not appropriate for people seeking long-term growth or compounding. It is a declining-value vehicle, shrinking toward zero as bonds mature. Its yield is modest. And for those in high tax brackets, the ordinary-income taxation of coupons makes it less efficient than a portfolio tilted toward qualified dividends or long-term capital gains.

## How do I research VBCC before investing?

Start with Vanguard's fund factsheet and prospectus. Confirm the weighted average time to maturity and the average credit rating to ensure they match your expectations. Review the fund's current holdings to understand which companies' bonds you would own and whether any have outsized weight. Compare the current yield to maturity against other fixed-income options available at the moment. Finally, verify that 2029 is truly your time horizon; if your actual deadline is 2027 or 2031, a different target-maturity fund might be a better fit. VBCC makes sense only if the maturity date and your financial needs are genuinely aligned.
