*The **Vanguard Russell 2000 ETF** (ticker: VTWO) is an exchange-traded fund that holds every company in the [Russell 2000 Index](/russell-2000/) — a universe of roughly two thousand U.S. public firms smaller than the large-cap benchmarks but large enough to have substantial [public float](/float/). VTWO is the small-cap counterpart to a total-market or large-cap fund, capturing the domestic equity segment that sits between mega-cap household names and micro-cap [penny stocks](/penny-stocks-investor/). It serves investors who want broad small-cap exposure without the complication of layering in value and growth judgments.*

## The Russell 2000: what it is and how it is constructed

The Russell 2000 identifies companies ranked by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) from roughly the thousand-and-first largest U.S. public firm down to about the three-thousand-th. In practice, that band spans companies with market values from several hundred million to roughly two billion dollars, though the exact boundaries shift with market movements and reconstitutions.

Russell Investments determines the membership annually, usually in June, reconstituting the index to match the updated rankings. When a company's market value grows, it may graduate from the Russell 2000 into the Russell 1000 (the large-cap index); when a company's value shrinks, it may drop from the 2000 into a smaller-cap index. New IPOs are added if they fit the universe; delisted companies are removed.

VTWO holds all members of the Russell 2000, weighted by market capitalization. The top fifty holdings — typically familiar names like Regional banks, healthcare providers, and technology services firms — make up a quarter or more of the fund's assets, while the bottom thousand holdings are more lightly weighted individually.

## The small-cap universe VTWO captures

Small-cap companies operate across every sector: manufacturing, financial services, healthcare, retail, technology, energy, and more. Some are regional banks with a single state's franchise; others are specialized software vendors or niche manufacturers. Many are growth-oriented; others are stable, dividend-paying businesses. The Russell 2000 makes no distinction between value and growth — it simply takes every qualifying company and weights it by size.

For an investor seeking pure exposure to the small-cap market without a tilt toward growth or value, VTWO delivers that. The fund's composition is truly diversified within the small-cap band: no single company dominates (the largest holding is typically less than 2% of assets), and the breadth of business types and geographies means [idiosyncratic risk](/idiosyncratic-risk/) is spread across many firms and industries.

## Characteristics and volatility

Small-cap stocks are more volatile than large-cap stocks, on average. They respond more sharply to economic news, interest-rate changes, and [sector rotations](/sector-rotation/). A [recession](/recession/) or [bear market](/bear-market/) often hits small-caps harder than blue-chip names: a large company like [Microsoft](/msft-stock/) or [Johnson & Johnson](/jnj-stock/) can weather a downturn with its [balance sheet](/balance-sheet/) and cash flow; a smaller company with less financial cushion may struggle more. Conversely, in economic expansions, small-caps often outpace large-caps.

Small-cap stocks are also less liquid. Daily trading volume in a mega-cap like [Apple](/aapl-stock/) is enormous and spreads are a penny; a typical Russell 2000 company trades less frequently and with wider spreads, making it harder and more expensive to build or exit large positions.

## Costs and trading profile

VTHR's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is roughly four to five basis points — one of the lowest available for a small-cap [index fund](/index-fund/). The low cost reflects the simplicity of tracking an index versus the index's reconstitution cost, which is built into that ratio over time.

VTWO trades on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) with good liquidity for a Vanguard product but less depth than a mega-cap ETF. Spreads can be one to two cents per share; in normal market conditions, an investor buying or selling a reasonable-sized position incurs minimal friction. In market stress or around the index's June reconstitution (when trading in underlying Russell 2000 stocks rises), liquidity can tighten.

## Risks and drawbacks

The core risks are small-cap-specific. Market downturns hit small-caps hardest; a 20% decline in the S&P 500 often coincides with a 30–40% decline in the Russell 2000. An investor with a low risk tolerance or a short time horizon is exposed to real drawdown risk.

A second risk is breadth and quality variability. The Russell 2000 includes genuinely strong, profitable businesses alongside highly speculative companies with unproven business models. The index does no quality filtering; it simply takes the bottom 2,000 by rank. Some positions will fail; the fund spreads that risk across the entire universe, but an investor in VTWO is assuming some holdings will not survive.

Tracking error can arise from reconstitution, cash drag, and market microstructure. The June reconstitution causes a large one-time reshuffle of holdings, incurring transaction costs; ordinary rebalancing as companies move in and out of the Russell 2000 can also create slippage. In taxable accounts, those costs and any realized gains are borne by the shareholder.

There is also a sector tilt to watch: the Russell 2000 has at times been heavily weighted toward financials (banks, insurance) or industrials relative to the broad market, and those allocations shift with reconstitution. An investor should be aware that VTWO's sector mix can deviate from the overall economy.

## Who uses it and how to research

VTWO is used by investors who want genuine small-cap exposure without tilting toward growth or value. It is a common core small-cap holding in diversified portfolios that layer broad market, small-cap, and international exposure. It is also popular in retirement accounts where low costs matter and the long time horizon lets volatility be absorbed.

The Russell 2000 Index factsheet (published by FTSE Russell) details the index's construction, membership criteria, and sector allocation. Vanguard's [fund prospectus](/fund-prospectus/) and fact sheet show VTWO's expense ratio, top holdings, and performance relative to the index. Historical small-cap performance data from financial data providers frames how the small-cap segment typically behaves in different market environments, informing expectations for VTWO's volatility and return patterns.
