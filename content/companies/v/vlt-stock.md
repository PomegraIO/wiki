> "A closed-end fund is a bond buyer that cannot sell back to the fund, so it trades at whatever the market will pay — creating opportunities for both misprice and folly."

Invesco High Income Trust II ([NASDAQ](/nasdaq/): VLT) is one of the oldest and largest [closed-end funds](/closed-end-fund/) specialising in high-yield [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/). Often called a "[junk bond](/junk-bond/)" fund, VLT invests primarily in [bonds](/bond/) issued by corporations below investment grade — firms with elevated default risk but higher [coupon rates](/coupon-rate/). The fund trades on the [stock exchange](/stock-exchange/) and distributes nearly all the interest it collects to shareholders, making it a favored vehicle for investors seeking yield in a low-interest environment.

## The high-yield bond market and VLT's niche

[High-yield bonds](/high-yield-bond/) are issued by companies whose credit quality precludes them from accessing investment-grade bond markets. Some are healthy businesses constrained by leverage from recent [acquisitions](/acquisition/) or private equity buyouts. Others are genuinely risky — companies in cyclical industries facing headwinds, firms with questionable competitive moats, or firms in early growth stages. The spreads (interest premiums) on these bonds are substantially wider than US Treasuries — often 300 to 500 basis points or more — because investors demand compensation for the risk of default.

VLT's role is to buy and hold a diversified basket of these bonds, collecting the [coupon payments](/coupon-payment/), and distributing that income to shareholders. The fund's portfolio typically holds several hundred individual bond positions, spreading [credit risk](/credit-risk/) across issuers and industries.

## Capital structure and how distributions are funded

VLT is a closed-end fund with a fixed capital base. Shareholders buy shares in the [secondary market](/secondary-market/), and the fund invests their capital in high-yield bonds. Each month, VLT collects interest from the bond portfolio and distributes nearly all of it to shareholders. The distribution yield is calculated as the annual distribution divided by the share price — not NAV, which is an important distinction.

Because VLT trades on an exchange, its share price can trade at a discount or premium to the fund's NAV. If VLT trades at a 10% discount and the underlying bonds are yielding 6%, the shareholder's yield on the purchase price can exceed 6.5%. Conversely, if VLT trades at a premium, the yield is compressed. This is where the mechanics of a closed-end fund create both opportunity and pitfall.

## Leverage and its role in distribution sustainability

Like most closed-end bond funds, VLT employs financial leverage — borrowing money to buy additional bonds beyond what shareholder capital alone supports. This leverage amplifies the interest income the fund can distribute, allowing it to offer higher distributions than an unleveraged portfolio could sustain.

The mechanics are straightforward: VLT borrows at a lower cost (say, 3%) and invests in bonds yielding 6%. The spread (3%) accrues to shareholders. This works well in stable market conditions, but becomes precarious if bond prices fall or [credit spreads](/credit-spread/) widen sharply. When bond prices decline, VLT's NAV falls, and leverage covenants may force the fund to sell bonds at unfavorable prices or reduce the [leverage ratio](/leverage-ratio-forex/). In the most severe stress scenarios, VLT could be forced to cut distributions sharply.

## Risk and the vulnerability of distributions

VLT's distributions are only as reliable as the creditworthiness of the bonds it holds. In economic expansion, defaults are rare and the fund's income is stable. In [recession](/recession/), [default rates](/default-rate/) spike, bond issuers cut or suspend coupon payments, and the fund's interest income declines. VLT has encountered periods of stress — most notably during the 2008 financial crisis and the 2020 COVID shock — when bonds in the portfolio defaulted or were in distress, forcing the fund to take losses.

A second pressure is [refinancing risk](/refinancing-risk/). High-yield bonds are typically issued on shorter terms than [investment-grade bonds](/investment-grade-bond/) — often 5 to 10 years. As they mature, issuers must refinance or repay. In periods when credit spreads are wide or credit availability is tight, a company may struggle to refinance maturing bonds, increasing default risk.

[Interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/) is a third consideration. If VLT's leverage is funded with floating-rate debt, rising [interest rates](/interest-rate/) increase the cost of funding and squeeze the spread between portfolio yield and borrowing cost. The fund's distribution may need to be cut to preserve capital or to meet leverage covenants.

## The discount-premium dynamic and hidden risks

VLT typically trades at a discount to NAV. This reflects both the general illiquidity of closed-end funds and, often, skepticism about the sustainability of distributions. A widening discount signals investor concern about the fund's portfolio or the reliability of distributions. When that discount reaches extreme levels (e.g., 15% or more), it sometimes represents an attractive entry point for value-minded investors — but sometimes it signals genuine distress.

The inverse risk exists too: if VLT trades at a premium to NAV, investors are paying more than the underlying bonds are worth. A premium can persist if the distribution yield is attractive and the fund has a loyal shareholder base, but it is a warning that new buyers may be overpaying for the income stream.

## Portfolio composition and diversification

VLT's portfolio typically spans dozens of industries and hundreds of bond issuers. The largest holdings are usually in sectors that naturally require leverage and issue high-yield debt regularly — real estate, energy, telecommunications, retail, healthcare. Portfolio composition shifts seasonally and as credit conditions change. During credit booms, VLT may take on incremental risk by adding lower-quality bonds. During credit stress, it may defensively shift to higher-quality (but still high-yield) positions.

An investor evaluating VLT should examine the quarterly holdings data to assess [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) — whether a few large positions dominate, and whether the fund is drifting into lower-quality issuers to sustain yield.

## Capital allocation and return on equity

VLT returns nearly all earned income to shareholders in distributions. It does not reinvest earnings or build capital. This is by design: the fund's purpose is to provide current income, not growth. As a consequence, VLT's NAV tends to drift downward over long periods if defaults or credit losses exceed new gains on the portfolio. Over decades, a high-yield bond fund that pays out all its income experiences gradual NAV deterioration — a fact that rarely troubles income-focused investors but should not be ignored.

## How to research Invesco High Income Trust II

Start with VLT's fact sheet and most recent [annual report](/10-k/), available on Invesco's website. Examine the portfolio composition, the average yield, and the leverage ratio. Compare VLT's distribution as a percentage of NAV (not share price) to get a sense of whether the fund is sustainable or consuming itself. Watch the trend in the discount or premium to NAV — a widening discount often precedes distribution cuts.

Track high-yield bond spreads and default rates published by credit agencies like Moody's and S&P. When spreads tighten dramatically, VLT's portfolio value may appreciate, but the fund's future yield may decline. When spreads widen sharply, the portfolio faces unrealised losses and refinancing stress increases. VLT's quarterly earnings calls and commentaries offer color on how the fund's managers assess credit conditions and their portfolio positioning. Finally, compare VLT's distribution history and current NAV to historical levels — this reveals whether the fund is generating gains or slowly eroding capital to [fund distributions](/fund-distribution/).
