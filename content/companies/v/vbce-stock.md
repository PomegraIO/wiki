The Vanguard Target Maturity 2031 [Corporate Bond](/corporate-bond/) ETF (ticker VBCE) is a [passively managed fund](/passively-managed-fund/) that holds investment-grade corporate [bonds](/bond/) with maturity dates clustered around 2031. It is one of a series of Vanguard target-maturity funds that offer a different approach to bond exposure: instead of tracking a conventional index that constantly rolls forward, these funds collect bonds on a timetable and allow them to mature naturally in the hand.

## The target-maturity concept

Most corporate bond funds track an index — such as the Bloomberg US Corporate Bond Index — that constantly reinvests proceeds into new bonds, effectively never maturing. A target-maturity fund operates differently. It starts with a tight mandate: hold bonds that will mature in a specific year (in this case, approximately 2031) and simply let them age. As a result, the fund becomes increasingly defensive over time. Early in its life, it faces ordinary bond-fund risks: credit losses if companies default, and price swings if [interest rates](/interest-rate/) move. But as the maturity date approaches and the bonds draw closer to repayment, the fund naturally becomes more stable and less sensitive to new market developments. By 2031, the fund will mostly consist of near-cash equivalents awaiting final [coupon payments](/coupon-payment/) and principal repayment.

This creates a fundamentally different experience from a rolling [index fund](/index-fund/). A conventional corporate bond fund is designed to be held indefinitely, constantly rotating into new issuance. A target-maturity fund, by contrast, is built with an expected endpoint: investors who buy it at inception and hold it through 2031 experience a natural progression from moderate [interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/) to minimal risk, ending with capital return at maturity.

## Composition and selection

VBCE holds bonds issued by a broad cross-section of US corporations across sectors — financials, industrials, technology, consumer goods, energy, and utilities. The fund does not deliberately concentrate in any single issuer or sector; it simply includes all eligible [investment-grade bonds](/investment-grade-bond/) that fall within its maturity window. This breadth means the [credit risk](/credit-risk/) is diversified across many companies, but it also means the fund will hold bonds from underperformers if they remain investment-grade through the [holding period](/holding-period/).

The credit quality floor is investment-grade (typically bonds rated BBB− or higher by major rating agencies), which excludes the riskier high-yield market but still includes companies facing moderate financial stress. As the bonds approach their 2031 maturity and issuers are forced to address refinancing needs or pay down debt, some bonds may upgrade or downgrade, but the fund's core holdings remain fixed unless a bond is actually downgraded to junk and removed.

The fund's maturity focus means it will gradually shift composition as bonds mature or are called early by their issuers. For this reason, the portfolio becomes increasingly concentrated in the actual bonds that still have time remaining, so later in the fund's life, the diversity benefit wanes somewhat.

## Costs and trading

As a Vanguard product, VBCE carries a low [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) typical of the firm's index-following funds. The fund is liquid and trades on NYSE Arca with normal [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/), though trading volume is much lighter than in broad corporate bond indices. Investors planning to trade VBCE should expect less liquidity than they would in a more widely held fund like BND or LQD.

The ETF structure means the fund settles in cash and can be bought or sold during market hours, unlike traditional [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/). No front-end or back-end loads apply.

## Risks particular to target-maturity funds

Credit risk is the dominant threat. Although the fund holds only investment-grade bonds, the definition of investment-grade can shift. A bond rated BBB at purchase might face downgrade pressure if its issuer's business deteriorates. In severe default scenarios, Vanguard could be forced to sell a downgraded bond or hold it as it drops below investment-grade.

Interest-rate risk is front-loaded. Early in the fund's life, when bonds still have eight to ten years to maturity, price sensitivity to changes in market interest rates is high. A rise in yields of one percentage point can knock ten percent off the fund's [net asset value](/net-asset-value/). This risk decreases as the fund ages and maturity approaches. By 2030, interest-rate sensitivity is minimal.

A third, less obvious risk is [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) in a narrow maturity window. Unlike a diversified bond fund that holds bonds of many maturities and benefits if the [yield curve](/yield-curve/) shifts in its favour, a target-maturity fund is forced to hold its bonds regardless of relative value. If the 2031 part of the curve becomes expensive relative to 2030 or 2032, the fund offers no flexibility to move along the curve for better returns.

There is also the possibility of early calls. Companies sometimes redeem [callable bonds](/callable-bond/) before maturity if interest rates fall, forcing the fund to reinvest redemption proceeds at lower yields. This is an ordinary feature of corporate bonds but becomes more likely if rates decline sharply.

## Who it is for and how to research it

VBCE suits investors with a specific liability or time horizon near 2031 — perhaps they plan to spend the money in 2030 or 2031 and want to de-risk as that date approaches. It can also appeal to investors who simply prefer predictable maturity over the indefinite horizon of a rolling fund.

The fund is not a core-and-hold vehicle unless the investor's time horizon truly aligns with 2031. Buying it in 2040 would be unusual, as the fund would be mostly matured and offer minimal income.

To research VBCE, start with Vanguard's fact sheet and prospectus, which detail the exact bonds held, the portfolio's weighted average maturity, and the credit-quality breakdown. Monitoring the fund's net asset value relative to net asset per share indicates whether it trades at a premium or discount to its underlying bonds. The portfolio composition updates regularly; looking at the ten or fifteen largest holdings shows whether the fund still feels diversified or if a few issuers dominate.

Understanding the broader context of corporate bonds — [credit spreads](/credit-spread/), refinancing cycles, and [default rates](/default-rate/) — helps contextualise VBCE's price movements. But the fund's defining feature is not its ability to pick winners among bonds but its mechanical march toward maturity, which means the fund's risk and return profile change fundamentally over time in ways that a conventional bond fund's never do.
