Vystar Corp develops and sells cleaning and disinfection products based on electrolyzed water technology — a process that converts saltwater into a hypochlorous acid solution without harsh chemicals. The company targets commercial customers, healthcare facilities, and government buyers, positioning its products as alternatives to bleach and quaternary ammonium compounds. Vystar trades on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) under the ticker VYST.

## The pivot that defined the company

Vystar's origin traces to its electrolyzed water technology, which the company identified as an underexploited alternative to conventional chemical cleaners. Unlike chlorine bleach or ammonia-based formulations, electrolyzed water uses an electrochemical process applied to saltwater to generate hypochlorous acid — a compound that the human immune system itself produces. The resulting solution is effective against bacteria, viruses, and fungi while being gentler on surfaces and workers than traditional disinfectants.

The strategic pivot came when Vystar recognized that the military and government procurement world valued two things above cost: safety and certification. Vystar pursued military contracts and approvals from the Environmental Protection Agency, positioning its products not as cheap commodity cleaners but as safer, EPA-registered alternatives that appeal to cost-conscious institutions willing to pay a premium for reduced chemical risk. This decision shaped the company's entire revenue model: rather than selling into competitive retail chains, Vystar focused on institutional channels — military bases, hospitals, school districts, and corporate facility managers.

## How the business operates

Vystar manufactures its base chemical solutions and sells them primarily through two channels: direct corporate and government sales, and a growing network of distributors. The company supplies both the chemistry itself and the generation equipment — the machines that facilities use to produce electrolyzed water on-site. This dual model creates recurring revenue: equipment sales bring upfront capital, while ongoing chemical purchases and equipment maintenance generate aftermarket demand.

The company's product line includes surface disinfectants, hand sanitizers, and specialized solutions for specific applications. Military contracts represent a meaningful portion of revenue; government buyers appreciate standardization and the reduced worker-safety liability that electrolyzed water solutions offer compared to harsher chemicals. Beyond the direct institutional channel, Vystar has explored retail partnerships, though consumer awareness of electrolyzed water technology remains limited compared to established brand names.

Manufacturing is centered in Nevada, where the company controls the production process. This vertical integration lets Vystar maintain quality and intellectual-property control, though it also means capital intensity and operational leverage — when sales are strong, the model is profitable; when they are weak, fixed manufacturing costs weigh on margins.

## The case for and against

The thesis for Vystar rests on three pillars. First, electrolyzed water is genuinely safer than bleach and many industrial disinfectants — a fact supported by EPA registration and growing awareness of occupational health in institutional settings. Second, military and government procurement operates on long cycles and contract lock-in; once Vystar wins a military base or healthcare system, repeat orders tend to follow. Third, the company's technology represents real differentiation at a time when cleaning-solution markets are consolidating around commodity players.

Against this, Vystar faces headwinds. The company is small, with limited resources for marketing compared to established chemical manufacturers like Clorox or Ecolab. Electrolyzed water technology, while effective, is not patentable in the way pharmaceuticals are — competitors can enter the space with their own electrochemical processes, making the technology a competitive advantage only as long as Vystar executes better than challengers. Additionally, Vystar's dependence on institutional sales, particularly military contracts, creates [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/): loss of a major contract can swing quarterly results significantly. The consumer channel has proven slower to develop than early optimists imagined, since retail buyers default to what they know.

## What to watch

Anyone researching Vystar should focus on contract wins and renewals — particularly government and military procurement announcements, which are often public and signal demand trajectory. The company's 10-K (SEC CIK 0001308027) details which customer segments drive revenue and how much depends on any single buyer. Watch the gross margin on direct sales versus distributor sales; direct contracts are typically higher-margin but harder to close, while distributors expand reach but take a cut. Quarterly earnings calls often include color on new customer traction and production capacity utilization.

The key metric for Vystar is not earnings or cash flow (as with larger companies) but customer concentration and contract length. If the company is signing long-term government agreements with expanding order volumes, it suggests the technology is winning adoption beyond pilot phases. Conversely, if major customers churn or contracts fail to renew, it signals the addressable market may be narrower than investors hoped. In a micro-cap with this profile, unit economics matter more than absolute scale — a profitable contract in one region may demonstrate a replicable business model across others.
