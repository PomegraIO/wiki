*The **Invesco S&P SmallCap Consumer Staples ETF** (PSCC) provides focused exposure to approximately 75–90 small-cap U.S. firms that produce or distribute staple consumer goods — packaged foods, beverages, household essentials, personal care items — businesses whose demand is typically inelastic across economic cycles. It is a passive [index fund](/index-fund/), not an active strategy, meaning it simply holds and rebalances the constituents of its benchmark index.*

## Small-cap staples in boom and bust

Staples companies earn their "defensive" label because people buy soap, bread, and toothpaste in both expansions and [recessions](/recession/). Yet at the small-cap level — companies with a few billion dollars in market value — that defensiveness is genuine but compromised. A major packaged-goods corporation like [Procter & Gamble](/pg-stock/) can negotiate with suppliers from a position of enormous scale, absorb cost shocks, and sustain margins even when demand slows. A small-cap staples company — a regional grocery chain, a niche food producer, a mid-size personal-care maker — operates at a different leverage point.

In downturns, demand for staples does hold. But smaller producers often cannot absorb input-cost [inflation](/inflation/) without passing it through to price-sensitive customers or accepting margin compression. They lack the financial firepower to invest in efficiency when capital is scarce. What they retain is volume stability, which is worth something: a smallcap food maker in a recession loses less than a smallcap technology firm loses.

In upswings, small-cap staples behave like other mature, low-growth stocks: they participate in earnings expansion if volume and pricing both accelerate, but they trail a market dominated by growth narratives and tech enthusiasm. Rising [interest rates](/interest-rate/) pose a particular hazard because staples companies with steady but unexciting cash flows become less attractive relative to [bonds](/bond/) — the multiple contraction can outweigh fundamental earnings gains.

## What the fund owns and why

PSCC holds the small-cap slice of the consumer staples world. The S&P SmallCap 600 is the exchange's index of roughly 600 U.S. companies in the size range of $500 million to $6 billion in [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/). Applying the staples filter narrows the universe to firms whose primary revenue is food, beverages, household goods, and personal care — roughly 80–100 eligible companies across all of small-cap. PSCC concentrates holdings to 75–90 to reduce rebalancing costs and improve tracking efficiency.

The fund is market-cap weighted, so larger small-cap staples companies exert greater influence on returns than smaller ones. This reflects real-world liquidity and importance but introduces concentration: a handful of large holdings within the small-cap space can drive performance. The universe is mostly overlooked by Wall Street analysts — regional food manufacturers, smaller beverage makers, niche personal-care brands — which can create pricing inefficiencies and opportunity for active investors but also means less information is available.

## Costs and structure

PSCC is a passive vehicle, so there is no active stock-picking fee, only the index-tracking [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) of approximately 0.39% per year. This is modest for a [sector-specific fund](/sector-specific-fund/) and substantially cheaper than an actively managed small-cap fund. The fund is highly liquid on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/), allowing entry and exit at tight spreads and minimal transaction costs.

The fund holds actual stocks, not swap contracts or leverage, making it straightforward and tax-efficient. It distributes [dividends](/dividend/) to shareholders regularly, capturing the income from the underlying holdings.

## Appropriate uses and audiences

PSCC is most useful for investors building a diversified small-cap allocation who want representation in the defensive sector, or for those making a tactical rotation into staples ahead of economic uncertainty. It is also suitable for anyone overweighting small-cap staples as a contrarian position. It is not appropriate as a substitute for broad market exposure or for investors seeking to avoid concentrated sector bets. Anyone buying PSCC should understand that they are betting specifically on small-cap staples to outperform, and should monitor that thesis over time.

## How to evaluate PSCC

Review Invesco's fund fact sheet and prospectus to understand the exact index methodology, current holdings, and fee structure. Examine the top ten holdings to see what kinds of companies dominate — are they packaged foods, beverages, or household manufacturers? Compare PSCC's trailing returns against the broader S&P SmallCap 600 index and against large-cap staples funds to understand how much the small-cap and sector tilts are contributing to performance. Tracking error — the difference between the fund's returns and its benchmark — indicates how efficiently Invesco manages the fund. Finally, observe PSCC's behaviour across a full cycle of economic boom, slowdown, and recovery to test whether the small-cap staples thesis holds in practice.
