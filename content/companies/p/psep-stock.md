*PSEP is a **Managed Outcome ETF** that seeks to pair protection against the first 15% of annual losses in the S&P 500 with participation in returns up to a predetermined cap, resetting each outcome period at the start of September.*

## What is a managed-outcome ETF?

A managed-outcome ETF uses derivatives — usually options — to engineer a payoff structure that trades off maximum upside against downside protection, locked in for a defined time window. PSEP specifically uses [FLEX options](/flex-options/) to build an annual outcome: it buffers the first 15% of losses but caps total return at a level set at the start of each period. This structure appeals to risk-averse investors who expect market volatility but want a floor, and to those willing to forgo some upside in exchange for the certainty of knowing their worst-case loss in advance.

The fund resets approximately every 12 months, always at the start of September. An investor who buys at the start of an outcome period and holds through the end locks in the promised buffer and cap; anyone who enters or exits mid-period experiences results that can diverge sharply from the published outcomes because the structure is point-to-point across the full period.

## How does PSEP's current outcome work?

For the September 2025–August 2026 period, the fund buffers losses of up to 15% — meaning if the S&P 500 falls 15%, PSEP loses nothing; if it falls 20%, PSEP falls 5%. Meanwhile, the cap sits at approximately 11.72% (before fees). If the S&P 500 returns 20% over the period, PSEP returns 11.72%. If it returns 5%, PSEP also returns 5%. These figures reset annually because the options pricing and the cap adjustment reflect the new 12-month forward outlook.

The fund carries an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) of 0.79%, which is paid out of returns and reduces both the realized upside and the effective buffer slightly. The annual reset is intentional: it allows Innovator to re-price the structure based on new market expectations and volatility levels, ensuring the buffer-and-cap pair remains calibrated for the fresh period ahead.

## What does PSEP actually hold?

PSEP holds the S&P 500 through its underlying exposure to the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (SPY), paired with the options overlay that creates the buffer and cap. It is a single-strategy fund — there is no [diversification](/diversification/) away from large-cap U.S. equities, no [bonds](/bond/), no alternatives. The buffer protects against S&P 500 drawdowns only; it does nothing against [sector rotation](/sector-rotation/), style shifts, or factors within the index. An investor in PSEP is still making a bet on the health and direction of the 500 largest U.S. companies; the ETF merely changes the shape of that bet.

## Who should consider PSEP?

The fund is designed for investors who expect continued market volatility, have a time horizon that aligns neatly with an outcome period or two, and would sleep better at night knowing their maximum annual loss in advance. It appeals to retirees or near-retirees who want equity participation without the risk of a 30% or 40% drawdown. It also attracts tactical traders who believe the cap is generous relative to their own return expectations and who value the optionality.

The key caveat is timing. An investor who buys PSEP on the final day of an outcome period inherits most of the downside risk but almost none of the buffer benefit, because the buffer is measured from the outcome period's start. Similarly, exiting mid-period locks in whatever gain or loss has accrued to that date, forfeiting the protective mechanism for the remainder of the period. Only full-period holders realize the promised buffer and cap.

## How to research PSEP as an investment

Start with Innovator's prospectus and summary prospectus for PSEP, which spell out the precise mechanics of the options strategy, the fees, and the reset dates. Watch the fund's fact sheet as each new outcome period begins to see what cap the fund's managers have set for the year ahead — that number is the key variable that determines whether the trade-off between buffer and cap looks attractive relative to your market outlook. Monitor the underlying S&P 500 performance against the outcome period calendar; there is no penalty for holding through reset, but knowing when the period boundaries fall helps you time entries and exits deliberately. The fund trades on the Cboe BZX exchange with reasonable daily volume, so execution should not be difficult. As always, holding any single ETF is a choice among many; this structure only makes sense for investors who genuinely value the defined outcome over the uncertainty of unhedged equity exposure.
