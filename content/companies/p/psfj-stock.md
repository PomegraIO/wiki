*PSFJ follows a **rules-based, regime-responsive** allocation system between large-cap U.S. equities and [Treasury bonds](/treasury-bond/), recalibrating its decision framework each July, intended to reduce drawdowns during market stress while participating in gains during healthier periods.*

> "A strategy that adapts to market regime rather than to a fixed static allocation can capture the best of both worlds—growth in benign times, protection during duress—if its adaptation is disciplined and systematic enough to avoid whipsaw."

## The regime-following premise

The core idea behind PSFJ is that markets do not behave uniformly: there are periods when equities dominate and risk is rewarded, and periods when volatility spikes, correlations break down, and defensive assets shine. A regime-following system reads signals in real time — momentum, volatility, breadth, valuation — and adjusts the portfolio's balance between risk assets and safety accordingly. During a [bull market](/bull-market/), PSFJ tilts heavily toward the S&P 500; during a correction or [bear market](/bear-market/), it shifts capital into Treasuries, dampening the draw-down.

The distinction from a simple "buy and hold" or from a preset rebalancing schedule (like annual rebalancing) is that PSFJ's shifts are **responsive to actual market conditions**, not to a calendar or a formula set once and locked away. If equities begin to show weakness relative to the regime signals, the fund moves; if they strengthen, it moves back. This reduces the chance of being overcommitted to equities during a sharp drawdown and maximizes the fund's participation when sentiment improves.

## Why July as the reset date?

PSFJ resets its regime-detection parameters each July, meaning the decision rules governing the next 12 months are recalibrated based on the prior year's market data and forward-looking estimates. The July timing is somewhat arbitrary — different Pacer Swan SOS variants reset in January, April, and July — but each has the same intent: to periodically refresh the system's understanding of normal volatility, typical correlations, and regime breakpoints, preventing the strategy from running on stale assumptions.

An investor entering PSFJ immediately after the July reset benefits from a fresh parameter set; one entering in, say, March is running on parameters set the previous July, which may have drifted from the updated assumptions. Over a full year, this difference usually matters little, but it can affect short-term positioning.

## How the equity-bond tilt works

On any given day, PSFJ holds some percentage in equities and the remainder in Treasuries — neither extreme is a typical outcome. The allocation is not static; it drifts week to week or month to month based on regime signals. When the system detects strengthening equity momentum and falling volatility, the fund gradually adds to equities. When it senses deteriorating breadth, elevated volatility, or fading trend, it rotates capital toward [bonds](/bond/). The shifts are bounded — the fund will not go 100% equities or 100% bonds — and they happen within a predetermined range set by the fund's rules.

This dynamic rebalancing is notably different from a static split like 60/40. In a 60/40 portfolio, when equities rise sharply, they drift to 70% or 80% of the total without action; the investor must rebalance back down, often right before a continuation of the bull move. PSFJ rebalances ahead of weakness (in theory) and avoids the self-inflicted mistake of being overcommitted at the peak of a move.

## The trade-off between smoothness and upside

The practical result of this regime-following is that PSFJ typically posts lower peak returns during strong bull markets compared to pure equity funds — the Treasuries allocation becomes a drag when equities are dominating. In the S&P 500's best years (up 25%, 30%, or more), PSFJ's capped participation (typically 20% to 70% equity allocation range) means the fund will lag significantly.

But in drawdown years, PSFJ's shift toward bonds reduces losses materially. A year when the S&P 500 falls 20% might see PSFJ fall 8% to 12%, depending on how promptly the regime detector shifted out of equities. Over a full [market cycle](/market-cycle/) (bull market, correction, recovery), PSFJ's lower volatility and smaller [maximum drawdown](/maximum-drawdown/) often produce competitive or superior risk-adjusted returns compared to pure equity, even after accounting for the fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/).

## Who benefits and who does not

PSFJ suits investors with moderate risk tolerance who want [stock market](/stock-market/) participation but need to avoid severe losses to their retirement plan or emotional commitment. It appeals to those who have experienced large drawdowns and found them unbearable. It is less suitable for investors with decades until retirement who can ignore short-term noise, or for those convinced that pure equities are the only way to capture long-term wealth, or for traders who want the raw leverage and volatility of concentrated positions.

## How to research PSFJ

Obtain Pacer's prospectus and the detailed fund fact sheet for PSFJ to understand the precise regime-detection signals and allocation rules. Calculate or request historical rolling returns and drawdowns for PSFJ versus a 60/40 equity-bond index to see whether the regime-following system has actually improved risk-adjusted returns. Watch the quarterly holdings and allocations to see the current equity/bond mix — a fund showing 20% equities signals the regime detector sees stress, while 80% equities signals confidence. Compare PSFJ's return patterns to other dynamic-allocation or target-risk funds (such as Vanguard's "Balanced" index or iShares' "Dynamic" core positions) to see whether Swan SOS's specific rule set has an edge. Understand the July reset timing: if you are planning to invest, entering shortly after the reset means you get a full year of fresh parameters, while entering mid-year means you carry stale parameters until the next July. The fund trades with reasonable daily volume and standard [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/), so execution should not be a barrier. The decision ultimately rests on whether you believe systematic regime-following adds value compared to simpler [diversification](/diversification/) or pure equity exposure—a question best answered by your own experience and time horizon.
