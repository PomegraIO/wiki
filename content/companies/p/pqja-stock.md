PGIM Nasdaq-100 Buffer 12 ETF - January is a structured exchange-traded fund that mirrors the Nasdaq-100 index while wrapping it in a protective buffer against losses. Like its sister products in the PGIM Nasdaq-100 Buffer family, it aims to deliver a portion of the index's gains while capping losses below a threshold (in this case 12 percent). The January designation indicates the annual reset date; a new buffer is struck every January with fresh options contracts tailored to market conditions at that moment.

## The core protective mechanism

The buffer works by pairing long stock exposure (the Nasdaq-100) with a put spread constructed in the derivatives market. The fund buys put options that pay off if the index falls more than 12 percent below its starting value, paying for those puts by selling (writing) call options that cap the fund's maximum upside. The net result is a defined-outcome envelope: losses between zero and 12 percent are absorbed; losses beyond 12 percent flow through to the investor one-for-one; gains above a calculated cap (roughly equal to the index's annual return, though the exact level varies with volatility and interest rates) are foregone.

The annual reset in January recalibrates the entire structure. Old options expire worthless or are closed out, and new puts and calls are written based on the index level and volatility at that moment. A January reset that occurs after a market rally means puts are cheaper to buy (because volatility typically contracts in rally periods), allowing either stronger protection for the same cost or more generous upside caps. A reset after a sell-off means puts are expensive, typically reducing the quality of the new protection.

## Segment breakdown: mechanics and risks

**Downside protection segment** — The 12 percent buffer is the main selling point. In a year when the Nasdaq-100 falls 5 percent, the fund loss is zero. In a year when it falls 18 percent, your loss is 6 percent. This protection has real value in sharp downturns, but it is time-limited to the calendar year in which it applies. Investors who buy the fund partway through the year receive proportionally less protection.

**Upside cap segment** — The trade for that protection is a cap on gains. If the Nasdaq-100 rises 25 percent in a year and your upside cap is 12 percent, you realize 12 percent gain while the index earned 25 percent. This drag compounds over years and can be substantial in bull markets. Investors should model their expected use: protection buys peace of mind in downturns, but the cost is meaningful in rallies.

**Optionality risk segment** — Buffers depend on options markets functioning normally. During extreme stress (when volatility spikes and bid-ask spreads widen), the actual protection can deteriorate or become difficult to exit. The value of the put protection also depends on the pricing model PGIM uses; in tail-risk scenarios, realized losses may exceed theoretical protection levels.

**Time-decay and reset risk segment** — As the calendar year progresses, the put options decay in value, meaning the protection gets weaker. A crash in December provides less cushion than a crash in January because the puts are closer to expiration. And the January reset, while it renews protection, resets at whatever market conditions exist at that moment — potentially at unfavorable levels if volatility is elevated or the index is depressed.

## Cost structure and structural considerations

The fund carries an expense ratio typically in the 0.5 to 0.8 percent range to cover the cost of the derivatives positions and the management of daily rebalancing. This is substantially higher than a plain Nasdaq-100 ETF (which might cost 0.2 percent) but is intrinsic to the protection the fund provides. Beyond the expense ratio, the fund incurs ongoing transaction costs as the manager adjusts the option portfolio to keep the protection in place, though these are usually not transparent to the investor.

PQJA trades on an exchange with moderate liquidity. It is far more specialized than a broad-market index fund, so bid-ask spreads may be wider and it may take longer to fill large orders. Redemption and creation are managed by authorized participants, as with all ETFs, but the underlying complexity means the fund is not suitable for very large institutional positions or for investors who trade frequently.

## Performance drivers and selection criteria

The fund's performance is driven by three distinct factors: the Nasdaq-100's return (the largest component), the cost of the protection (expressed as a drag on gains), and optionality moves (the delta of the embedded put and call positions as volatility and the index level change). In calm markets where the index rises steadily and volatility remains stable, the upside cap represents pure performance drag. In volatile or falling markets, the protection's value shines but is offset by the fact that gains are still capped.

PQJA is most attractive to investors who expect the Nasdaq-100 to deliver positive returns over the coming year but fear a sharp drawdown within that year and are willing to forgo some upside to buy genuine downside cushion. It is less suitable for passive buy-and-hold investors with long time horizons (who should simply buy the index and ignore short-term volatility), for aggressive growth investors unwilling to cap upside, or for those who believe markets will soar significantly.
