*PJFG is an actively managed exchange-traded fund that bets on concentrated growth — the fund holds fewer than 50 carefully selected US stocks the manager believes are growing faster and more sustainably than the broader market will reward. It is a vehicle for investors who accept style risk (periods of underperformance during value rallies) in exchange for the possibility of outperformance during growth-favourable markets, and who trust PGIM Jennison's stock-picking capability.*

## The core approach

[Growth investing](/growth-investing/) rests on a simple idea: buy companies expanding their earnings faster than the average business, because the market will eventually recognize that growth and reward it with a higher valuation. PJFG pursues this strategy with extreme conviction. Rather than holding a representative sample of all growth stocks — which would still mean dozens or hundreds of positions — the portfolio concentrates on the highest-conviction ideas. The fund typically holds 40 to 50 securities, meaning every position is meaningful to performance. If the top five holdings comprise 20% of the portfolio, they move the needle far more than they would in a diversified index.

PGIM Jennison, the manager, sources these ideas from its team of sector analysts and portfolio managers who spend time with company management, competitors, and customers. The process is fundamentally bottom-up: the goal is not to bet on which sectors will outperform, but rather which individual companies have sustainable competitive advantages and are growing earnings at rates the market is still underestimating. A company might be in a slow-growth sector but still qualify for PJFG if its management is taking market share or innovating faster than peers.

## The composition of a concentrated growth portfolio

Because PJFG holds so few positions, its sector mix and character can drift significantly from the broad market. Technology and healthcare usually dominate concentrated growth portfolios, because both sectors contain many companies growing at above-average rates. Consumer discretionary and industrials may be underweighted or absent entirely; utilities and energy almost never appear. The portfolio skews toward mid-cap and large-cap companies — the fund is not hunting for micro-caps — but larger companies with genuine revenue acceleration and margin expansion stand out in a concentrated universe where mediocre growth is culled.

The portfolio rebalances actively. Rather than holding for decades, the manager trades when convictions change. If a company reaches its growth targets and valuation becomes stretched, it is likely to be sold; if a new idea emerges that meets the high bar for addition, the portfolio adapts. This is what distinguishes PJFG from a static growth index ETF: the manager is making year-round choices about what belongs in the portfolio.

## Costs and the active-management premium

PJFG charges an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) higher than a passive growth-index ETF would, reflecting the cost of active management, research, and frequent trading. That additional cost is a real hurdle: the fund must outperform a passive alternative by at least the annual fee in order to break even. Over long periods, most active managers fail this test, which is why many investors default to [index funds](/index-fund/). PJFG does not guarantee outperformance, only the manager's commitment to selecting and concentrating on the highest-probability growth opportunities.

The ETF structure keeps some of those costs in check. Unlike a [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/), PJFG has no sales load (commission on purchase) and no 12b-1 distribution fees. It trades on an exchange, so buying and selling is instantaneous at the market price, not delayed until the end of trading day. Redemptions and creations happen behind the scenes, keeping the fund's [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) closely aligned with its market price.

## The growth-value cycle and drawdown risk

The central risk of PJFG is style timing. Concentrated growth portfolios perform spectacularly during [bull markets](/bull-market/) when investors reward faster-growing companies with premium valuations and when each correct stock pick multiplies gains. But during periods — and they do arrive — when the market rotates toward value (cheaper, slower-growth, mature companies), concentrated growth portfolios can underperform by wide margins. Rising [interest rates](/interest-rate/) often trigger such rotations, because higher [discount rates](/discount-rate/) make far-future earnings less valuable, hitting growth stocks hardest.

Because the portfolio is concentrated, the underperformance during such periods is amplified. A diversified growth portfolio might underperform by 10 percentage points; PJFG might lag by 20 or more. An investor who buys at a peak and rides out a multi-year underperformance cycle could face significant losses in real terms. Conversely, after such cycles — once the growth narrative reasserts — the same concentration works in the opposite direction, producing outperformance that can be substantial.

## Researching PJFG

The fund's prospectus and fact sheet explain the investment mandate and the performance history relative to the Russell 1000 Growth Index (a reasonable comparator). Quarterly holdings reports reveal which companies the manager holds and in what size. The fund's website typically includes commentary from the portfolio manager outlining the major themes and risks as they see them. An investor evaluating PJFG should ask: Is the manager's view on where growth will emerge next credible? Are the current holdings priced for near-perfection, or is there margin for safety if execution falters? How has the fund behaved in the last three value rotations, and can I tolerate that volatility?
