> The consumer that is confident buys more. The momentum says which ones will do it first.

PEZ is an exchange-traded fund that combines two investment ideas: sector focus on consumer discretionary companies and a momentum-selection system. Consumer discretionary includes retailers, restaurant chains, automakers, leisure companies, and others that sell products people want but do not strictly need. Their sales tend to rise when the economy is strong and confidence is high, and fall sharply in recessions. PEZ takes those cyclical stocks and applies Dorsey Wright's momentum system to pick the ones showing the strongest upward momentum, then rebalances every quarter.

## The cyclical sector bet

Consumer discretionary stocks are inherently cyclical. When unemployment is low, wages are rising, and consumers feel confident, they upgrade their wardrobes, eat out more, buy new cars, and plan vacations. Company earnings and stock prices rise. When recession looms or unemployment spikes, they cut back. The same companies that were roaring in the boom can crumble in the downturn.

This is the opposite of consumer staples, which includes grocers and household-goods makers. People buy those no matter the economy. But they also do not push the stock higher when times are good. Consumer discretionary offers more upside in expansions and more downside in contractions.

PEZ is betting that cyclical consumer companies are worth owning and that owning the ones with the strongest momentum — the ones already rising and seeing improving earnings — will outperform the broader cyclical group.

## Momentum in a sector context

PEZ applies the same Dorsey Wright system used by other Invesco momentum funds, but narrows the universe to the consumer discretionary sector. This narrowing changes the character of the fund. A broad momentum fund owns large tech stocks when they are rallying and shifts toward other sectors when momentum turns. PEZ, by contrast, is always staying within consumer discretionary. It is a bet that this sector will deliver momentum, not a pure momentum strategy unconstrained by sector.

This focus can amplify the sector's moves. When consumer spending is strong and discretionary stocks are rallying, PEZ can be a powerful lever. When the sector turns, it can fall faster than the broader market. A pure momentum fund might have rotated into staples or healthcare earlier, but PEZ stays in cyclical, following the momentum within that box.

## How the fund selects holdings

Each quarter, Dorsey Wright's system ranks all large and mid-cap consumer discretionary stocks on price momentum, relative strength, and earnings revision trends. The highest-ranked stocks become PEZ's core holdings, weighted to emphasise the strongest momentum signals. The list is then rebalanced, usually at the start of a new quarter. This means PEZ is dynamically shifting within the sector as momentum leaders change.

In a strong expansion, the fund might hold a heavy weight in homebuilders and furniture retailers. In a slowdown but where there is still momentum, it might shift to discount retailers or streaming services. It is systematic and disciplined, not subjective.

## The sector bias

Because PEZ is locked to consumer discretionary, it is a concentrated bet. It will lag in recessions and during periods when investors favour defensive stocks. It will outrun in expansions and bull markets. The sectors it includes — retail, restaurants, automakers, hotels, leisure — all face distinct pressures. Retail is under structural pressure from e-commerce. Restaurants face labour inflation and food costs. Automakers compete globally and face electric-vehicle transition. Some of these tailwinds and headwinds play out over years.

PEZEZ is not a weather-the-cycle, hold-forever fund. It is a tactical tool for investors who believe consumer discretionary will outperform and who want the added leverage of owning the momentum leaders within that sector.

## Returns, volatility, and timing risk

Like all momentum strategies, PEZ amplifies returns in bull markets and amplifies losses in bear markets. It also faces a sector rotation risk: if consumer discretionary falls out of favour relative to other sectors, PEZ will underperform even if its momentum discipline is working perfectly. An investor in PEZ is making two bets: that consumer discretionary will do well, and that buying the best-performing ones within that sector will add value.

The quarterly rebalancing creates some timing risk. If the market drops sharply on the rebalancing day, the fund sells holdings that have fallen (taking losses) and buys fresh ones. This is the cost of a systematic approach. Over long periods, the discipline has proven valuable for other Invesco Dorsey Wright funds, but there is no guarantee that it will work the same way for a sector-constrained version.

## Costs and tax efficiency

PEZ charges an expense ratio that covers Invesco's management, the Dorsey Wright system, and the quarterly rebalancing. The ratio is reasonable but not the cheapest for a sector ETF. Turnover is moderate, which is good for tax efficiency in taxable accounts. The fund trades on an exchange with adequate liquidity.

## Who should consider it

PEZ suits investors who believe consumer discretionary will outperform in the near term, who understand the sector's cyclical nature, and who want a disciplined system for owning the momentum leaders within that sector rather than picking individual stocks. It is useful as a tactical, satellite position around a broader portfolio, especially during economic expansions when consumer spending is robust.

It is not suitable for investors seeking stability, defensive positioning, or exposure to the broadest market. It is not a core holding. It is a tactical tool for investors with conviction about the economy and the consumer sector.

## How to research PEZ

Start with Invesco's fund fact sheet and prospectus, which detail the Dorsey Wright selection criteria and the consumer discretionary universe. Look at PEZ's historical performance during expansions and recessions. See how it has behaved relative to a broad market index and a non-momentum consumer discretionary fund. Understand the current holdings and their momentum rankings. Check the turnover and tax efficiency. Finally, assess your own economic view. If you think the consumer will weaken, PEZ is not the right tool. If you think it will thrive and you want leverage to the momentum within that group, PEZ becomes compelling.
