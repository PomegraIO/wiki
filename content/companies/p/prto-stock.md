The RCN Pareto Strategic Allocation ETF is an [actively managed fund](/actively-managed-fund/) designed to balance growth and stability by dynamically allocating across stocks, [bonds](/bond/), and alternative investments. Rather than track a fixed index, the fund applies a rules-based allocation strategy informed by the Pareto principle — the observation that a small number of holdings or shifts often produce outsized effects — to adjust its mix in response to changing market conditions. It trades on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) under the ticker PRTO.

Where most diversified allocation funds settle into a static target (such as 60% stocks and 40% bonds, held year after year), PRTO is built to move within a defined band. The fund uses a systematic framework to decide when to emphasize equities over fixed income, when to add commodity or alternative exposure, and when to take risk off the table. This approach appeals to investors who want [diversification](/diversification/) and discipline without delegating every decision to a passive index formula.

The fund's philosophy rests on the observation that different asset classes do not move in lockstep. Stocks rally when the economy strengthens; bonds often gain when growth stalls and rates fall. Alternatives such as real assets or [managed futures](/managed-futures/) can carve out their own patterns. By holding a mix and rebalancing when certain pieces move far from their target weight, a portfolio captures this diversity rather than concentrating all bets on one direction.

Because PRTO is actively managed, it carries a higher [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) than a passive counterpart — a meaningful difference for long-term holders who pay that cost every year regardless of whether active decisions add value. The fund is transparent about its allocations and rebalancing activity, updating holdings regularly so that investors can see exactly how the manager is positioning the portfolio. That transparency is one reason active exchange-traded products have gained traction in recent years: unlike some actively managed [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/) that reveal holdings only quarterly, an ETF can be tracked and scrutinized in real time.

The fund is appropriate for investors who want broad exposure across stocks and bonds but find pure passive allocation funds too rigid. It appeals most to those with intermediate to long time horizons who are comfortable with the fee structure and the possibility that active allocation calls might occasionally lag a simpler approach. For someone building a core portfolio and preferring not to tinker with the mix themselves, PRTO offers a delegated but flexible alternative to a static allocation model.

As with any actively managed strategy, the critical question is whether the active decisions add enough return above fees to justify the cost. Investors researching PRTO should compare its performance net of expenses against equivalent passive allocations and should understand exactly how the strategic rebalancing framework works — the fund's fact sheet and prospectus spell this out. The fund trades with typical ETF liquidity on major exchanges, so entry and exit costs are usually minimal for individual investors.
