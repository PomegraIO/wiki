## What does PSCT hold and what does it expose me to?

PSCT tracks the S&P SmallCap 600 Information Technology Index, giving investors exposure to technology and software companies with [market capitalizations](/market-capitalization/) typically between 1 and 10 billion dollars. These are established, publicly traded firms—software developers, semiconductor designers, IT services providers, digital-platform operators, and data analytics companies—smaller than the mega-cap tech giants but with real revenues, customer bases, and competitive positions. The fund holds roughly 100 to 150 stocks across the breadth of the technology sector: application software, systems software, semiconductor equipment, IT infrastructure, digital services, and related categories. The exact composition changes only when the index reconstitutes, typically quarterly.

## How does small-cap technology differ from the tech stocks most investors know?

Large technology companies like [Apple](/aapl-stock/), [Microsoft](/msft-stock/), and [Nvidia](/nvda-stock/) are profitable, cash-generating machines with pricing power, global scale, and barriers to competition. Small-cap technology companies are different. Many are in growth mode—investing heavily in product development and sales, reinvesting profits rather than paying [dividends](/dividend/), depending on continued market expansion to justify their valuations. This makes small-cap tech highly sensitive to sentiment. When investors are optimistic about growth, small-cap technology stocks can outperform by wide margins. When sentiment turns—especially when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) rise and make future profits worth less in today's dollars—small-cap tech often falls sharply. The volatility is higher and the swings are larger than for a diversified technology index or a large-cap technology fund.

## Why does PSCT swing more than the broader technology sector?

PSCT is a pure play on small-cap technology, with no large-cap anchoring. It also carries a technology sector concentration—all the holdings are information technology companies, with no defensive healthcare, consumer staples, or utilities to cushion downturns. During booms in investor appetite for technology and growth, these combined factors amplify upside. During slowdowns, especially when interest rates rise and capital becomes scarce, they amplify downside. An investor in PSCT is making a directional bet on both small-cap performance and technology sector strength; the fund does not diversify away either risk.

## How liquid is PSCT as a trading vehicle?

PSCT is highly liquid for practical purposes. The fund trades millions of shares daily on major U.S. [stock exchanges](/stock-exchange/), and [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) are typically tight—usually a penny or two on the fund's price. An investor can enter or exit a position efficiently without moving the market. The underlying holdings—small-cap technology stocks—are less liquid than mega-cap names, but because PSCT holds a basket of 100+ stocks, its own liquidity benefits from aggregation. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is low, in the range of 0.4% annually or less, typical of passive [index funds](/index-fund/).

## Who should own PSCT and who should not?

PSCT appeals to investors bullish on small-cap technology, confident that smaller innovative technology companies will outperform over the investor's time horizon, and comfortable with the volatility that accompanies them. It can serve as a tilted technology exposure within a diversified portfolio, capturing growth and innovation in categories that large-cap technology alone might miss. It also suits tactical positioning: an investor who believes interest rates are about to fall and sentiment toward growth technology will improve might use PSCT to gain that levered exposure.

PSCT is not a holding for investors with low volatility tolerance or short time horizons. It is not a core, diversified position; it is a sector and size tilt. An investor who is uncomfortable seeing their money experience steep drawdowns should not own PSCT at meaningful sizes. Similarly, an investor who needs capital stability should avoid concentrated technology exposure, especially at the small-cap end where individual company risks can overwhelm sector trends.

## What are the real risks of holding PSCT?

The primary risks are sector concentration, small-cap volatility, and interest-rate sensitivity. PSCT holds only technology companies, so a secular decline in technology profitability or investor appetite for tech will hit the entire fund. Small-cap stocks are less liquid and less widely covered than large-cap, which can create information gaps or price inefficiency. Many small-cap technology companies are growth-focused and not yet profitable, so their valuations depend on discounted future earnings—a calculation that becomes much less generous if interest rates rise or economic growth slows. Market dislocations can hit small-cap portfolios harder than large-cap ones, particularly during risk-off periods when fund outflows force selling.

## How should I research and monitor PSCT?

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet from Invesco, which lay out the index methodology, the current holdings, and the expense ratio. Understanding the breakdown between profitable and pre-profitable companies, and between different technology sub-sectors (software, semiconductors, IT services, etc.), clarifies what you actually own. Monitor technology sector trends, interest-rate expectations, venture-capital funding flows, and the earnings results of major technology companies for context. Comparing PSCT's performance to large-cap technology funds and to the broader S&P 500 shows periods when small-cap tech leads or lags. Finally, understand PSCT not as a core holding but as a tactical or thematic position, suited to times when you expect small-cap technology to outperform.
