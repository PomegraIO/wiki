*The **Invesco National AMT-Free [Municipal Bond](/municipal-bond/) ETF** (PZA) focuses on tax-exempt municipal [bonds](/bond/) — debt issued by states, cities, and public authorities — that are specifically exempt from the federal [Alternative Minimum Tax. For investors](/alternative-minimum-tax-investor/) in higher brackets and states with low or no income tax, the after-tax yield on municipals can exceed taxable bonds significantly, and the AMT restriction narrows the universe further to bonds that pass particular scrutiny.*

## The appeal of AMT-free bonds

Municipal bonds ordinarily pay interest that is exempt from federal income tax. For someone in the 37 percent federal bracket, this creates enormous tax efficiency: a 4 percent muni yield is equivalent to roughly 6.3 percent taxable yield. That gap is often wide enough to make municipals the choice for high-income earners.

The [Alternative Minimum Tax](/alternative-minimum-tax/), however, is a parallel tax system that applies to certain taxpayers and disallows certain deductions — including, critically, the deduction for interest on many municipal bonds. So-called "AMT-preference bonds," typically those issued to finance private activities (sports stadiums, airport facilities, industrial parks), trigger AMT calculations. For investors already near or above the AMT threshold, these bonds offer no real tax exemption.

PZA sidesteps this problem by holding only bonds that escape AMT treatment. Most are general-obligation bonds issued by states and cities to finance essential public infrastructure — schools, highways, water systems. The fund's universe is therefore smaller than a broad muni fund, but for the investors it is built for — typically high earners who are either already subject to AMT or close enough to worry about it — that smaller universe is exactly right.

## Holdings and structure

PZA holds upward of 2,000 individual municipal bonds spread across issuers nationwide. The portfolio skews toward investment-grade (BBB and above), so [credit risk](/credit-risk/) is generally modest. The fund's average maturity sits in the intermediate range, typically around 6 to 9 years, a middle ground that avoids both the interest-rate sensitivity of long bonds and the low yields of short bonds.

Because the fund rolls over on a schedule and holds hundreds of individual names, it functions more like a diversified bond portfolio than a leveraged or complex product. Distributions arrive monthly, representing collected interest minus any realized losses on bonds the fund has sold. The fund can also swap bonds to capture tax losses in down markets — a feature that makes the after-tax return even more attractive for taxable accounts.

Invesco, the fund's sponsor, is one of the largest ETF issuers and runs numerous muni-focused products. PZA is not highly specialized; it is a core-holding muni vehicle aimed at a specific tax bracket and preference.

## The mathematics of tax-free income

Because muni interest is federal-tax-free for most investors, comparing the yield directly to a taxable bond is misleading. A 4 percent muni yield, for someone in the 37 percent federal bracket, is equivalent in after-tax terms to a taxable yield of about 6.3 percent — calculated as 4 / (1 - 0.37). For someone in the 24 percent bracket, the same 4 percent muni is equivalent to 5.26 percent taxable. The higher your marginal rate, the more attractive munis become.

Add to that the fact that PZA is screened for AMT exemption, and the fund is offering what amounts to a high-quality, tax-efficient fixed-income holding for a specific slice of taxpayers. The trade-off is that yields are lower than taxable bonds of comparable duration — the market prices in the tax benefit. Someone in a low bracket or holding the fund in a tax-deferred account does not benefit from that pricing and is arguably giving up yield with nothing to show for it.

## Risks and limitations

Muni bonds are not risk-free. Credit risk exists — issuer defaults happen, and PZA's [diversification](/diversification/) helps but does not eliminate that risk. [Interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/) applies: when rates rise, the value of existing bonds falls. Because PZA holds intermediate-maturity bonds, a 1 percent rise in rates might reduce the fund's value by 5–7 percent.

Liquidity can also tighten in stressed markets. Individual municipal bonds often trade in smaller size and wider [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) than stocks, and during market dislocations (such as 2008 or 2020) muni liquidity can dry up, making the fund harder to trade quickly without moving the market. The fund itself is reasonably liquid, but the underlying bonds are not.

Perhaps most important: AMT exemption is not universal tax exemption. While federal income tax is avoided, some states tax muni interest from issuers outside the state, and all investors must pay [Social Security](/social-security-personal/) and Medicare taxes on the full muni income. For the target audience — high earners in states with no income tax or residents of the state issuing most of the bonds the fund holds — this is often not a real limitation. For others, the tax benefit is less clear-cut.

## Who PZA is for and how to evaluate it

This fund works best for federal-tax-bracket investors (typically over 200k annual income) who live in states with no income tax or hold the fund in an account focused on low-tax-bracket states. It is less attractive for those in lower federal brackets, those who will spend the income on Medicare or other means-tested programs, and those holding it in a retirement account where the tax exemption is wasted.

When evaluating PZA, compare its after-tax yield to taxable alternatives (corporations, Treasuries) and to other muni funds. Look at the fund's [effective duration](/effective-duration/) (a measure of interest-rate sensitivity) and credit composition. Monitor the tax-loss-harvesting activity in the fund — prospectuses and fact sheets note realized losses. And check your own expected marginal tax rate: the math of tax equivalence only works if you actually face that rate.
