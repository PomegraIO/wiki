*The **PL Growth and [Income ETF](/income-etf/)** — ticker PLGI — is a diversified fund that holds both stocks and [bonds](/bond/), seeking to generate current income through [dividends](/dividend/) and interest while providing capital appreciation from equity price movements, targeting investors who want exposure to both growth and stability.*

## What kinds of assets does PLGI actually hold?

PLGI divides its portfolio between two main buckets: equities and fixed-income securities. The equity portion emphasizes stocks that pay dividends—companies that are stable, mature, and return cash to shareholders regularly. These tend to skew toward utilities, consumer staples, health care, and financial services rather than high-growth technology. The fixed-income portion holds a mix of government bonds, [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/), and potentially other income-producing securities. The exact allocation between stocks and bonds may vary but typically hovers around 60% equities and 40% fixed income, or some similar split that the fund's managers or index-building process determines. The aim is to create a portfolio that generates meaningful income from both camps.

## Why would an investor choose a blended fund like this?

The traditional case for PLGI rests on two needs. Some investors need current cash flow from their portfolio—retirees, for instance, who want to live partly off investment returns. A pure stock fund is volatile and uncertain on income; a pure bond fund is low-growth and doesn't hedge [inflation](/inflation/). PLGI attempts to split the difference. The dividend portion of the stock holdings provides visible income that compounds year after year; the bond holdings provide stability and predictable interest payments.

A second appeal is simplicity. Instead of assembling a separate portfolio of stocks and bonds and rebalancing between them, an investor can own a single fund that does all of that internally. This is particularly useful for smaller accounts where managing two or three separate positions creates tax friction and psychological complexity.

A third appeal is risk smoothing. In years when stocks fall sharply, the bond allocation typically holds up better or even rises (if [interest rates](/interest-rate/) fall), which dampens total portfolio volatility. Conversely, when stocks soar, the equity portion participates in that gain. The bonds act as a speed bump rather than a brake.

## What does "income" actually mean in this context?

Income in PLGI comes from two sources. The dividend payments made by the stocks—often 2% to 3% annually, sometimes higher, rarely lower—flow through to shareholders. The interest payments made by the bonds—currently ranging widely depending on the [bond maturity](/bond-maturity-corporate/) and credit quality—also flow through. These payments may be distributed monthly, quarterly, or at another interval; the fund's prospectus specifies this. Dividends on stocks are often taxed at more favourable rates than ordinary income if held in a taxable account, while bond interest is typically taxed as ordinary income. The effective yield of the fund (total income divided by price) is the starting point for understanding what an investor should expect, though past yields do not predict future ones.

## How much is growth versus income?

This is where PLGI's name captures the trade-off: the fund pursues *both* simultaneously, which is inherently a compromise. A portfolio that is 60% stocks and 40% bonds will participate in [stock market](/stock-market/) gains, but not fully—if stocks rise 20%, a 60/40 portfolio rises closer to 12%. Similarly, if stocks fall 20%, the portfolio falls closer to 12%. The income component is more generous than a pure [growth fund](/growth-fund/) would provide, but less than a bond-heavy portfolio. The fund is not meant for investors chasing maximum capital appreciation or maximum current income; it is meant for those whose horizon, needs, and temperament call for something in between.

## What are the costs, and how does volatility compare?

PLGI charges an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) that is moderate for a blended fund. The ratio covers the costs of holding and rebalancing the stock and bond positions. The fund's volatility—the up-and-down swings in price—is lower than a pure stock fund but higher than a pure bond fund, roughly reflecting the 60/40 mix. This means PLGI should experience smaller drawdowns during crashes and smaller gains during rallies than a full equity portfolio.

## What are the real risks?

One risk is that the fund is stuck between two stools: in a strong [bull market](/bull-market/), the bond allocation acts as a drag, and a pure stock portfolio outperforms; in a [bear market](/bear-market/), the bond allocation softens the blow but does not eliminate it. A retiree hoping to never see a significant portfolio decline might still be disappointed by a 10–15% drop in PLGI during a market crisis.

A second risk is interest-rate sensitivity. If interest rates rise sharply, bond prices fall, and the bond portion of PLGI declines. This is particularly acute for longer-dated bonds. A rising-rate environment can hurt both stocks and bonds simultaneously, breaking the traditional expectation that bonds provide a hedge.

A third risk is that the stock portion, by emphasizing dividend payers, naturally tilts away from growth and toward mature, slower-expanding companies. In decades when growth beats value, PLGI lags. Conversely, in downturns when dividend-paying stability is favoured, PLGI outperforms.

## How would you research and compare PLGI to alternatives?

Start by reading the fund's prospectus to understand the exact rules governing how the stock-to-bond ratio is set and when rebalancing occurs. Examine the actual holdings: what are the largest stock positions, and what is the average [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/)? What are the largest bond holdings, and what is their average maturity and credit quality? Compare PLGI's performance over full [market cycles](/market-cycle/)—bull markets, bear markets, rising-rate environments—to a simple 60/40 portfolio of [index funds](/index-fund/) (a broad stock fund and a broad bond fund), which achieves the same allocation at lower cost. Understand your own cash-flow needs and risk tolerance: do you genuinely need monthly or quarterly income, or would you be satisfied with total return? A blended fund like PLGI is well-suited to the former, less so to the latter.
