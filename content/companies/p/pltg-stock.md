> Leverage is not free — PLTG offers amplified returns, but the cost of daily hedging and reset mechanics ensures that holding periods longer than days will see the fund underperform a simple 2X leveraged long position.

PLTG is a leveraged fund with one transparent goal: to move twice as fast as Palantir Technologies on a daily basis. When PLTR rallies 1%, PLTG aims to climb 2%. When PLTR falls 1%, PLTG should fall roughly 2%. The fund uses derivatives and leverage to achieve this 2X multiple and resets that leverage daily to prevent the mathematical drift that would otherwise erode returns over time. The appeal is straightforward for a trader or short-term bullish investor: greater upside capture. If PLTR is expected to move sharply higher in the next few days, PLTG magnifies that move. But leverage cuts both ways. A 5% fall in PLTR becomes a 10% loss in PLTG. Volatility—even neutral-to-sideways volatility where PLTR ends the week where it started—will decay PLTG's value faster than it decays PLTR's. This is the cost of the daily reset: the fund must rebalance its derivative positions every evening, selling some long exposure on up days and buying it back on down days, a practice that bleeds capital in choppy markets.

## The daily reset design and its mathematical logic.

A fund that simply borrowed 1X of assets and invested 2X in PLTR stock would face a severe decay problem. Suppose PLTR swings up 10% on day one, then down 10% on day two. The simple 2X position would gain 20% on day one, but that gain becomes the new starting point for day two, when a 10% fall erases 20% of the now-larger position. The ending result would be a loss, not a break-even. Leverage Shares avoids this by resetting the leverage to exactly 2X against the day's starting price each evening. This means PLTG's performance track on a day-by-day basis locks to 2X the daily PLTR move, preventing the compounding loss that would occur in a pure leverage structure. The trade-off is the cost of rebalancing. Every evening, the fund must adjust its positions—buy or sell derivatives, pay bid-ask spreads, and implement the reset. Over time, these costs accumulate. In a volatile, sideways market, they can be substantial. Over a trending market with clear daily direction, the reset cost becomes less noticeable relative to the amplified gains.

## Why volatility decay compounds the damage of time.

If PLTR moves sideways or whipsaws without clear direction, PLTG will lose value even if PLTR ends the period roughly where it started. A simple example illustrates this: PLTR rises 5% on day one (PLTG up 10%), then falls 5% on day two. After the daily reset, PLTG falls 10% from its elevated day-one close, landing below where it started even though PLTR is flat. Over extended periods—weeks or months—this decay can be dramatic, especially in high-volatility environments. PLTG is designed for holding periods of hours or days, not quarters. PLTG's expense ratio covers the cost of leverage, derivatives management, and daily rebalancing. These fees are higher than a simple stock ETF because the fund is doing active work every day to maintain the 2X relationship. Additionally, the bid-ask spread on PLTG can be wider than on liquid mega-cap stocks. The combination of fees and spreads matters most to day traders and short-term holders; a buy-and-hold investor in PLTG should expect those costs to erode returns significantly over weeks or months.

The fund may also hold cash or short-term fixed-income to balance the delta and maintain the leverage target.
