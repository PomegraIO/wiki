*The **Invesco BuyBack Achievers ETF** — ticker PKW — is an exchange-traded fund that holds U.S. companies selected on the basis of their commitment to share repurchases. Rather than trying to pick winners by guessing which firms will outperform, PKW bets that management teams with a track record of consistently buying back their own shares are signalling confidence in [intrinsic value](/intrinsic-value/) and disciplined capital allocation.*

## What does PKW actually track, and how does the selection work?

PKW follows an index of American companies that have repurchased at least a threshold amount of their own shares over a recent trailing period — usually requiring sustained buyback activity over the past few years, not just a single large one-time event. The index screens for companies across all major sectors and capitalizations, which means PKW can hold anything from mega-cap technology firms to mid-sized industrials, as long as they meet the buyback threshold. The philosophy underlying the index is straightforward: a company that buys back shares in the open market is making a public bet that its stock is undervalued. When a management team has a track record of actually executing repurchases, that buyback discipline becomes a filter for capital-allocation quality.

## Why is PKW equally weighted rather than market-cap weighted?

PKW uses equal-weighting rather than market-cap weighting, meaning each constituent receives roughly the same dollar amount rather than letting the largest companies dominate. This design choice introduces a tilt toward smaller, more neglected names within the buyback universe, which can amplify both returns and volatility relative to a conventional cap-weighted approach. Because it is equal-weighted and rebalances periodically, PKW trades at higher portfolio turnover than a market-cap-weighted fund would. That turnover is necessary to maintain equal weights as individual stock prices move, but it does drive a slightly higher tax bill for taxable account holders and generates more trading costs than a passive index tracker.

## What are the real risks that come with buyback-focused investing?

Buyback-focused strategies carry built-in risks that do not trouble a simple market-cap-weighted fund. First, buyback announcements and actual execution are not the same thing — a company may announce a large buyback program and never fully deploy the capital, particularly if the stock price rises sharply or the business hits headwinds. Second, a buyback can be a double-edged signal. Strong [free cash flow](/free-cash-flow/) and a plan to return it is healthy; an aggressively timed buyback near market peaks, when shares are expensive, can be a waste of shareholder money. The index rule captures the fact of growing buybacks but not their timing relative to valuation — so a portfolio of PKW holdings might inadvertently contain companies buying back shares at unfortunate prices. Third, the fund's equal weighting means it will drift over time as holdings appreciate or depreciate, requiring rebalancing that can trigger turnover and tax costs. Equal weighting also creates exposure to smaller names within the index, which trade with wider spreads than mega-cap stocks and carry more [idiosyncratic risk](/idiosyncratic-risk/).

## How does PKW trade and what are the costs?

The fund carries an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) in the range typical for passive [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/) tracking a straightforward index — generally under 0.50% annually. PKW trades on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) like any stock, with volumes and [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) that reflect its size as a well-established fund with billions in assets under management. Invesco publishes the underlying index composition regularly, and the fund's holdings are transparent, so researchers can easily check which companies are included and verify the buyback logic for themselves.

## What is the best way to research PKW?

Start with the NASDAQ US BuyBack Achievers Index methodology, published by NASDAQ and maintained by Invesco, which explains the selection and weighting rules in detail. The fund's fact sheet and annual holdings file show the current portfolio composition. Review the prospectus for fee structure, tax efficiency, and any restrictions on which buyback definitions the index uses. Track the fund's top holdings and their recent buyback announcements to verify that companies are actually executing their stated programs. For context, compare PKW's performance and holdings to a standard U.S. equity [index fund](/index-fund/) and to competitors such as other buyback-focused strategies to understand whether the selection rule is adding value or simply replicating the market at higher cost. Like all exchange-traded funds, PKW trades at prices set by the market; nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell.
