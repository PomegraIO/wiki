What does PFI track?

PFI is an exchange-traded fund that provides exposure to stocks in the financial sector — banks, insurance companies, investment firms, asset managers, and related financial services — chosen and weighted according to momentum-based criteria. Rather than owning all financial stocks in proportion to their market capitalization, PFI applies a systematic methodology that emphasizes stocks exhibiting strength relative to their own history and their peers. The Dorsey Wright methodology, which shapes the fund, combines price momentum with relative strength and technical analysis to select and rank holdings.

How does the Dorsey Wright approach work?

The Dorsey Wright framework evaluates stocks on the basis of relative strength and momentum — the notion that stocks that have been performing well tend to continue performing well in the near term, and that technical factors such as relative price position and trend matter for timing. Unlike a simple market-cap index, which owns every stock proportionally to its size, Dorsey Wright screens apply objective criteria to identify which financial stocks are in uptrends and which have positive momentum relative to their sector and to the broader market. Stocks meeting these criteria receive weights based on their strength scores; weaker performers receive lower weights or may be excluded entirely. The approach rebalances periodically as momentum scores change, rotating the portfolio to maintain an emphasis on the strongest performers at any given time.

What are the costs and liquidity mechanics?

PFI trades on an exchange throughout the day like a stock, allowing investors to buy or sell shares at real-time market prices without waiting for a daily net asset value calculation. The fund's expense ratio reflects the ongoing cost of running the momentum-screening process and rebalancing to track the Dorsey Wright methodology. Trading costs arise from the rebalancing itself as holdings are adjusted; however, these are typically modest because the rebalancing schedule is predictable. Individual investors pay no transaction fee to buy or sell PFI shares — only the bid-ask spread, the same cost incurred when trading any stock.

What are the real risks in a momentum-based financial fund?

Momentum strategies carry two principal risks. First, momentum can reverse abruptly. A stock or sector in a strong uptrend may face a sudden downturn for fundamental reasons or technical reasons, and a systematic momentum strategy has no built-in protection against catching the top of a move. Second, PFI's focus on the financial sector concentrates risk in a single industry. While the Dorsey Wright selection may improve returns within financials, it does not diversify away the sector's exposure to interest rates, credit cycles, regulatory changes, and economic slowdowns. In periods when financial stocks underperform the broader market, PFI amplifies that underperformance relative to a diversified equity portfolio. Momentum strategies also tend to underperform in low-volatility, sideways markets where price trends flatten and relative strength signals become choppy.

Who is PFI suited for?

PFI appeals to investors who believe momentum has predictive power and who are comfortable concentrating within a single sector in pursuit of that edge. It also suits traders who view financial stocks as attractive but seek a systematic, rebalancing approach rather than individual stock-picking. The fund's daily liquidity makes it accessible for both long-term and tactical holdings. Those seeking broad diversification or a smoother sector exposure typically find a traditional financial-services index fund more suitable.

How would someone research PFI?

A prospective investor should examine the Dorsey Wright methodology in detail — exactly how stocks are scored, which factors matter most, and how often the portfolio rebalances. The fund's prospectus and fact sheet lay this out. Comparing PFI's holdings and sector allocation to a traditional financial-sector index reveals how differently the momentum approach allocates capital. Reviewing the fund's historical relative performance versus such an index in different market conditions (strong uptrends, reversals, flat periods) shows whether momentum added value or suffered from whipsaw. The fund's website maintains current holdings and historical performance data useful for this comparison.
