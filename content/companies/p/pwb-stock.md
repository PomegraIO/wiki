*The **Invesco Large Cap [Growth ETF](/growth-etf/)** (PWB) tracks a capitalization-weighted index of large American companies selected using a growth criterion — companies that are expected to grow earnings faster than their peers — and delivers that exposure at low cost through an exchange-traded fund.*

The universe of large-cap US stocks is often split along a single axis: growth versus value. That division is less about a company's intrinsic quality and more about how the market has already priced it. A company trading at a high [earnings multiple](/earnings-multiple/), whose investors are betting heavily on future growth, is labelled "growth." A company trading at a low multiple, whose growth prospects are modest, is labelled "value." PWB tilts explicitly toward the first camp.

The fund tracks the Invesco Large Cap Growth Index, which holds companies with [market capitalizations](/market-capitalization/) above a threshold that keeps the universe to the largest 500 to 600 US companies. From that pool, the index selects companies based on a growth score — variables like earnings growth rate, sales growth, and earnings revisions. Companies with higher growth scores earn larger weights in the index; slower-growth names are excluded or lightly weighted. The index then floats with market capitalizations, so a company that becomes much more valuable gains weight automatically.

This systematic tilt toward growth has profound consequences. The fund will hold a concentration of technology and healthcare companies, which have historically been the sectors with the highest expected growth. It will hold fewer financials, utilities, and energy companies — sectors associated with stable [dividends](/dividend/) and modest growth. Over the past two decades, that tilt has been a very lucrative one; technology and growth stocks have dramatically outperformed value, and PWB has ridden that trend.

The fund is entirely passive, meaning it does not employ stock pickers to decide which growth companies to favor. Instead, it mechanically holds all index constituents in their index weights. That passivity is a huge cost advantage. Active [growth funds](/growth-fund/) charge 0.5 to 1.5 percent annually to employ managers who try to outpick the market. PWB charges a fraction of that because there is nothing to pick; the index does the choosing. That cost efficiency means the fund's returns before fees closely track the index, and the fund's returns after fees beat most actively managed competitors.

PWB trades on a [stock exchange](/stock-exchange/) as an ETF, which offers two practical conveniences that traditional index [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/) do not. First, [intraday liquidity](/intraday-liquidity/) — you can buy or sell during market hours at market prices, rather than waiting for the fund to price once a day. Second, tax efficiency — the ETF structure can distribute capital gains less frequently, allowing long-term holders to minimize taxes. For taxable accounts, that structure matters; the costs of tax-inefficient mutual funds compound over decades.

The fund's concentration in growth came with a cost in recent years when growth stocks fell out of favor and value stocks surged. From 2021 to 2023, PWB lagged the broader market index because its technology and growth-heavy portfolio underperformed. That is the trade-off of any tilt: it works when the tilted factor is in favor and hurts when it is out. A diversified investor might own both PWB and a complementary [value fund](/value-fund/) to neutralize the bet.

The fund has become a vehicle for a specific kind of investor: one who believes that large-cap American companies — particularly in technology and healthcare — will generate strong earnings growth, who wants to express that view cost-efficiently without active management, and who is comfortable with the [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) and volatility that growth stocks sometimes experience. Over the long run, growth stocks have been rewarded for their growth, but the path is not smooth.

PWB also means access to the most liquid and widely held large-cap companies on Earth. These are names that dominate market indices worldwide, that trade in markets around the globe, and that are followed by thousands of analysts. The information environment is dense, the trading spreads are tight, and the risk of permanent loss from fraud or incompetence is low — though no fund can eliminate [market risk](/market-risk/) itself.

For a long-term investor comfortable with growth-stock volatility and seeking the broadest and most cost-effective way to own the growth corner of the large-cap US market, PWB serves as a simple, low-friction vehicle.
