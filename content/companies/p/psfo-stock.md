The Pacer Swan SOS Flex ETF is a specialized fixed-income alternative designed to generate income through a systematic options strategy. Rather than holding stocks or [bonds](/bond/) in the traditional sense, PSFO sells out-of-the-money [call spreads](/call-spread/) against an equity index, collecting [option premiums](/option-premium/) each month in exchange for capping the fund's upside. The strategy resets annually in October, which is why the product name includes that reference—it is one of several Swan SOS products from Pacer ETFs that employ similar mechanics on different schedules.

## How the strategy works

PSFO operates through a structured monthly cycle. Each month, the fund sells call spreads on the [Nasdaq](/nasdaq/) 100 or similar equity index—meaning it sells a [call option](/call-option/) at one [strike price](/strike-price/) and simultaneously buys a call at a higher strike to cap its loss. The premium collected from selling the call exceeds the cost of the protective call purchased, creating a net credit that flows to shareholders. This premium collection is the fund's primary income source. The strategy is reset each October, meaning new spreads are initiated and any accumulated gains or losses from the previous period are recognized.

The mechanics are rigid by design. By selling calls, the fund agrees to cap its gains if the market rises sharply. If the underlying index stays flat or falls, the fund keeps the full premium. If it rises modestly within the call spread, the fund still profits up to the strike price where the short call begins. Only if the market rises significantly above the upper strike does the fund lose money—but the long call it purchased caps that loss. This structure turns the risk-reward equation on its head: the fund benefits from stagnation and loses from strong rallies.

## Risk profile and the cost of income

The core risk is opportunity cost. In years when equity markets rise strongly, PSFO's gains are capped by the short call strikes. A shareholder in PSFO will materially underperform the broad market in bull years. This is not a hidden cost—it is the price of steady monthly income. The fund is also exposed to [gap risk](/gap-risk/), where the market opens sharply above the call strike, creating losses that the long call may not fully offset if there is a significant gap. Liquidity of the options used matters too; if the underlying options markets dry up, the fund may struggle to execute its strategy.

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is meaningful, reflecting the active management and transaction costs required to reset call spreads monthly. Because the fund is synthetic and does not hold a diversified portfolio of underlying securities, it lacks the [diversification](/diversification/) protection of a traditional equity fund and carries [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) in a single index. In a severe [bear market](/bear-market/), the fund will lose money even as its option spreads contain the loss—but the loss still materializes.

## Ideal use case

PSFO is not a core holding or a buy-and-hold investment for growth. It is a tactical income tool for investors who expect choppy or range-bound markets and want to harvest volatility premiums without owning stocks outright. It works best as a satellite position in a portfolio with other equity exposure, or for investors who have strong conviction that markets will trade sideways. The monthly income is regular but not guaranteed—it comes from options premium, which rises and falls with realized and [implied volatility](/implied-volatility/).

## How to research PSFO

Start with the fund fact sheet and prospectus on Pacer's website, which details the exact call spread strikes, the historical premium collected, and the annual reset mechanics. The fund's homepage typically shows [current yields](/current-yield/) and the cumulative return of the strategy, though these figures should be understood as backward-looking. Review the annual and monthly reports to see which strikes were used and how often the fund hit its cap or took losses when the market gapped higher. Compare PSFO's returns to a simple holding of the Nasdaq 100 or a broad [equity ETF](/equity-etf/) over the same period to see the drag in [bull markets](/bull-market/) and the benefit in flat or down years. Check the daily options volume on the Nasdaq 100 to understand whether the underlying options market has the liquidity needed to support this strategy at scale.
