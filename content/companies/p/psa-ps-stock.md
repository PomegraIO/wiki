*Public Storage is the operator of the largest portfolio of climate-controlled self-storage facilities in the United States, collecting monthly rent from individuals and small businesses seeking secure, accessible storage — and generating additional revenue from locks, boxes, insurance, and premium access.*

## Core storage unit rentals

The foundation of Public Storage's revenue is monthly rent from tenants who lease climate-controlled units. The company operates approximately 2,900 facilities across 47 states, containing roughly 230 million square feet of rentable space. One in every five self-storage units in America is a Public Storage unit.

A typical customer is an individual moving for a job, someone in the midst of a life transition (divorce, downsizing, relocation), or a small business needing overflow inventory space. Rents vary by geography and market conditions — rural areas see unit rents of US$50 per month, while dense coastal markets (Los Angeles, New York, San Francisco) command US$250–350 per month or higher. The company's portfolio is concentrated in high-density markets where real estate costs are high and the population base is large.

Occupancy rates typically range between 80 and 95 per cent across the portfolio. The company manages occupancy through pricing — in tight markets with low available supply, it pushes rents higher; in softer markets, it may offer move-in specials or hold prices steady. Once a customer has rented a unit and placed belongings inside, the customer faces switching costs. Moving to a competitor requires renting a truck, hiring help, updating electronic access credentials, and the hassle of reconfiguring a new space. Most customers renew month to month rather than incur these costs, which gives Public Storage reliable pricing power.

Revenue from base unit rentals is extremely predictable. The company can forecast occupancy and average rent per unit with reasonable accuracy from one quarter to the next, making the business attractive to income-focused investors. This predictability is enhanced by the fact that demand for storage is tied to demographic disruption — job moves, divorces, family changes, business needs — rather than to economic expansion or contraction alone.

## Ancillary products and services

Beyond the base rent from unit leases, Public Storage generates revenue by selling products and services to tenants. These include locks, packing supplies (boxes, tape, bubble wrap), climate-control upgrades, and customer insurance. Customers purchase these items in part because of convenience — they are available on-site — and in part because having them from the storage company ensures compatibility with the unit and reduces the hassle of shopping elsewhere.

Ancillary revenue is modest as a percentage of total revenue — typically a low-single-digit percentage. However, nearly all of this revenue is gross profit, because customers do the work of gathering and purchasing the items. There is no incremental labour, no incremental facility cost. A customer buying a US$30 lock or a box of packing supplies contributes nearly all of that amount to gross profit.

These ancillary services also have a customer-retention benefit. A tenant who has already purchased locks and climate-control upgrades, and whose unit is configured with those items, has even higher switching costs. Investing a little more in accessories or insurance makes leaving less likely.

## Operations, maintenance, and facility management

Once a facility is built and filled with tenants, the cost structure is largely fixed. Public Storage must pay property taxes, insurance, and routine maintenance on each facility. It must also maintain basic labour — a manager or caretaker, security measures, grounds upkeep, and repairs. Many facilities are partially automated with electronic access systems, reducing the need for on-site staff.

The key insight is that these operating costs do not scale with occupancy in the short term. A facility that is 70 per cent occupied still requires the same property-tax bill, the same insurance premium, and roughly the same maintenance budget as one that is 95 per cent occupied. As occupancy rises, the incremental revenue falls almost entirely to gross profit. This operational leverage is what creates the attractive margin profile — gross margins in self-storage typically run 40–60 per cent of revenue, among the highest in real estate.

The company deploys centralized technology systems across the portfolio to manage reservations, customer access, billing, and maintenance scheduling. This scale allows Public Storage to negotiate better rates with insurance providers, maintenance contractors, and property-tax assessment firms than smaller competitors could achieve.

## Acquisition, development, and portfolio expansion

Public Storage expands its portfolio in two ways: acquiring existing facilities from other operators, and developing new facilities on land the company owns or has acquired.

Acquisitions target facilities in prime locations — typically in high-density markets where competition is strong and real estate values are high. The company competes with other buyers to acquire existing properties. Success requires disciplined pricing; overpaying for an acquisition destroys long-term returns because it reduces the yield on the capital deployed.

Development requires identifying land, securing permits, constructing a facility, and leasing units to tenants — a multi-year process. Development in expensive markets (California, New York, Texas, Florida) is capital-intensive. The company must be patient, as returns accrue gradually as occupancy ramps.

Both acquisition and development are funded through a combination of operating cash flow, debt, and occasionally equity issuance. Because Public Storage is a REIT required to distribute 90 per cent of taxable income to shareholders, it cannot hoard cash for large expansion pushes. Growth is therefore constrained to what operating cash flow can fund (after the mandatory dividend) and what additional leverage the balance sheet can support.

## Digital and customer-facing services

Public Storage has invested in technology to streamline how customers find, lease, and access units. The company operates an online reservation system where customers can browse available units, compare prices and features, and complete leases remotely. Customers access their units via electronic locks rather than traditional keys, which reduces the need for on-site staff and improves security.

Digital services also reduce customer-acquisition costs. Customers increasingly search for storage online; a robust digital presence ensures they find a Public Storage facility when they search. Digital channels also enable the company to market dynamic pricing — varying rent based on demand and occupancy in real time — in ways that would have been impractical with traditional leasing.

## Capital allocation and returns to shareholders

As a REIT, Public Storage must distribute at least 90 per cent of taxable income to shareholders via dividends. This constraint shapes capital allocation: the company favours returning cash to shareholders over retaining it to fund expansion.

The result is a dividend-focused business. Shareholders buy Public Storage stock expecting a steady, reliable dividend that grows modestly over time. The company has increased its dividend consistently over decades. The dividend is the primary attraction for income-focused investors and institutions with a need for reliable cash flow.

The company can still grow through acquisitions and development by issuing debt or new equity, but the default posture is to grow the dividend. This alignment encourages management to focus on cash generation and operational efficiency rather than on aggressive growth that would require reinvesting most earnings.

## Research and investor considerations

The annual 10-K filing (SEC CIK 0001393311) provides the most complete picture of the business. Look for trends in occupancy rates, average rent per unit, and year-over-year rent growth. These metrics show whether the company is successfully pushing pricing, managing occupancy, and capturing the benefit of supply-demand dynamics in each market.

Watch the company's capital-expenditure plans and acquisition activity. Does management forecast significant development, or is it focused on organic growth of the existing portfolio? Understanding the cadence of capital deployment reveals growth ambitions and return expectations.

Quarterly earnings calls provide useful colour on regional performance. Public Storage operates across multiple geographic markets; some may be overbuilt while others are undersupplied. Tracking where management is emphasizing new growth and where it is playing defence reveals near-term earnings momentum and capital-allocation priorities.

Monitor the dividend level and growth rate — it is the core metric for shareholders. A growing dividend signals confidence in cash flow and supports the stock's appeal to income investors seeking predictable, growing distributions.
