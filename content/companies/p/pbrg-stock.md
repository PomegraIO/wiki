*The **Leverage Shares 2X Long PBR Daily ETF (PBRG)** is a leveraged exchange-traded fund that seeks to deliver twice the daily percentage change of Petrobras (a Brazilian oil and gas company that trades on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker PBR). It is issued by Leverage Shares, a London-based ETF sponsor, and trades on the American Stock Exchange.*

## What the fund tracks and how it works

PBRG targets a simple but consequential objective: to move twice as much as PBR stock on a daily basis. If PBR rises 1%, PBRG aims to rise 2%; if PBR falls 2%, PBRG aims to fall 4%. This amplification is achieved through derivatives — the fund does not buy large quantities of Petrobras shares but instead uses financial instruments like swaps and futures to create the desired leverage. Each day, the fund resets its leverage ratio, meaning it recalibrates its positions to ensure it will be twice the stock's move for the following trading day.

Petrobras is the state-controlled oil and gas explorer and producer for Brazil, with exposure to global crude markets. It is among the largest oil companies by production and has operations across offshore and onshore fields. Tracking Petrobras means PBRG is fundamentally a commodity play, exposing the holder to the price of crude oil, geopolitical events in and around Brazil, and the company's own operational and financial performance.

## The leverage reset and decay risk

Leveraged ETFs reset daily, and that mechanics matters more than it sounds. Because the reset happens once a day at market close, leveraged ETFs amplify *daily* moves, not longer holding periods. Over weeks and months, the fund often underperforms (or overperforms, if the market is falling) what naïve math would predict. This phenomenon is called decay or slippage, and it is worst when markets are volatile and choppy.

A simple example: suppose PBR moves up 1% on day one, then down 1% on day two, returning to its starting price. A 2X leveraged fund would move up 2% on day one (doubling the 1% gain) and then down 2% on day two (doubling the 1% loss). After two days, the underlying stock is flat, but the 2X leveraged fund is down 0.04%. That loss accumulates over time. The choppier the market, the worse the decay. During volatile periods or in markets that trade sideways, holding PBRG for months often means watching it lose money even if the underlying stock ends the period breakeven or modestly higher.

The fund is, by design, a tool for tactical positioning — a way to amplify a conviction about the direction of PBR over days or weeks — not a long-term core holding. Holding it through a slow, grinding decline is a recipe for outsize losses; holding it through a volatile, sideways drift is a way to watch capital decay even if you get the direction right.

## Costs and trading mechanics

Like most ETFs, PBRG charges an ongoing expense ratio — the annual fee charged to shareholders. Compared to a plain index ETF or a stock position, the cost is higher, partly because the fund must constantly adjust its swap and futures positions to maintain the daily leverage ratio. The fund is reasonably liquid, trading on the AMEX with spreads that are typically not prohibitive during market hours, though volume is modest compared to flagship broad-market funds.

## Who should own it, and when

PBRG is not for most investors. It is not a portfolio holding; it is a tactical instrument. A trader convinced that Petrobras will rise meaningfully over the next two to four weeks might use PBRG to amplify that view. Someone who wants long-term commodity exposure to Brazilian energy should own shares of Petrobras directly or use a plain, non-leveraged energy or commodity fund. Someone who owns PBRG expecting it to be a long-term wealth-builder is likely to be disappointed by decay, fees, and the toll of compounding leverage through inevitable downsides.

The fund is best understood as a short-term volatility amplifier, not an investment vehicle. Petrobras itself is a commodity-driven business with all the volatility that entails; leverage simply speeds up both gains and losses. A shareholder considering PBRG should be clear about the holding period, the level of conviction required to justify the decay cost, and the possibility that even a correct directional call can be undermined by market choppiness or a sudden reversal during the holding period.

## How to research this fund

Start with Leverage Shares' fact sheet for PBRG and the prospectus, which detail the exact derivatives strategy, the daily reset mechanism, and the full fee schedule. Next, understand Petrobras itself — its cash flow, its debt, its exposure to crude prices and Brazilian politics — by reading the company's annual reports and investor presentations. Then run a historical simulation: look up the price of PBR and PBRG at the same point in the past (say, a year ago) and observe how much decay occurred. Compare what a 2X bet on PBR would have returned versus what PBRG actually delivered. This ground-truth exercise often makes the leverage-decay dynamic visceral in a way that an abstract description does not. Finally, consult the fund's daily returns against PBR's returns to verify that it is still tracking the 2X objective; over time, performance drift in leveraged products is not uncommon, and it is always worth verifying.
