*The TCW Transform Systems ETF (PWRD) is an [actively managed fund](/actively-managed-fund/) that invests in companies whose business models center on digital transformation — helping other organizations adopt cloud platforms, data analytics, artificial intelligence, automation, and other technologies to modernize their operations. Issued and managed by TCW, a Los Angeles-based asset manager, PWRD operates without the constraints of a static index, allowing its portfolio managers to shift allocations as the transformation landscape evolves.*

PWRD is fundamentally different from index-tracking ETFs because it is not married to a published benchmark. TCW's team reviews the landscape of technology companies involved in digital transformation — software-as-a-service providers, cloud platforms, data and analytics vendors, cybersecurity firms, automation-software makers, and systems integrators — and picks the stocks they believe are best positioned to benefit from the secular shift toward digital-first operations. This discretion means PWRD can overweight an emerging subcategory (like AI-driven software startups) or underweight a legacy position if the thesis changes, without waiting for an index to rebalance.

The digital-transformation theme is broad enough to encompass multiple sub-themes. During a period when cloud migration is the primary driver, PWRD might emphasize pure-play cloud vendors. Later, as enterprise adoption matures, the fund might shift toward automation software or artificial-intelligence applications. This flexibility is the advantage of active management. The cost, naturally, is that the fund's performance depends entirely on whether TCW's team is making good calls; there is no index to blame if they make poor stock picks.

PWRD's portfolio tends to be concentrated in large-cap software and IT-services names — the biggest, most-liquid targets — but with smaller positions in mid-cap specialists and occasionally emerging high-growth firms. The fund typically holds between 30 and 50 stocks, which is not deeply diversified by absolute standards but is focused enough that TCW can have a genuine point of view on each position. The concentration means that a single stock's success or failure has material impact on returns, both up and down.

The fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is material. Active management of any kind costs more than passive indexing because you are paying managers to research, decide, and trade. TCW discloses the ratio in the prospectus, and prospective investors should compare it against the historical excess returns (or underperformance) to determine whether the team has historically earned its fee. Many thematic-ETF managers do not, which is why passive thematic offerings (index-based funds tracking digital-transformation or AI indices) have proliferated.

One structural point: because PWRD is growth-oriented and concentrated on technology and software, it carries higher volatility than broad-market indices. During risk-on periods when technology stocks are in favor, PWRD can shine. During rotations to value or when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) rise sharply and growth becomes expensive, PWRD can suffer material drawdowns. The prospectus and fund documentation explain the risk profile and provide [historical volatility](/historical-volatility/) metrics.

The prospectus and factsheet detail the fund's current holdings, the investment process, and the management team's approach. Compare PWRD's historical returns (net of fees) against a passive digital-transformation index (if one exists) or against a technology-sector ETF to determine whether TCW's active management has added value. Watch the turnover: a thematic fund that trades heavily might be generating significant tax consequences and trading costs. The fund's website and third-party platforms show current holdings and sector breakdowns.

PWRD appeals to investors who believe that digital transformation is a durable, secular opportunity and who are willing to pay for active expertise to navigate it. For buy-and-hold, low-cost index investors, the fees and concentration make it an expensive bet. For conviction-level thematic investors comfortable with higher volatility, it is one option among several ways to gain exposure to the theme.
