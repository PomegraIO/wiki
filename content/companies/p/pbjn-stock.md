The **PGIM S&P 500 Buffer 20 ETF - June** (PBJN) is an exchange-traded product designed to deliver a simplified return profile: your losses are cushioned (you keep the first 20 per cent of downside), but your gains are capped (you surrender any annual returns above 20 per cent). Each June, the fund resets the contract — new [strike prices](/strike-price/) are set for the next twelve months of observation, and the cap-and-buffer terms are recalibrated based on the market environment at that moment.

## How the buffer and cap work in practice

The fund holds S&P 500 stocks (typically a representative sample, though the exact holdings vary slightly with rebalancing). On top of that equity position, PGIM layers an options strategy that defines the outcome boundaries. If the S&P 500 rises 25 per cent in a calendar year, PBJN rises 20 per cent. If the S&P 500 falls 10 per cent, PBJN stays flat. If the market falls 30 per cent, PBJN falls only 10 per cent (losing 30 minus the 20-per-cent buffer). This trade — accepting an upside cap in exchange for a downside cushion — is explicit and fully disclosed.

The June reset is the critical mechanical event. When the observation period ends in June and a new one begins, PGIM buys or sells options to establish the new cap and buffer for the following year. These resets can occur at very different market prices and [implied volatility](/implied-volatility/) levels. In a low-volatility market, downside protection is cheap to buy, so the cap might be 20 per cent while the buffer stays at 20 per cent. In a high-volatility market, protection is expensive, so the same premium budget might buy a less generous cap (say 18 per cent) to preserve the 20-per-cent buffer. The prospectus outlines how PGIM handles these details, and each reset produces a fact sheet specifying the exact terms for the coming year.

## Who this appeals to and why

PBJN attracts investors with three profiles: risk-averse individuals nearing retirement, tactical allocators expecting volatility without conviction on direction, and behavioural investors aware that losses hurt more than gains feel good. For these investors, PBJN is not a bet but a mechanism. The fund does not try to beat the S&P 500; it replicates it while modifying the volatility profile. Over a full decade of normal market history, PBJN would likely trail the index by the cumulative cost of options protection — perhaps 0.5 to 1.5 per cent per year.

## The costs and the reset mechanics

The defined outcome structure is not free. PGIM charges an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) that covers fund operations and the cost of buying and managing the options contracts that create the buffer and cap. That ratio is higher than a plain [S&P 500 index](/sp-500-index/) fund (often by 0.3 to 0.6 per cent annually) because options have a cost. Additionally, the June reset can trigger capital gains — if the fund is forced to unwind positions to implement the new options strategy, investors may receive distributions. Tax-aware investors should discuss this with an accountant; the tax drag can be material in accounts that are not sheltered.

The reset window itself introduces a timing phenomenon. An investor who holds PBJN through a reset will see the cap and buffer recalculated; if volatility has risen sharply, the new cap might be lower than before. An investor who buys PBJN right after a June reset is locking in the fresh terms; one who buys just before a reset is holding stale terms. This is not hidden, but it is a detail that separates PBJN holders who pay attention from those who do not.

## Researching PBJN

Anyone considering PBJN should read the fund's prospectus (available on the SEC's EDGAR system and on the PGIM website). The prospectus spells out the exact mechanism, the costs, the tax treatment of resets, and how PGIM will handle various market scenarios. Model the outcome across different return scenarios — a +15 per cent year, a +30 per cent year, a –10 per cent year, a –25 per cent year — and verify that the cap and buffer actually align with your risk tolerance. Check PGIM's website for the most recent fact sheet, which shows the current cap and buffer terms, the expense ratio, and performance history. Compare PBJN's long-term return to the S&P 500's — if PBJN has significantly underperformed over the past few years, ask why (it is likely that the market has delivered strong returns above 20 per cent, capping PBJN's gains). None of this is a recommendation to buy or sell; it is a map of what the fund does and how to verify whether it suits your needs.
