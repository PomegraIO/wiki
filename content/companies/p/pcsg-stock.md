> "Five managers, one portfolio: diverse lenses on the same opportunity set, disciplined by a single framework."

The Polen 5Perspectives strategy rests on an uncommon premise: that different skilled investors, using different frameworks and asking different questions, can each add value to a single diversified portfolio. Rather than handing the fund to one manager—even a talented one—Polen brings together five distinct investment teams, each with its own philosophy on small- and mid-cap growth. One might emphasize free-cash-flow generation; another, management team quality and operator incentives; a third, competitive positioning and market-share gains; a fourth, financial-statement health and balance-sheet resilience. The fifth brings yet another lens. The fund holds stocks selected by all five, weighted equally, so the investor gets five different perspectives on the same universe of small and mid-sized growth companies.

### The Appeal of Collaborative Investing

Single-manager funds rest on the skill of one decision-maker or team. If that manager has a bad year—or hits a bad cycle—the fund suffers. Diversifying across managers should theoretically reduce that [idiosyncratic risk](/idiosyncratic-risk/). The 5Perspectives structure takes this further: it is not a simple blend of five separate portfolios (which would dilute active management), but rather a collaborative process in which positions must pass the scrutiny of all five perspectives. A company that only one manager likes does not make the cut; one that resonates with multiple managers gets a higher conviction weight. This forces consensus around the strongest ideas and surfaces weaknesses (governance red flags, deteriorating economics, [execution risk](/execution-risk/)) that a single lens might miss.

### What the Fund Actually Holds

The portfolio is typically 50-80 holdings, concentrated relative to the broader small-cap universe (which includes thousands of companies) but diversified relative to a single-manager small-cap fund. The companies are small- and mid-cap, meaning [market capitalizations](/market-capitalization/) roughly between $500 million and $10 billion, though the exact definitions shift with market conditions. These are firms large enough to have established products and revenues but small enough that a handful of good decisions (or bad ones) can meaningfully move the share price. The fund tilts toward growth—companies with expanding revenue and earnings—rather than cheap or distressed names, though not every holding must be a pure-play high-flyer; some mature companies with reliable growth get included if they meet the five perspectives' criteria.

### Sector and Economic Sensitivity

Because small- and mid-cap growth is economically sensitive, the fund's performance tends to lag large-cap in [recessions](/recession/) and outpace in strong expansions. Technology, healthcare, and consumer discretionary often make up meaningful portions of the portfolio, though the exact sector mix depends on what each of the five managers sees as attractive. In periods of falling [interest rates](/interest-rate/) and animal spirits, growth stocks (and small ones especially) can deliver spectacular returns; in rate-hiking cycles or recessions, they can fall sharply. An investor in PCSG should be comfortable with that volatility, viewing the fund as a core growth holding rather than a stabilizing anchor.

### The Risks of Concentration and Youth

Small companies carry idiosyncratic risks that large ones do not. A single bad quarter, a key-person departure, or competitive pressure from a larger rival can move a small-cap share price 20 percent or more. The fund's five-perspective approach helps, but does not eliminate, that risk. Additionally, small-cap markets are less liquid than large-cap, so the fund may pay wider [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) when trading, and large sales or purchases by the fund can move the underlying stock prices. The fund's prospectus lists the holdings, and examining the business fundamentals of the largest positions gives a sense of the fund's concentration and risk profile. Many small-cap names are in earlier stages of growth and have not proven themselves through a full [business cycle](/business-cycle/); past operational success offers no guarantee of future performance.

### Monitoring and Research

The fund fact sheet details the top holdings, the sector and market-cap breakdown, and the [historical volatility](/historical-volatility/) of returns. Compare PCSG's returns against small- and mid-cap growth benchmarks (such as the Russell 2500 Growth Index) to understand whether the five-perspective approach is adding value or detracting. Watch the fund's turnover (the percentage of the portfolio traded annually), which reveals how stable the manager's conviction is around core holdings. Review the biographies and track records of the five investment teams; this is a concentrated bet on their skill, and understanding their backgrounds and philosophy is essential. During market dislocations, monitor the fund's bid-ask spread and whether its share price drifts meaningfully from its [net asset value](/net-asset-value/)—both signs of liquidity stress. Over a full [market cycle](/market-cycle/), the goal is to understand whether the collaborative model is delivering better risk-adjusted returns than a single talented manager would, and whether the fund's small- and mid-cap exposure aligns with your overall portfolio's size and growth objectives.
