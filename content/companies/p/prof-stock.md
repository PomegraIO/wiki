Profound Medical is a medical-device company building a platform of MRI-guided, minimally invasive systems for thermal ablation — the destruction of diseased tissue using heat, guided and controlled by real-time imaging. The company's lead product, TULSA-PRO, targets prostate disease; its second platform, Sonalleve, addresses bone tumors, uterine fibroids, and gynecologic conditions. Both systems sit at the intersection of three broad trends in medicine: the shift toward less invasive procedures, the power of artificial intelligence to guide real-time clinical decisions, and the precision that MRI imaging enables.

## The competitive landscape in prostate disease

Prostate cancer and benign prostate hyperplasia (enlarged prostate) are among the most common male health conditions, particularly in aging populations. Traditional treatments range from active surveillance (watching and waiting) to radiation therapy, surgery, and hormone therapy. Each carries trade-offs. Surgery (radical prostatectomy) can cure localized cancer but often causes incontinence and erectile dysfunction. Radiation can preserve sexual and urinary function better than surgery but involves months of treatment and carries long-term risks of damage to neighboring tissues. Hormone therapy works systemically but carries metabolic side effects.

Newer minimally invasive approaches — focal ablation, which targets only the cancerous tissue rather than removing the whole gland — promise to reduce these side effects. Focal therapy with cryotherapy (freezing) and high-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) already exist, but both struggle with the same challenge: precision. Cryotherapy creates an ice ball that is visible on ultrasound but freezes tissue indiscriminately within that zone, risking collateral damage to the urinary sphincter and nerves critical to erectile function. HIFU, similarly, generates focal heat but without real-time imaging that covers the entire treatment zone, making it difficult to avoid critical structures.

TULSA-PRO enters this market by combining three capabilities: MRI guidance (which gives excellent soft-tissue contrast), real-time temperature monitoring during treatment, and AI-assisted mapping of the prostate anatomy to identify and protect the structures the procedure should spare. The procedure is performed through a single transurethral (through the urethra) access point, making it truly minimally invasive. A robotic arm, controlled by the physician, delivers focused ultrasound energy that raises tissue temperature to ablative levels — roughly 90 degrees Celsius — while the MRI and AI system confirm that critical zones (the urinary sphincter, the neurovascular bundles responsible for erectile function) remain cool.

## Clinical evidence and the path to adoption

Profound has published clinical data showing that roughly 80% of patients treated with TULSA-PRO remain free from additional treatment at five years. This is a meaningful figure: it speaks to durable efficacy without claiming cure (which would overstate the result for what is, functionally, an ablation of diseased tissue in a gland that can develop new disease over time). The procedure preserves continence and sexual function in a much higher proportion of patients than radical prostatectomy.

The company obtained FDA clearance for TULSA-PRO in 2021, opening the U.S. market. Medicare coverage followed, a critical validation — without it, adoption is slow because older men, the primary population at risk for prostate cancer, rely on Medicare. Reimbursement is now established, removing the largest adoption barrier. The company is now working through the gradual adoption cycle that medical devices follow: initial treatment in high-volume academic centers and well-resourced urology practices, gradual spread as more urologists gain training and confidence, and ongoing clinical publishing to build the evidence base and counter skepticism from surgeons and radiation oncologists with existing market share.

## How Profound competes

Profound's competitive advantage is technological: the combination of MRI guidance, AI-assisted anatomy mapping, and real-time thermal monitoring is unique. Competitors in focal therapy (Modus Instruments, Exact Imaging, others) offer HIFU systems without MRI guidance, or offer cryotherapy, both of which lack the precision TULSA-PRO provides.

But the competition is not only direct. TULSA-PRO also competes against the incumbents — surgery and radiation — which are entrenched, reimbursed, and performed by thousands of trained physicians. A patient choosing focal therapy must be convinced that preserving function justifies the smaller margin of treatment (focal therapy treats one lesion; surgery removes the whole gland and thus eliminates any risk of future cancer in that gland, though new cancer can develop elsewhere). This is a clinical and cultural battle as much as a technological one. Winning requires publishing long-term data, training urologists, and gradually shifting institutional practice toward focal therapy as a standard option.

## Sonalleve and the broader platform

Profound's second platform, Sonalleve, is an MRI-guided focused ultrasound system for thermal ablation of bone metastases (cancer that has spread to bone), osteoid osteoma (a benign bone tumor), desmoid tumors, and gynecologic conditions including uterine fibroids and adenomyosis. These are smaller markets than prostate disease, but all face similar clinical problems: current treatments (surgery, chemotherapy, radiation) carry significant side effects, and there is strong demand for less invasive alternatives.

Sonalleve is an established product (used in Europe and Canada for several indications) and is moving through the FDA approval process in the United States. Its competitive position is similar to TULSA-PRO — the MRI guidance and AI-assisted treatment planning offer precision that competing technologies (radiofrequency ablation, cryotherapy) do not. However, the gynecologic and bone tumor markets are smaller and more fragmented than prostate disease, meaning adoption will be slower and the revenue opportunity is more modest.

## The business model and profitability path

Profound is a commercial-stage company, not yet profitable at the company level, though TULSA-PRO procedures generate positive [contribution margins](/contribution-margin/). The company makes money from the sale and placement of systems (hardware), consumables (disposables used during each procedure), and service contracts. The business is capital-intensive: building, certifying, and placing MRI-guided ultrasound systems requires significant R&D and regulatory investment. But once systems are installed and the installed base grows, recurring revenue from consumables and service becomes a growing margin contributor.

The path to profitability depends on three things: continued steady adoption of TULSA-PRO, successful FDA approval and market launch of Sonalleve, and discipline on operating expenses during the growth phase. Profound competes for capital and investor attention against much larger medical-device companies and against other early-stage medtech firms with alternative technologies or markets. The competitive pressure is less about price (reimbursement is set by Medicare and insurers) and more about whether the company can execute on product development and market adoption fast enough to reach profitability before capital runs out or investor sentiment shifts.

## How to research Profound as an investment

Anyone studying Profound should begin with the company's investor relations site and its SEC filings (CIK 0001628808). The [annual report](/10-k/) (20-F, since the company is Canadian) details the clinical status of each product, regulatory milestones, the installed base of systems, and the revenue trajectory. Watch for announcements of new procedural data and any regulatory updates on Sonalleve; these are the events that move the stock.

Key metrics to follow: the number of TULSA-PRO systems installed and in use, the procedure volume per system (adoption speed), gross margins (consumables and service contracts drive these), and burn rate (how fast the company is spending cash). Compare TULSA-PRO's reimbursement rate and adoption trajectory to other recently launched focal therapy technologies and to the broader trend toward minimally invasive treatment. Understanding the competitive moat — whether the AI guidance and MRI integration are defensible through patents or whether competitors can replicate the platform — matters for long-term value. As always, medical-device companies carry regulatory risk; any adverse safety signal or failed clinical trial can derail a product. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or hold the stock; it is a map of the company, its technology, and where the returns will or will not come from.
