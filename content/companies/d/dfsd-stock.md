*The **Dimensional Short-Duration Fixed [Income ETF](/income-etf/)** (ticker DFSD) is an exchange-traded fund that holds [investment-grade bonds](/investment-grade-bond/) with shorter maturities and durations, selected through Dimensional's systematic, factor-based methodology. It targets investors who want [bond](/bond/) exposure but prefer lower sensitivity to interest-rate swings than a traditional intermediate or long-term bond fund.*

## The role of short duration in a portfolio

Duration is the measure of how much a bond's price moves when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) change. A bond with a duration of two years will lose about 2 per cent of its value if rates rise by one percentage point; a bond with a duration of seven years will lose roughly seven per cent. DFSD tilts toward bonds with shorter durations—typically in the one-to-five year range—so the fund itself has lower duration and therefore lower [interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/).

The trade-off is yield. Shorter-duration bonds offer lower [coupon rates](/coupon-rate/) than longer-dated ones; investors accept lower current income in exchange for price stability. For savers who need to keep money in a bond-like vehicle but cannot tolerate large price swings, or for portfolios already tilted long through equities, short-duration bonds serve as a ballast.

## How Dimensional selects the holdings

Rather than simply buying the most liquid short-duration bonds in a predetermined index, Dimensional applies quantitative screens to the universe of eligible securities. The process favours bonds that offer better relative value—higher yields for the duration taken—while maintaining broad [diversification](/diversification/) across sectors and issuers. The fund may hold government bonds, investment-grade [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/), and agency debt, as long as they fit the duration and credit-quality criteria.

This is not a passive index-tracking fund, but it is not market-timing or active security-picking in the traditional sense. It is systematic and disciplined, following rules that update as market conditions shift.

## Holdings and portfolio characteristics

DFSD's portfolio likely comprises hundreds of individual bonds or bond positions, keeping no single holding too large. This breadth is deliberate—it reduces [idiosyncratic risk](/idiosyncratic-risk/) and keeps the fund trading like a true fixed-income vehicle rather than a bet on a handful of credits. The weighted-average maturity and duration are disclosed in the fund's fact sheet and updated regularly; they typically sit well below those of a broad intermediate bond fund.

Because the underlying bonds are investment-grade, the fund avoids high-yield ([junk) bonds](/junk-bond/), which carry greater [credit risk](/credit-risk/) but also higher potential returns. The screening is all about selection within the investment-grade universe.

## What to watch

The key metric is the fund's yield relative to its duration. A fund that yields 3 per cent with a duration of 2.5 is offering different value than one yielding 2.5 per cent with a duration of 2.5; the first is more attractive. [Expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) matters too—a fund charging 40 basis points is meaningfully more expensive than one charging 15 basis points when yields are already modest.

Interest-rate expectations matter for any fixed-income fund. If rates are expected to rise, even short-duration bonds will decline, though less sharply than longer-dated ones. Conversely, if rates fall, short-duration bonds offer less upside capture than intermediate or long-term bonds. The fund is a tool for a specific job in a portfolio—ballast, stability, some current income—not a way to outrun interest-rate markets.

## Research and comparison

The fund's prospectus, fact sheet, and holdings list (updated daily on most fund sites) show the exact composition. Comparing DFSD to other short-duration ETFs—and there are several competitors—means looking at the expense ratio, the actual duration and yield, the average [credit rating](/credit-rating/) of the portfolio, and the volatility history. A fund with slightly higher yield but also slightly higher duration trade-off may not be better if it means taking unwanted interest-rate risk.

The SEC's EDGAR database and the fund sponsor's website carry all regulatory filings and performance data needed to evaluate this fund honestly.
