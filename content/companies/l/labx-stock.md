> **"Leverage bets on daily direction, not on hold time"** — a line that captures everything LABX is built to do, and everything it is not.

LABX is a 2X leveraged exchange-traded fund that aims to deliver twice the daily return of the LG AI Index, a benchmark focused on companies believed to be driving artificial intelligence adoption and development. Like all [leveraged ETFs](/leveraged-etf/), it resets its positioning at market close each day, which makes it a tool for traders and tactical rotations rather than a buy-and-hold investment. For anyone trading AI themes on a day or week basis, LABX offers the price action a single-day wager on the sector demands. For anyone treating it as a core holding, it is a wealth destruction machine.

## What it tracks, and the leverage behind it

The LG AI Index sits as the underlying — a custom benchmark that weights companies across artificial intelligence, semiconductors, cloud computing, and supporting infrastructure around the idea of who powers or deploys AI systems. LABX magnifies the daily return by a factor of two, meaning on a day when the index rises 1%, the fund targets a 2% gain; on a 1% down day, it targets a 2% loss.

The issuer, Tradr Innovations, maintains this leverage through a mix of index futures, options, and direct equity holdings, rebalancing at the close of each trading day to restore the 2X target ratio. This daily reset is the crux: it keeps the fund mechanically pure for a single day's bet, but it introduces a tax over longer horizons called volatility decay.

## The volatility-decay trap

Imagine an index that rises 10%, then falls 10%, returning to its starting point. A 2X leveraged daily-reset fund does not return to its starting point. On the up day it rises 20%; on the down day it falls 20% of the new, higher value. The result is a net loss, despite the index being flat. This decay accelerates in choppy or sideways markets.

Empirically, holding a 2X leveraged daily-reset fund for a year or more almost always underperforms simply owning the underlying index with 2X leverage applied once at entry. That gap can easily be 5–20 percentage points annually, depending on market choppiness. LABX is therefore a day-trader's or week-trader's instrument. Anyone holding it for months or years should expect to see the leverage work against them as often as for them.

## Costs and who trades it

LABX carries an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) that reflects the costs of daily rebalancing and the [index licensing](/index-licensing/) fees — typically 1.5% to 2% annually. [Bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) are generally tight during market hours, as the fund trades a meaningful daily volume. It is settled T+2, like any equity.

The fund appeals to:
- Tactical traders who believe the AI sector will move sharply in the next week or two and want magnified daily exposure.
- Rotation players betting that capital will flow into AI-heavy names ahead of a specific event or earnings cycle.
- Hedge portfolios or short-term tactical sleeves inside larger accounts, used as a convex bet rather than core.

It does not appeal to buy-and-hold investors, retirees, or anyone uncomfortable with the idea that their position could lose 40% in a single bad month even if the underlying index is only down 15%.

## Risk, decay, and use cases

LABX's real risks are concentration (the AI theme is itself concentrated), the leverage (which cuts both ways), and the mathematical drag of daily reset in sideways or choppy markets. Someone who shorted LABX at a market top and held for six months would almost certainly have made money even if the AI index rebounded, simply because volatility decay would have sapped the fund's long value.

The best use case for LABX is a money manager who constructs a tilt toward AI in the next 2–5 trading days and closes the position before the end-of-week, capturing the magnified daily swings without allowing decay to accumulate. Misuse is buying it as a long-term AI bet — that is precisely what the structure is engineered to sabotage.

## How to research LABX

The fund's prospectus (available through Tradr Innovations or the SEC) lays out the daily reset mechanics, the leverage limit, and the risk factors explicitly. Read the section on volatility decay; it is the conceptual heart of the instrument. The LG AI Index's composition and methodology is published by the [index provider](/index-provider/) and worth reviewing to see which stocks actually drive the fund's moves. Most importantly, check the fund's year-to-date and rolling 1-year performance against the underlying index: if the fund is significantly underwater, you are watching volatility decay at work in real time, a visceral reminder of why "leverage + buy and hold" is nearly always a losing formula.
