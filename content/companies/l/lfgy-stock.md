*The **YieldMax Crypto Industry & Tech Portfolio Option [Income ETF](/income-etf/)** (ticker LFGY) is an exchange-traded fund that holds a basket of publicly traded cryptocurrency and technology companies, then systematically sells [call options](/call-option/) against those holdings to generate monthly income for shareholders.*

This is a strategy called "buy-write" or "[covered call](/covered-call/)" — the fund owns the underlying companies (the buy part) and contracts away some of their upside potential (the write, or sale, of calls) in exchange for collecting [option premiums](/option-premium/). Those premiums flow to fund holders as distributions. The trade-off is deliberate: investors accept a ceiling on gains in exchange for steady income whether the market rallies or goes sideways.

## What the fund holds and what it tracks

LFGY targets companies in two overlapping categories. The first is crypto-exposed businesses — exchanges like Coinbase, custody providers like [Bit Digital](/btbt-stock/), and blockchain infrastructure firms. The second is the technology sector more broadly, reflecting the overlap between crypto operations and high-tech software and hardware companies. The fund is not a pure cryptocurrency play (it does not hold [Bitcoin](/bitcoin/) or [Ethereum](/ethereum/) directly) but rather equity in companies whose profits are tied to digital assets and blockchain adoption.

The selection is curated rather than mechanical — the fund managers choose which names to include rather than tracking a preset index. This gives them latitude to tilt toward companies they believe offer the best combination of value and volatility suitable for call selling, since higher volatility makes the premiums more attractive.

## The mechanics of income generation

Each month, the fund sells call options on the companies it owns. A call option is a contract that gives someone the right to buy a stock at a fixed price by a future date. The fund collects the option premium (the upfront cash paid by the [option buyer](/option-buyer/)) in exchange for this contract. If the stock stays below the [strike price](/strike-price/) by the [expiration date](/expiration-date/), the option expires worthless, the fund keeps the premium, and the call can be sold again next month. If the stock rises above the strike price, the option is exercised, the fund's shares are called away, and the premium turns out to have been quite cheap compensation for giving up the gain.

This monthly cycle is what generates the high yield — LFGY typically distributes several percentage points per year in option premiums. For comparison, most equity funds distribute little or nothing; most [bond](/bond/) funds yield a few percent. LFGY's yield comes from deliberately capping upside, so it is sustainable only as long as the fund's holdings do not rally too sharply.

## Who this is for and the real risks

The fund appeals to income-focused investors who own long-term tech and crypto exposure and want to enhance returns with steady distributions, or to traders who like the crypto / tech sector but are neutral to slightly bearish on near-term moves and are happy to sell strength.

The primary risk is opportunity cost: if LFGY's holdings soar, holders will have given up much of that gain to the option buyers in exchange for premiums that looked reasonable at the time. The volatility that makes options valuable also means the fund's share price swings — if the tech sector tanks, LFGY falls in step, then the high yield is scant consolation. There is also [reinvestment risk](/reinvestment-risk/): if [interest rates](/interest-rate/) fall and volatility shrinks, the new call premiums the fund collects will be lower, and distributions will decline. Finally, concentrated positions in crypto-linked equities carry the sector [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) inherent to any bet on a nascent asset class.

Unlike a leveraged or [inverse ETF](/inverse-etf/), there is no daily reset decay or structural degradation — LFGY is a straightforward single-month call-selling strategy repeated indefinitely. But the strategy itself demands active management and rebalancing, so the fund carries an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) higher than a passive index tracker.

## How to research it

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, available on the issuer's website. These will detail the exact portfolio holdings, the strike prices at which calls are being sold, the frequency and timing of monthly distributions, and the expense ratio. The historical distribution rate gives clues to the sustainability of yield under various market conditions — look back several years to see whether distributions held steady in a downturn or dropped sharply.

For the broader context, review the composition of crypto and tech stocks more widely, and understand the volatility of both sectors. Covered call ETFs work best for investors who are indifferent or slightly cautious and happy to harvest income at the expense of upside. If conviction is bullish, a plain buy-and-hold position in the underlying companies will outperform in a rally.
