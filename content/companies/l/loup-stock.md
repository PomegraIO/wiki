*The **Innovator Deepwater Frontier Tech ETF** (LOUP) is a concentrated portfolio of smaller and emerging technology companies built on deep proprietary research into innovation trajectories. Rather than track an index, the fund manager applies analytical screens across software, semiconductors, biotech tools, and emerging IT infrastructure to construct a portfolio of companies believed to be solving frontier problems.*

## The research lens

The fund's approach is not index-following but conviction-based. Innovator, the sponsor, and the Deepwater investment team conduct original research into where technological change is creating durable competitive advantages. The holdings typically span software infrastructure, including database and data-analytics companies; semiconductor design and fabrication tools; biotech and drug-discovery platforms; cybersecurity; and emerging areas in telecommunications or artificial intelligence. The portfolio is small — often 25 to 40 distinct holdings — which concentrates both the research conviction and the volatility. A single holding can represent 3–5% of the fund, far more than a passive index would allow.

## What lives in the portfolio

Securities in the fund tend to be smaller firms that are past the venture-capital stage but still years away from the mega-cap status of established tech giants. Many are profitable; some are still unprofitable but generating strong revenue growth. The fund does not restrict itself to U.S. companies, so international pure-plays in software infrastructure or semiconductors appear alongside American firms. Because the portfolio is rebalanced based on the manager's conviction about the strength of each company's moat and addressable market, the holdings change over time as conviction shifts or as companies mature.

## Costs and volatility

The fund carries an active [management fee](/management-fee/) reflecting the ongoing research and [rebalancing discipline](/rebalancing-discipline/) required. Because the underlying holdings are smaller-cap and often less liquid than blue-chip technology stocks, trading the fund during volatile market periods may incur wider [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/). The concentrated portfolio amplifies both upside and downside — in years when innovation stocks surge, LOUP can outpace broader technology indices considerably; in downturns, the lack of [diversification](/diversification/) across market segments can mean sharper losses. The volatility is intentional: it reflects the higher risk of smaller, less-established businesses, offset by the prospect of outsized gains if the research thesis proves right.

## Why concentration over breadth

The fund's philosophy is that the most insight comes from deep knowledge of a narrow domain. Rather than hold 500 technology companies to maximize diversification, it holds 25 to 40 to ensure the portfolio reflects genuine high-conviction bets. This mirrors the approach of venture capital or [hedge funds](/hedge-fund/) focused on a single theme. The tradeoff is that [idiosyncratic risks](/idiosyncratic-risk/) — company-specific failures, execution stumbles, or lost market share to competitors — are not diluted across a large portfolio. A single firm's bankruptcy or stalled product cycle affects the fund's returns more directly.

## Tracking and research implications

The fund does not track a published index, so there is no "benchmark" in the traditional sense against which its performance is measured. Instead, investors are trusting the Deepwater research team's ability to identify frontier technology companies before the broader market recognizes their value. That judgment call is the fund's central appeal and its central risk. How to evaluate it: study the fund's recent holdings, look up each company's latest financial reports and product announcements, and ask whether the research thesis for each seems plausible. The fund's prospectus outlines the investment strategy, and fact sheets typically list the top ten holdings and sector allocations, which gives a flavor of where conviction currently sits.

## The sector concentration question

Because the portfolio is tilted toward software, semiconductors, and biotech tools rather than distributed evenly across technology, the fund is sensitive to technology-sector downturns and to shifts in corporate spending on IT and software tooling. A broad [recession](/recession/) that reduces corporate capital expenditure can pressure the entire portfolio simultaneously. Conversely, in periods of heavy technology spending or when small-cap innovation stocks outperform large-cap megacaps, the fund can deliver outsized returns.

The fund trades under the ticker LOUP and settles like any other ETF. Its price reflects the collective value of its holdings and the market's perception of the Deepwater team's stock-picking ability. As an actively managed vehicle, there is an ongoing assessment embedded in its trading: do you believe the research justifies the fee and the [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/)?
