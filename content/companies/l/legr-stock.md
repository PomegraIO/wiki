## What does LEGR actually hold?

LEGR holds companies that develop, deploy, or benefit from technologies that change how financial transactions occur. That includes financial software providers, companies building blockchain infrastructure, payment processors working on new rails, and firms that are reinventing settlement, custody, or trading. The fund includes large software companies with fintech exposure alongside smaller pure-play fintech firms, global companies, and some startups acquired by larger players. Most holdings are in developed markets, though emerging-market fintech companies are included where the index captures them.

## Why is the theme called "innovative transaction and process"?

The name reflects two streams. Transaction innovation means reimagining how money moves—faster settlement, cheaper cross-border payments, decentralized exchanges. Process innovation means changing how financial institutions operate internally—automation, better fraud detection, regulatory compliance through software. Both themes power companies in the holdings. It is a reasonably broad mandate, which means the fund covers multiple theses rather than betting heavily on one technology.

## How is the underlying index constructed?

LEGR follows the Indxx Innovative Transaction & Process Index, which is rules-based rather than discretionary. Indxx, an index provider, defines criteria for which companies qualify: they must earn revenue from fintech-related business lines or must be leveraged to fintech adoption. Companies are selected and weighted according to a methodology that aims to be transparent and reproducible. The index reconstitutes periodically, removing companies that no longer fit the theme and adding new ones as fintech evolves.

## Is LEGR actively managed or passive?

LEGR is passive in the sense that it tracks an index rather than having a stock-picker make buy-and-sell decisions. But the index itself represents a thematic choice—the decision to isolate fintech companies rather than holding the entire market. In that sense, it is more concentrated than a broad market index and more exposed to a specific trend. The expense ratio is lower than active funds but higher than broad market index ETFs, reflecting the specialized research required to maintain a fintech-focused index.

## What are the main risks?

Fintech is genuinely transformative, but the pace and direction of change are uncertain. Some holdings may be disrupted by technology shifts or competition. Smaller fintech companies are volatile; larger diversified companies with fintech exposure may not capture as much fintech upside. Regulatory crackdowns on cryptocurrency, payments, or lending can hurt multiple holdings at once. Valuation risk is real too—technology stocks often trade at high multiples, and a down shift in growth expectations can cause painful drawdowns. Geographic concentration in developed markets, while lowering volatility, means the fund misses fintech growth in Asia.

## Is this a Bitcoin or blockchain bet?

Not directly. LEGR does not hold Bitcoin or other cryptocurrencies. It holds companies that work with or on blockchain technology—software makers, miners, exchanges, custody providers. Some holdings have deep crypto exposure; others touch blockchain tangentially. If cryptocurrency remains niche or faces severe regulation, many LEGR holdings could suffer. If crypto adoption accelerates, many will prosper. The fund is leveraged to fintech more broadly, but crypto is a material theme within it.

## Who is LEGR for?

Investors who believe financial technology will outperform the broader market and want diversified exposure to that trend. Also useful for allocators building a thematic sleeve around fintech innovation. It is less suitable for conservative investors, those uncomfortable with technology volatility, or those who view fintech skeptically. Long-term investors should view fintech as a multi-year structural theme, not a trade.

## How to research LEGR?

Start with the Indxx methodology document and the prospectus, which detail how the index is constructed and which holdings qualify. Look at the portfolio composition by holding size, sector, and geography to understand concentration. Review the fund's performance relative to the broad technology sector and the broader market—this tells you whether fintech themes are driving returns. Compare the expense ratio to other thematic fintech funds and to broad technology ETFs. Monitor regulatory developments around cryptocurrency, payments regulation, and international fintech rules, as these move the entire sector simultaneously.
