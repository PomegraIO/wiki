*The **Liberty One Tactical Income ETF** (LOTI) is an actively managed fund designed to generate steady current income through a mixture of dividend-paying stocks, preferred shares, bonds, and options-based income strategies. Rather than tracking a fixed index, a portfolio manager actively selects and rebalances holdings to maximize monthly distributions while managing downside risk — a tactically flexible approach built for investors prioritizing cash flow over long-term capital appreciation.*

## The equity income core: dividend stocks

LOTI builds its income foundation on dividend-paying stocks — mature companies known for steady cash distributions to shareholders and, often, histories of raising dividends year after year. These tend to be large-cap or blue-chip companies in sectors where cash generation and shareholder returns are strategic priorities: utilities, consumer staples, financials, telecommunications, and real estate. These companies typically grow slowly but reliably and dedicate substantial earnings to distribution payments, making them the cornerstone of an income-focused portfolio.

A portfolio manager selects companies based on dividend history, sustainability of the payout, and the underlying business fundamentals. Unlike a passive index that would hold every dividend-paying company regardless of quality, LOTI's manager exercises judgment to avoid holding companies at risk of cutting their dividend. That active screening reduces the risk that a major holding becomes a capital loss.

## Preferred shares: higher yield with senior status

LOTI also holds preferred stock — a hybrid security that ranks above common equity but below debt in a company's capital structure. Preferred shares typically offer fixed dividend rates substantially higher than the common stock of the same company, often in the 5–8% range. In the event of bankruptcy, preferred holders are paid before common shareholders but after creditors. For an income fund, preferred stock offers more yield than most corporate bonds without accepting the full equity risk of owning common shares.

The tradeoff is complexity: preferred shares are less liquid than common stock, their values are sensitive to interest-rate movements, and there is credit risk if the issuing company faces distress. But for investors willing to accept those characteristics, preferreds can deliver meaningful income that common stocks alone would not generate.

## Options-based income: covered calls

LOTI uses covered-call strategies to extract additional monthly income from its stock holdings. The mechanics are straightforward: the fund writes (sells) call options against shares it already owns, collecting an option premium upfront in exchange for capping the upside if the stock rallies above the strike price. If the stock stays flat or falls, the fund keeps the shares and the premium, then sells new calls the following month. If the stock rises above the strike, it is called away and the position closes.

Covered-call writing reduces upside capture — if a holding rallies sharply, the position is capped and the fund forfeits that additional gain. But it generates consistent monthly premiums that otherwise would not materialize, and many income-focused investors accept the capped upside as a reasonable tradeoff for higher current cash flow. The strategy works best in flat to mildly bullish markets; in bull markets, the opportunity cost of missing upside becomes material.

## The fixed income sleeve

LOTI typically allocates a meaningful portion to bonds — government securities, investment-grade corporate debt, and in some cases higher-yielding instruments like bank loans or structured products. The exact allocation varies tactically based on the portfolio manager's assessment of bond valuations, credit spreads, economic conditions, and the prevailing interest-rate environment.

When bond yields are high and rising, a larger fixed-income allocation offers compelling income without unreasonable risk. When bond yields are very low and credit spreads are tight, the manager may reduce bonds and increase the equity portion instead. This tactical flexibility is the fund's key advantage over a passive index that holds a fixed allocation regardless of conditions.

## Monthly distributions and yield mechanics

LOTI pays distributions monthly — a powerful draw for retirees and income-focused investors who want predictable cash flow. That monthly payout combines dividends and interest collected from the portfolio, option premiums captured, and occasionally capital gains if positions are rebalanced at a profit. The exact distribution amount varies each month based on what the underlying holdings generated and the fund's ongoing expenses.

The current yield — the annualized monthly distribution divided by the fund's share price — is sensitive to the fund's trading premium or discount to its net asset value. When LOTI trades above its NAV, the current yield appears artificially low; when it trades at a discount, the yield is artificially inflated. Astute investors compare the fund's actual trailing distributions to its NAV, not just the quoted current yield.

## Risks and the income tradeoff

LOTI's higher current yield comes with material tradeoffs. Covered calls reduce upside capture during strong equity rallies. The focus on dividend-paying stocks and fixed income means the portfolio naturally gravitates toward lower-growth, mature companies; capital appreciation is not the goal. And the allocation to bonds and preferred shares introduces credit risk — if economic conditions deteriorate, these securities can decline sharply in value.

Options strategies introduce timing risk as well: a stock could gap sharply lower, creating losses that the call premium does not offset. The monthly distribution mechanism can also mislead: a fund with a 7% current yield may be returning a mix of income and capital, gradually eroding the principal if the underlying investments are not actually generating sustainable income. Investors should scrutinize whether LOTI's distribution is truly supported by dividend and interest income or is partially eating into NAV.

## Research and suitability

LOTI appeals to retirees, near-retirees, and income-focused investors who want monthly cash flow without the work of managing individual dividend stocks and bonds, and who are comfortable with active management. It is not suitable for growth-oriented investors or those with long time horizons who can afford to wait for capital appreciation.

To evaluate LOTI, begin with the fund's fact sheet and prospectus, which disclose the manager's philosophy and current holdings. Review the actual distributions paid over the last year to understand the true income being generated. Compare LOTI's current yield to competing income ETFs — broad dividend funds, preferred-stock funds, or bond funds — to assess whether the blended approach offers better risk-adjusted income. Watch the fund's premium or discount to NAV; a widening discount signals investor skepticism and may warrant investigation. Finally, examine the largest holdings to understand the dividend stability, credit quality, and sector exposure driving the fund's performance.
