The LifeX 2035 Income Bucket ETF (ticker: LDDR) is built around a specific idea: that an investor approaching or in retirement may prefer to structure their portfolio as a series of time-based "buckets" rather than as a unified, balanced whole. The fund holds securities timed to deliver cash around 2035 — a year chosen as a moment when the fund's purchaser expects to need stable income.

## The bucket concept in practice

The fund's strategy reflects a decades-old investment idea: instead of trying to time the market or optimize [asset allocation](/asset-allocation/) in a conventional sense, divide the portfolio by when you need the money. If you anticipate needing cash flow for the years 2025 to 2030, hold [bonds](/bond/) and high-dividend stocks maturing or paying out during that window. If you have a longer horizon within the retirement period, hold longer-maturity bonds and growth investments that target a later date. LDDR is specifically focused on the 2035 cohort — designed to deliver income and principal preservation for a 10-to-15-year time window centered on that year.

The fund holds a portfolio of bonds, bond-like securities, and dividend-paying equities selected to generate income and reduce the need to sell principal to fund spending. The exact mix can vary, but typically includes investment-grade [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/), government bonds, and [dividend](/dividend/) aristocrats or other reliable dividend-payers. The portfolio is less focused on growth than a traditional equity fund and less focused on [yield pickup](/yield-pickup/) than a [high-yield bond fund](/high-yield-bond-fund/). Instead, it aims for a stable, inflation-adjusted income stream over its horizon.

## Structure and maturity

LDDR is not a traditional fund that holds positions indefinitely. Like other "bucket" or "interval" strategies, it is designed to mature and be managed toward a specific date. As 2035 approaches, the fund's managers shift the portfolio toward shorter-maturity securities and higher cash allocations, reducing volatility and principal risk as the endpoint draws near. For an investor retiring in 2035, this becomes a simple bridge: the fund provides the income needed and gradually de-risks as the target year arrives.

The fund trades as an ETF, meaning daily liquidity and transparent pricing. Distributions arrive regularly as the underlying holdings pay dividends and interest, or as the managers harvest gains to distribute to shareholders. The fund's prospectus outlines the anticipated path of distribution amounts (which may decline in some years and rise in others) and the expected final maturity or restructuring around 2035.

## Risk and suitability

The fund's primary risks are [interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/) (if rates rise sharply, bond prices fall), [credit risk](/credit-risk/) (if issuers of the held bonds face distress), and opportunity cost (if a different allocation would have performed better). Because LDDR is laddered toward 2035 rather than optimized for maximum return, it sacrifices upside in favor of predictability. In a long [bull market](/bull-market/), a traditional diversified portfolio might outpace it. In a sharp downturn, LDDR's income focus may cushion the blow.

The fund is not suitable for investors who need the portfolio to grow substantially in real terms. It is not a vehicle for someone who retires and then needs the portfolio to fund 30 more years. It is built for a defined, relatively near-term horizon — someone who will retire around 2035 and needs a portfolio structured to deliver income then.

## A shifting landscape

LDDR is part of a growing category of interval and bucket-focused ETFs, reflecting the reality that many investors think about retirement in terms of years rather than absolute market returns. As the cohort it targets (those retiring around 2035) moves into that life stage, the fund's composition and distribution will be managed accordingly. Investors using the fund need to think in terms of their own retirement timeline: is 2035 truly the right target, or would an earlier or later vintage fund better suit their needs?

A reader investigating LDDR should begin with its prospectus and fact sheet, which specify the exact holdings, maturity ladder, and expected distribution schedule. The fund's performance relative to both a balanced portfolio and to other income-focused or bucket-strategy funds reveals whether the approach is delivering value. Monitoring the distribution yield and total return separately is important: a fund paying strong current income but losing principal value may appear attractive until the full return is measured. For those near or considering a 2035 retirement, looking at how the fund is actually positioned — the credit quality of its bonds, the dividend-paying power of its equities, the maturity distribution — tells whether it is likely to deliver the stability and income promised.
