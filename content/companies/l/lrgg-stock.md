*The **Nomura Focused Large [Growth ETF](/growth-etf/)** (LRGG) is an exchange-traded fund that tracks a concentrated portfolio of large US growth companies selected on the basis of earnings growth and price momentum.*

This is not a company — it is a [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/) that exists to hold shares of other companies. ETFs are bundles of stocks wrapped into a single ticker that trade on an exchange like any ordinary stock. LRGG is run by Nomura, a large Japanese financial services firm, and holds roughly 50 stocks from the largest and most profitable US companies. The fund's core job is simple: buy large companies that are growing their earnings and showing rising share prices, and deliver those holdings to investors at a low cost.

## What the fund holds

LRGG is a concentrated play on US large-cap growth. Unlike a broad [index fund](/index-fund/) that might hold 500 stocks or more, LRGG hand-picks around 50 of them. The selection methodology leans on two signals: which large companies are growing their earnings at the fastest rate, and which of those are showing upward price momentum (shares trending up rather than down). The idea is that these two signals, in combination, identify companies that are not just performing well but that the market itself is beginning to favour.

Because it is concentrated, any single holding represents a meaningful portion of the fund. A typical broad [growth fund](/growth-fund/) spreads its capital equally across hundreds of stocks; LRGG piles more of your money into the ideas it likes best. This is a deliberate choice that tilts the return profile: in years when the largest growth companies soar, concentration amplifies the gain. When they stumble, concentration amplifies the loss.

The fund's holdings overlap substantially with the largest US technology companies and fast-growing industrial and financial firms. Because the portfolio is concentrated and weighted toward the most prominent names, LRGG is not fundamentally different in character from simply owning a handful of large-cap growth stocks oneself — the difference is the fractional share and the ease of trading.

## Costs and how it trades

LRGG trades on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) under its ticker. Because it is an ETF, not a mutual fund, you buy and sell shares whenever the market is open, at prices set by buyers and sellers in real time — not at a single price set once a day like a traditional mutual fund. This liquidity comes with typical ETF mechanics: a [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) (the difference between the price you pay and the price you receive if you sell immediately) and the possibility of a small premium or discount to the fund's underlying holdings.

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) — the annual fee Nomura charges to manage the fund — is modest, measured in basis points (hundredths of a percent). This is typical for large-cap [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/), particularly concentrated ones that do not require expensive active stock research.

## The case for concentration

Concentration has a clear appeal: if you believe you can identify the fastest-growing, most-promising large companies, holding only your favourites should beat an index of 500. The momentum overlay adds another layer: the theory is that strong recent price performance is a signal of fundamental strength that will persist.

The risk, conversely, is equally clear. Concentration is a bet on the specific stocks you hold. If your 50-stock portfolio contains 15 major technology companies and the entire technology sector stumbles, the damage is asymmetric. A broad index fund would feel that stumble too, but spread across 500 stocks; LRGG feels it concentrated in a smaller set. The fund has no cushion from companies doing well in sectors it ignores.

## Momentum as a timing signal

The incorporation of price momentum deserves scrutiny. Momentum has a long history in academic research — a tendency for shares that have risen to keep rising, and shares that have fallen to keep falling. But momentum is explicitly a short-term signal; it captures what the market is doing right now, not what it will do next. A share that has risen for the past year may be priced to perfection, meaning the next year brings reversion. LRGG's methodology bets this will not happen — that momentum is self-perpetuating or that the momentum signal genuinely predicts future earnings growth. Whether that bet pays off depends heavily on market conditions and the precise moment you own the fund.

## How to evaluate LRGG

Anyone considering this fund should ask a few straightforward questions. First: What is the overlap between LRGG and my other holdings? If you own a broad growth fund or an index fund already, LRGG will concentrate you further in large technology and growth stocks you may already own in abundance. Doubling up is not inherently wrong, but it is worth seeing clearly.

Second: How has the momentum signal performed over actual [market cycles](/market-cycle/)? LRGG is relatively young, so historical returns are limited. Look at the fund's prospectus or fact sheet for performance relative to a broad growth index during upturns and downturns. In [bull markets](/bull-market/), concentrated growth tends to shine; in corrections or sideways markets, it often lags.

Third: Do you believe in the earnings growth and price momentum signals, or do you prefer to own everything? If your conviction is in the broad strength of the US economy and its largest companies, a cheaper index fund that owns all 500 of them might suit you better than picking 50.

LRGG is neither a bargain nor a rip-off — it is a direct bet on the power of concentrated, growth-flavoured, momentum-tilted investing. Whether that bet is right depends entirely on your own investment thesis and risk tolerance.
