*An exchange-traded fund focused on the largest US publicly listed companies—selected through careful scrutiny of financial quality and valuation—managed by the Touchstone investment team.*

## Portfolio construction

LCF holds typically 30 to 50 large-cap US stocks. Smaller set than a typical [S&P 500 index](/sp-500-index/) fund, larger than a hyper-concentrated value play. The selection process emphasizes companies with predictable earnings, reasonable leverage, and solid competitive positioning. Names spanning technology, healthcare, finance, industrials. No hard sector limits, but the managers avoid high-debt, cyclical situations and balance-sheet-light businesses where the moat is unclear.

Valuation discipline is built in. The fund's mandate specifies a weighting methodology: no single position dominates, cap weighting with meaningful floors on smaller positions. This prevents the fund from becoming a de facto megacap bet—[Apple](/aapl-stock/), [Microsoft](/msft-stock/), and [Nvidia](/nvda-stock/) get fair weight but not 20 percent of the portfolio in aggregate.

## Trading and mechanics

The fund trades daily on the relevant US exchange. [Bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) are tight given that LCF is held largely by institutional and high-net-worth accounts. Creation and redemption work via the standard ETF mechanism: [authorized participants](/authorized-participant/) bring in stock baskets and redeem fund shares, or vice versa, which keeps the fund's price aligned with its [net asset value](/net-asset-value/). Touchstone publishes holdings daily and an intraday NAV every 15 seconds during market hours.

Rebalancing happens quarterly. When positions drift from their target weights due to market movement, the managers trim winners and add to losers, which generates some turnover and trading costs. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is modest relative to the active research involved, and Touchstone publishes a prospectus that clearly lays out how holdings are selected and weighted.

## Real considerations

Ownership is suitable for investors who want large-cap exposure but think the index is overweighted to the absolute largest mega-caps and underweights reasonable-sized companies with solid franchises. The focus on quality-at-reasonable-price appealed to value-conscious allocators, especially in periods when pure large-cap growth is expensive. Risks include the obvious: the managers' stock picks could underperform, or the concentrated portfolio could lag when the market rotates toward different styles or sectors. If growth-stage technology is where returns come from, a large-cap focused fund will lag. If the managers are proved wrong on a major thesis, drawdown can be sharp.

Liquidity depends on position size—core holdings are large, highly liquid names, but less frequently traded positions may see wider spreads. The fund is appropriate for taxable accounts in part because portfolio turnover is modest and the fund distributes capital gains rarely.

## How to build your view

Check the fund's website for current holdings and the prospectus for the detailed selection criteria. Review trailing 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year returns net of fees versus the Russell 1000 or S&P 500. The quarterly reports offer manager commentary on big market moves and portfolio positioning. If you want to audit the valuation discipline, download the current holdings and compare them to the index constituent valuations—you'll see which ideas are favored and which avoided. Track Touchstone's other funds and research team reputation; a strong track record elsewhere is a useful signal.
