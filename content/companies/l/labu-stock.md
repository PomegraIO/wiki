LABU is a bet on biotech that trades with three times the sector's volatility. Hold the S&P Biotech Index for a day where it rises two percent; LABU rises roughly six percent. A down day in biotech of two percent becomes a six-percent loss in LABU. The leverage amplifies both directions, which is why the fund markets itself exclusively to tactical traders and explicitly warns that it is not suitable for long-term investors.

The fund achieves this leverage through financial derivatives rather than by borrowing cash. It uses [futures contracts](/futures-contract/) and swap agreements to establish positions that move three times the underlying index. These derivatives are liquid and widely traded, making the mechanism relatively clean and transparent. The cost is in the fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/), which is higher than an ordinary stock fund to cover the complexity of derivatives management.

The silent killer in LABU is volatility decay. This is not a cost or a fee but a consequence of mathematics. The fund rebalances every single day to maintain its 3X multiple. On a day where the index rises and then falls back to where it started, the fund has compounded losses. Consider: a 20 percent downside followed by a 25 percent upside gets you back to roughly even on the index. LABU loses roughly 60 percent on the down move and gains 75 percent on the up move—but the 75 percent is applied to a 40 percent smaller base, resulting in a net loss even though the index is flat. This mathematical drag is relentless in sideways or volatile markets.

The empirical reality is grim for long-term holders. The biotech sector is one of the most volatile in equities—regulatory announcements, FDA approvals and rejections, clinical trial results, and interest-rate sensitivity all drive sharp daily and weekly swings. That volatility is what decay preys on. A biotech investor holding LABU for a year, even if the index were flat, could watch the fund decay substantially. Add in the realistic scenario of a volatile but flat index, and the decay can easily exceed ten to twenty percent annualized, entirely independent of whether you made the right sector call.

The fund is designed and explicitly marketed for a specific narrow use: traders with a days-to-weeks time horizon making a tactical bet on the biotech sector's near-term direction. A trader who believes biotech will move sharply higher over the next two weeks and wants to express that with leverage might buy LABU, execute the trade within that window, and sell. The leverage amplifies a correct short-term call. A trader wrong on the near-term direction also gets amplified losses, but the time window is short enough that decay has not had time to compound into a catastrophic drag.

This is not a fund for buy-and-hold investors. The prospectus says so explicitly. The expense ratio is higher. The daily mathematical drag is relentless. Holding it through a normal volatile year will degrade the position, even if the sector ends the year roughly where it started. Holding it for years is essentially value destruction relative to owning the index directly.

The biotech sector itself is compelling: it is a frontier of human medicine, with innovations that can transform health outcomes. But owning that sector should be done through careful company research, low-cost [index funds](/index-fund/), or tactical plays confined to time horizons measured in days or weeks—not through a 3X leveraged vehicle held for years. The leverage is a tool, not a free lunch.

Buyers of LABU should have a clear thesis (biotech will rise substantially in the near term), a clear time horizon (days, weeks at most), and a clear exit plan (sell when the thesis plays out or when a time limit is reached). Without those discipline structures, LABU becomes a way to turn tactical uncertainty into accelerated losses. The fund's prospectus includes warnings and educational materials about decay; reading those before committing capital is not optional. The fund sponsor also provides tools and calculators on its website showing the impact of volatility and [holding periods](/holding-period/) on decay—these are worth running through with realistic assumptions before trading.

LABU moves fast, has liquid markets, and is transparent in its mechanics. It is a sophisticated tool for a narrow, well-defined use case. Outside that use case, it is expensive and mathematically punishing.
