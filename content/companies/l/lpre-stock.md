*The **Long Pond Real Estate Select ETF** (LPRE) is a concentrated [real estate fund](/real-estate-fund/) that holds REITs ([Real Estate Investment Trusts](/real-estate-investment-trust/)) and real estate operating companies, built on the conviction that disciplined exposure to property assets can deliver compelling returns. The fund brings institutional real estate expertise into an ETF wrapper, combining liquidity with active stock selection in the real estate space.*

> Real estate is not a sector — it is an alternate financial system that sits alongside equities and bonds, anchored in physical assets, leverage, and cash flows that have little correlation with traditional stock performance.

That statement animates the fund's construction. Rather than treat real estate simply as a stock-market sector alongside healthcare or energy, LPRE positions real estate as a distinct asset class that warrants its own dedicated capital allocation. This framing has implications for how the fund selects holdings and manages concentration.

## What the fund holds

The portfolio typically includes 30–50 individual positions, concentrated in [residential real estate](/residential-real-estate/) companies (apartment operators, [single-family rental](/single-family-rental/) firms), commercial property managers (office, retail, industrial), specialized real estate (data centers, self-storage, cell towers), healthcare facilities REITs, and sometimes [mortgage REITs](/mortgage-reit/) (which lend against real property rather than owning it). The allocation shifts based on the fund manager's assessment of which property types offer the best risk-return tradeoff at any given moment. In periods when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) are rising, the fund may reduce exposure to mortgage REITs and emphasize operational property companies. When [vacancy rates](/vacancy-rate-property/) are climbing in office, it may lighten office holdings and shift toward industrial or data-center assets.

This active management within the real estate universe is the fund's key distinction from a passive real estate index ETF. Rather than mechanically holding every real estate security in a cap-weighted index, Long Pond applies research into individual company fundamentals, property quality, and capital-allocation discipline.

## Why leverage and debt matter for real estate

Real estate companies are defined by leverage. A typical apartment REIT might finance 50–60% of its portfolio value with debt, using mortgages against the underlying buildings. This amplifies both returns and risks. When rents rise and property values climb, leverage makes the equity returns spectacular. When rents stagnate or property values fall, the debt obligations become onerous relative to cash flow. The fund's selection process evaluates each holding's debt level, debt maturity, [refinancing risk](/refinancing-risk/), and the stability of underlying cash flows. A company with creeping vacancy rates and maturing debt faces refinancing risk that could be invisible to an investor who looks only at the [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/).

## The dividend structure and tax implications

Real estate companies, particularly REITs, are required by law to distribute at least 90% of their taxable income to shareholders as [dividends](/dividend/), making them high-yielding relative to broader equities. LPRE's portfolio therefore generates substantial quarterly and annual dividend income. This is an advantage for investors seeking cash flow, but it comes with a tax penalty: REIT dividends are taxed as ordinary income rather than at capital-gains rates, making them less tax-efficient in taxable accounts than dividend-paying stocks or stock-focused ETFs. For tax-advantaged accounts like 401ks or IRAs, this is not a concern. For taxable accounts, it is a material cost.

## Risks specific to the real estate sector and this fund

Real estate returns are highly sensitive to interest rates. When [central banks](/central-bank/) raise rates to combat [inflation](/inflation/), borrowing costs climb, property valuations compress (because future cash flows are discounted at a higher rate), and existing debt becomes more expensive to refinance. The sector can suffer sharply in rising-rate environments, as happened in 2022–2023. Conversely, when rates fall, real estate rebounds. The fund's concentrated portfolio (30–50 holdings) means that a handful of poor company decisions or unexpected operational failures — a major tenant bankruptcy, catastrophic property damage, regulatory changes affecting a particular property type — can materially dent returns. A broad-based real estate index would dilute those shocks across thousands of holdings.

Secondly, real estate markets are regional. A concentrated fund that overweights certain geographies or property types becomes sensitive to local economic conditions, zoning changes, and migration patterns. The pandemic's accelerated shift to remote work crushed some office-heavy REITs while benefiting logistics and data-center firms. The fund's selection process should account for these trends, but concentrated bets on the wrong property types or regions can backfire.

## How to research this fund

Start with the fund's fact sheet, which lists the top ten holdings and the sector breakdown — how much of the portfolio is in apartments, industrial, office, data centers, and so on. That breakdown tells you what real estate bets the manager is making. Look at the trailing dividend yield and compare it to a real estate index ETF, understanding that LPRE's active management either adds or subtracts value relative to that passive baseline. Examine the portfolio's aggregate [loan-to-value ratio](/loan-to-value-ratio/) and interest-coverage metrics — these are real estate–specific measures of financial health. A portfolio of levered companies with tight interest coverage is vulnerable to a rate shock. Review the [fund prospectus](/fund-prospectus/) for the manager's investment criteria and risk factors specific to the holdings. Finally, ask yourself whether you have a meaningful edge in understanding which property types and geographies will outperform — if not, a passive real estate index might serve you better at a lower fee.

LPRE trades on the exchange during regular hours and can be held in any account. Its price reflects the collective value of its holdings and the property market's health, making it a direct bet on real estate fundamentals rather than a diversified equity play.
