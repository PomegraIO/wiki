*"The best investment opportunities often sit in places everyone else is looking away from."*

The **LOGIQ Contrarian Opportunities ETF** (ticker LCO) is built on this premise. Managed by the LOGIQ investment team, the fund constructs a portfolio of US equities that have fallen from favor or remain overlooked—companies selling at significant discounts to [intrinsic value](/intrinsic-value/), trading below book value, or facing temporary setbacks that the fund's managers believe are overblown.

## The contrarian thesis

Most active funds follow momentum or quality screens. LOGIQ's approach is inverted: the managers hunt for stocks that have been marked down sharply because of a single-period miss, a [sector rotation](/sector-rotation/), or simple indifference from larger funds. The candidates are companies with underlying profitability, real assets, and competitive capacity—but priced as if they are broken. The fund's managers conduct deep fundamental analysis to distinguish temporary pressure from structural decline. When they find the former, they buy.

The portfolio typically holds 40 to 60 names, weighted by the managers' conviction in the valuation opportunity and the expected time frame for correction. Some positions are near-term trade ideas; others are longer-term theses where the managers expect years for the market to recognize the value. Because the fund is actively managed, allocations can shift when the manager believes the risk-reward has changed—a contrarian opportunity that was a bargain at 40 percent of book value is less interesting at 90 percent.

LOGIQ publishes detailed holdings daily, so the fund is transparent about what is owned. The managers also publish quarterly commentaries explaining their philosophy and recent trading activity.

## The contrarian's advantage and risk

The potential edge is real: if most investors are biased toward growth or momentum, the contrarian can find pricing dislocations that resolve over time. A deeply unloved stock can compound faster than one the market adores, if the manager's thesis proves correct. But the flip side is equally important: stocks are often cheap for good reasons. The market may be right that the business is in secular decline, or that the [balance sheet](/balance-sheet/) is riskier than it appears, or that management is incapable of fixing the problem. [Contrarian investing](/contrarian-investing/) requires exceptional skill at distinguishing the two cases.

Liquidity in the fund itself is good—it is an ETF with daily trading and tight spreads given its assets. But some of the positions LOGIQ holds may be smaller, less-traded names with less favorable bid-ask dynamics. A position in a truly overlooked microcap is illiquid; the fund structure handles that through [authorized participants](/authorized-participant/) and creation-redemption, but the underlying stock's illiquidity is a real risk if you need to exit quickly.

## Performance and volatility

Contrarian portfolios often endure sustained underperformance during rallies in popular names. If growth and momentum are driving returns, a value-heavy contrarian fund will lag. The fund's price can also swing sharply on news—a successful turnaround thesis multiplies on release of better results, while a thesis that proves wrong can trigger sharp losses. Investors in LCO should expect higher volatility than the broad market in many periods.

## How to evaluate it

Request the prospectus and quarterly reports from LOGIQ's website. Review the fund's trailing returns versus the Russell 2000 Value or S&P 500 Value index, which benchmarks similar strategies. Look at the top 10 holdings and research those companies yourself—what makes them contrarian? What is the manager's thesis, and do you buy it? Check the fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) and turnover; high turnover in a contrarian portfolio suggests active trading and can generate tax drag in a taxable account, while stable turnover might indicate the managers' theses remain conviction-level. Download the manager commentary from the most recent quarterly report and read it carefully—it will explain the investment philosophy and give you a sense of how the managers think about identifying opportunities. Finally, ask yourself whether you have conviction in the thesis: if you do not believe the market is systematically wrong about overlooked value, this fund is probably not right for you.
