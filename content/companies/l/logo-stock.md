It is designed for investors who believe that brand strength creates a durable competitive moat that generates superior returns over time.*

## The thesis: durable brand moats in consumer markets

LOGO's foundation rests on a specific investment thesis: that the strongest consumer companies are those whose brands transcend mere functional performance. They have built something customers choose actively, often across generations, and that emotional connection creates pricing power — the ability to raise prices without losing customers. A Coca-Cola drinker or a Hermès customer does not switch simply because a competitor offers a cheaper alternative. This brand loyalty is rare, valuable, and creates a durable competitive moat.

The fund screens for that rare brand strength across global consumer markets. It includes players in soft drinks and juices, confectionery and packaged foods, personal care and beauty, luxury goods, restaurants, and consumer healthcare products. The selection logic excludes companies competing primarily on price, scale, or commodity-like functionality. Every holding is chosen for demonstrated ability to maintain market share and margins even as it raises prices — a discipline that creates superior economics and justifies higher valuations.

## A concentrated selection, not a broad market

Unlike a traditional consumer-sector index that would hold thousands of companies ranging from established giants to struggling commodity producers, LOGO is selective and concentrated. It holds roughly 80 to 100 companies, all screened for brand power. This selectivity introduces single-company risk: the portfolio is weighted enough that an adverse event at a major holding — regulatory action, supply-chain disruption, loss of key talent, or unexpected shift in consumer preference — can move fund returns more sharply than a wider index would. A regulatory threat to a large multinational beverage company or a sudden scandal at a luxury-goods icon can move LOGO several percentage points.

The geographic mix is biased toward the United States and developed Europe, where brand equity has been longest established and most thoroughly monetized. But LOGO is global: many holdings generate significant revenue from emerging markets, Asia, and other regions where branded consumer goods command premium prices even as incomes rise.

## Costs, liquidity, and performance drivers

LOGO charges an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) of approximately 0.55% annually — modest for a curated, custom-index approach but higher than a passive broad-market index. The fund trades on the exchange with reasonable liquidity; [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) are typically tight for moderate positions, reflecting steady investor interest in the branded-consumer theme.

Fund performance hinges on two factors. First, the broader health of consumer spending: when households have rising incomes and confidence, they spend more on branded and premium goods. Second, whether those premium brands actually retain their pricing power through economic cycles. In [recessions](/recession/) or periods of high [inflation](/inflation/), even loyal customers sometimes trade down to cheaper alternatives; the brand moat narrows. Conversely, in periods of rising wealth and confidence, branded luxury and premium goods materially outperform cheaper alternatives.

Geographic and currency exposure adds another layer. A meaningful portion of LOGO's companies earn revenue from Europe, Asia, and emerging markets but report in [US dollars](/us-dollar/). Currency movements — a strong or weak dollar relative to the euro, pound, or yen — therefore introduce volatility independent of the companies' underlying business performance.

## Research and the branded-consumer thesis

LOGO is suitable for long-term investors who believe brand power is a legitimate source of competitive advantage and who are comfortable holding a concentrated portfolio of global consumer leaders. To research the fund, start with the prospectus and current holdings list from Ritholtz Wealth Management, which detail the selection criteria and index methodology. Then read recent earnings reports from several of the largest holdings to understand how they manage pricing, maintain margins, and measure customer loyalty. Industry publications and consumer research track brand strength, pricing trends, and customer switching rates — these provide context for whether the fund's central thesis — that strong brands retain pricing power — is actually holding up in real time.
