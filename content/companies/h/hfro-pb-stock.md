Highland Opportunities & [Income Fund](/income-fund/) (NYSE: HFRO-PB) is a [closed-end fund](/closed-end-fund/) structured to generate income and capital appreciation by allocating opportunistically across multiple asset classes. The fund is managed by NexPoint Asset Management, L.P., which oversees the deployment of capital into liquid credit, structured credit, [opportunistic real estate](/opportunistic-real-estate/), and special situations investments.

## The opportunistic fund across cycles

Closed-end funds that follow opportunistic strategies behave distinctly differently from their market-neutral or core-strategy peers across economic cycles. Highland's fund faces expansion and contraction in opportunity flow, pricing of available assets, and the quality of yield available in different market regimes. During periods of economic strength and tight [credit spreads](/credit-spread/), yield-focused funds often trade at discounts to [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) because investors chase growth and demand higher returns, and the fund's floating-rate income tends to compress. Conversely, during downturns or periods of credit stress, opportunities multiply—distressed credit widening, real estate dislocations, and special situations—but investor redemptions and market volatility increase, sometimes pushing the fund's share price even further from its NAV.

The fund's structure changed materially in June 2023 when it broadened from a more tightly focused strategy into this opportunistic multi-asset approach. This shift reflects a pragmatic response to the changing nature of opportunity sets: single-strategy funds can find themselves fighting headwinds in mature or crowded segments, whereas a multi-strategy mandate allows the manager to rotate capital toward whatever pocket of the market offers the most attractive risk-adjusted returns at any given time. The trade-off is that investors must have faith in the manager's discretion and stock-picking ability, rather than in a clearly defined process.

## Distribution and shareholder structure

Highland distributes substantially all of its net investment income to shareholders, typically on a monthly basis. The regular distribution for common shares has been announced at $0.0385 per share, though the actual distribution amount and composition may vary depending on portfolio income, realized gains, and capital market conditions. In boom cycles when credit spreads are narrow and real estate leasing markets are healthy, distributions tend to consist primarily of current net investment income. In downturns, when the fund experiences realized losses or capital draw-downs, management may elect to return return of capital or hold distributions steady by drawing on reserves, a practice that protects shareholders from the appearance of declining distributions even as the fund's underlying earning power temporarily weakens.

The fund also issues preferred shares, including its 5.375% Series A Cumulative Preferred Shares, which occupy a senior position in the capital structure and receive preferential treatment in distributions. This layered capital structure allows the fund to lever its common equity and potentially amplify returns, though it also introduces additional fixed obligations that constrain flexibility during stressed periods.

## Asset allocation and the search for yield

The fund's core mandate is to allocate flexibly across liquid credit, structured credit products such as [collateralized loan obligations](/collateralized-loan-obligation/) and [securitizations](/securitization/), opportunistic real estate—including both operating properties and non-performing loan portfolios—and private or semi-private market investments where prices have dislocated or demand is temporarily thin. The emphasis on opportunistic allocation is central to understanding how this fund performs across the credit and economic cycle.

In early-cycle recoveries when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) are falling and credit spreads are compressing, the fund typically realizes capital gains in its fixed-income holdings as prices rise toward par, but new-money yields decline. This forces managers to either extend duration or venture into riskier credit or more illiquid structures to maintain a target yield. By mid-cycle, when the economy has normalized and spreads are tight, the fund often faces a yield desert—short-term rates are stable, [credit risk](/credit-risk/) is priced tightly, and the best opportunities are scarce. In late-cycle periods, before a downturn, the fund may hold its nose and accept lower-quality credits or take duration risk. Then, when the cycle turns and distress emerges, the fund can redeploy into attractive opportunities, though the path to doing so often involves mark-to-market losses on existing holdings first.

## How investors can research the fund

Anyone interested in Highland should begin with the fund's annual or semi-annual shareholder report (available through the SEC's EDGAR database under CIK 0001710680), which details portfolio composition, the maturity profile of holdings, and the breakdown of income by source. The quarterly or annual earnings releases provide color on recent distributions and any portfolio moves. Closed-end fund tracking sites publish the fund's current trading premium or discount to NAV, which is a useful lens: a persistent discount often suggests the market is skeptical about the manager's performance or the attractiveness of available opportunities, while a premium suggests confidence.

For context, the fund's benchmark—the [Credit Suisse](/credit-suisse/) Leveraged Loan Index or similar credit measures—provides a floor for comparison, though the multi-strategy nature of Highland means it will not track any single index closely. The fund's performance over a full credit cycle (typically 3-5 years) matters far more than quarterly returns, since opportunities are inherently timing-dependent and lumpy.
