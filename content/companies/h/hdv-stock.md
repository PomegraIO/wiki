*The **iShares Core High [Dividend](/dividend/) ETF** (ticker HDV) tracks a portfolio of large-cap U.S. companies selected for reliable, above-average dividend payments — neither a narrow yield trap nor a growth-at-any-cost play, but a middle path built on profitable, mature firms expected to keep paying out rising cash to shareholders.*

HDV is one of the flagship dividend-focused exchange-traded funds, managed by BlackRock's iShares division. It holds roughly 70–100 stocks chosen from the universe of companies with a [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) above $3 billion — large enough to have the financial maturity and stability to support sustainable dividends, but not constrained to only the absolute mega-cap behemoths. The fund's distinguishing rule is that it privileges both yield and quality: a company must pay a dividend above the market median to be considered, but it cannot qualify if its [dividend payout ratio](/dividend-payout-ratio/) (the share of earnings paid out) is too aggressive or if its [balance sheet](/balance-sheet/) shows signs of stress. This filters out dividend-value traps — firms paying large [current yields](/current-yield/) from cash or declining earnings that will eventually have to cut.

The underlying index, the Morningstar [Dividend Yield](/dividend-yield/) Focus Index, applies a mechanical screen: start with all stocks with above-median dividend yields, then remove the bottom third by quality metrics (chiefly [return on equity](/return-on-equity/) and payout sustainability). The result is a portfolio weighted toward classic dividend sectors — financials, utilities, consumer staples, energy — but tilted toward companies with durable competitive positions and pricing power rather than purely defensive plays struggling to maintain their payouts. It is a quality-dividend fund, not a yield-chasing fund, which is a meaningful difference in a rising-rate environment or during an economic cycle downturn.

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is low, characteristic of the passive ETF format — currently below 10 basis points annually (0.1%), which means most of what a shareholder earns from dividends and price appreciation is theirs to keep. The fund trades on major U.S. exchanges with tight [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/), so liquidity is not a practical concern for most investors. Because it holds large, actively traded stocks, it has minimal tracking error relative to its benchmark.

One of the central questions for any dividend-focused fund is how it behaves across the economic cycle. In booms, when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) are low and growth stocks command premium valuations, dividend stocks often lag — a drag that HDV shareholders have experienced multiple times. Conversely, in downturns or high-rate environments, dividend stocks become more attractive relative to growth plays, and the fund benefits from relative outperformance. Over a full cycle, the higher income return (from the dividend yield) and the lower volatility of large-cap dividend payers typically offer a smoother ride than more growth-oriented equity indices, though at the cost of potentially lighter capital appreciation in sustained [bull markets](/bull-market/).

The fund is designed for buy-and-hold investors seeking a regular income stream — it pays out dividends quarterly, and the yield is typically somewhere in the 3–4% range, higher than the broader market average. For tax-aware investors, most of the income is [qualified dividend](/qualified-dividend/) income, which carries favorable tax treatment in taxable accounts. The main risk is not so much that the fund will suffer lasting losses as that the dividend yield may disappoint in a period of rising corporate tax rates, cost [inflation](/inflation/) that eats into margins, or widespread dividend cuts across cyclical sectors like energy or finance.

HDV is often used as the "core" holding in a three-bucket income strategy: paired with a [bond](/bond/) fund for fixed income and perhaps a higher-yielding [closed-end fund](/closed-end-fund/) or preferred-stock fund for even more current cash flow. It is broad and plain enough to serve as a satellite holding in a diversified portfolio, and transparent enough that a researcher can easily see its holdings ([BlackRock](/blk-stock/) publishes daily data) and understand why the yield is what it is.

For someone researching the fund, the iShares fact sheet and the fund's prospectus lay out the exact methodology and historical performance. Compare HDV against competing dividend-focused vehicles — VYM (Vanguard High Dividend Yield ETF) and SCHD (Schwab U.S. Dividend [Equity ETF](/equity-etf/)) — to see how the yield, the expense ratio, and the underlying quality filter differ. The Morningstar Dividend Yield Focus Index itself is fully transparent, and tracking its composition quarter to quarter is the clearest way to see which companies passed the dividend-quality test and why.
