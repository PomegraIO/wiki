*The **HANMI FINANCIAL CORP** (HAFC) operates as a regional community bank with deep roots in the Korean-American market, headquartered in the Los Angeles area. It distinguishes itself among small-cap [financial institutions](/fisi-stock/) by concentrating its lending and deposit-taking activities on both first and second-generation Korean-American customers and businesses, a niche that has shaped its credit culture, risk management, and geographic footprint for decades.*

## Filing Lens: The Core Business from Regulatory Disclosures

HANMI's regulatory filings disclose a straightforward commercial banking model built on traditional deposit-gathering and loan origination. The bank's [10-K](/10-k/) annual report itemizes the composition of its loan portfolio — residential mortgages, [commercial real estate](/commercial-real-estate/), small business loans, and consumer credits — each category reflecting the credit needs of the communities it serves. Filings emphasize that the bank accepts deposits and lends them out locally, a model that ties profitability directly to interest rate spreads and the volume of loans it can originate and hold to maturity. The bank's [balance sheet](/balance-sheet/) structure — measured through [assets](/), [liabilities](/), and stockholders' equity — is the skeleton on which all operational claims rest.

## The Geographic Moat and Korean-American Banking

What separates HANMI from nationwide competitors is its hyper-focused customer base. The bank has built institutional knowledge in serving Korean-American entrepreneurs and households across California, particularly in Los Angeles and surrounding areas. This creates a distinct lending advantage: the bank understands the collateral (real estate, business equipment), the cash flow patterns (family businesses, entrepreneurial ventures), and the cultural preferences (deposits held over generations, relationship-based credit decisions) that characterize its market. A reader of the 10-K will find disclosure of loan concentrations by geography and borrower type; these concentrations are not weakness but strategic focus. The bank's loan officers speak the language and understand the businesses they finance, whether a Korean restaurant, a medical practice, or a commercial property in Koreatown.

## Deposit Liability Structure and Funding Strategy

The bank's filing documents its funding sources in the notes to consolidated financial statements. HANMI gathers deposits from consumers and businesses in its service areas, classifying them as demand deposits (checking), savings deposits, and time deposits. The regulatory requirement to disclose [deposit](/)-by-rate and maturity informs how the bank manages interest rate risk. When [interest rates](/interest-rate/) rise, the cost of funding the balance sheet rises; when they fall, existing deposits become cheaper relative to new borrowing. The bank's ability to retain low-cost deposits — achieved partly through customer relationships and partly through the deposit products it offers — directly affects its profitability. Filings detail the bank's reliance on core deposits versus wholesale funding; community banks like HANMI typically depend on retail deposits and rarely tap institutional money markets.

## Portfolio Composition and Credit Cycles

The 10-K's loan table breaks down the portfolio by purpose and stage of the economic cycle. Commercial real estate lending, which often carries substantial exposure to local economic conditions, typically comprises a large share of community bank portfolios. HANMI's own disclosures show how much it has lent to real estate developers, landlords, and construction companies in its market. Residential mortgage lending rounds out the consumer credit side. The bank's charge-off rates and non-performing loan disclosures are filed quarterly and annually; these are the primary metrics by which investors assess credit quality. A borrower in financial stress who stops paying is reported as past-due or non-performing; the bank's disclosure of these accounts reveals how well its underwriting and monitoring have worked. For a Korean-American focus lender, any major economic downturn affecting small business or real estate investment in California becomes a risk factor disclosed in the MD&A (Management Discussion & Analysis) section of the filing.

## Capital Requirements and Regulatory Supervision

HANMI, as a bank holding company, faces [securities and exchange commission](/securities-and-exchange-commission/) oversight through periodic filings and regular examination by banking regulators. The bank must maintain minimum capital levels — [common stock](/common-stock/) equity, [Tier 1 capital](/tier-1-capital/), and total risk-weighted capital — to absorb losses and protect depositors. Filings include the bank's capital ratios, which are compared against regulatory minimums; compliance is not optional. The bank also submits to examination and receives ratings from its primary federal regulator. These regulatory constraints limit how fast the bank can grow its balance sheet (it must hold equity sufficient to support new lending), constrain dividend and buyback actions, and shape strategic decisions around [acquisitions](/acquisition/) or branch closures. A reader of the filing can trace how much of the bank's earnings are retained versus paid out to [shareholders](/), partly as a function of these capital rules.

## Earnings Composition: The Net Interest Margin Story

The 10-K's [income statement](/income-statement/) reveals the bank's earnings sources. The largest line is typically net interest income — the spread earned between what the bank pays on deposits and what it receives from loans. On a simple basis, this is asset yield minus funding cost. Widening that spread, while managing credit losses, is the core of banking profit. HANMI's filings detail net interest margin by period, showing how changes in competitive deposit pricing and loan volume affect this metric. Non-interest income — fees from services, loan origination charges, and other ancillary revenue — usually contributes 20–40% of total income. Operating expenses include personnel costs (salaries for lenders and support staff), occupancy (rent or owned branch facilities), and technology infrastructure. The efficiency ratio — operating expense divided by revenue — is filed and compared across peers; a higher ratio signals that the bank spends more to earn each dollar of revenue.

## Dividend and Shareholder Returns

The filing's supplemental disclosures cover [dividends](/dividend/) paid to shareholders, buybacks, and other return-of-capital actions. HANMI's history of dividends (disclosed in the 10-K and supplemented by proxy statements) shows the confidence management has in stable, low-volatility earnings. ## Cross-Cycle Stability and Recession Risk

Community banks are cyclically sensitive — in severe [recessions](/recession/), loan losses spike and deposits may flee to larger, systemically safer institutions. HANMI's filings from prior crises (such as 2008–2009 or 2020) provide evidence of how it weathered stress. The bank's regulatory capital ratios, loan loss reserves, and disclosure of problem loans are the artifacts that let a careful reader assess whether the bank is resilient or vulnerable to the next downturn.
