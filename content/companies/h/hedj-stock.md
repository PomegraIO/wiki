| **What it holds** | Large-cap European equities (roughly 270–350 stocks across major EU, UK, and developed-market Europe) |
|---|---|
| **What it hedges** | Currency exposure — the fund neutralizes euro, pound, and other European currency movements against the dollar |
| **Who sponsors it** | WisdomTree Investments, an independent ETF firm |
| **Why hedge currency** | Isolates the equity return from the euro/dollar and pound/dollar moves that can overwhelm stock performance |
| **Expense ratio** | Roughly 50–60 basis points, moderate cost for an actively hedged regional fund |
| **Equivalent unhedged fund** | HEDJ's sister fund EWI (iShares MSCI EAFE ETF) offers European exposure without hedging |

WisdomTree Europe Hedged Equity Fund is a vehicle for investors who want European equity exposure but do not want to bet on currency swings. The fund tracks the WisdomTree Europe Hedged Equity Index, which holds the constituent stocks of developed Europe (primarily the eurozone, the UK, and Switzerland) weighted by fundamental factors like [dividends](/dividend/) and earnings, but it removes the foreign-exchange volatility by selling euros forward and buying dollars — a mechanical currency hedge that resets continuously.

The distinction matters most when currencies and equities move in opposite directions. If European stocks rise but the euro weakens sharply against the dollar, an unhedged European fund holder captures the stock gain but loses some of it in [currency depreciation](/currency-depreciation/); a hedged holder locks in the stock return and is indifferent to the currency move. Conversely, if European stocks rise and the euro strengthens, an unhedged investor wins twice, but a hedged investor only captures the equity gain. Over different periods, hedging helps or hurts, but the point of HEDJ is not to outguess currencies — it is to eliminate them as a variable.

The fund's holdings are weighted by [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/) and earnings, not pure [market cap](/market-capitalization/), which means it overweights profitable, high-yielding companies relative to a pure-cap-weighted European index. This fundamentals-based slant gives the fund a mild value tilt; in periods when growth stocks outperform, HEDJ may lag, but in periods favoring value and dividends, it may outperform an unhedged peer.

The hedge itself carries a small cost. WisdomTree runs the currency forwards daily, and the [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) of roughly 55–60 basis points includes both the fund's operational costs and the [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) implicit in the hedging trades. The fund is liquid — it trades on [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) with typical spreads of a few basis points — and because the underlying stocks are among Europe's largest, replication is straightforward and tracking error is minimal.

This fund's cyclical story differs from an unhedged European equity fund in one key way: it decouples from European [monetary policy](/monetary-policy/). When the [European Central Bank](/european-central-bank/) is hiking rates and the euro strengthens, an unhedged European fund often benefits from the currency tailwind. When the ECB cuts and the euro weakens, the currency headwind can undercut stock gains. HEDJ removes that macro layer and lets you own European equities as a pure-equity play. This matters most for investors who are overweight U.S. stocks already and are adding Europe for [diversification](/diversification/) without taking on additional currency exposure, or for those who have a specific view on European companies but not on the euro.

To evaluate HEDJ, compare its returns to EWI (WisdomTree Europe Equity) over periods where the euro moved significantly in different directions — a sharply weakening euro may make hedging expensive in hindsight, while a steadily strengthening euro makes it a bargain. Look at the fund's actual expense ratio and the daily hedging cost embedded in its tracking error. Consider your broader portfolio: if you already have dollar-based assets and significant U.S. equity exposure, a hedged European fund adds cleaner diversification; if you want currency diversification as well, an unhedged version may be more appealing.
