*[HERTZ GLOBAL HOLDINGS, INC](/htz-stock/), trading as **HTZ**, operates one of the world's largest vehicle rental networks. The company's business is tightly regulated at every operational layer: vehicle safety standards, insurance and liability frameworks, environmental compliance for its fleet, franchising rules that govern its agency networks, and state and local licensing for each location. Understanding Hertz requires mapping the regulatory obligations that govern fleet acquisition, maintenance, employee licensing, and customer liability across dozens of jurisdictions.*

## Vehicle Safety Standards and Fleet Compliance

Every vehicle in Hertz's fleet must meet National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) standards. These standards cover crashworthiness, braking performance, tire safety, and electronic stability systems. Hertz cannot place a vehicle on the rental lot unless it meets federal safety standards, and it cannot modify a vehicle in a way that defeats safety systems. If NHTSA issues a safety recall, Hertz must address the defect in every affected vehicle in its fleet before the vehicle can be rented. Failure to comply with recalls can result in substantial fines and liability exposure for the company.

Hertz must also maintain equipment compliance: brakes, tires, lighting, and emissions systems must pass regular inspection. In many states, rental companies are required to conduct safety inspections at intervals (often every 1,000 miles or before re-rental). Documentation of these inspections is auditable; if Hertz neglects inspections and a rented vehicle causes an accident because of a mechanical defect, the company faces liability and potential regulatory enforcement for unsafe vehicle operation.

The company maintains an internal fleet management system to track maintenance, recalls, and inspection schedules. Regulators can audit these records; any evidence that Hertz knowingly rented unsafe vehicles exposes the company to criminal liability and civil damages.

## Vehicle Emission and Environmental Standards

Hertz's fleet must comply with EPA emissions standards. Gasoline and diesel vehicles must meet tailpipe emission limits for nitrogen oxides, particulate matter, and other pollutants. If Hertz acquires vehicles that later fail emissions tests or have emissions defects, the company shares liability with the manufacturer. In the case of the Volkswagen emissions scandal, rental companies and fleet operators faced regulatory scrutiny because they unknowingly rented non-compliant vehicles.

In some states and cities, air quality regulators impose fleet composition rules on large vehicle operators. California's Clean Air Act directives, for example, encourage large fleets to transition to lower-emission vehicles. Some jurisdictions offer incentives for battery electric vehicle adoption or impose fees on higher-emission vehicles. Hertz's procurement decisions are increasingly shaped by these environmental mandates; procuring the wrong vehicle mix can result in penalties or incentive clawbacks.

Tire waste is also regulated. End-of-life tires from Hertz vehicles must be disposed of according to state environmental rules. Some states impose tire recycling fees; Hertz must track and comply with these requirements.

## Rental Agreement Liability and Insurance

Hertz must comply with state consumer protection laws governing rental agreements. Terms regarding liability, damage waivers, and pricing disclosures are subject to state regulation. In many states, Hertz cannot legally exempt itself from liability for personal injury or wrongful death caused by its negligence. Damage waivers (loss damage waivers, or LDWs) are also regulated; some states restrict or prohibit their use, while others require explicit disclosures. If Hertz's rental agreement violates state law, the company faces liability for recovery of rental fees or civil damages.

Insurance is a central regulatory issue. Hertz maintains commercial auto liability insurance covering bodily injury and property damage. The amount and coverage are subject to state-mandated minimums. In some states, Hertz may be required to provide primary coverage even if a renter carries personal auto insurance. Rental company practices are regularly scrutinized; if Hertz customers claim that the company's insurance practices prevented recovery for damages, state insurance commissioners may investigate.

Hertz also manages customer identity verification and rental eligibility. Federal law prohibits renting to individuals without valid driver's licenses. Age restrictions vary by state; some states allow 18-year-olds to rent, others require 21. Hertz must verify driver eligibility for every rental, and failure to do so can result in liability if an unqualified driver causes injury.

## Franchising and Agency Network Compliance

Much of Hertz's business operates through franchised locations and agency partnerships. These arrangements are governed by state franchise disclosure laws and motor vehicle dealer laws. Most states require franchise disclosure documents (Franchise Disclosure Documents, or FDDs) to be filed or registered with the state before offering franchises. Hertz's FDD must accurately disclose the franchise relationship, costs, support provided, and historical performance. Misrepresentations in franchise materials can trigger state enforcement actions and private litigation by franchisees.

Some franchisees are independent agents operating under Hertz's brand. State dealer laws may classify these agents as dealers, imposing licensing and bonding requirements on Hertz. If Hertz fails to ensure its agents meet state licensing standards, the company faces regulatory liability. Additionally, rental locations must often obtain local business licenses and comply with city/county zoning and operational regulations.

## Employee Licensing and Background Checks

Hertz employees who handle vehicle keys and manage customer interactions must meet state requirements. Many states require background checks for employees with access to customer information. Hertz must comply with Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) rules when conducting background checks, including providing notice and opportunity for applicants to dispute results. If Hertz hires an employee with a disqualifying criminal record or fails to conduct required background checks, it faces liability if that employee harms a customer.

In some states, Hertz drivers (for shuttle services) must hold valid driver's licenses and may require additional insurance or bonding. Employees working at airport rental locations must comply with airport security and badging requirements, which vary by airport.

## Consumer Protection and Disclosure Requirements

State attorneys general and consumer protection agencies scrutinize rental company practices. In recent years, regulators have focused on undisclosed fees, misleading damage estimates, and aggressive add-on sales. Hertz's pricing disclosures must be clear; any mandatory fees (facility charges, taxes, airport fees) must be disclosed before the customer commits to the rental. Damage inspection procedures must be transparent and fair; Hertz must not charge for pre-existing damage or use fabricated damage claims to increase revenue. If a state's consumer protection office receives complaints that Hertz engages in deceptive practices, an investigation may follow, resulting in civil penalties or a consent order requiring operational changes.

## Data Privacy and Security

Hertz collects substantial customer information: driver's license numbers, payment card data, address, phone number. This data must be protected under state privacy laws and the CCPA (in California). Payment card data is subject to PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard). If Hertz experiences a data breach, it must notify affected customers and may face regulatory action if it failed to implement reasonable security measures. Given the sensitive nature of customer data (driver's license numbers are prime identity theft targets), regulatory expectations for Hertz's cybersecurity practices are high.

## Airport and Concourse Regulations

Many Hertz locations operate on airport premises. Airports impose their own operational regulations, safety requirements, and concession agreements. The concessionaire agreement specifies service levels, staffing, pricing caps, and operational standards. If Hertz fails to meet service standards—long wait times, unavailable vehicles, incomplete damage inspections—the airport operator can terminate the concession or impose penalties. Airport security requirements, including employee badging and background checks, fall on Hertz. Additionally, some airports impose environmental requirements; if an airport mandates a minimum percentage of electric vehicles in rental fleets, Hertz must comply or lose its concession.

## Regulatory Disclosure and Investor Considerations

Hertz's [10-K](/10-k/) filing discloses material litigation, regulatory proceedings, and compliance risks. Investors should examine disclosures on safety recalls, pending litigation related to liability claims, franchise disputes, and regulatory investigations. The company's insurance expense and claims history reveal the incidence and severity of accidents and liability exposure. Material changes in environmental regulations, particularly mandates for vehicle fleet electrification, affect Hertz's capital allocation and operating costs. Any regulatory enforcement action—such as a consumer protection settlement—can signal operational or compliance problems. Monitoring Hertz's regulatory landscape is essential to understanding long-term business durability and profitability.
