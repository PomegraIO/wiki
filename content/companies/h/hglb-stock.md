Highland Global Allocation Fund seeks to deliver long-term capital appreciation and current income by deploying capital across a broad spectrum of global securities and alternative investments. Launched in 2019 and managed by NexPoint Asset Management — a private investment firm with expertise in real estate, credit, and alternative strategies — HGLB is designed to offer shareholders exposure to diversified sources of return: equity upside, income from [bonds](/bond/), and yield from unconventional investments like senior loans.

The fund operates as a closed-end investment company, trading on the [New York Stock Exchange](/new-york-stock-exchange/) under HGLB. Unlike open-end [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/), which redeem shares daily at [net asset value](/net-asset-value/), HGLB issues a fixed number of shares that trade on the exchange at market prices. This structure affords managers flexibility to hold illiquid assets — private securities, structured debt, senior loans — without needing to maintain cash for daily redemptions. It also creates the possibility that shares trade at a premium or discount to NAV, a feature that can amplify or dampen returns depending on investor sentiment toward the fund.

The fund's distribution policy is specific and transparent: it targets an annual distribution equal to 8.5 percent of the fund's average net asset value, computed as the mean NAV over the final five trading days of each calendar year. This means distributions are reset annually and paid out monthly, providing shareholders consistent cash flow regardless of the underlying portfolio's performance in any given year. This level-distribution approach is common among [closed-end funds](/closed-end-fund/) aiming to reward income investors; it also means that in strong years, distributions may exceed current earnings, drawing down reserves, while in weak years, distributions may exceed NAV declines, supported by capital gains or reserves.

The mandate is deliberately broad. The fund may invest in U.S. and foreign equities across developed and emerging markets; investment-grade and lower-grade fixed-income securities; senior secured loans to corporations; and alternative investments including [hedge fund](/hedge-fund/) strategies, derivatives, and other instruments. This flexibility is the fund's conceptual advantage: the manager can tilt toward equities when valuations are attractive, rotate into bonds when yields are compelling, or access specialized opportunities like structured credit or private lending. It is also a source of complexity and risk: a fund this broad is difficult for shareholders to understand precisely, and performance depends critically on manager skill in [asset allocation](/asset-allocation/).

Recent developments have reshaped the fund's positioning. In October 2025, the fund's net asset value surged by nearly 19 percent (approximately $2.24 per share), driven substantially by an investment position in a [data center REIT](/data-center-reit/) called Fermi, Inc. This windfall signals how a concentrated, well-timed position can drive outsized fund returns. It also raises a question about whether the move reflects intentional tilting toward high-growth infrastructure or a fortunate but concentrated bet that raises portfolio risk.

Alongside the NAV increase, the fund announced the addition of Scott Johnson as a second portfolio manager, joining James Dondero. This shift suggests recognition that managing a multi-asset, global mandate requires broader expertise or simply more hands to execute the allocation process across many opportunity sets. Johnson's background will signal whether the fund is shifting toward a particular asset class or investment philosophy.

The fund's distribution-to-NAV ratio is worth monitoring. If distributions are consistently higher than the fund's earnings, management may be drawing down capital or using leverage to sustain the 8.5 percent target. Such a pattern cannot continue indefinitely without eroding the fund's real value. Conversely, if NAV is growing faster than distributions, the fund is accumulating capital, which may signal either disciplined deployment or opportunity hoarding. The gap between the fund's stock price and NAV is also informative: a sustained discount may signal that the market doubts the manager's investment case, while a premium may reflect investor enthusiasm for the portfolio.

For shareholders, HGLB offers a multi-asset alternative to holding separate stock and bond funds, with professional management and the potential for tactical tilting across global markets. The tradeoff is fee burden, opacity about specific holdings, and exposure to manager decisions rather than direct index exposure. The recent additions to personnel and the significant NAV lift from alternative investments suggest the fund is evolving toward a more active, opportunity-driven model. How that unfolds will determine whether shareholders receive value from the flexibility or find themselves paying for complexity that does not translate to outperformance.
