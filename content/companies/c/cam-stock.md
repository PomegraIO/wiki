*The **AB California Intermediate Municipal ETF** (ticker CAM) is an exchange-traded fund that invests exclusively in intermediate-term [municipal bonds](/municipal-bond/) issued by California state and local governments. The fund targets investors—particularly those in high-tax brackets or high-tax states—who prioritise tax-advantaged income over capital appreciation, offering yields that are free from federal taxation and, for California residents, free from state and local income tax as well.*

Municipal [bonds](/bond/) issued by California municipalities carry a special status in the tax code. Interest income from these bonds is exempt from federal income tax, and when held by California residents, also exempt from California state income tax and most local taxes. This double or triple exemption makes the yields on California municipal bonds attractive to high-income earners who would otherwise owe substantial tax on bond interest—particularly those earning above the top federal bracket or subject to the [Net Investment Income Tax](/net-investment-income-tax/).

CAM focuses specifically on intermediate-maturity bonds, meaning the portfolio holds obligations with remaining maturities typically between five and twelve years. This maturity band occupies the middle ground of the [yield curve](/yield-curve/): longer bonds offer higher yields than short-term bonds but carry more [interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/), whilst shorter bonds offer greater stability but lower income. Intermediate bonds strike a balance, providing meaningful yield while avoiding the extreme volatility of longer-duration portfolios. The fund is managed by AllianceBernstein, one of the largest institutional asset managers, and holds a diversified mix of issuers across California's municipalities, school districts, utility authorities, and other public entities.

The appeal of municipal bonds to California's wealthy is straightforward arithmetic. A high-earner in California faces federal rates up to 37% plus California state income tax of up to 13.3%, combining for marginal rates above 50% on certain income. A California municipal bond yielding 4% delivers the equivalent of roughly 8% pre-tax income to a 50%-bracket taxpayer. No [corporate bond](/corporate-bond/) or Treasury of similar duration can match that after-tax value. This advantage makes municipal bonds a staple of wealthy Californians' portfolios, and CAM offers that exposure in liquid, transparent, low-cost form.

The risks worth acknowledging are familiar to any bond investor. [Interest rates](/interest-rate/) and bond prices move inversely: if rates rise, existing bonds decline in value. Because CAM holds intermediate bonds, a significant rise in long-term rates would depress the fund's price, though the effect is less severe than for longer-duration bonds. [Inflation](/inflation/) is a secondary risk: if inflation accelerates, the real (after-inflation) value of the fixed income erodes. For California municipalities specifically, [credit risk](/credit-risk/) is low relative to corporate bonds—default is rare—but cannot be dismissed entirely. Budget crises or demographic shifts can strain municipal finances, and in rare cases lead to downgrades or losses.

CAM's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is low, typically well under 0.2% annually, reflecting the fund's passive construction and the straightforward nature of holding a basket of bonds. The fund trades throughout the day on an exchange, and its spread is usually tight relative to its [net asset value](/net-asset-value/), making it cost-effective to buy and sell. Yields fluctuate with market conditions and the underlying bonds' coupons, but the fund's after-tax appeal for California high-earners remains consistent as long as California's tax rates remain high relative to the rest of the nation.

Any investor considering CAM should verify its current maturity profile, credit quality breakdown, and yield distribution in the fund's fact sheet. The aftermarket for California municipal bonds is less liquid than Treasuries or corporates, but the fund's diversified holdings and underlying market depth mean that liquidating shares in CAM itself is straightforward. For California residents in high tax brackets, municipal bonds are a foundational holding, and CAM offers an efficient vehicle to access a curated portfolio of intermediate-maturity California obligations without the expertise or capital commitment required to build such a portfolio independently.
