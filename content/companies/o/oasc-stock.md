*The **OneAscent Enhanced Small and Mid Cap ETF** (ticker OASC) holds smaller and mid-sized U.S. companies, weighted not simply by market value but tilted toward companies exhibiting quality characteristics—strong [balance sheets](/balance-sheet/), reliable profitability—and reasonable valuations. It blends traditional index investing with quantitative factor tilts, aiming to deliver better-than-market returns at a lower cost than active stock-picking.*

### What does "small and mid cap" actually mean?

Small and mid-cap stocks are companies smaller than the mega-cap giants ([Apple](/aapl-stock/), [Microsoft](/msft-stock/), Berkshire) but still significant enough to trade actively and have measurable earnings and cash flow. Broadly, mid-cap typically means companies with [market capitalizations](/market-capitalization/) between $3 billion and $10 billion; small-cap ranges from about $500 million to $3 billion, though there is no universal definition. OASC likely covers the universe from the smallest reliably tradeable stocks up through the mid-cap range, capturing a swath of companies that includes the next layer of American business outside the household names. Included might be regional banks, specialized manufacturers, regional retailers, and emerging healthcare and technology firms.

### Why own small and mid-cap stocks at all?

Over very long periods, small-cap stocks have delivered higher average returns than large-cap stocks, though with higher volatility and wider [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/). The logic is intuitive: larger companies are more mature and stable; smaller companies have more upside runway if they execute well but also greater bankruptcy risk. For investors with long time horizons and the temperament to tolerate equity drawdowns, the higher long-term return potential of the small-cap universe can be attractive. OASC offers a simple way to gain that exposure rather than picking individual small firms, which requires more research and carries company-specific risk.

### What does "enhanced" and "quality-tilted" mean?

Standard small-cap [index funds](/index-fund/) own all the stocks in their universe, weighted by market capitalization. OASC uses a factor-based approach: it applies quantitative filters to overweight stocks that exhibit quality characteristics—strong [return on equity](/return-on-equity/), low leverage, consistent earnings—and reasonable valuations—price-to-earnings and [price-to-book ratios](/price-to-book-ratio/) that are not stretched. This tilt is "enhanced" in the sense that it is not pure market-cap weighting; it is a rule-based deviation designed to favor higher-quality businesses at reasonable prices. The goal is to capture the return premium that quality tends to deliver while staying close enough to a broad small-cap index to keep costs low.

### How does the fund actually build its portfolio?

The fund likely uses a quantitative screener to rank all eligible small and mid-cap stocks on metrics such as profitability, balance-sheet strength, earnings stability, and valuation. It then weights the portfolio to emphasize the highest-ranked companies and de-emphasize the lower-ranked ones, while maintaining enough breadth to keep individual position sizes reasonable and avoid [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/). Unlike an [actively managed fund](/actively-managed-fund/) where a person chooses stocks based on conviction, the portfolio construction is rule-based and systematic. This mechanistic approach keeps costs low and removes human emotion from stock selection, but it also means the fund is locked into its quality criteria and cannot step aside if the market is pricing quality very differently from historical norms.

### What are the real risks of this strategy?

Value and quality stocks can underperform growth stocks for years at a time. During periods when the market rewards high-growth, expensively-valued companies over stable, profitable ones, a quality-tilted fund will lag. The small-cap space is also more prone to [liquidity risk](/liquidity-risk/)—some stocks may trade in thin volumes, making it harder to buy and sell without moving the price. Because the fund owns smaller companies with smaller analyst coverage, there is more chance of a truly bad surprise that moves the stock sharply. And because quality is a factor that "rotates" in and out of favor, overweighting it can create periods of significant underperformance that test an investor's conviction.

### What are the holdings and sector exposures?

OASC holds hundreds of small and mid-cap stocks across all major sectors. The quality tilt means it likely has less exposure to very capital-intensive businesses with thin margins (traditional mining, utilities, commodity businesses) and more to businesses with durable competitive advantages and strong cash-generation (healthcare, software, financial services, select consumer firms). The exact sector mix depends on where quality is cheapest; in some years, quality small-cap companies might be more prevalent in healthcare; in others, in industrials. The prospectus or fact sheet specifies the current holdings and their sector breakdown.

### How to research the fund?

Start with OneAscent's fact sheet and prospectus, which detail the selection criteria, the index the fund benchmarks against, and the current [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/). Compare OASC's performance to a pure small-cap market index to see how much the quality tilt has helped or hindered. Look at the current holdings to understand the quality characteristics being sought and confirm the small and mid-cap universe aligns with your expectations. The fund's trading volume and bid-ask spread indicate how easy it is to enter and exit; more liquidity is better for larger positions. And consider whether the cyclical nature of factor tilts—where quality, value, and growth go in and out of favor—fits your willingness to hold through periods when the fund lags the broader market.
