The Pacer BlueStar Digital Entertainment ETF (ticker: ODDS, [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/)) is a [passively managed fund](/passively-managed-fund/) that holds equities in companies whose primary or significant revenue comes from digital forms of entertainment — online gaming, video streaming, esports, mobile games, and creator platforms. It emerged as investors recognized that entertainment consumption was structurally shifting from traditional broadcast and cinema toward digital distribution, and the fund codifies that narrative as an investable theme.

## The story before ODDS

For nearly a century, entertainment was a vertically integrated, geographically bound system: Hollywood studios controlled film production and owned theater circuits; broadcast networks owned airwaves and schedules; record labels controlled music distribution through retail. This model was remarkably stable. A viewer watched prime-time television on the network's schedule; a listener bought recorded music in a shop; an audience attended movies at cinemas. The gatekeepers controlled when, where, and what the public consumed.

The internet shattered this architecture. By the 2010s, the largest revenue pools in entertainment had migrated elsewhere. Mobile gaming generated more industry revenue than console gaming. Streaming-video subscribers outnumbered cable-television subscribers in many markets. Esports tournaments drew millions of online viewers and hundreds of millions in sponsorship. Independent creators built audiences of millions through platforms like YouTube and Twitch, bypassing traditional studios entirely. The shift was real and durable: it was not about a cycle but about a permanent reallocation of attention and spending.

For investors, this transformation was frustrating to capture efficiently. A broad technology index held gaming and streaming companies, but diluted among cloud infrastructure, semiconductors, advertising networks, and enterprise software. A media index held cable and film studios alongside some digital exposure. There was no single fund offering concentrated access to digital entertainment as a theme. Pacer Financial, a financial-services firm focused on niche indices and [thematic ETFs](/thematic-etf/), responded by creating the BlueStar Digital Entertainment Index and the ODDS fund to give investors that concentration.

## How the BlueStar index identifies digital entertainment

The index uses a rules-based methodology: it screens the investable universe for companies that derive a meaningful minimum percentage of revenue from digital entertainment — typically 15% to 25%, though the exact threshold is proprietary to the index. A company that generates 30% of revenue from gaming qualifies; one that generates 5% may not. This filter prevents ODDS from simply holding all technology stocks or all consumer discretionary stocks, and instead focuses specifically on the entertainment angle.

The resulting portfolio spans subsectors. Large game publishers, console makers with strong gaming franchises, and streaming-video operators form the core. Esports infrastructure companies, online gambling and casino operators, and mobile-game platforms fill the middle. Creator-economy platforms and digital-media companies round out the holdings. The index rebalances quarterly or on a regular schedule, meaning the fund evolves as companies' business mixes shift — a hardware maker pivoting toward gaming gets added; a pure-play gaming company acquired by a conglomerate and subsequently diluted gets downweighted.

This fluidity reflects the reality of modern businesses: digital entertainment is not a separate economic sector but a revenue stream embedded in diversified companies. A company that makes consumer electronics, cloud-infrastructure services, or entertainment-software tools may qualify for inclusion precisely because its gaming or streaming division is material to its overall economics.

## The investment thesis and its limits

ODDS embodies a simple thesis: digital entertainment will continue displacing traditional broadcast, cinema, and recorded-music models, and ownership of the companies driving that displacement offers meaningful returns. The thesis is rooted in observable facts. Younger audiences spend more time on gaming and streaming than on traditional television. Advertising dollars increasingly flow toward digital platforms and esports sponsorships. Console and mobile gaming install bases dwarf cinema audiences.

But the thesis has limits. First, traditional media is more durable than digital natives expected. Television still commands enormous advertising and viewership; theatrical films still draw audiences; music licensing still generates revenue through radio and physical media. Legacy media companies have learned to compete digitally, launching streaming services and gaming divisions of their own, often with greater capital reserves and distribution reach than pure-play digital-entertainment companies. Second, many ODDS holdings are growth companies with valuations built on strong revenue-expansion expectations. If growth sentiment wanes, or if a company misses growth targets, share prices can decline sharply — disproportionately more than blue-chip stocks. Third, some holdings operate in regulatory gray zones. Online gambling faces different rules in different jurisdictions; esports betting is illegal in some places; loot boxes in games face scrutiny from regulators worldwide. A major regulatory shift can reshape the economics of an entire subsector within ODDS.

## Volatility, concentration, and market conditions

ODDS is materially more volatile than broad stock indices because its holdings are concentrated on growth, youth-focused products, and rapidly evolving consumer preferences. When growth stocks rally on enthusiasm about future earnings and low [interest rates](/interest-rate/), ODDS tends to outperform; when growth sentiment sours or fear of slower growth emerges, the fund can decline sharply. The sector also carries specific concentrated bets: the future of console gaming, the path to profitability for streaming services, the regulatory treatment of certain gambling and esports verticals.

During the pandemic, ODDS benefited enormously from lockdown-driven gaming and streaming adoption and from a broader market enthusiasm for growth stocks. Valuations expanded. In subsequent years, as interest rates rose and growth sentiment cooled, the fund faced headwinds. The real question for investors is whether the underlying thesis — digital entertainment as a durable structural shift — survives multiple cycles, or whether it is a growth bubble prone to correction.

## Costs, composition, and who ODDS is for

ODDS carries an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) typical of thematic ETFs: higher than a broad [S&P 500 index](/sp-500-index/) fund but lower than actively managed [sector funds](/sector-fund/), because the fund simply holds the BlueStar index without paying for research or stock-picking. Trading occurs on NASDAQ with minimal [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) because the fund attracts reasonable daily volume. The fund's composition typically holds 30 to 60 companies, weighted by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) or the index provider's formula, offering [diversification](/diversification/) across subsectors but remaining concentrated relative to holding all equities.

ODDS appeals to investors convinced that digital entertainment is a durable theme deserving concentrated exposure, and who want that conviction reflected in a single holding rather than building a custom portfolio of dozens of stocks. It is not for income seekers; most holdings reinvest earnings and prioritize growth. It is also not a pure play on any single entertainment subsector — a decline in console gaming does not destroy the fund if streaming platforms surge. The real risk is that digital entertainment slows, that traditional media proves more durable than disruptors assumed, or that specific regulatory moves harm core holdings.

## How to research ODDS

Start with Pacer's prospectus and the BlueStar Digital Entertainment Index documentation, which explain the exact definition of "digital entertainment" and the selection methodology. Review the current top 15 holdings to understand what companies and business models are driving the fund. Compare ODDS's sector and subsector breakdown: what proportion is game publishers, streaming platforms, esports, hardware, or infrastructure? That breakdown reveals where the fund's real bets lie.

Examine the fund's performance during different market regimes — how did ODDS behave when growth stocks surged, versus when they crashed? Read recent earnings reports and presentations from the fund's largest holdings to understand the drivers: new game launches, subscriber growth, regulatory shifts, or competitive pressure. Finally, compare ODDS's trajectory to individual holdings and thematic competitors to understand whether the fund's diversification is delivering value, or whether a concentrated bet on one or two large gaming publishers or streaming operators would have performed similarly.
