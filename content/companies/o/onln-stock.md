## What does ONLN actually own?

The ProShares Online Retail ETF holds stocks of companies that generate revenue primarily through online channels — internet retail giants like Amazon and [Shopify](/shop-stock/), online travel agencies like Expedia and Booking, digital marketplaces like eBay, and pure-play e-commerce businesses. The fund is not limited to merchants; it includes any company whose business model is built on selling goods or services to consumers over the internet. That includes web-based services like DoorDash (food delivery) and [Airbnb](/abnb-stock/) (accommodation rental).

## How does a fund this concentrated behave?

ONLN holds somewhere between forty and seventy stocks, depending on how ProShares defines "online retail" at any rebalance. The result is not a diversified [index fund](/index-fund/); it is a bet on a specific narrative. The fund is heavily weighted toward mega-cap tech leaders, particularly Amazon, which alone can represent twenty to thirty percent of the portfolio. This concentration means ONLN's performance is not that different from buying a handful of the largest e-commerce names. The fund moves in lockstep with the success or failure of a small number of companies, all of which are highly correlated with each other.

## Why is the online retail sector cyclical?

The core cyclicality is consumer spending and discretionary purchases. When the economy is strong and household income is rising, consumers spend freely online. They are less price-sensitive and more willing to try new merchants and brands. E-commerce companies expand margins and reinvest in growth. ONLN soars.

In a [recession](/recession/), online retail companies face a squeeze from both sides. Consumer spending contracts, especially on discretionary items. But the companies themselves also cut capital spending and try to manage costs, which limits growth. More important, many internet retail companies operate on thin margins — Amazon operates at single-digit profit margins despite its scale — so any decline in revenue hits earnings hard. ONLN can experience severe drawdowns in downturns even though e-commerce as a whole has not gone anywhere.

Furthermore, the cyclicality varies by business model. Amazon primarily competes on price and selection, so it struggles when consumers tighten spending. But Etsy, which sells handmade goods and vintage items, can suffer even more in downturns because its customers are price-sensitive. Conversely, during boom years, Amazon's ability to undercut and scale overwhelms smaller competitors, so ONLN can be dominated by Amazon's cycle. During busts, Amazon might hold up better than specialized retailers, so the composition of the fund and the correlation structure change radically.

## What are the structural risks?

Concentration is the obvious danger. Amazon's dominance means ONLN is partly a bet on Amazon's execution, valuation, and margins. If Amazon stumbles on cost control or loses market share, the entire fund suffers. Owning ONLN is not meaningfully different from owning Amazon plus a few smaller bets.

Competition is fierce and structural. New entrants can launch e-commerce sites with little capital, and existing brick-and-mortar retailers can pivot online. This keeps margins under pressure and means some of ONLN's holdings will be disrupted by new models or forced into price wars that erode profitability. Unlike utilities or consumer staples, which have defensible moats, many online retailers are in perpetual competitive danger.

Margin compression is chronic. The biggest e-commerce companies have trained consumers to expect free shipping and easy returns, which makes profitability difficult. A company like Amazon can absorb lower margins because of scale, but smaller online retailers are squeezed. Any new entrant who undercuts on price can rapidly gain share. This structural pressure means ONLN is never a defensive holding.

Finally, the fund is highly correlated with growth-stock sentiment and with the fate of Amazon specifically. In periods when growth stocks fall from favor or when Amazon faces regulatory scrutiny or faces an earnings miss, the entire fund can decline sharply. The lack of [diversification](/diversification/) across business models and profit sources makes ONLN volatile and cyclical.

## When does ONLN thrive?

ONLN thrives in booms, when consumer confidence is high, job creation is strong, and household savings are ample. The 2020–2021 pandemic period was extraordinarily favorable because lockdowns forced all commerce online and Amazon in particular benefited from the tailwind. That was not a normal environment.

ONLN also thrives when internet penetration is increasing globally and when new categories of commerce are moving online. Groceries, pharmaceuticals, and financial services are only recently moving to digital channels in many countries, so there is still a tailwind. But that tailwind will eventually exhaust itself.

## How to research ONLN

Start by looking at the prospectus and the fund's top ten holdings. What percentage is Amazon? If it is more than twenty-five percent, you are essentially buying Amazon plus a satellite portfolio. Compare ONLN's performance to Amazon's performance; if they move in lockstep, then owning the fund offers no benefit over owning the stock directly.

Look at ONLN's performance during the last recession and during the last [bull market](/bull-market/) to see the swings. What was the [maximum drawdown](/maximum-drawdown/)? How long did it take to recover? These numbers show you the true cycle of the bet.

Finally, ask yourself: do you believe e-commerce is still moving up as a share of total retail spending? If yes, then ONLN captures that trend. If you think e-commerce penetration has plateaued and margin pressure is now the dominant force, then ONLN is a deteriorating business at an uncertain price.
