## What ONDL does

ONDL attempts to double the daily return of its benchmark — the Defiance On-Demand Index — through financial leverage. On a day when the underlying index rises 1%, ONDL is designed to rise 2%. Conversely, when the index falls 1%, ONDL should fall 2%. This amplification is achieved using a combination of options, futures, and borrowing, and the leverage is reset every trading day.

The underlying index itself is a thematic basket of companies in cloud computing and subscription software — similar in spirit to the broader ProShares On-Demand ETF (OND), but managed directly by Defiance. The specific holdings vary and are not tracked against a published benchmark the way a standard ETF would be.

## Daily reset mechanics and volatility decay

The "daily reset" structure is central to how ONDL works and why it carries unique risks. Each morning, the fund's leverage is recalibrated to target exactly 2X exposure. This means that on a day when the underlying moves up or down, the fund rebalances positions to restore that ratio. In a steadily climbing market, this frequent rebalancing compounds gains nicely — the leverage amplifies each daily up-move. But in a market that rises 10%, then falls 5%, then rises 8%, the daily resets can work against you. The fund locks in losses on down days before recovering on up days, a mismatch that erodes value over time even if the underlying index ends higher than it started.

This is called **volatility decay**, and it affects all leveraged daily-reset funds. A simple example: suppose the underlying index is at 100, ONDL is at 100, and the index then swings up 20% to 120, then down 20% to 96. The underlying is now at 96. ONDL, reset daily, will have lost more than 20% twice — losing leverage on the way down each day — and will end well below 96. The higher the volatility, the more severe the decay.

## Who uses ONDL and when

ONDL is built for traders with a short-term bullish thesis on cloud stocks — investors expecting cloud software to rally over days or weeks, not years. Holding it for months through a range-bound market is almost guaranteed to destroy value. A buy-and-hold investor should never own this fund; the math of daily compounding ensures you will fall behind the 2X leveraged return you imagined over anything but the shortest periods.

Professional traders use [leveraged ETFs](/leveraged-etf/) tactically: buying them ahead of expected rallies and exiting quickly. Retail investors sometimes buy them without understanding the decay risk and hold them through a market correction, suffering outsized losses.

## Costs and liquidity

ONDL's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is typically 0.90–0.95%, substantially higher than the non-leveraged OND (around 0.70–0.75%). The extra cost reflects the fund's need to constantly rebalance and maintain its leverage. Trading volume is modest compared to mega-cap ETFs, and spreads can be wider, so slippage on entry and exit can add to costs.

## Risks beyond volatility decay

Leverage is unforgiving in crises. A 20% crash in the underlying index is a 40% crash in ONDL — enough to wipe out years of gains in a single week. The fund does not provide any downside protection; it is pure leverage with no hedge.

There is also [counterparty risk](/counterparty-risk/): the use of derivatives (futures, swaps, options) to achieve leverage means ONDL depends on the stability of clearing houses and derivatives markets. During periods of extreme stress, those mechanisms can seize or become prohibitively expensive.

## How to research ONDL

If you are considering this fund, start with Defiance's prospectus and understand precisely how the leverage is achieved — through futures, swaps, or options. Compare the fund's daily returns against double the underlying index return over a week or month; small discrepancies due to fees are normal, but large ones signal tracking error or hidden costs. Backtest the fund's behavior in periods of high volatility — look up returns during the 2020 COVID crash or the 2022 rate-hiking cycle — to see how decay has affected performance. Ask yourself honestly: are you holding this for weeks or months? If the answer is months or years, do not buy it. If weeks or days and you have a specific bullish thesis on cloud software, ONDL can be useful — but only with a clear exit plan before volatility or mean reversion erodes your position.
