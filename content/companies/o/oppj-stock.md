*The **WisdomTree Japan Opportunities Fund** (OPPJ) is an exchange-traded fund that invests in small-cap and mid-cap Japanese companies selected for earnings growth and improving cash flows. It targets the less-visible corner of Japan's equity market—not the multinational blue-chips like Toyota or Honda, but smaller, domestically focused businesses with real business momentum.*

> "Japan's smaller companies are where the growth is, and where valuations often miss the story." 

## What OPPJ actually holds and targets

OPPJ tracks an index of Japanese stocks outside the country's largest 100 or so companies. The fund screens for positive earnings growth and improving cash flows, excluding companies in financial distress or secular decline. This means the holdings skew toward smaller industrial manufacturers, regional banks, consumer discretionary businesses, and technology firms competing mainly in the Japanese market. The companies typically have [market capitalizations](/market-capitalization/) in the low-to-mid billions—large enough to have real earnings and analyst coverage, small enough to trade below the radar of most global investors.

WisdomTree, an index specialist and ETF sponsor, publishes the underlying index quarterly and rebalances OPPJ's holdings accordingly. The fund is rule-based rather than discretionary, following a published methodology so investors can understand exactly why a company is held or removed.

## Why focus on Japanese small-cap, and what makes the case?

Japan's largest companies—the names that appear in global [index funds](/index-fund/)—are largely mature, export-focused multinational corporations. They generate steady cash flows but often face slow growth. Deeper into Japan's equity market lies a different ecosystem: regional manufacturers, technology firms, family businesses, and regional lenders that serve local and domestic markets. Many have competitive advantages in their niches and are less saturated with global capital and analyst attention.

The Japanese market as a whole is older and slower-growing than the US equity market; that's a structural fact. But within Japan, smaller, more profitable companies sometimes trade at discounts that reflect this overall-market skepticism rather than their own fundamentals. OPPJ's growth filter attempts to identify companies where that disconnect exists.

## The currency factor and cost structure

OPPJ is denominated in [US dollars](/us-dollar/) and trades on NYSE Arca, so investors buying it absorb currency exposure—the fund's returns depend not only on how Japanese stocks perform in yen but also on the yen-to-dollar exchange rate. A stronger yen boosts OPPJ's returns in dollar terms; a weaker yen drags on them. For a US investor wanting pure exposure to Japanese equity valuations and earnings, that currency component is a feature; for one trying to hedge [currency risk](/currency-risk/) separately, it complicates the picture.

WisdomTree charges a modest [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/), typical for an index-tracking [equity ETF](/equity-etf/). The fund's liquidity is solid; OPPJ trades with reasonable spreads and steady volume, making entry and exit efficient for most investors.

## Risks and concentration

Small-cap and mid-cap stocks are more volatile than the largest, most liquid names. OPPJ's volatility tends to exceed that of Japan's megacap index. The fund's rule-based selection—favoring profitable, growing companies—screens out deeply troubled firms but does not prevent individual holdings from stumbling. Earnings can disappoint; growth can stall. The Japanese economy's structural challenges (aging population, modest [nominal GDP](/nominal-gdp/) growth, competition from China and Asia) are a headwind for the economy overall, though not insurmountable for individual companies.

OPPJ's concentration differs from a cap-weighted Japan index: because it filters for growth and earnings, it may overweight sectors where those factors cluster (technology, some industrials) and underweight sectors that are profitable but stagnant.

## How to research and monitor OPPJ

Start with WisdomTree's fact sheet and index documentation, which list the fund's holdings, sector weightings, and the exact methodology for selecting and rebalancing companies. The prospectus details the fund's strategy and risks. To understand the case for Japanese small-cap exposure, research Japan's economic outlook, currency trends, and how Japanese corporate earnings have evolved relative to the US market.

Monitor OPPJ's annual and quarterly performance relative to a benchmark like Japan's broader Topix index; if OPPJ significantly lags or leads its benchmark, ask whether the growth/earnings filter is working as intended. Watch the fund's holdings for material changes after each rebalance, since they reflect shifts in what the index methodology considers profitable and growing. Currency movements matter as much as underlying stock performance, so tracking the yen's direction adds useful context.
