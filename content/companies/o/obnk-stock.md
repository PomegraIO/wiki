The Osprey BONK Trust is a Delaware trust that buys [Bitcoin](/bitcoin/) and holds it for investors who trade shares of the trust on public markets. Think of it this way: Osprey manages a pile of real Bitcoin in secure storage. They let people buy pieces of that pile by trading OBNK shares. It trades on OTC Markets under the symbol OBNK. The trust is structured so that if you own a share, you own a fractional piece of the actual Bitcoin sitting in custody.

## What it actually does

The Osprey BONK Trust holds Bitcoin. That's the core function. An investor sends money in, the trust buys Bitcoin, stores it securely with a professional custody provider, and issues shares that trade publicly. Each share represents a claim on a piece of the Bitcoin. If Bitcoin goes up in price, the shares go up. If Bitcoin goes down, the shares go down.

The trust structure means you do not own the Bitcoin directly—you own the trust that owns the Bitcoin. This matters for taxes and for practical reasons. You do not have to deal with a private key, a [cryptocurrency exchange](/cryptocurrency-exchange/), or self-custody headaches. The trust handles all that. Osprey Funds, the operator, manages the custody, the buying and selling, and the administrative work of running a public trust.

## The difference from owning Bitcoin directly

If you buy Bitcoin on a cryptocurrency exchange, you control the private key and own it outright. You can move it, send it, or spend it. You are also responsible for keeping the key safe, using the exchange's security, and filing the correct tax forms.

With OBNK, you own a tradable share. You can buy it and sell it like any other stock, through a regular brokerage account. You do not manage keys or worry about exchange hacks. But you rely on Osprey's custody provider, and you pay a small annual fee to the trust for custody and management. The tradeoff is simplicity and liquidity versus control and slightly lower costs.

## Who can invest and why

The trust is open to [accredited investors](/accredited-investor/)—people or institutions that meet income and net-worth thresholds set by the [Securities and Exchange Commission](/securities-and-exchange-commission/). Accredited status exists because regulators consider these investors sophisticated enough to evaluate complex or novel investments without as much protection as the general public gets.

The minimum investment is ten thousand dollars. That is higher than buying Bitcoin on a retail exchange, but it reflects the institutional nature of the trust and the custody costs involved. Institutional investors—pension funds, [hedge funds](/hedge-fund/), foundations, and wealthy individuals—use structures like this for a few reasons. They want a liquid, auditable way to hold Bitcoin without managing their own security. They want to hold crypto in a regulated trust that their auditors understand. They want to trade quickly without the friction of moving Bitcoin off an exchange.

## The grantor trust structure and tax implications

A grantor trust is a specific legal structure where the person who created the trust is treated as the owner for tax purposes, even though legally the trust owns the asset. For OBNK, shareholders own the trust indirectly and have tax obligations based on their proportional ownership of the underlying Bitcoin.

This structure was chosen for specific tax and regulatory reasons: it keeps the trust simple, avoids [double taxation](/double-taxation/), and clearly establishes ownership stakes. Shareholders get a K-1 form (or equivalent) showing their share of any gains or losses on the Bitcoin held, and they owe taxes on those gains the same way they would if they owned Bitcoin directly. The trust itself does not pay taxes on the gain—the shareholders do.

## Custody and operational risk

Osprey does not hold the Bitcoin itself; it contracts with professional [custodians](/custodian/) that specialize in storing digital assets for institutions. These custodians use offline storage, insurance, and multi-signature security to reduce the risk of theft or loss. This is different from holding Bitcoin on a public exchange (where the exchange holds it and has its own security problems).

The [operational risk](/operational-risk/) in a trust like this is limited. Osprey cannot arbitrarily change the rules, skim assets, or take the Bitcoin. The trust is a legal entity with disclosure obligations. The Bitcoin sits with a regulated custodian. If Osprey Funds shut down or defaulted, the Bitcoin would remain with the custodian and could be distributed to shareholders. That said, shareholders are ultimately relying on the competence and security posture of both Osprey and its custody provider.

## How shares price and what moves them

The shares trade at prices set by supply and demand on OTC Markets. Theoretically, the price of one share should track the Bitcoin price divided by the share count. But real-world trading is messier. If there are few buyers and sellers on any given day, the [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) can be wide, and a large trade might move the price. Shares sometimes trade at a small premium or discount to the underlying Bitcoin value, depending on how liquid the market is and how popular the trust is at any moment.

OBNK shares follow Bitcoin's price movements, which are volatile. Bitcoin can swing twenty or thirty percent in days based on news, regulation, market sentiment, and macroeconomic conditions. Anyone investing in OBNK is betting on Bitcoin price appreciation and willing to live with that volatility.

## Alternatives and why people choose this

There are other ways to own Bitcoin publicly. Spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds, which became available in the United States starting in 2024, offer similar exposure with potentially lower fees and deeper liquidity because they trade on major exchanges instead of OTC Markets. There are also Bitcoin futures, Bitcoin options, and other derivative products that provide exposure without owning Bitcoin directly.

OBNK's advantage is simplicity, institutional-grade custody, and a trust structure that some investors and fiduciaries prefer for legal reasons. The main disadvantage is liquidity and price transparency—OTC Markets trading is less transparent and active than major exchange trading. The annual custody and [management fee](/management-fee/) is also a real cost that reduces the net return compared to Bitcoin itself.

## How to research Osprey BONK Trust

Start with the trust's SEC filings, particularly the annual N-CSR report or equivalent documentation that describes the holdings, fees, and any changes to the custody arrangement. The SEC CIK 0002042444 identifies the entity in the SEC database. The filing will detail the Bitcoin holdings, the custodian used, and any risks flagged by management.

Track the Bitcoin price independently (through major exchanges or price aggregators) and compare it to the trading price of OBNK to see whether the trust is trading at a significant premium or discount. That gap is a sign of either opportunity or illiquidity. Check the bid-ask spread regularly to understand how liquid the shares are. As with any leveraged or volatile instrument, OBNK is suitable for investors with a high risk tolerance and a long time horizon.
