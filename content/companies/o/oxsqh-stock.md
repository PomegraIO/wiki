Oxford Square Capital operates as a closed-end investment company focused on deploying capital into middle-market secured loans and debt instruments. The company's core purpose is to generate regular investment income and potential capital appreciation by lending to and investing in business enterprises that typically have annual revenues between fifty million and several hundred million dollars. The investment thesis rests on the proposition that middle-market borrowers — companies too large for banks' typical relationship managers to handle closely, yet too small to access capital markets directly — will pay a meaningful premium to access flexible, responsive capital from non-traditional sources.

## Secured middle-market lending

The foundation of Oxford Square's business is the origination and management of secured loans to middle-market companies. These loans typically have collateral backing them — whether equipment, inventory, real estate, or other business assets — which means the investor has a claim on specific property if the borrower defaults. Secured debt is lower-risk than unsecured debt from a creditor's perspective, though it requires careful underwriting, appraisal of the collateral, and ongoing monitoring as business conditions change.

Oxford Square evaluates prospective borrowers based on their cash flows, management team, industry, competitive position, and the quality of the proposed security. The loans are structured with various terms — some shorter-dated and floating-rate, others longer-dated and fixed — to match the risk profile of the borrower and the return requirements of the fund. Because middle-market companies often face higher borrowing costs than blue-chip corporations, they are willing to pay higher [interest rates](/interest-rate/) to access capital, which means the yields on these loans can be attractive relative to more-commoditized lending.

The company may also invest in subordinated debt, mezzanine securities, and equity stakes in portfolio companies, though the bulk of capital is deployed into senior secured loans. Each investment is sized to fit within the overall portfolio risk envelope, and the company maintains [diversification](/diversification/) across industries, borrower sizes, and collateral types.

## Capital structure and how the company generates returns

Oxford Square is organized as a [closed-end fund](/closed-end-fund/), which means it has a fixed amount of capital and issues shares that trade on public exchanges — in this case [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/). Shareholders buy and hold the shares; the company does not continuously issue and redeem shares like a [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/). The price of the shares reflects both the underlying [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) of the portfolio and investor sentiment at any given time — closed-end funds often trade at a discount to or premium to their net asset value depending on demand.

The company generates returns through two primary channels. The first is current income from interest payments on the loans and debt instruments in the portfolio. Middle-market secured debt typically carries floating rates tied to benchmarks like [SOFR](/sofr/) or LIBOR-plus fixed spreads, which means interest income rises and falls with broader interest rates. This regular interest income is the primary source of cash the company uses to pay [dividends](/dividend/) to shareholders.

The second return stream is capital appreciation. As portfolio companies mature, improve their operations, or achieve stronger valuations, the value of the debt instruments may increase — either because the credit quality improves and the spreads narrow, or because the underlying collateral becomes more valuable. Additionally, some investments include equity kickers or warrants that could appreciate if a portfolio company exits or refinances at a higher valuation.

## Portfolio composition and diversification

Oxford Square typically maintains a diversified portfolio across dozens of underlying investments spread across various industries — healthcare, business services, technology, light manufacturing, and others. The company maintains investment criteria around company size, [leverage ratios](/leverage-ratio-forex/), and industry preferences, though these may shift based on market conditions and available opportunities.

The portfolio's composition changes over time as new loans are originated, existing loans are repaid or refinanced, and the company exits positions. The [annual report](/10-k/) and quarterly documents provide detailed information on the largest holdings, the geographic distribution, the industry breakdown, and the concentration of the portfolio — all of which affect the overall risk profile of the fund.

## Market environment and interest rate sensitivity

The economics of middle-market lending are heavily influenced by interest rates and credit market conditions. In low-rate environments, borrowing costs decline, competition for deals increases, and lenders must make larger loans or accept lower spreads to maintain yield. In high-rate environments, borrowing costs rise, borrowers face pressure, but lenders can capture wider spreads on new originations. The existing portfolio's value is sensitive to changes in interest rates as well — when rates rise, the value of fixed-rate loans in the portfolio may decline because investors require higher yields to compensate for the opportunity cost.

[Credit cycles](/credit-cycle/) also affect the business. In economic expansions, companies refinance smoothly, defaults are low, and new opportunities emerge. In downturns, companies struggle with leverage, defaults rise, and recoveries in bankruptcy become critical to returns. Oxford Square's performance over any multi-year period reflects both the starting interest rate environment and the credit cycle through which it lived.

## How to research Oxford Square Capital

Investors interested in closed-end funds focused on middle-market lending should begin with the company's most recent annual and quarterly reports, available through the SEC (CIK 0001259429). Key metrics to understand include the portfolio composition by industry and collateral type, the weighted-average yield and spread of the loan portfolio, the [default rate](/default-rate/) and recovery rate on problem credits, the net asset value per share, the share price relative to net asset value (the discount or premium), and the [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/).

The company also publishes periodic investor presentations and webcasts discussing portfolio performance and market outlook. Because the fund is not a operating business but an investment vehicle, understanding Oxford Square requires careful attention to the underlying portfolio quality, the investment process, and the current macroeconomic environment. A reader should weigh the company's loan origination discipline against the broader credit cycle and consider how interest-rate changes would affect both the income generation and the valuation of existing holdings.
