Leverage Shares 2X Long OSCR Daily ETF (NASDAQ: OSCG) is a single-stock leveraged exchange-traded fund engineered to track twice the daily percentage change of Oscar Health Inc. (OSCR). It uses financial derivatives and daily rebalancing to amplify returns on a day-to-day basis. The structure is transparent and mechanical: if Oscar rises 1% in a day, OSCG aims to rise 2%; if Oscar falls 1%, OSCG falls 2%. But this daily reset mechanism carries a profound hidden cost. Hold the fund for weeks or months and you discover that compounding losses from volatility decay—the drag that accumulates when prices move up and down—almost guarantee underperformance relative to the stated 2x multiplier, and can produce losses even if the underlying stock is flat. OSCG is a tool for tactical traders, not buy-and-hold investors.

## The daily-reset mechanism and volatility decay

The fund rebalances its portfolio each trading day at the close to maintain its 2x exposure ratio. On day one, if Oscar moves +1%, OSCG moves +2%. At the close, the fund's derivatives and cash are reset so that the next day, the fund is again positioned to deliver exactly 2x the daily return. The next day, the process repeats.

This daily rebalancing differs fundamentally from static leverage. A mutual fund could borrow once and hold twice as many Oscar shares indefinitely, achieving a constant 2x exposure. But OSCG resets daily, which produces an asymmetric effect over time. If Oscar rises 1% on Monday and falls 1% on Tuesday, the underlying stock ends flat (up 1% then down 1% on that base). But OSCG rises 2% on Monday and falls 2% on Tuesday, leaving it down in total despite the underlying returning to its starting price. This mathematical property—called volatility decay or convexity drag—is the price paid for daily reset leverage.

The longer the holding period and the more volatile the underlying stock, the worse volatility decay becomes. A stock that whipsaws will erode OSCG's returns steadily.

## Tactical trading versus long-term holding

OSCG is designed for traders who expect Oscar to move in one direction in the next few hours or the next day. If you think Oscar will jump on earnings and you want to amplify that bet, OSCG delivers 2x the upside (and 2x the downside if you are wrong). The leverage magnifies both profits and losses within that single-day or multi-day window.

But buy OSCG and hold it for a month in a stable, slightly choppy market, and volatility decay will have eroded your return even if your thesis about Oscar's direction was correct. The fund is not built for that use. Nearly every financial professional warns that leveraged daily-reset ETFs are tactical instruments—useful for traders but toxic for buy-and-hold investors. The prospectus itself states this clearly, and the fund's risk factor section does not mince words about the dangers.

Oscar Health is a health insurance company, cyclical and sensitive to medical cost inflation, regulatory changes, and competition from larger rivals. A 2x leveraged fund amplifies that cyclicality. If Oscar declines 20% in a sustained downturn, OSCG falls 40% on the first day, then faces further decay if the decline persists. The risks compound: bid-ask spreads, the gap between net asset value and trading price, and forced liquidations during sharp moves add layers of loss beyond the leverage alone.

## Costs and tracking mechanics

The expense ratio is modest, measured in basis points. The real costs are elsewhere: the bid-ask spread when trading, any gap between net asset value and market price, and most importantly, the accumulating drag from volatility decay. A flat market for a month can silently erode returns; the lost value does not show up in the stated fee but is very real to the shareholder.

Tracking error is inherent to the structure. The fund aims to deliver 2x the daily return, but hedging costs, borrowing rates, settlement delays, and the fund manager's operational overhead mean actual returns rarely align exactly with the 2x target. Over multiple days, these small misalignments compound into meaningful divergence.

## How to research and trade OSCG

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, available through Leverage Shares and major brokers. The prospectus details the daily reset mechanism, the costs, and the risks; read the sections on leverage and volatility decay carefully. Compare the fund's actual returns against Oscar Health stock over daily, weekly, and monthly periods—the widening gap will illustrate volatility decay in action.

Understand Oscar Health's business fundamentals by reading its SEC filings and earnings reports; a healthcare insurer that declines 10% in a bad quarter becomes a 20% decline in OSCG. Trade OSCG only when you expect meaningful single-day or multi-day movement in Oscar shares; avoid it when the underlying stock is illiquid or halted.

And remember: leveraged daily-reset ETFs are tradeable instruments, not investments. Buy them for tactical exposure, take profits or losses quickly, and exit before volatility decay erodes the position.
