*__Invesco Mortgage Capital Inc.__ (ticker **IVR**) is a mortgage real estate investment trust that holds agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS), non-agency MBS, and other credit-sensitive fixed-income assets. The company's filings reveal a business built on the spread between the cost of funding mortgages and the yield earned on pools of residential loans, meaning its returns depend directly on the shape of the interest-rate curve, the pace of mortgage prepayments, and the stability of its leverage.*

## How the Business Funds Itself and What It Earns

Invesco Mortgage Capital's filings emphasize one fundamental dynamic: the firm borrows money at one rate (via repo and other secured borrowing) and invests in mortgages yielding a higher rate. The gap, or net interest margin, is what the shareholders keep. Most of the portfolio consists of agency mortgage-backed securities—loans guaranteed by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac—meaning credit risk sits with the government, not the company. This focus lowers default risk but concentrates the portfolio's sensitivity to interest-rate movements and prepayment speeds. When mortgage rates fall, homeowners refinance, and the securities in IVR's portfolio prepay faster, forcing the firm to reinvest at lower yields. When rates rise, prepayments slow, and IVR's existing holdings stay locked at outdated [coupon rates](/coupon-rate/) while the cost of new funding may climb. The regulatory disclosures detail how the firm manages this duration mismatch through hedging instruments like interest-rate swaps and Treasury futures.

## Capital Structure and Leverage

As a mortgage REIT, IVR is required to distribute at least 90 percent of taxable income to shareholders each year, which shapes how the company funds growth. Rather than [retained earnings](/retained-earnings/), the firm raises capital through new stock issuances and most critically through borrowed funds. Its 10-K filings disclose the extent of leverage—typically three to four dollars of debt for every dollar of equity—used to amplify returns on a thin margin. The leverage itself carries risk: in a [liquidity crisis](/liquidity-crisis/) or a sharp move in overnight funding costs, the firm faces pressure to sell assets quickly, potentially crystallizing losses. IVR's disclosures on its [repurchase agreements](/repurchase-agreement/) (repo) show it refinances this debt constantly, rolling over short-term borrowings that may be recalled if market conditions sour or if lenders lose confidence in mortgage collateral.

## Sector Positioning and Macroeconomic Exposure

The mortgage REIT sector as a whole is subordinate to the [Federal Reserve](/federal-reserve/)'s policy stance and the overall trajectory of long-term [interest rates](/interest-rate/). Invesco Mortgage Capital's own filings acknowledge that periods of rising rates compress net interest margins and erode the market value of held securities—a dynamic made clear during tightening cycles. Conversely, falling or stable rates favor the spread business and boost the appeal of fixed income relative to equities. The company also faces secular headwinds: as Americans hold mortgages longer (and refinance less often) and as the Fed's [balance sheet](/balance-sheet/) shrinks, the pool of MBS available for purchase can become scarce, squeezing margins across the entire mortgage REIT industry. IVR's filings note concentration in agency MBS as both a strength (credit risk absorbed by government) and a constraint (yields are lower, leaving less room for error in funding costs).

## Risk Exposures Emphasized in Disclosures

The firm's risk factors are detailed prominently: [prepayment risk](/prepayment-risk/) (rates fall, reinvestment at lower yields); [extension risk](/extension-risk/) (rates rise, duration extends and market value falls); funding risk (borrowing costs rise suddenly or sources dry up); and basis risk (hedges do not perfectly offset underlying exposures). IVR also notes [operational risks](/operational-risk/) tied to the mortgage servicing ecosystem—delays in loan payoff, delinquencies, and servicer advances that can disrupt cash flows. The 10-K includes [sensitivity analysis](/sensitivity-analysis-valuation/) showing how changes in mortgage rates impact book value and net interest income, quantifying losses in scenarios where long-term rates spike or spreads widen.

## Scale and Competitive Position

As a manager of mortgage portfolios, Invesco Mortgage Capital competes with other [mortgage REITs](/mortgage-reit/), insurance companies, pension funds, and the Fed's own holdings for available MBS supply. Filings reveal that the company is mid-sized in the mortgage REIT universe—smaller than giants like Annaly or New York Mortgage Trust, but large enough to access wholesale funding markets and to employ sophisticated hedging. The Invesco brand affiliation (the firm is ultimately owned by and operates under the investment-management umbrella of Invesco Ltd.) provides stability in funding access and operational infrastructure, though IVR operates as a separate public company with its own board and management.

## What Matters When Studying This Company

Readers analyzing IVR should prioritize the quarterly 10-Q filings, which update portfolio composition, weighted-average coupon, duration, and leverage metrics each quarter. The annual 10-K provides deeper context on the firm's funding strategy and risk management framework. Key metrics to track are net interest margin (the spread between asset yields and funding costs), book value per share (which fluctuates with mark-to-market losses and gains), the loan-to-value ratio on repo borrowings, and prepayment speeds on the underlying mortgage pools. IVR's dividend, typically paid monthly, must be scrutinized for sustainability—whether it is backed by actual net interest income or whether the firm is slowly eroding capital by paying out returns it has not earned.
