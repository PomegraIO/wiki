IWDL is a UBS [structured product](/structured-product/) targeting investors who believe value—cheap, dividend-paying large-cap stocks—will outperform growth. It applies 2x leverage via daily rebalancing, meaning the product resets each day to maintain exactly twice the exposure of the underlying index. It is not a fund; it is an unsecured promissory note, so [credit risk](/credit-risk/) sits with the issuer.

## The mechanics: What is a 2x leveraged factor play?

The underlying index selects large-cap US stocks on a value tilt: lower [price-to-book ratios](/price-to-book-ratio/), lower [price-to-earnings ratios](/price-to-earnings-ratio/), higher [dividend yields](/dividend-yield/) than the broader market. IWDL holds a synthetic exposure to that index and levers it 2x through daily rebalancing. The total-return methodology reinvests all [dividends](/dividend/) into the position, so the portfolio compounds.

Daily rebalancing means selling when the index rises above target leverage (locking in gains at elevated levels) and buying when it falls below target (locking in losses at depressed levels). Over months, this friction costs performance. In sideways markets where the value index bounces but makes no net progress, rebalancing drag can be severe.

## The value cycle and IWDL's behaviour

Value stocks have wild cycles. In rational environments—when earnings are stable, [inflation](/inflation/) is mild, and [discount rates](/discount-rate/) are steady—cheap stocks deliver higher returns than pricey growth stocks. This period favors IWDL: the leverage amplifies the value outperformance.

In the opposite state—when growth dominates, investors chase technology and momentum, and market valuations are detached from traditional earnings—value stocks stagnate or decline. The leverage then amplifies the underperformance. The past 15 years have been brutal for pure value plays. The 2009–2019 period saw growth stocks dramatically outpace value, and leverage made that worse for IWDL holders.

Reversal periods can be sharp. In late 2022 and early 2023, value rebounded as [central banks](/central-bank/) raised rates and growth stocks sold off. IWDL holders who had held through the drought captured amplified gains on the rebound. That rebound also benefited from reduced rebalancing drag: in a strongly rising market, the daily reset is a smaller headwind.

## The compound cost of leverage

A 2x ETN is not a simple 2x daily return. In a rising market, it approximates 2x the index return minus friction. In a [flat market](/flat-market/), the daily rebalancing drag compounds against you—the product can lose 5–15 percent annually even if the underlying index moves nowhere. In a declining market, it amplifies losses even faster.

Volatility is the silent killer. The higher the swings in the underlying index, the more expensive daily rebalancing becomes. Value stocks are not as volatile as small-cap growth stocks, but they are volatile enough. An investor expecting steady returns on 2x value exposure will be disappointed.

## The factor and the cycle

[Value investing](/value-investing/) is itself cyclical. Every few years, value stocks enjoy a narrative—"the next mean reversion"—and investors rotate in. Every few years they fall out of favor and capital rotates to growth, quality, or momentum. IWDL's returns swing on that cycle, amplified by the 2x lever.

The underlying index composition also matters. Which stocks define "value"? Companies with low multiples and high yields. These often include financials, energy, and mature industrial stocks. In rising-rate environments, these can be attractive. In falling-rate environments, they can underperform. An investor choosing IWDL is not just betting on value; they are betting on the sector composition of the value universe and the interest-rate regime.

## The issuer and the note structure

IWDL is an unsecured note backed by UBS. If the bank fails, holders face counterparty loss. UBS's [credit rating](/credit-rating/) has recovered since 2008, but the structural risk remains. Additionally, the daily rebalancing cost is built into the product's returns; it is not a transparent separate fee.

## How to use IWDL

This product is a tactical position for investors who believe value is underpriced relative to growth and want amplified exposure to that bet. It is not suitable for buy-and-hold investors, especially in low-volatility periods. The cost of daily rebalancing erodes long-term returns, particularly in sideways markets.

An investor considering IWDL should monitor the underlying value index and the performance gap between value and growth. When value is significantly cheaper than growth by traditional metrics—and when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) or [inflation expectations](/inflation-expectations/) suggest a regime shift in value's favor—IWDL can outperform. In other environments, the leverage amplifies value's underperformance.

Holdings and turnover matter. Read the prospectus and the latest fact sheet from UBS to understand which sectors and companies comprise the index. Watch how the [leverage ratio](/leverage-ratio-forex/) drifts during volatile weeks—large swings force larger rebalancing trades.
