## What defines the AI Revolution lens?

The Wedbush AI Revolution ETF takes a broader thematic approach than its infrastructure-focused sibling. Rather than focusing narrowly on the power-and-semiconductors theme, IVES captures companies that are central to the development and deployment of artificial intelligence across the economy. That includes AI software vendors, cloud-infrastructure providers that host AI workloads, semiconductor designers, data-service companies that train and feed models, and enterprise software vendors integrating AI into their core products.

The fund's philosophy reflects the view that artificial intelligence is not a single industry or even a single technological wave, but a broad re-platforming that touches most sectors and compels companies to make strategic choices about how to compete in an AI-enabled world. The winners are those building the models, the tools that run them, and the software and services that help companies adopt them.

## The holdings and selection

IVES typically holds 40 to 80 companies globally, with a tilt toward the United States where much of the highest-profile AI development is concentrated. The fund captures the obvious large-cap names (the training-data and model giants, the major chip designers) and extends into mid-cap companies and smaller specialists that have positioned themselves as essential plumbing in the AI ecosystem.

The fund's universe is broader than IVEP's, which means it captures not only infrastructure but also software vendors, services, and companies whose business models involve licensing, fine-tuning, or deploying AI capabilities. A cloud-infrastructure provider is in the fund. An enterprise database company that has integrated AI into its platform is in the fund. A startup or smaller specialist in AI reasoning or simulation might be included. The result is that IVES captures the theme at multiple levels of the stack, giving it more [diversification](/diversification/) than a purely infrastructure-focused approach but also more vulnerability to the definition of what counts as "AI-exposed" broadening or narrowing with sentiment.

## Concentration and sector exposure

Like IVEP, IVES is a concentrated fund — it holds far fewer companies than a broad index, and it is tilted heavily toward technology and communications sectors where AI adoption and development are most visible. That concentration gives the fund focus and the ability to track the AI thesis, but it also means performance can diverge sharply from the broader market. In periods when tech stocks are out of favor, or when sentiment around AI shifts from euphoria to skepticism, the fund can experience sharp drawdowns.

The fund is also tilted toward US-listed companies, reflecting where much of the large-scale AI development and venture capital has accumulated, though it does include some exposure to AI-related companies in other markets.

## Thematic concentration and narrative risk

IVES is designed to track a narrative — the story of AI's integration into the economy — rather than a mechanical index. That narrative can shift as the technology evolves, as regulatory scrutiny changes, and as investor sentiment swings. If AI capabilities plateau temporarily, or if a major disappointment surfaces (safety concerns, regulatory crackdowns, underwhelming results from expensive deployments), the entire thematic frame can deflate, and the fund's price can fall regardless of the underlying companies' [intrinsic value](/intrinsic-value/).

Conversely, the fund can outperform if the narrative accelerates — if visible, high-value applications of AI emerge faster than the market expected. A thematic fund is fundamentally a bet on the continued importance and visibility of the theme; when that bet is right, returns can be strong, but when sentiment cools, the punishment is often severe.

## Who holds it and how to evaluate it

IVES is a concentrated, theme-based holding, not a core equity position. It appeals to investors who are convinced AI will be among the most important drivers of economic growth and shareholder returns over the next several years and who are willing to take concentrated exposure to that thesis.

To evaluate the fund, review the prospectus to understand the exact inclusion criteria and the current top holdings. Compare its performance against tech-heavy indices (the Nasdaq-100, the tech sector of the S&P 500) to see whether the AI focus is adding value or simply moving the fund around on a trajectory already set by the broader tech sector. Track how the fund's holdings and exposure change as new companies go public or as existing holdings' relationships to AI shift.

Also consider the fund's sensitivity to [interest rates](/interest-rate/) and sentiment cycles. Concentrated, growth-themed funds tend to struggle when investors rotate toward value or when the macro backdrop shifts toward higher rates or [inflation](/inflation/). The fund is best suited for investors with higher risk tolerance and the discipline to rebalance rather than panic when drawdowns arrive.

## The broader context

The AI Revolution narrative is compelling, but it is also already widely acknowledged in markets. Most large technology companies have substantial valuations priced in around their AI prospects. A concentrated bet on the AI theme works if AI deployment accelerates even faster than the market expects, or if a new generation of winners emerges from smaller or less-obvious companies. It underperforms if sentiment on AI moderates or if execution disappoints relative to the hype. Understanding that dynamic — and your own conviction about AI's trajectory — is essential before committing capital to a concentrated thematic fund.
