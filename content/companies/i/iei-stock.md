The iShares 3-7 Year [Treasury Bond](/treasury-bond/) ETF (ticker: IEI) holds a basket of US Treasury [bonds](/bond/) that mature between three and seven years from today. The fund is one of the most liquid and widely held Treasury ETFs, favored by conservative investors seeking safe, interest-bearing assets without the friction of buying individual bonds directly. It occupies the middle ground of the Treasury curve—not as yield-starved as short-term instruments, yet not as volatile as bonds maturing decades out.

## What IEI tracks and why this maturity slice

IEI replicates the ICE US Treasury 3-7 Year Bond Index, which consists of federal government debt notes maturing in that three-to-seven-year window. The fund holds dozens of individual Treasury securities, each backed by the full faith and credit of the US government. As bonds mature or the index rebalances, the fund adjusts its holdings to maintain the window. The economics are straightforward: hold Treasury bonds, collect the coupons, pass the income through to shareholders, manage turnover, and keep costs vanishingly low.

The 3-7 year band is neither accidental nor arbitrary. Treasuries maturing in under three years move only slightly when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) change, so they offer little price upside or downside—a tradeoff some accept for stability. Those maturing far in the future (20, 30 years) are highly sensitive to rate movements, swinging sharply when the [Federal Reserve](/federal-reserve/) adjusts policy. The 3-7 slice sits in the working middle: it provides meaningfully higher yield than short bonds, captures the essence of [interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/), and avoids the extreme swings of the long end.

## How duration and rate moves work in IEI

Duration measures how sensitive a bond's price is to shifts in interest rates. A duration of four years means a one-percentage-point rise in yields corresponds roughly to a four-percent decline in the bond's market price. IEI's portfolio typically has a duration in the four-to-five-year range, placing it right in the sweet spot of the Treasury market. When rates fall, the fund's price rises; when rates climb, it falls.

But here is the critical nuance: for an investor holding IEI to maturity—until all underlying bonds mature—duration is irrelevant. What matters is the coupon income received along the way; the final principal is guaranteed. Only traders who sell before maturity care about market price swings. This distinction between mark-to-market volatility (cosmetic for long-term holders) and actual loss (the cost of early exit) shapes how different investors view the fund.

## Costs, liquidity, and structure

IEI's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is low, typically well under 0.10% annually, reflecting both the simplicity of tracking a Treasury index and the scale of BlackRock's iShares platform. The fund trades on [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) with deep volume and tight [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/), making it cheaper to buy or sell than dealing in individual bonds. Monthly distributions give shareholders their share of coupons earned, which can be reinvested or withdrawn.

The fund's implementation is mechanical: it holds physical Treasuries, not derivatives or futures. This transparency makes IEI straightforward—no [counterparty risk](/counterparty-risk/), no leverage, no exotic structures. What you see is what you get.

## Who IEI is for and how to research it

IEI suits conservative investors, retirees, and institutions needing fixed-income ballast. It is particularly useful for anyone who would otherwise construct a Treasury ladder (buying individual bonds maturing at intervals) but prefers the simplicity and liquidity of an ETF.

To research IEI, start with the fund's factsheet and prospectus from iShares. These show the current duration, weighted average maturity, and yield distribution—all evergreen guides to what the fund owns. Watch the yield of newly issued 5-year [Treasury notes](/treasury-note/), which set the rate floor for IEI's holdings. Monitor Federal Reserve policy statements and economic data, since the fund's price swings track interest-rate expectations. And consult the Treasury Department's [yield curve](/yield-curve/) data to understand where the 3-7 band sits relative to the rest of the market. These inputs reveal whether IEI is rich or cheap relative to the raw underlying bonds and what returns to expect in different rate scenarios.
