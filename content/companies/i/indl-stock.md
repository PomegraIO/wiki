Direxion's Daily MSCI India Bull 2X ETF (ticker: INDL) is a leveraged exchange-traded fund that amplifies movements in the MSCI India index by a factor of two each trading day. Like all [leveraged ETFs](/leveraged-etf/), INDL is a tactical instrument designed for traders making short-term bets on upward price movement — not a buy-and-hold fund for long-term investors. Understanding what INDL does, and crucially what it does *not* do, is essential to avoiding the wealth-eroding pitfalls that catch many who buy leveraged products without grasping their mechanics.

## What INDL tracks and how it works

INDL follows the MSCI India index, a cap-weighted measure of roughly 700 Indian equities spanning industrials, information technology, pharmaceuticals, and financials. On any given day that the Indian markets move up, INDL is engineered to move up by two times as much. If the index gains 1 percent on a Tuesday, INDL should gain roughly 2 percent. If the index falls 1 percent, INDL falls roughly 2 percent. This 2x leverage is accomplished through the fund's use of derivatives — chiefly equity index futures and swaps — that give it the economic exposure to twice the index without the fund itself needing to own all those shares directly.

The fund is rebalanced every single day at the close to maintain that 2x [leverage ratio](/leverage-ratio-forex/). This daily rebalancing is the source of both INDL's appeal and its critical flaw. On days the market surges, the leverage magnifies gains and pulls new money into the position (the fund buys more exposure to stay at 2x). On days the market falls, the leverage magnifies losses and forces the fund to sell exposure to bring itself back to the 2x ratio. Over short horizons — days or a few weeks — this daily reset can work in a trader's favor if the market moves decisively in one direction. Over longer periods, the mechanics destroy value.

## The volatility decay trap

The most important thing to know about INDL is that it will almost certainly underperform 2x the return of the underlying index over periods longer than a few weeks. This is not a flaw in the fund; it is a mathematical inevitability called volatility decay or daily decay. Here is why: suppose the index falls 10 percent in week one, losing a dollar of value, and then rises 11 percent in week two, nearly recovering to its starting price (down 0.1 percent total). INDL, meanwhile, falls 20 percent in week one and rises 22 percent in week two. But 22 percent gains on a portfolio that is now 20 percent smaller leaves it significantly worse off than it started — perhaps down 5 percent. The index barely moved; INDL is down a lot. Over months or years, with the kind of normal market volatility investors encounter, this drag becomes a steady, compounding drag on returns.

This decay accelerates when volatility is high — precisely when emerging markets like India can be most unpredictable. Currency fluctuations, regulatory changes, monsoon-linked agricultural shocks, geopolitical tensions, and India's own policy shifts can drive sharp, unexpected swings that trigger repeated buy-and-sell cycles inside INDL, each one compounding the drag. Investors who bought leveraged India ETFs during calm periods and held through periods of high turbulence have watched their positions evaporate faster than the underlying index alone would have fallen.

## The investor base and use case

INDL is bought almost exclusively by active traders and tactical specialists, not by long-term savers. A trader who believes India's equities will rally over the next two or three days might use INDL to capture that bet with less capital at risk than buying the index itself — or with the same capital at risk, earning a larger expected payoff. A [hedge fund](/hedge-fund/) might use INDL to hedge a short position in Indian equities or to quickly scale exposure without waiting for large share purchases to settle. These are legitimate uses for people who understand the mechanics and watch their positions daily.

The fund is not appropriate for anyone with a multi-year time horizon, and it is actively harmful for retirees or savers who buy it thinking it will deliver 2x the index's returns over a decade. Those people will almost certainly end up with negative returns even if the index itself doubled. Too many retail investors have learned this lesson the hard way.

## Size, liquidity, and structure

INDL's assets under management have ebbed and flowed with appetite for leverage and for India exposure. Direxion manages a whole family of leveraged sector and country ETFs, and INDL is a smaller member of that stable. Shares trade on the NYSE, typically with reasonable [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) during market hours, though volume can be thinner than broader index trackers. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is higher than a simple [index fund](/index-fund/) — typically in the 0.8 to 1.0 percent range — a necessary cost of funding the leverage and managing the derivatives positions.

The fund's structure as an ETF means it trades continuously on the exchange like a stock, not priced once daily like a [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/). This allows a trader to exit a position instantly if sentiment shifts, but it also means the intraday price can diverge slightly from the underlying index. For traders watching positions by the hour, those small divergences matter; for anyone holding overnight, they are noise.

## The India context

INDL's underlying index gives it exposure to the broad sweep of India's economy — a population of 1.4 billion, manufacturing that is gradually absorbing some of the scale once concentrated in China, a growing tech and software export sector, and rising domestic consumption as incomes increase. India's [stock market](/stock-market/), while still smaller than those of developed markets, has been one of the world's better performers over the past decade. A trader bullish on India's longer-term trajectory might use INDL as a levered way to express that thesis, accepting that the leverage multiplies both the upside and the downside over a given week or month.

But the fundamental constraint remains: INDL is not a patient investor's tool. Anyone tempted to hold it for more than a few weeks should pause, do the volatility-decay math, and consider whether a simple, unleveraged India index fund might better suit their actual time horizon and tolerance for watching the position disintegrate in choppy markets.

## How to research it

Start with Direxion's [fund prospectus](/fund-prospectus/) and factsheet for INDL, which clearly spell out the daily reset mechanics and the risk of volatility decay — most serious investors read these before buying any leveraged product. The fund's daily holdings are published, showing the index futures and swaps that create the 2x exposure. For traders, watching INDL's intraday movements against the MSCI India index itself over a few days reveals how accurately the leverage mechanism works. Compare INDL's rolling returns over weeks and months against 2x the MSCI India's returns to see volatility decay in action. And for anyone considering this fund, a honest reckoning of whether you will actually hold it for days rather than months or years is the single most important question.
