> "Small-cap stocks are volatile enough on their own. Doubling that volatility via daily rebalancing turns a long-term investment into a short-term tactical gamble."

IWML applies 2x leverage to a US size-factor index—primarily mid-cap and small-cap stocks with characteristics associated with higher returns over the long run: newer growth profiles, lower analyst coverage, and higher earnings-growth expectations. Issued by UBS as an unsecured note, it rebalances daily to maintain exactly 2x exposure and reinvests all returns, compounding within the product rather than paying distributions.

## The size factor and why small caps offer different dynamics

Small-cap stocks are systematically different from large caps. They are younger, less profitable, faster-growing, and far more volatile. Academic research suggests that, over very long periods, small-cap stocks have delivered higher returns than large caps—the "size premium." This premium supposedly rewards investors for bearing the illiquidity, information risk, and operational uncertainty inherent in smaller, less-followed companies.

IWML is designed to capture that premium with 2x leverage, in theory delivering twice the outperformance during periods when small caps lead. In practice, the product is far more sensitive to small-cap rotation cycles and the rebalancing drag that accumulates in volatile markets.

## The boom-and-bust cycle for small caps

Small-cap outperformance is feast or famine. In periods of strong economic growth, low unemployment, and expanding credit—especially in the early stages of a recovery—small caps outperform dramatically. Investors' risk appetite is high, and smaller companies with higher leverage and faster growth appeal more than dividend-paying giants. The 2003–2007 period, the 2009–2010 recovery, and the 2021–2022 reflation trade all saw small caps lead.

Conversely, in crises, recessions, or periods of economic uncertainty, small-cap stocks get hammered. They have less cash, higher debt ratios relative to assets, and lower access to capital markets. When credit tightens or growth expectations fall, they decline sharply. The 2008 crisis, the 2020 pandemic sell-off, and the 2022 rate-hike cycle all saw small caps underperform large caps.

IWML amplifies this cycle. In a small-cap rally, a 2x product can deliver exceptional gains. In a small-cap bear market, it can lose 30, 40, or even 50 percent in months. An investor holding IWML through a recession faces not just exposure to small-cap underperformance but leverage-amplified losses.

## The rebalancing penalty in high-volatility markets

Small-cap stocks are far more volatile than large-cap stocks. Daily volatility can exceed 2–3 percent, and weekly moves of 5–10 percent are not unusual. IWML rebalances daily to maintain its 2x leverage against this volatile index, which extracts a substantial cost.

Consider a hypothetical: the underlying size index swings up 3 percent one day and down 3 percent the next. Over those two days, the index is flat. A static 2x position would also be flat. But IWML rebalances: on day one it holds 2x exposure and gains 6 percent, then rebalances down. On day two the position is smaller but the index falls 3 percent. The net result after two days is a loss—not flat—purely due to rebalancing friction. Over months, in a volatile-but-sideways market, this drag compounds to a significant underperformance.

Small-cap indices are especially susceptible to this drag because the component stocks are volatile and the rebalancing costs (bid-ask spreads) are higher for smaller, less-liquid stocks.

## Factor rotation and the size premium's uncertain future

The size premium—the long-term outperformance of small caps—has been questioned in recent decades. From 2000 onward, small caps have underperformed large caps in many periods, and the premium has been inconsistent. Some researchers attribute this to higher costs for small-cap investing (wider spreads, higher fees), others to structural changes in markets (indexation, passive flows), and others to the fact that small caps have become less neglected and thus less mispriced.

IWML's returns depend on the size premium existing and being strong. In periods when large-cap mega-cap stocks are in favor (as they were in the 2010s and early 2020s), IWML underperforms even more steeply than an unleveraged small-cap index would.

## Illiquidity and hidden costs

Small-cap stocks trade less frequently and with wider bid-ask spreads than large-cap stocks. When IWML rebalances daily, it is trading these less-liquid securities, incurring higher spreads. The quoted expense ratio for the product does not fully capture these trading costs—they are embedded in the daily rebalancing slippage. An investor should expect that the actual annual drag from costs will exceed the stated expense ratio.

## Credit risk and the unsecured structure

IWML is an unsecured note issued by UBS. In a financial crisis or a period of extreme market stress, if UBS comes under pressure, the value of IWML could fall not only because of small-cap underperformance but also because the underlying credit quality of the issuer deteriorates. This two-way risk—market risk plus issuer risk—is often overlooked by investors focused on the small-cap exposure.

## When small-cap leverage works

IWML shines during early-cycle recovery periods when economic growth is accelerating, credit is loose, risk appetite is high, and investors are willing to back higher-leverage, smaller, faster-growing companies. In these periods, a 2x leveraged small-cap product can deliver spectacular returns. The 2009–2010 recovery after the financial crisis and the 2021 reflation trade both offered such windows.

Outside of these periods, IWML is typically a drag on a portfolio. Its high volatility and rebalancing costs make it unsuitable as a core holding. Investors using IWML do so tactically—holding it for 3–12 months around clear cyclical inflection points where small-cap outperformance is expected—not as a long-term position.
