*The **INQQ The India Internet ETF** ([NASDAQ](/nasdaq/): INQQ) is a narrowly focused fund that bets on the growth of internet, technology, and digital commerce companies that operate in or derive a significant portion of their revenue from India. It is not a diversified [index fund](/index-fund/) or a play on the Indian economy as a whole; it is a thematic wager on the idea that India's digital revolution — driven by a young, growing population, rising smartphone penetration, and expanding internet access — will create a generation of dominant internet companies similar to what happened in the United States and China. The fund holds a relatively small number of stocks, typically in the range of 20 to 40, concentrated in companies that fit the internet and technology theme.*

India has more than 1.4 billion people, and it is one of the youngest large countries on Earth. For decades, most Indians had no access to broadband internet or smartphones; that has changed radically in the past 10 to 15 years. As connectivity has expanded, e-commerce platforms, digital payment systems, social media, and software companies have emerged to serve a market that skipped the desktop internet era almost entirely and jumped straight to mobile. INQQ is built on the premise that this shift is ongoing and that the companies riding that wave will deliver substantial returns to shareholders. The fund's concentrations in holdings and its thematic focus mean it is a high-risk, high-conviction bet, not a diversified core portfolio holding.

The universe of Indian internet stocks is smaller and less liquid than the American market, but it is large enough to support a fund. Companies like Infosys, Tata Consultancy Services, and Bharti Airtel are household names in India and globally traded, with exposure to software, IT services, and telecommunications. Smaller, faster-growing e-commerce and fintech firms add the growth angle, though many are not yet publicly traded or are too small to be included in a passive index. INQQ's manager needs to pick and choose among the available companies, selecting those with the strongest growth prospects and the most credible management teams.

The fund's concentration is both its strength and its weakness. By narrowing the focus to internet and technology, INQQ avoids the drag of holding stable, low-growth, non-technology sectors of the Indian economy — utilities, banking, industrials, that might be part of a broad Indian stock index. That focus allows the fund to rise sharply when Indian internet companies are in favor, as they were through much of the 2020s when emerging-market tech was hot. But the concentration also means that if sentiment turns, or if any major holding stumbles, the pain is acute. There is no [diversification](/diversification/) cushion into non-tech sectors because they are explicitly excluded.

[Currency risk](/currency-risk/) is real for a fund holding Indian rupee-denominated stocks. When the rupee weakens against the dollar, the dollar-based investor in INQQ sees the value of their holdings fall just because of exchange rates, independent of how well the underlying companies perform. If the rupee strengthens, that becomes a tailwind. Over long periods, currency movements can be as significant as stock performance, so this is not a minor consideration for a U.S. investor.

Liquidity in the holdings also matters. Indian [stock exchanges](/stock-exchange/) are active, but they do not have the same volume and ease of trade as the [New York Stock Exchange](/new-york-stock-exchange/). If INQQ needs to adjust its portfolio quickly or if many shareholders decide to exit at once, the fund might face trading challenges or widening [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/). Most of the time this is not an issue, but in market stress or a sharp reversal in sentiment toward Indian assets, illiquidity could become a problem.

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) for a thematic, actively managed ETF like INQQ tends to be higher than a passive, broad-market index fund, reflecting the work required to identify and monitor internet-focused companies in an emerging market. The question for an investor is whether that active management skill and the thematic focus justify the cost. If the Indian internet thesis plays out as the fund managers believe, the returns could be substantial enough that the fees fade into the background. If the thesis does not hold — if Indian internet growth slows, if competition from global tech giants intensifies, or if Indian regulations become hostile to foreign-owned tech platforms — then the concentration and the fees compound the pain.

Upstream, INQQ depends on the continued expansion of internet access and digital commerce in India. The regulatory environment matters enormously. India's government has been supportive of the digital economy, but rules around data protection, foreign investment, and competition have been tightening in recent years. Any major regulatory shift could reshape the economics of the companies the fund holds. Downstream, INQQ serves investors who believe deeply in the India growth story, have a high risk tolerance, and are willing to endure volatility for the possibility of outsized returns.

An investor researching INQQ should start with the fund's prospectus and holdings list, understanding which companies are included and what proportion of the portfolio each represents. The fund's fact sheet shows the allocation across sectors and the geographic breakdown (whether holdings are headquartered in India or are subsidiaries of multinational companies). Reading about India's digital economy — the state of e-commerce penetration, the trajectory of digital payment adoption, the regulatory climate for tech — is essential context for evaluating whether the premises underlying the fund are sound. Comparing INQQ's performance to a broader Indian equity index or to other emerging-market tech funds offers perspective on whether the concentration is paying off. And tax implications matter too; rapid trading in emerging-market stocks can trigger short-term capital gains in a taxable account, so holding INQQ long-term in a tax-deferred retirement account might make more sense than trading it frequently.

The fund is inherently speculative and illiquid compared to broad U.S. equity index funds, and it should represent only a small portion of a diversified portfolio unless an investor has a genuinely high risk tolerance and a long time horizon. But for investors convinced that India's digital future is a genuine opportunity and willing to bet meaningfully on that conviction, INQQ offers a concentrated, thematic way to do so.
