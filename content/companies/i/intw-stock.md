| **Aspect** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| **Underlying** | Intel Corp. (INTC) common stock |
| **Leverage** | 2x daily return (not 2x notional amount) |
| **Reset frequency** | Daily—the fund rebalances at market close each trading day |
| **Expense ratio** | Qualitatively higher than standard ETFs, reflecting borrowing and derivative costs |
| **Best suited for** | Traders seeking short-term tactical exposure, not buy-and-hold investors |
| **Key risk** | Volatility decay—compounding losses over time in sideways or volatile markets |

INTW seeks to amplify Intel stock price movements through leveraged derivatives and borrowing. On a day when Intel rises 1 percent, INTW aims to rise 2 percent; on a day Intel falls 1 percent, INTW targets a 2 percent decline. This daily reset feature is crucial: the fund does not simply buy Intel with 2-to-1 borrowed leverage and hold it static. Instead, it trades daily to maintain the target 2x exposure ratio. That daily reset, while offering precision on a day-to-day basis, creates a mechanical disadvantage over longer periods.

The mathematics of leverage expose holders to a phenomenon called volatility decay. Suppose Intel is flat over two weeks but swings plus 5 percent, then minus 5 percent. Over that month, Intel ends where it started. INTW, resetting daily, will end below where it started because of compounding. The first-week 10 percent gain (2x the 5 percent move) generates a larger base for the second week's 10 percent loss (2x the 5 percent drop). That pattern of daily compounding disadvantage is not a feature of the fund but a mathematical artifact of how leveraged products work. The longer the holding period and the higher the volatility, the worse the decay.

This decay is especially punishing in choppy or sideways markets. An investor convinced Intel will be higher in a year but willing to endure weekly swings should not use INTW. The fund will likely underperform simple Intel stock ownership substantially because each week's volatility will have compounded away value. Only in a strongly directional market—Intel rising every day for three straight months—does the leverage consistently deliver what a holder might expect.

GraniteShares finances the leverage through borrowing and derivatives, incurring costs that are reflected in a higher expense ratio than unleveraged Intel exposure would carry. The fund also faces slippage from rebalancing—buying or selling pieces of Intel or derivative positions at prices slightly worse than the ideal—every single day. For someone holding INTW for weeks or months, those daily costs and slippages compound and drag returns relative to simply owning INTC outright, even if INTC has risen.

The broader risk is that Intel's business can be disrupted or weakened. Leveraging an already-volatile tech stock amplifies the impact of bad news. A quarterly earnings miss or competitive threat that would shave 8 percent off Intel stock could shave 16 percent off INTW in a day. Unlike a simple stock, where a loss can eventually be recovered, leverage that loses money compounding daily can erode capital faster than recovery becomes realistic.

INTW is a tactical instrument, not a strategic holding. Traders use it to amplify a near-term bullish view—"I expect Intel to jump 10 percent in the next three weeks"—because the doubled leverage will push INTW up roughly 20 percent in that scenario. But a buy-and-hold investor with a multi-year conviction in Intel is almost certainly better served by owning INTC shares without the leverage, the daily friction, the volatility-decay drag, and the compounding headwinds embedded in a 2x daily reset structure.

The prospectus explains the exact derivatives and leveraging instruments GraniteShares uses, the historical decay pattern relative to 2x the underlying stock return, and the daily rebalancing mechanism. Factsheets and fund data sites show INTW's performance relative to 2x Intel's actual return, which isolates the impact of decay and fees. A reader should always confirm that past performance numbers show INTW versus 2x INTC, not INTW versus INTC itself—that distinction reveals the true cost of the leverage and the magnitude of volatility decay incurred.

INTW appeals to traders making a tactical bet on Intel's near-term direction and comfortable with the compounding risks of daily leverage. It is unsuitable for long-term investors, for anyone who cannot afford to lose money quickly, or for those seeking simple stock exposure without complex leverage mechanics and daily reset compounding.
