*The **iShares S&P SmallCap 600 Growth ETF (IJT)** narrows the small-cap universe to companies exhibiting growth traits: positive earnings momentum, improving operational metrics, and business expansion. For investors seeking exposure to the highest-conviction growth plays among smaller companies, IJT offers a screened, diversified vehicle that avoids the slowest-growing and most cyclical members of the small-cap peer group.*

## Key facts at a glance

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Index tracked** | S&P SmallCap 600 Growth Index |
| **Holding count** | Approximately 300 stocks |
| **Universe** | U.S. small-cap companies (roughly $2B–$10B market cap, though the cap floor shifts with market conditions) |
| **Selection method** | Growth characteristics: earnings momentum, sales growth, profit margin expansion, relative price-to-earnings |
| **Weighting** | Market-capitalization weighted; largest holding typically 1-2% of portfolio |
| **Sector bias** | Tends toward industrials, technology, consumer discretionary; lighter in utilities and staples |
| **Expense ratio** | Low—in line with typical broad iShares equity ETF fees |
| **Dividend yield** | Modest; growth stocks pay less to shareholders than value stocks do |
| **Trading volume** | High; the fund is very liquid |

The S&P SmallCap 600 is itself a niche index, covering companies between roughly $1.5B and $10B in market capitalization. IJT then filters that universe by growth characteristics, selected via a quantitative screen that ranks companies on forward earnings growth, historical earnings-per-share growth, earnings revision trends, and relative profitability measures. The screen then divides the 600 small-caps roughly in half: the 300 highest-growth scores become the Growth index that IJT tracks; the slower-growth half form the Value index (tracked by a separate iShares product).

## How growth screening shapes the portfolio

By isolating the faster-growing half of the small-cap universe, IJT tilts the portfolio toward companies in expansion mode: hiring, investing in new products, entering new markets, or scaling operations that are already working. This naturally biases the portfolio toward higher-revenue-growth rates, faster earnings expansion, and stronger profitability relative to peers. It also skews the sector mix: growth-oriented small-caps tend to be technology companies, software and services firms, industrial innovators, and consumer-discretionary names. Utilities, financial services, and consumer staples are underrepresented because those sectors typically grow earnings slower and pay out more in dividends, characteristics of value stocks rather than growth.

The screening is rules-based and objective, not active manager judgment. S&P publishes the exact formula; the index reconstitutes and rebalances on a published schedule (typically quarterly). This means a company can move in or out of the Growth index if its fundamental metrics change, but the methodology is transparent and systematic.

## Relative risk and volatility

Small-cap growth stocks are higher volatility than large-cap growth or the overall equity market. IJT amplifies this: within the small-cap universe, it selects the ones with the strongest growth signals, which tend to be more volatile, further out on the risk curve, and more sensitive to sentiment shifts. In bull markets, growth outperforms value, and small-cap growth outperforms large-cap growth—IJT's holding can soar. In bear markets and recessions, growth underperforms, and the smaller, more leveraged businesses suffer more.

The portfolio is also less diversified than a broad index of all 3,000+ US stocks. Three hundred holdings is diversified relative to concentrated portfolios (say, 20-30 stocks), but IJT still carries sector and growth-style concentration risk. Tech-heavy years favor IJT; healthcare- or energy-heavy years may not. A sudden shift in interest rates favors or penalizes growth stocks unpredictably.

Liquidity of individual small-cap stocks can matter in the holdings: they are more liquid than penny stocks but far less liquid than mega-cap FAANG stocks. A massive market shock that freezes credit or triggers panic selling can disrupt small-cap trading.

## Who this fund serves

IJT appeals to growth-oriented long-term investors with a tolerance for volatility who believe smaller companies can grow earnings faster than large ones and will be rewarded for it. Hedge funds and tactical traders use IJT to express a "small-cap growth will outperform large-cap or value" view. Asset allocators use it as a satellite holding alongside broader US equity exposure to tilt toward growth and smaller size. Riskier investors—early-career savers, younger retirees, anyone with a 10+ year horizon—may favor IJT over bond-heavy allocations.

It is less suitable for conservative investors, those near or in retirement (principal preservation matters more), anyone unable to stomach 30-40% drawdowns (which small-cap growth can experience in recessions), or those expecting a major market correction. And because it is a growth screen applied to a small-cap universe, it is not a diversified bet on "all small-caps"—it is a bet on growth, expressed through small companies.

## How to evaluate IJT

Start with the S&P SmallCap 600 Growth Index factsheet, published on S&P's website, which lays out the index's methodology, sector breakdown, and current holdings. Compare IJT's sector allocation to the broad S&P 1500 Composite Index or other large-cap benchmarks to feel the tilt toward growth and smaller size.

Examine the top 10 holdings to understand the kinds of companies in the portfolio. Then compare IJT's one- three-, and five-year returns to the S&P SmallCap 600 Value Index (tracked by IJH) to judge the relative performance of growth versus value in small-caps over different cycles. In years when growth dominates, IJT will show higher returns; in value years, it will lag.

Check the expense ratio against competing small-cap growth ETFs from Vanguard and other providers to ensure you are not overpaying. Verify that the fund's tracking error (the fund's return versus the index return) is negligible—if it is not, something is off. Monitor the fund's dividend yield relative to the S&P 500: lower yield on IJT than the broad market is normal and expected for growth stocks.

Finally, understand your own risk appetite and investment horizon. Small-cap growth can double over a few years in strong environments and lose half its value in recessions. Holding IJT requires the temperament to stay invested through both. For investors who find that difficult, a diversified portfolio mixing IJT with large-cap value and bonds is more durable than IJT alone.
