**Structure & positioning.** InPoint is a REIT registered with the SEC, which means it must distribute at least 90 percent of taxable income to shareholders as [dividends](/dividend/). The company owns and leases [commercial real estate](/commercial-real-estate/) — office buildings, industrial warehouses, mixed-use properties — across secondary and tertiary U.S. markets rather than in major metros. Portfolio concentration is high; a handful of properties and tenants drive the majority of revenue.

**Property economics.** Leases are fixed-rate or indexed; higher occupancy and stable tenancy drive returns. Vacancy and tenant defaults cut directly into distributable income. Capital improvement and tenant-improvement costs must be factored into cash flow. The company relies on external capital — bank debt and equity offerings — to acquire new properties and refinance maturing debt. Real estate IRR depends on [acquisition](/acquisition/) price, lease rates, operating costs, and eventual disposition.

**Capital structure.** InPoint has used preferred shares (the "PA" designation) to raise capital, which ranks senior to common equity for dividends but is subordinate to debt. The capital stack matters: higher leverage amplifies returns when property values and rents rise, but also amplifies losses if rents fall or vacancies spike. Interest-rate environment shifts directly affect funding costs and [refinancing risk](/refinancing-risk/).

**The dividend question.** The [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/) attracts income investors. The actual dividend sustainability depends on whether the company's [funds from operations](/funds-from-operations/) exceed its [payout ratio](/payout-ratio/). If funds from operations shrink — because rents soften, occupancy dips, or interest costs rise — the dividend comes under pressure. REIT accounting separates taxable income (which must be distributed) from cash-based funds from operations; the two can diverge sharply.

**Market headwinds.** Commercial real estate faces structural challenges. The pandemic accelerated remote work, lowering office occupancy and rent growth in many markets. Industrial properties have held up better on e-commerce strength, but high vacancy in office directly threatens any REIT with significant office exposure. Rising [interest rates](/interest-rate/) increase the company's cost of capital and lower the present value of future rents. Insurance and property-tax costs have climbed.

**Tenant [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/).** If a small number of tenants represent the bulk of rent, loss of one major tenant is material. Tenant credit quality is therefore critical: a tenant in financial stress or bankruptcy triggers vacancy and lost income during re-leasing. The company's ability to re-lease space depends on local market conditions and the competitiveness of the property. Properties in weak markets or with aging infrastructure may face years of vacancy or forced rent cuts.

**How to assess.** Read the 10-K for property-by-property revenue breakdowns, tenant concentration, lease expirations (the schedule of rent resets), occupancy rates, and management commentary on market conditions and refinancing. Compare funds from operations to the [dividend payout ratio](/dividend-payout-ratio/). Note any impairments or write-downs, which signal overpaid acquisitions or deteriorated property values. Watch quarterly reports for trends: occupancy, rent growth (or declines), and renewal rates tell you whether the underlying real estate is holding value or sliding.

**The risk profile.** InPoint is small and illiquid, trading OTC. It has limited institutional analyst coverage. The preferred shares add complexity to the capital structure. If the portfolio faces stress — tenant defaults, collapsing rents in a given market, or a major refinancing at higher rates — there is limited financial flexibility. Common and preferred shareholders risk both dividend cuts and principal impairment. This is appropriate for patient, income-oriented investors comfortable holding illiquid securities with real downside risk.
