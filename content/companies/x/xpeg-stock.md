*The **Leverage Shares 2X Long XPEV Daily ETF** (ticker XPEG) is built around a single, concentrated bet: that XPeng, a Chinese electric vehicle and autonomous-driving software company, will move decisively upward — and the investor wants that move amplified by 2x leverage.*

> "Leverage magnifies the right call; it annihilates the wrong one."

## What XPEG actually does

XPEG borrows money and stock to achieve 2x daily exposure to XPeng Inc. (ticker XPEV). If XPEV rises 1 percent in a day, XPEG aims to rise 2 percent. If XPEV falls 1 percent, XPEG falls 2 percent. The mechanism is daily reset leverage: at each market close, Leverage Shares recalculates the fund's exposure and rebalances it back to exactly 2x, preparing for the next day. This reset structure makes XPEG suitable for traders holding days to weeks, not months or years.

The appeal is simple and honest: if you believe XPeng stock will rally, XPEG lets you amplify that conviction. A $5,000 position might yield $500 if your thesis is right and XPEV rises 5 percent. But the mirror is equally harsh: a 5 percent fall in XPEV means a $500 loss on that same position, and the leverage adds cost.

## The concentrated bet

XPEG is not diversified in any meaningful sense. It is not a sector play on electric vehicles or Chinese technology; it is a single-stock bet with a 2x lever attached. This concentration is the fund's entire point — the investor has a thesis about XPeng specifically and wants to amplify it. But concentration also means that any setback unique to XPeng — product delay, regulatory trouble, supply-chain disruption, or competitive pressure — will be felt twice as hard.

XPeng is also listed on Chinese exchanges, subject to Chinese regulatory oversight, and exposed to geopolitical risk around US-China relations. Foreign regulatory action, a listing delisting threat, or tightening capital controls in China would flow directly into XPEV's price and XPEG's value. These are not risks that a broad equity portfolio faces in the same way.

## Volatility decay and the math of daily reset

In a market where XPEV oscillates — rising 2 percent one day, falling 1.8 percent the next — XPEG will compound those moves at 2x, and the decay will be severe. Over a week of sideways trading, XPEG could lose 5 to 10 percent while XPEV is essentially flat, entirely due to volatility decay. This is not a flaw; it is a mathematical property of leverage and daily resets that every investor in such a product must understand before buying.

The decay is especially harsh in choppy markets or during earnings seasons when individual stocks whipsaw. If your thesis is that XPEV will make a smooth, directional move over days or weeks, XPEG serves you well. If XPEV instead bounces around, you will lose money to decay even if your overall direction is eventually correct.

## The costs of leverage and borrowing

Leverage Shares charges an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) to cover fund management, borrowing costs, and the daily rebalancing operation. That fee is higher than a plain stock or a traditional ETF because leverage requires active management and carries borrowing costs. Additionally, when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) are elevated, the cost of borrowing money to maintain the leverage becomes a visible drag on returns. This is not a one-time fee; it is a daily or weekly erosion that compounds over time.

## Forced liquidation and extreme scenarios

In extreme market stress, Leverage Shares might not be able to maintain 2x leverage. If XPeng's stock gaps down 20 percent in a single day due to crisis, news, or regulatory action, the fund could suffer losses that exceed the leverage multiple in unexpected ways. The prospectus details these scenarios and the circumstances under which the fund might suspend operations or force [liquidation](/liquidation/).

## Who buys XPEG, and when

XPEG is for a trader with a medium-term tactical view on XPeng and comfort with leverage and [currency risk](/currency-risk/). It might be used to amplify a multi-week view ahead of a product launch or earnings event. It is never appropriate for a buy-and-hold investment, a retirement account, or an investor unfamiliar with how leverage and daily resets compound losses in choppy markets.

To evaluate whether XPEG fits a trading strategy, review your hypothesis about XPENG's direction and timeframe. If you are confident in a clear directional move over a specific number of days or weeks, and you have sized the position to account for the full loss if you are wrong, then XPEG is a transparent tool for that bet. If you are unsure about direction, unclear on your timeframe, or hoping for a longer [holding period](/holding-period/), XPEG is likely to disappoint due to decay and costs. Read Leverage Shares' prospectus carefully, especially the sections on daily reset mechanics, and model the fund's performance under scenarios of different volatility to build intuition for how leverage and reset will behave if your thesis takes longer to play out than expected.
