*The **Franklin Minnesota Municipal [Income ETF](/income-etf/)** (FTMN) is an exchange-traded fund that holds [municipal bonds](/municipal-bond/) issued in Minnesota, offering residents of that state the combined benefit of federal tax-free income and Minnesota income-tax-free income.*

## Why a state-specific fund exists

A Minnesota resident who owns a municipal [bond](/bond/) issued by a Minnesota city gets a special tax deal. The interest is exempt from federal income tax. It is also exempt from Minnesota state income tax. That double exemption is rare and powerful. A Minnesota bond yielding 3% is worth more to a Minnesota resident paying federal and state taxes than a national municipal bond yielding the same rate. The math is simple: keep more of what you earn.

A fund like FTMN lets a Minnesota investor buy that double tax benefit without picking individual bonds. Instead of researching Minneapolis school bonds or St. Paul port authority bonds one at a time, the investor buys shares of the fund and owns a slice of a diversified portfolio of Minnesota municipal issuers.

## What the fund holds

FTMN holds municipal bonds issued by Minnesota cities, counties, school districts, utility districts, ports, and other local and state agencies. The fund favors [investment-grade bonds](/investment-grade-bond/) — typically A-rated and above — from creditworthy issuers. This means it skips the riskiest segments of the muni market and stays with borrowers that have strong tax bases or solid revenue streams.

The fund includes bonds from major issuers like the City of Minneapolis, Hennepin County, and the Metropolitan Airports Commission, alongside smaller school districts and utility systems. A typical holding might be a [general obligation bond](/general-obligation-bond/) from a Minnesota city, backed by property taxes, or a [revenue bond](/revenue-bond/) from a water authority, backed by user fees. By holding a large number of these bonds, the fund eliminates the risk that any single issuer's financial troubles will wreck the portfolio.

## Tax benefit and yield trade-off

The magic of Minnesota-issued bonds is their tax treatment. But that magic comes with a price: yields on Minnesota bonds are lower than yields on similar non-Minnesota bonds. A Bond issued by the City of Minneapolis might yield 2.5%, while a comparable bond issued by a city in a state with no income tax might yield 3.2%. The Minnesota bond is worth more to a Minnesota resident after taxes, even at the lower rate.

FTMN captures that yield at a lower cost than buying individual bonds. A retail investor buying a single bond has to pay a [broker](/broker/) spread and often a purchase minimum. An ETF pools thousands of bonds and spreads those costs across thousands of shareholders. The fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is modest — typical for [bond ETFs](/bond-etf/) — and trading is easy on any day the [stock market](/stock-market/) is open.

For a Minnesota resident in a high tax bracket, the after-tax yield on FTMN often exceeds the after-tax yield on a taxable [corporate bond](/corporate-bond/) fund, even though the stated yield is lower. For a Minnesota resident in a lower bracket, or for anyone living outside Minnesota, the fund makes much less sense: the federal tax exemption remains, but the Minnesota state tax exemption is lost, and other funds might offer higher yields with less hassle.

## Who this fund is for

FTMN is narrowly designed. Its ideal investor is a Minnesota resident with significant federal and state income-tax liability who wants a portion of their portfolio generating tax-free income. The resident might be retired and living on portfolio income, or a high-income professional looking to shelter some earnings.

For a non-resident, FTMN is rarely the best choice. The Minnesota state tax exemption is lost, and the yields are not high enough to compensate. A non-resident investor hunting for tax-exempt municipal income is usually better served by a national municipal fund or a fund focused on states with higher yields.

The fund's value also depends on future tax policy. If Minnesota raises income taxes, the benefit of Minnesota bond ownership rises. If Minnesota taxes fall, the benefit shrinks. Those living in Minnesota and planning to stay should find the fund most attractive.

## Duration and interest-rate risk

Like all bond funds, FTMN is sensitive to interest-rate movements. When rates rise, the prices of existing bonds fall; when rates fall, prices rise. A Minnesota resident buying FTMN for long-term tax-exempt income can ignore those price swings if they plan to hold shares until the underlying bonds mature. But a holder needing to sell before maturity should be aware that rising rates mean a lower share price.

The fund typically holds intermediate-duration bonds — a mix of bonds maturing in five to ten years. This means the fund is moderately sensitive to rate changes, not as volatile as a long-term bond fund but more volatile than a short-term money-market fund.

## How to evaluate the fund

The prospectus should be the starting point. It details exactly which Minnesota issuers the fund may hold, the credit quality criteria, and the risks.

For ongoing tracking, watch the fund's average [credit rating](/credit-rating/) and yield. A shift toward lower-rated bonds signals increased [credit risk](/credit-risk/). Compare the fund's yield to that of non-Minnesota investment-grade municipal funds; if FTMN's yield is unusually low, it may reflect concentration in a weaker segment of the Minnesota market.

Check the fund's holdings periodically, available on the [Franklin Templeton](/fgdl-stock/) website. See which Minnesota issuers are largest; a single large issuer in distress could impair returns more than [diversification](/diversification/) usually allows.

For Minnesota residents, FTMN offers a straightforward, low-cost way to get tax-exempt income. For others, a national municipal fund or a higher-yielding fixed-income vehicle likely works better.
