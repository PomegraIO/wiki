*The Schwab Fundamental U.S. Small Company ETF (FNDA) is a passively managed, exchange-traded fund that holds a diversified portfolio of U.S. small-cap equities selected according to fundamental criteria rather than [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) — favouring companies with strong profitability, cash generation, and balance-sheet quality over size alone.*

## What is the Schwab Fundamental U.S. Small Company ETF?

FNDA tracks the [Fundamental Index](/fundamental-index/) Investable Universe Small Company Index, a stock selection methodology created by Research Affiliates and licensed to Schwab for this fund. Rather than weight its holdings by market cap (the approach used by a traditional capitalization-weighted index), FNDA selects small-cap companies and weights them according to their fundamental scores — measures of profitability, cash flow, [dividend](/dividend/) payout, and book value. This approach aims to capture the small-cap premium (the historical tendency for smaller companies to outperform larger ones over long periods) while avoiding concentration in the most expensive names within the small-cap universe.

The fund holds roughly 450–550 companies, each with a market capitalization in the small-cap range (historically $300 million to $2 billion at index construction). The [diversification](/diversification/) across this many holdings mitigates the [idiosyncratic risk](/idiosyncratic-risk/) of any single company, though small-cap portfolios are still more volatile than their large-cap counterparts.

## How the fund is structured and managed

FNDA is managed by Schwab Index Advisors, part of [Charles Schwab](/charles-schwab/) & Co. The fund's approach is passive — it follows a mechanical rule-based index rather than relying on human stock-pickers to decide what to hold. This reduces costs, keeps turnover moderate, and removes the need to forecast which specific small-cap managers will outperform over time. Schwab rebalances the fund annually, typically in September, which involves selling holdings that have grown too large in the index and buying those that have fallen in weight. This systematic rebalancing can capture some gains from the mean reversion of stock prices, though the effect is modest.

The fund itself is structured as an exchange-traded fund, meaning it trades on a [stock exchange](/stock-exchange/) (in this case, [Nasdaq](/nasdaq/)) at prices set by the market throughout the day, rather than being priced once per day like a traditional [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/). This allows investors to buy and sell shares intraday, much like owning any individual stock.

## Costs, trading, and liquidity

FNDA's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is competitive for a fundamental-index small-cap fund, typically in the region of 0.20–0.30% annually, which translates to roughly $200–300 per year for every $100,000 invested. This is higher than a plain market-cap-weighted small-cap [index fund](/index-fund/) (such as a total U.S. small-cap tracker), because the fundamental indexing methodology requires more frequent data collection and rebalancing than a passive market-cap approach. For investors seeking a small-cap allocation, however, the additional cost is modest relative to the potential benefit of a different weighting scheme.

The fund is highly liquid, with typical daily volume in the tens of thousands of shares. Most [limit orders](/limit-order/) will fill at or near the underlying index value, and trading spreads — the difference between bid and ask prices — are usually pennies. This liquidity is important for retail and institutional investors who want to move in and out of the position without distortion in execution price.

## The logic and risks of fundamental indexing

The rationale for weighting by fundamentals rather than market cap is that large, popular companies can become overpriced, while smaller, less-favoured companies can become cheap. By holding a basket weighted toward undervalued, profitable small-caps, the fund attempts to capture value that the market has overlooked. This strategy does not always pay off — there are extended periods during which growth-focused, large-cap investing outperforms value and small-cap strategies, and during those periods FNDA will trail the broad market.

The real risks to FNDA are structural. Small-cap stocks are inherently more volatile than large-cap stocks, more sensitive to economic downturns, and less liquid when markets are stressed. The fund's focus on profitable, quality companies reduces (but does not eliminate) the risk of owning truly distressed or low-quality businesses, but it does not protect against a broad small-cap selloff if the overall economic environment deteriorates. Additionally, the fundamental-indexing methodology can concentrate holdings in sectors and industries that are undervalued at any given time — which can be an advantage or a disadvantage depending on how those sectors perform.

## Who is FNDA for and how to research it

FNDA is best suited to investors with a longer time horizon (five years or more) who believe that small-cap value stocks will outperform the broader market and who want a diversified way to gain that exposure. It is not a trading vehicle or a hedge; it is meant to be a core holding within a diversified portfolio.

Prospective investors should begin with Schwab's [fund prospectus](/fund-prospectus/) and fact sheet, which lay out the selection criteria, current holdings, sector allocations, and performance history. Compare FNDA's returns against a traditional market-cap-weighted small-cap index fund to gauge whether the fundamental-indexing approach has added or subtracted value. Review the fund's portfolio composition to understand its sector and style biases — a value-focused small-cap fund will naturally be overweight to financials, energy, and industrials and underweight to technology and consumer discretionary relative to a cap-weighted small-cap index. Most importantly, assess whether the fund's philosophy aligns with your own investment thesis and risk tolerance.
