*[FBS Global Ltd](/stock/) (FBGL) is an online brokerage firm that operates a retail trading platform where retail customers trade foreign exchange (forex), commodities, cryptocurrencies, and contract-for-difference (CFD) products. The company generates revenue primarily through spread income—the markup between buying and selling prices—and commissions on customer trades. Its business model is built on high-volume, low-margin trading from a globally distributed retail customer base.*

## How Retail Forex and CFD Trading Platforms Operate

FBS operates an electronic trading platform where retail customers deposit cash, execute buy and sell orders on forex and other instruments, and generate profit or loss based on price movements. A customer deposits $1,000, opens a position betting that the EUR/USD exchange rate will rise, and if the rate rises by 100 pips (basis points), the customer's position is worth $1,500 or more depending on leverage. If the rate falls, the position is worth less. FBS's execution infrastructure must match customer buy and sell orders with counterparty liquidity (usually from [market makers](/market-maker-trading/) or FBS's own operations), settle trades, and manage the customer's account balance and margin requirements.

The mechanics are operationally intensive. FBS must operate a trading platform (matching engine, order entry interface, risk management systems), settlement systems (clearing trades, calculating P&L, adjusting account balances), and customer support infrastructure. Every customer trade must be executed within milliseconds, and the system must handle thousands of simultaneous users without outages. A platform crash during market hours leads to customer losses and regulatory consequences.

## Revenue Model and the Spread

FBS's primary revenue comes from the spread—the difference between the price FBS offers to a customer to buy an instrument and the price it offers to sell. When a customer buys EUR/USD at 1.0950, FBS may have bought that position itself at 1.0948, pocketing 2 pips per unit of volume. On large trade volumes, small per-trade margins accumulate to significant revenue. FBS may also charge commissions on trades, particularly on equities and commodity contracts.

The retail forex and CFD market is highly price-competitive. Customers shop for the tightest spreads and the lowest commissions; FBS must balance competitive pricing with profitability. If FBS offers 1-pip spreads on major forex pairs while competitors offer 1.5 pips, FBS attracts volume but earns lower revenue per trade. Market volatility affects spreads; during quiet periods, FBS can offer tight spreads; during market stress, spreads widen, and revenue per trade increases but customer activity may decline.

## Leverage and Customer Deposits

FBS allows retail customers to trade with leverage—multiplying their purchasing power with borrowed capital. A customer with $1,000 might trade a $50,000 position using 50:1 leverage. Leverage amplifies both gains and losses; a 2% move in the underlying instrument is a 100% move in the customer's account (in either direction). High leverage creates frequent account [liquidations](/liquidation/); a customer's $1,000 is wiped out if the market moves against them by 2%.

High customer account liquidations mean that FBS is constantly receiving new deposits from new or existing customers seeking to replace lost capital. This creates a business dynamic where FBS must acquire new customers at steady volumes to maintain a stable customer base. Customer [acquisition](/acquisition/) costs (advertising, referral commissions, sign-up bonuses) are significant. FBS and its competitors spend heavily on digital marketing, sports sponsorships, and influencer partnerships to drive customer sign-ups. The customer lifetime value must exceed the acquisition cost, but with high leverage and frequent liquidations, many retail traders are "one-shot" customers who lose their capital and never trade again.

## Regulatory Landscape and Licensing

FBS's licensing and regulatory status is complex. The company likely operates multiple subsidiaries in different jurisdictions, each licensed by local financial regulators. FBS may be licensed in Cyprus, the UK, Singapore, or other financial centers, with different regulatory requirements in each jurisdiction. Regulatory compliance is a major operational and cost burden; regulators impose capital requirements, customer protection rules, marketing restrictions, and leverage limits. Regulatory violations can result in fines, license suspension, or shutdown.

The retail forex and CFD industry faces increased regulatory scrutiny worldwide. Many regulators have identified that retail customers lose money in aggregate—studies show that 70–90% of retail traders lose money—and have implemented protections such as leverage caps and negative balance protection (the trader cannot owe more than the deposit). These protections reduce FBS's profitability and limit leverage, which reduces customer trading volumes and revenue per customer.

## Platform Technology and Liquidity Provision

FBS's platform must handle high trading volumes with low latency. The company invests in trading infrastructure (servers, network connections, order matching engines) and must maintain uptime and performance even during peak trading hours or market volatility. Any platform outage or slowdown during volatile trading periods damages customer confidence and creates liability; a customer may sue FBS if the platform crashes and the customer is unable to liquidate a losing position.

Liquidity is operationally critical. When a customer wants to buy EUR/USD, FBS must provide the other side of the trade. FBS can either match customer buy orders with customer sell orders (market making among customers) or hedge by taking positions itself or buying liquidity from larger market makers. Managing liquidity sources, hedging open positions, and avoiding excessive directional exposure requires sophisticated risk management. If too many customers bet on EUR/USD rising and few bet on it falling, FBS's own account swells with short positions; if EUR/USD then rallies sharply, FBS loses money.

## Customer Support and Churn

FBS's customer base is highly geographically dispersed; customers may be in Europe, Asia, Latin America, or Africa. Support must be available 24/5 (forex markets are closed on weekends) in multiple languages. Support staff handle customer complaints, [margin call](/margin-call-forex/) notifications, account verification, and deposit/withdrawal processing. High customer churn means that FBS is continuously supporting customers through losses, disputes, and account closures; this support burden is a drag on profitability.

Customer disputes are common in retail forex. A customer may claim that FBS filled an order at a worse price than was available, or that spreads widened unfairly, or that the platform crashed at a critical moment. Handling these disputes requires documentation, investigation, and often customer compensation. Regulatory bodies also receive customer complaints, and FBS must respond to investigations and remediate violations if found.

## Network Effects and Traffic Flow

FBS benefits from network effects to a limited degree; a platform with many active traders generates tighter spreads and better liquidity, which attracts more traders. But this advantage is weak; a trader does not particularly care if FBS has 10,000 or 100,000 other traders; the trader cares about spreads, commissions, and platform performance. FBS competes on price and platform quality, not on network effects.

FBS likely also operates an affiliate program, where influencers, bloggers, and trading educators promote FBS in exchange for commissions on customer sign-ups. These affiliates drive customer acquisition and act as an outsourced sales force. However, affiliate channels can also drive low-quality customers (those likely to lose quickly) and create [reputational risk](/reputational-risk/) if affiliates promote misleading trading advice.

## Profitability Drivers and Sustainability

FBS's profitability is driven by customer acquisition volume, average deposit size, customer retention (lifetime value), and operational efficiency (spread management, cost control). In a saturated market with intense competition, profitability margins are thin. FBS must achieve massive trading volumes to generate meaningful profit. Any decline in customer acquisition (due to changed advertising costs, regulatory restrictions, or market saturation) immediately impacts profitability.

The company also faces [systemic risk](/systemic-risk/); if a major market dislocation or [flash crash](/flash-crash-2010/) occurs, customer losses spike, customer churn increases, and regulatory scrutiny intensifies. A sustained market downturn that reduces trading activity can cause a sharp drop in FBS's revenue and profitability. The business is thus sensitive to market conditions, regulatory changes, and technology disruption (competition from zero-commission platforms, [cryptocurrency exchanges](/cryptocurrency-exchange/), or other fintech providers).
