*The Schwab Fundamental International Small [Equity ETF](/equity-etf/) (FNDC) is a [passively managed fund](/passively-managed-fund/) that tracks small-cap equities from developed markets outside the United States, using fundamental-quality metrics rather than pure [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) to weight its holdings.*

## Coverage and composition

FNDC provides exposure to approximately 400–500 small-cap equities from developed markets in Europe, Asia, and the Pacific region — including the United Kingdom, Germany, France, Switzerland, Canada, Japan, Australia, and Nordic countries. Companies are included if they fall within the small-cap size band (historically €150 million to €2 billion in market capitalization at index construction) and meet the fund's liquid trading criteria. The geographic [diversification](/diversification/) across multiple developed markets and time zones reduces idiosyncratic country or sector risk and ensures reasonably broad exposure to international small-cap opportunity.

The fundamental-weighting approach applies the same methodology as Schwab's U.S. small-cap fund: companies are selected and weighted based on profitability, free cash generation, book value, and [dividend](/dividend/) payout rather than by market cap. This is particularly relevant for international small-caps, where the market can leave entire categories of profitable, well-managed businesses lightly valued simply because they lack Wall Street following or analyst coverage.

## Currency and hedging considerations

FNDC is denominated in [U.S. dollars](/us-dollar/) but holds stocks priced in foreign currencies — euros, pounds, yen, [Australian dollars](/australian-dollar/), and others. The fund does not hedge this currency exposure back to the dollar, meaning that U.S. investors face both the performance of the underlying stocks and the movement of foreign currencies relative to the dollar. In years when the dollar weakens, foreign returns are magnified for dollar-based investors. In years when the dollar strengthens, foreign returns are diminished. This currency exposure is a feature for investors who believe diversification into multiple currency zones reduces risk, and a risk for those who want pure equity exposure stripped of forex movements.

## Costs and market accessibility

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is typically around 0.25–0.30% annually, higher than U.S. equity [index funds](/index-fund/) due to the additional complexity of tracking an international index and the higher trading costs associated with less-liquid international small-cap markets. For a $100,000 position, this translates to roughly $250–300 per year. The fund is tax-efficient in that it minimizes distributions and does not attempt active trading; most tax liability comes from capital appreciation rather than turnover.

FNDC trades on the [Nasdaq](/nasdaq/) during U.S. market hours, which may create a mismatch with trading hours in the company's home countries. For example, a Japanese small-cap stock that gaps significantly at the open of Tokyo trading will already be incorporated into the next day's FNDC pricing when the market opens in the U.S. This asynchronous pricing is normal for international funds and does not impair long-term performance, though it can create minor execution friction for traders trying to enter or exit during volatile market sessions.

## Structural characteristics and risks

Small-cap stocks, whether domestic or international, are more volatile than large-caps, face less analyst coverage, and have thinner trading markets. International small-caps compound these risks: they are subject to foreign exchange fluctuation, less familiar regulatory environments, less English-language disclosure, and in some cases geopolitical or credit concerns specific to their home countries. Companies in smaller economies face fewer growth opportunities and may have less access to capital than U.S. counterparts.

The fundamental-weighting approach mitigates some of this risk by selecting companies with strong profitability and balance-sheet quality, but it does not eliminate the inherent volatility or the [liquidity risk](/liquidity-risk/) of small-cap markets. A severe selloff in international equities or a flight of capital from small-cap securities can cause FNDC to decline sharply and sometimes without a ready buyer if liquidity evaporates.

## Performance drivers and suitability

FNDC's long-term performance depends on whether international small-cap profitability outpaces growth in developed domestic markets and whether currency headwinds or tailwinds average out over multi-year periods. Many investors have found it difficult to justify the costs and complexity of international small-cap exposure relative to a broad U.S. market fund, and performance results have often lagged domestic small-caps, particularly in periods of dollar strength.

FNDC is suited to investors with a global allocation framework who want small-cap exposure outside the U.S., who understand and are comfortable with [currency volatility](/currency-volatility/), and who have a time horizon of at least five years. It is best held as a satellite position within a diversified portfolio, not as a core holding, because the costs and risks are meaningfully higher than a domestic broad-market fund.

Before investing, review the fund's prospectus, examine its current holdings and country/sector allocations, and assess the historical performance through a full [market cycle](/market-cycle/), including periods of dollar strength and weakness.
