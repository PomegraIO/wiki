## What the AlphaDEX methodology screens for

The [Nasdaq](/nasdaq/) AlphaDEX Industrials Index is built on a proprietary screening process that ranks industrial stocks on two opposing factors: **[relative valuation](/relative-valuation/)** (hunting for cheap stocks) and **relative momentum** (favoring stocks gaining upward momentum). The algorithm pulls candidates from the broad Nasdaq industrial universe, filters to exclude very small companies, and then weights them by a composite score.

The "alpha" in AlphaDEX refers to the aim of outperforming the standard market-cap-weighted industrial benchmark — in this case, the broader industrial sector as it appears in the Nasdaq. Instead of owning every industrial stock in rough proportion to its market value, FXR owns a smaller, handpicked set of names that the AlphaDEX screen believes are attractively priced and gaining traction.

This is not active management in the traditional sense: no human portfolio manager picks stocks on belief or research. Instead, the algorithm applies fixed rules every quarter to rebalance the portfolio. It is rule-based, transparent, and systematic — what some call "smart beta" or "[factor investing](/factor-investing/)."

## Composition and sector tilt

FXR typically holds 40–80 industrial stocks, a concentrated group relative to a full-market industrial index which might contain several hundred names. The holdings lean toward mid-cap and some smaller large-cap names, avoiding the mega-cap industrials that dominate cap-weighted portfolios. Industrials as a sector spans machinery and equipment, aerospace and defense, electrical equipment, construction and engineering, transportation, and diversified industrial conglomerates.

Within that broad category, the AlphaDEX tilt means FXP will overweight firms that appear cheap on metrics like price-to-book or price-to-earnings relative to their growth prospects, while also favoring those showing positive relative strength or price momentum. A machinery company that has fallen out of favor and started recovering might rate highly; a conglomerate trading at a premium multiple despite slowing earnings would rank lower.

The portfolio rebalances quarterly, adding new stocks that meet the screen's criteria and removing those that no longer qualify. This mechanical approach means turnover is moderate — higher than a passive [index fund](/index-fund/) but lower than active managers making frequent tactical moves.

## When and why to use FXR

FXR appeals to investors who believe the industrial sector is attractive but want to tilt their exposure toward value and momentum rather than hold the sector market-cap-weighted. An investor bullish on manufacturing and infrastructure, for instance, might prefer FXR to a broad industrial ETF because it overweights names with momentum and relative cheapness.

The fund is also useful for investors interested in factor investing — the idea that certain quantifiable stock characteristics (value, momentum, quality, etc.) tend to produce higher returns over long periods. By owning a rules-based, transparent screen built on those factors, an investor gains exposure to the factor without relying on any manager's judgment.

The concentrated portfolio and the exclusion of mega-caps mean FXR will behave differently from the market's traditional industrial index, particularly in years when mega-cap industrials outperform the rest of the sector. A year in which [Caterpillar](/cat-stock/), [Boeing](/ba-stock/), or 3M hugely outperform smaller industrials will see FXR lag a cap-weighted alternative.

## Costs and structure

FXR is a standard ETF traded on NASDAQ like a stock. Its [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is moderate, typically in the range of 0.60–0.70 percent annually — higher than a passive cap-weighted industrial ETF but reasonable given the proprietary screening and quarterly rebalancing. The [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) is usually tight because FXR trades with reasonable volume.

## The risks of factor-tilted screening

Factor investing assumes that the chosen metrics — in FXR's case, value and momentum — will outperform the market over time. That assumption has historical support, but it is not guaranteed in any given period. Value and momentum screens can underperform for years (as happened during much of the 2010s when growth stocks dominated) before rotating back into favor.

A concentrated portfolio of 40–80 stocks carries higher volatility than a broad index, and the elimination of mega-cap names can be costly in periods when market leadership is heavily concentrated among the largest firms. If industrials fall sharply, FXR will usually fall further because it is concentrated and carries more mid-cap exposure.

There is also "backtest overfitting" risk — the possibility that the AlphaDEX screen worked brilliantly on historical data but may not continue to outperform going forward. Every quantitative screen must be viewed with some skepticism, because the factors that worked in the past may have become crowded or may have lost their edge.

## How to research FXR

Begin with First Trust's product documentation and fact sheet, which explains the AlphaDEX methodology in detail and shows the fund's actual holdings. Compare FXR's historical returns and volatility to a broad industrial ETF (such as the Vanguard Industrials ETF or the broader SPDR sectors) to understand how often and by how much FXR has outperformed or lagged.

Examine the portfolio turnover and the specific holdings at different market points to see how the screen behaves in various economic climates. Read the prospectus to understand the rebalancing rules and the exact criteria for inclusion. And monitor whether the AlphaDEX factors — value and momentum in industrials — are performing in the current market environment or whether they have fallen out of favor.

For investors considering FXR as a core industrial position, the critical question is whether they believe the factor tilt (value and momentum) will outperform a cap-weighted alternative over their time horizon. If they do, FXR is an elegant way to capture that belief systematically.
