## What does FLAU track, and why Australia?

The Franklin FTSE Australia ETF holds the universe of large, mid, and small-cap companies listed on the [Australian Securities Exchange](/australian-securities-exchange/), weighted by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) and filtered by the FTSE All-Cap Australia Index methodology. Australia is a developed market and a major global economy, yet one that many U.S.-based portfolios underweight or ignore — a gap FLAU is designed to fill. For investors seeking geographic [diversification](/diversification/) beyond North America and Western Europe, Australia offers distinct sector tilts and currency exposure not found in home-market indices.

## The Australian economy and what the fund really owns

Australia's equity market is shaped by the country's economic reality: it is a wealthy, advanced economy heavily dependent on natural resources — [iron ore](/iron-ore/), coal, [natural gas](/natural-gas/), and agricultural exports fuel much of its income. The ASX 500 and FTSE All-Cap index therefore overweight materials (mining giants), energy (oil and gas), and financial services (the big four banks), while technology and consumer discretionary are thinner than in the U.S. or UK. A dollar invested in FLAU is a bet on the Australian economy and, implicitly, on commodity prices and Chinese demand for Australian raw materials, since China is Australia's largest export customer.

This creates a structural difference from a U.S. or European equity fund. Australian banks have been among the country's most valuable companies for decades — stable, profitable, and dividend-paying. Miners like BHP and [Rio Tinto](/rtntf-stock/) are household names in Australia and globally significant. Real estate (REITs) is a meaningful sector. Technology, by contrast, is smaller. FLAU thus gives equity exposure to a different economic model than the American one.

## Costs, currency, and trading mechanics

FLAU trades in [U.S. dollars](/us-dollar/) on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) despite holding Australian-listed shares underneath. This introduces [currency risk](/currency-risk/): if the [Australian dollar](/australian-dollar/) weakens against the U.S. dollar, the fund's NAV in dollar terms falls even if Australian stocks stay flat. Conversely, if the Aussie strengthens, FLAU benefits from that currency movement on top of any stock-price gains. An investor seeking pure Australian equity exposure without currency exposure would need a hedged [share class](/share-class/); FLAU is unhedged, meaning currency fluctuations flow through directly.

The fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is moderate, typical for developed-market geographic ETFs from [Franklin Templeton](/fgdl-stock/). Liquidity is generally tight, especially during hours when the ASX is closed and U.S. traders have no real-time [price discovery](/price-discovery/). [Dividend yield](/dividend-yield/) tends to be higher than in U.S. equity funds, reflecting Australian companies' strong cash generation and shareholder-return orientation; [dividends](/dividend/) distribute quarterly.

## Why own Australian equities?

Investors hold FLAU for two primary reasons: belief that Australian equities will outperform on merit, and geographic diversification. Australia's currency is often seen as a commodity proxy — it rises when raw materials are in demand and falls in deflationary scares — so the fund offers a way to gain indirect commodity exposure through equity rather than futures. The country's banking system is stable and profitable, offering dividend income that appeals to income investors. And for those building a globally diversified portfolio, Australia is a natural building block: a developed, liquid, transparent market that is simply not North America or Europe.

The risks are equally clear. A slowdown in Chinese demand for Australian commodities ripples through the entire market. Rising global [interest rates](/interest-rate/) can pressure both financial shares and real estate. Currency movements can work for or against you. And Australia's relative size — much smaller than the U.S. or European markets — means that a single-country bet carries more specific risk than a global diversified fund.

## How to research FTSE Australia

The FTSE All-Cap Australia Index prospectus details the exact selection rules, sector weightings, and constituent companies. Franklin Templeton publishes holdings and factsheets. Compare FLAU's returns to other Australian [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/) and to the broader MSCI Australia Index to understand how methodology choices affect performance. Track the fund's top ten holdings — usually dominated by the banking sector and miners — to grasp what moves the fund most. Watch the Australian dollar exchange rate against the U.S. dollar; a sustained [depreciation](/depreciation/) can be a headwind even if Australian stocks themselves rally.
