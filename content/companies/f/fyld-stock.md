*Cambria Foreign Shareholder Yield ETF (FYLD) seeks [dividend](/dividend/) income from companies outside the United States, targeting stocks that return capital to shareholders through distributions and [share buybacks](/share-buyback/).*

Cambria Investments, the fund sponsor, designed FYLD to address a simple observation: developed markets outside North America contain thousands of mature, profitable companies that reward shareholders with dividends—often at higher yields than comparable US stocks. FYLD holds a portfolio of non-US equities selected for their willingness and ability to distribute cash. The fund tracks no published index; instead, it uses proprietary screening to identify foreign stocks meeting Cambria's definition of shareholder yield—a combination of [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/) and buyback activity relative to market price.

The appeal lies in income generation and valuation discipline. Many international companies, particularly in Europe and developed Asia, have paid steady dividends for decades and view distribution to shareholders as a core responsibility of management. Those dividends often arrive with currency movements layered on top: a US investor in FYLD receives payments denominated in euros, pounds, yen, or other foreign currencies, which can amplify returns when those currencies strengthen against the dollar or can detract when they weaken. The fund is denominated in [US dollars](/us-dollar/), but the underlying exposure is broadly foreign.

## Composition and strategy

FYLD holds roughly 50 to 100 stocks across developed markets—primarily Europe, Japan, and developed Asia—with typically minimal exposure to emerging markets. The portfolio tilts heavily toward financials, utilities, energy, and consumer staples, sectors that historically pay larger dividends than technology or growth-oriented fields. The screening process emphasizes companies with stable earnings, strong [balance sheets](/balance-sheet/), and a demonstrated pattern of returning cash. Cambria rebalances the holdings periodically and sells positions that no longer meet its yield or valuation criteria.

The fund does not hedge [currency risk](/currency-risk/), meaning fluctuations in exchange rates flow directly through to returns. A rally in the euro benefits holders; a dollar surge during a period of risk-off sentiment can work against them. This unhedged exposure is a deliberate choice that makes FYLD a play on both foreign dividend stocks and foreign-currency movements.

## Costs and trading

FYLD charges an annual [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) in the range typical for active or semi-active [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/). Trading occurs on US [stock exchanges](/stock-exchange/), and the fund maintains reasonable liquidity through its sponsorship and the active management team behind it. The [dividend distributions](/dividend-distribution/) themselves arrive quarterly or semiannually, depending on the payment schedules of underlying holdings, and are taxable to US investors in the year received—a material consideration for accounts outside tax-deferred vehicles.

## Who it is for and the real risks

FYLD appeals to income-focused investors comfortable with international equity exposure and willing to accept [currency volatility](/currency-volatility/). It suits those seeking higher dividend yields than the US market currently offers and [diversification](/diversification/) outside home-country stocks. It is less suitable for investors seeking broad market returns or those uncomfortable holding significant foreign currency exposure.

The fund carries several distinct risks. **Currency risk** is substantial—a strengthening US dollar erodes returns even if the underlying stocks perform well. **Interest-rate sensitivity** affects both the attractiveness of dividends and the valuations of dividend-payers; rising rates can make [bonds](/bond/) more competitive relative to income-paying equities. **Geopolitical and regulatory risk** in Europe and Asia can affect dividend sustainability; governments sometimes tax dividends heavily or even restrict distributions during crises. **[Concentration risk](/concentration-risk/)** emerges because dividend-paying stocks cluster in specific sectors like banking and utilities; an downturn in those sectors ripples through the [fund. **Valuation](/fund-valuation/) risk** applies if the market re-rates dividend stocks as a group—what appears to be a cheap, generous yield today could signal trouble and recompense investors by delivering losses.

## How to research FYLD

Investors should start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, which detail the screening criteria, current top holdings, sector and regional breakdowns, and the expense ratio. Compare the dividend yield against peers—other international dividend ETFs and the broader international equity market—to assess whether the premium or discount is justified. Track the fund's currency exposure by reviewing the geographic allocation and noting which countries' currencies dominate the holdings. Watch whether the dividend distributions remain stable during market stress; a sharp decline could signal underlying weakness. Finally, review the fund sponsor's philosophy and track record on Cambria's other offerings to assess consistency and management credibility. An investor should also monitor [tax-loss harvesting](/tax-loss-harvesting/) opportunities; currency losses in foreign equity positions can offset gains elsewhere in a taxable account.
