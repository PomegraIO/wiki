## The case for short-term bonds

Bond prices move in inverse proportion to [interest rates](/interest-rate/): when rates rise, prices fall; when rates fall, prices rise. A bond that matures in two years experiences much smaller price swings than a bond maturing in twenty years, all else equal. That difference in price sensitivity is called duration. Short-duration bonds are more resistant to rate changes. A conservative investor, or one expecting rising interest rates, might prefer that stability even if the yield is lower.

FTMS takes that principle and applies it to municipal bonds. The fund focuses on municipal bonds due to mature in the near term — typically in the range of two to five years from the time of purchase. This gives investors federal tax-exempt income, the advantage of municipal bonds, but with the lower price volatility of a shorter-maturity profile.

## What FTMS holds

The fund holds a diversified portfolio of investment-grade municipal bonds across numerous issuers and geographies. [General obligation bonds](/general-obligation-bond/) from cities and counties sit alongside [revenue bonds](/revenue-bond/) from utilities and transit authorities. The fund avoids the lowest credit tiers but includes bonds from issuers of varying financial strength, as long as they are investment-grade. The geographic breadth spans the full United States, not focused on any single state, so an investor in any state captures the federal tax benefit.

The emphasis on near-term maturity gives the portfolio a self-cleansing property: each year, bonds age and eventually mature, returning principal. The fund then reinvests those proceeds into new short-maturity bonds, maintaining the short-duration profile. This constant rolling forward means the portfolio is always anchored to the near term, regardless of the broader interest-rate environment.

## Yield, duration, and the investor it suits

The trade-off in FTMS is straightforward: shorter maturity bonds yield less than longer ones. A municipal bond maturing in five years might yield 2.5%, while a similar bond maturing in twenty years yields 3.5%. An investor in FTMS earns the lower yield but avoids the larger price swings that come with longer duration.

That balance appeals to several types of investors. A retiree living off income may prefer a short-duration fund because falling bond prices could force sales at a loss if the portfolio needs to be tapped unexpectedly. An investor concerned that interest rates will rise may buy short-duration bonds to avoid locking in low yields, preferring to reinvest at higher rates as bonds mature. Someone with a known future liability — a college tuition bill in three years, say — can buy FTMS and plan to sell shares as the maturity date approaches, with minimal rate risk in between.

## The cost of capital preservation

The lower yield of short-duration bonds is the price of lower duration risk. In a rising-rate environment, both the fund and a longer-duration fund lose principal value, but the short-duration fund's losses are smaller. In a falling-rate environment, a longer-duration fund gains more. Over a full [market cycle](/market-cycle/), the lower yield of the short-duration fund compounds into a material difference in total return. Investors should be clear about whether the insurance against rate risk is worth the foregone yield.

## Credit and liquidity considerations

FTMS holds investment-grade municipal bonds, so [credit risk](/credit-risk/) is lower than in high-yield municipal funds. A [recession](/recession/) or a specific issuer's financial distress can still cause defaults, but the fund's [diversification](/diversification/) and focus on creditworthy borrowers limit that risk. During an economic downturn, however, even some investment-grade issuers may see [credit ratings](/credit-rating/) downgraded, which can depress prices.

Municipal bonds trade in a less transparent and less liquid market than stocks or [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/). The ETF structure provides daily liquidity — an investor can sell on any trading day — but the underlying bonds might be harder to liquidate quickly without a price concession if the fund needs to sell a large position in a stressed market.

## Tracking the fund

The prospectus details the fund's maturity target range and credit quality criteria. The fact sheet shows the current weighted-average maturity and yield, key metrics for understanding the fund's position.

Investors should monitor the fund's average maturity and credit quality distribution over time. A gradual lengthening of maturity might signal the fund manager drifting from the short-term mandate. A shift toward lower-rated bonds raises credit risk. Compare FTMS's yield to that of other short-term municipal funds and to money-market funds; if yields have compressed sharply, the fund may offer poor value.

In a rising-rate environment, FTMS will outperform longer-duration funds on a price-stability basis but will lag on total return if rates then fall. In a falling-rate environment, the fund will underperform longer-duration alternatives on capital gains. For an investor prioritizing stable prices and predictable, tax-exempt income over total-return maximization, FTMS offers a straightforward vehicle.
