*A **[Real Estate Investment Trust](/real-estate-investment-trust/)** — or REIT — is a company that owns property (office buildings, shopping centres, apartments, warehouses) and must by law distribute most of its taxable income as [dividends](/dividend/) to shareholders. The **Fidelity Real Estate Investment ETF** (FPRO) bundles dozens of these into a single holding, giving an investor exposure to property markets without having to pick individual REITs or own physical real estate.*

Real estate generates income in two ways: rent collected from tenants, and the long-term appreciation of the property itself. A traditional property owner captures both directly but has to manage tenants, repairs, and regulatory compliance. A REIT does that work for you and must distribute ninety per cent of taxable income to shareholders each year, so the cash flow is substantial and reliable. FPRO, managed by Fidelity, holds a diversified portfolio of REITs across property types — residential, commercial office, logistics warehouses, data centres, hotels, healthcare facilities, and others. That [diversification](/diversification/) reduces the bet on any single sector or economic cycle.

The fund is actively managed, meaning Fidelity's team selects and weights individual REITs rather than mechanically replicating an index. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is reasonable for active management, and the fund trades on the [stock exchange](/stock-exchange/) with good liquidity. Most REITs in the fund distribute their required ninety-per-cent payout quarterly or monthly, so FPRO shareholders receive steady income — often higher than the [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/) of the average stock.

The real advantage and the real risk sit at the same table. Real estate income is somewhat recession-resistant because people still need somewhere to live and work needs storage space and logistics hubs. But commercial property cycles are longer than stock-market cycles: office buildings and retail can suffer for years if remote work or online shopping changes tenant demand structurally. [Interest rates](/interest-rate/) matter deeply too, because real-estate values are sensitive to the [discount rate](/discount-rate/) applied to future rents, and REITs often use leverage to finance [acquisitions](/acquisition/) — when rates rise sharply, both the value of properties and the [cost of debt](/cost-of-debt/) climb in ways that can squeeze returns. [Inflation](/inflation/) can help some REITs (rents rise with prices) but hurt others (input costs rise, squeezing margins).

FPRO is a tool for investors who believe real estate will outperform over the long term and want property exposure without the friction of physical ownership. Someone saving for retirement in a tax-deferred account might hold it for the income stream; a younger investor saving in a taxable account must account for the dividend-tax drag, since REIT distributions are taxed as ordinary income, not capital gains. The fund's prospectus details the methodology for selecting holdings, and the fact sheet and holdings list show precisely which REITs and property types are overweighted. A reader researching the fund should monitor broader property-market trends — retail vacancy, office occupancy, multifamily rental rates — because REITs are fundamentally exposed to the health of the underlying real estate.
