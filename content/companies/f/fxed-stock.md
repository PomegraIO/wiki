*The **Sound Enhanced Fixed [Income ETF](/income-etf/)** (ticker FXED, listed on NYSE) emerged from a partnership between First Trust and Sound Shore Trust to offer active management within the [bond](/bond/) markets. Rather than passively tracking a fixed-income index, the fund applies discretionary judgment to select bonds and manage portfolio duration — the time-weighted exposure to [interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/) — with the goal of outperforming a traditional bond benchmark.*

## Origins and the sound-shore approach

Sound Shore Trust is an investment advisor with a history of disciplined fixed-income management rooted in fundamental credit analysis. When the firm partnered with First Trust to launch FXED, it brought that analytical tradition into an ETF wrapper, combining the transparency and daily liquidity of an exchange-traded product with active bond selection.

The fund's philosophy centers on understanding [credit risk](/credit-risk/) — the probability that a bond issuer will pay its obligations and on what terms. Rather than holding all bonds in index proportion, Sound Shore's managers evaluate individual issuers, credit-rating trends, and valuations to decide which bonds to overweight and which to underweight. This allows the fund to tilt toward credits they view as mispriced or safe, away from those they consider overvalued or fragile.

## The fixed-income universe FXED holds

FXED invests across the broad U.S. fixed-income landscape: government bonds, investment-grade [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/), mortgage-backed securities, and related instruments. The fund does not specialize in high-yield [junk bonds](/junk-bond/) or emerging-market debt; it stays within the more liquid, investment-grade core. Within that universe, the managers make active choices about sector and credit exposure.

At any given time, the fund might overweight corporate bonds relative to a benchmark if they believe spreads are attractive, then shift back toward government bonds if corporate credit becomes expensive. It might extend duration — increasing sensitivity to [interest rates](/interest-rate/) — if yields are generous, or shorten duration to reduce rate risk if valuations are stretched. These decisions rest on the portfolio managers' view of the economic outlook, Fed policy, and relative value across the bond market.

## Active management in a passive wrapper

What distinguishes FXED from a typical passive bond [index fund](/index-fund/) is this layer of discretion. A passive [bond ETF](/bond-etf/) simply holds every bond in its index in proportion to market value, making no judgments about credit quality or relative value. Sound Shore's team, by contrast, can tilt the portfolio toward their convictions while still maintaining broad [diversification](/diversification/).

This approach has strengths and weaknesses. When the managers are right — when they correctly anticipate [credit cycles](/credit-cycle/) or spot mispriced bonds before the market reprices them — the fund outperforms. When they are wrong, the fund underperforms by an amount equal to their active bets plus the [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/).

## Cost and trading

FXED charges an expense ratio typically in the range of 0.4% to 0.5% annually, in line with actively managed bond funds but higher than passive bond index funds like those offered by Vanguard or iShares. The fund trades on the NYSE with reasonable liquidity for most investor sizes.

Bond ETFs carry some nuances relative to stock ETFs. Because the underlying bond market is less transparent and less liquid than equity markets, the gap between the ETF's [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) and its trading price can sometimes widen during periods of stress. FXED, holding [investment-grade bonds](/investment-grade-bond/), is less vulnerable to this than a high-yield ETF would be, but it remains a consideration during market dislocations.

## Interest-rate risk and duration management

The primary risk in any bond fund is interest-rate risk. When interest rates rise, bond prices fall; when rates fall, bond prices rise. FXED's duration — a measure of how much the fund's value swings for a given move in rates — fluctuates based on the managers' decisions. A longer duration amplifies both upside and downside in rising-and-falling-rate scenarios.

Credit risk is the second major factor: if an issuer's creditworthiness deteriorates, its bonds lose value regardless of overall interest rates. By actively selecting bonds, FXED's managers aim to avoid the worst credit deteriorations, but they cannot eliminate the risk. A severe [recession](/recession/) or financial crisis can cause even investment-grade bonds to fall in value.

## The evolution from concept to now

When FXED launched, the broader bond market was benefiting from years of low rates and declining interest-rate volatility. The fund's approach — active credit selection combined with duration flexibility — worked well in an environment where managers could generate value by being selective. As rates rose in recent years, the dynamics changed: duration became the dominant driver of returns, and broad rate exposure mattered far more than individual credit bets.

That shift has tested the fund's premise. Going forward, whether Sound Shore's active bond-selection skills can consistently beat a passive benchmark depends on their ability to spot credit opportunities and manage duration tactically in an environment of higher volatility and wider swings in interest rates.

## Research and due diligence

Prospective investors should examine the fund's holdings, sector allocation, and average duration in the prospectus and fact sheet. Comparing FXED's performance against a passive benchmark like the Bloomberg Aggregate Bond Index, and against competitors like Vanguard's Total Bond Market ETF, reveals whether the active layer is adding value or just charging a fee. Understanding the portfolio managers' current view on credit risk, interest rates, and duration positioning helps an investor assess whether the fund's bets align with their own market outlook.

Bond funds are best held as core fixed-income positions in a diversified portfolio; they are not suitable vehicles for tactical duration betting unless the investor has high conviction in interest-rate forecasts.
