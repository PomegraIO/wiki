*The **First Trust Consumer Discretionary AlphaDEX ETF** (ticker FXD, listed on [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/)) holds U.S. equities in the consumer discretionary sector — retailers, restaurants, automakers, luxury goods producers, and entertainment companies — and ranks them using the AlphaDEX methodology. Rather than holding all stocks in equal-weighted or market-cap-weighted fashion, the fund applies a rules-based, factor-driven selection process to overweight those it deems likely to outperform.*

## AlphaDEX as a stock-ranking system

AlphaDEX is First Trust's proprietary methodology for selecting stocks within a given sector. It operates on the principle that certain financial characteristics — such as growth rates, profitability, momentum, valuation, and quality measures — can identify stocks with better risk-adjusted return prospects. The system ranks each company in the consumer discretionary space on these factors, then over-weights those scoring highest and under-weights or excludes those scoring lowest.

This approach sits between pure passive index investing and traditional active management. It is not a human portfolio manager making discretionary calls based on conviction or company visits. Instead, it is a systematic, back-tested process designed to capture [factor premiums](/factor-premium/) — the historical tendency for certain kinds of stocks to outperform others. By focusing on consumer discretionary names with strong growth, improving profitability, or attractive valuations, the fund aims to beat a cap-weighted index of the same sector.

## The consumer discretionary sector inside FXD

Consumer discretionary includes companies whose fortunes rise and fall with consumer spending on non-essentials — restaurants, hotels, clothing retailers, automotive, entertainment, homebuilding, and luxury goods. This sector is cyclical by nature: it thrives in expansions when incomes are rising and unemployment is low, and it suffers in [recessions](/recession/) when consumers cut back on discretionary purchases. Because FXD focuses exclusively on this sector rather than holding a diversified portfolio across all industries, it carries higher cyclical risk than a broad market ETF.

The fund typically holds between 40 and 70 stocks, smaller than a sector index but larger than a concentrated stock-picker's portfolio. The AlphaDEX rules determine which businesses get weighted more heavily. A restaurant chain improving its margins and growing same-store sales might earn a higher ranking; a department store struggling with inventory and declining traffic might score lower. The weighting adjusts regularly as the rankings update, creating turnover in the portfolio.

## Expense ratio and structure

FXD charges an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) around 0.6% to 0.7% annually, a premium to passive sector [index funds](/index-fund/) but reasonable for a rules-based active strategy. The fund trades on NASDAQ with adequate liquidity. Because it uses a systematic methodology rather than hiring a star stock-picker, the fee is lower than a traditional actively managed consumer discretionary fund might charge, while still reflecting the cost of implementing the AlphaDEX screening and rebalancing process.

## Sector concentration and volatility

Holding only consumer discretionary stocks introduces sector risk that a diversified investor might prefer to avoid. Consumer discretionary typically outperforms during the early and middle phases of an economic expansion, when confidence is rising and spending power is strong. It significantly underperforms during recessions and periods of defensive rotation. FXD magnifies this cyclicality; it will outperform the broad market during growth periods and lag during slowdowns.

The factor tilts embedded in AlphaDEX add a second layer of risk. By preferring higher-momentum and higher-growth names, the fund may overweight expensive stocks that are performing well right now but might deflate if growth slows. The focus on profitability and quality attempts to offset this, but sector timing remains a meaningful driver of returns.

## Tax and rebalancing

The fund rebalances periodically as the AlphaDEX rankings shift, creating capital gains and turnover that can generate tax consequences in taxable accounts. The turnover rate is typically moderate to high, meaning it creates more taxable events than a static index fund would. For tax-sheltered retirement accounts, this is immaterial; for taxable investing, it reduces after-tax returns and warrants consideration.

## Research and evaluation

Anyone considering FXD should review the fund's holdings and factor methodology in the prospectus, then compare its sector composition against a passive consumer discretionary index like the Vanguard Consumer Discretionary ETF. The AlphaDEX process is documented publicly; understanding which factors it emphasizes most heavily can reveal whether those factor tilts align with an investor's market outlook.

Because the fund's performance is tied to both the consumer discretionary sector's cyclical health and the specific factor choices embedded in AlphaDEX, evaluating it requires looking at both the sector's position in the [business cycle](/business-cycle/) and the factors' recent performance. An investor bullish on consumer spending and confident in momentum as a stock-selection factor might find FXD appealing; one concerned about recession or skeptical of factor-based screens might prefer a diversified broad-market approach.
