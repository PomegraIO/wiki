*The **Franklin Municipal [Income ETF](/income-etf/)** (FTMU) is an exchange-traded fund that holds a diverse portfolio of investment-grade [municipal bonds](/municipal-bond/) across the United States, offering tax-exempt interest income without geographic restriction or special credit-quality focus — a plain-vanilla approach to municipal [bond](/bond/) investing through an ETF wrapper.*

Municipal bonds are the evergreen play for a US investor seeking income that escapes the federal tax collector. The idea is simple: lend money to a city, county, or state to finance schools, roads, water systems, and other public works. The borrower repays with interest, and that interest is exempt from federal income tax — a benefit the federal government grants to make such borrowing cheaper and easier. FTMU packages that benefit into an exchange-traded fund.

The fund's approach is agnostic. It holds bonds from across the country, across different sectors — general government, utilities, transportation, education — and across the maturity spectrum. The sole constraint is credit quality: the fund focuses on [investment-grade bonds](/investment-grade-bond/), those rated A and above by major rating agencies. This keeps [credit risk](/credit-risk/) manageable without tilting the portfolio toward any particular state, city, or sector.

The advantage of that breadth is simplicity and [diversification](/diversification/). An investor who wants tax-exempt income but has no strong conviction about which region of the country offers the best risk-reward can use FTMU as a one-fund core holding. Unlike state-specific funds such as those targeting Minnesota or New Jersey, FTMU has no state tax exemption benefit, only federal. For any resident outside a handful of high-income-tax states, that loss is small; the flexibility and diversification offset it.

[Franklin Templeton](/fgdl-stock/), as the issuer, manages the fund's portfolio by selecting which municipal bonds to buy and hold, aiming to optimize the balance between yield and credit quality. The fund trades on an exchange like any stock, and an investor can buy or sell shares at the closing price on any trading day. The ETF structure keeps costs low compared to a traditional actively managed municipal bond [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/).

The fund's yield reflects both the interest paid by the underlying bonds and the credit quality mix. In a strong economy where borrowing is cheap, municipal bond yields compress, and FTMU's yield falls. In a [recession](/recession/) or when rates rise sharply, yields widen, and the fund may offer more attractive income. An investor should compare FTMU's yield against short-term municipal funds and longer-duration alternatives to assess whether it sits attractively in the [yield curve](/yield-curve/) at any given time.

Duration — the fund's sensitivity to interest-rate changes — is typically moderate. A portfolio of bonds maturing across a range of years will have an intermediate duration, perhaps six to eight years. That means a one-percentage-point rise in rates produces a price decline of six to eight percent, and a one-percentage-point fall produces a comparable gain. For an investor holding the fund to maturity or with a long time horizon, those intermediate-term price swings should be navigable. For someone who might need the money soon, a shorter-duration fund like the Franklin Short-Term Municipal Income ETF could be preferable.

Credit risk is managed but real. Investment-grade bonds do default, and a severe recession can push some issuers toward insolvency. The fund's diversification across hundreds of issuers and geographic regions limits the damage from any single default. Still, a broad credit-quality decline during a significant economic contraction could impair the fund's value. The prospectus details the credit-quality composition and the fund's approach to assessing credit risk.

Municipal bonds trade in an [over-the-counter market](/over-the-counter-market/), less transparent and less liquid than [stock exchanges](/stock-exchange/) or [corporate bond](/corporate-bond/) markets. FTMU solves that with an ETF wrapper, enabling daily liquidity for shareholders. The underlying bonds might be harder to trade in size without a price concession, but the fund structure gives individual investors access to that difficult-to-trade asset class at reasonable cost.

For a high-income earner in a taxable account, FTMU provides a straightforward vehicle for capturing tax-exempt income. The fund avoids the complexity of picking individual bonds and the risk of concentrating in a single state, sector, or issuer. It also sidesteps the higher credit risk of below-investment-grade municipal funds, suitable for an investor prioritizing capital preservation alongside income. For a non-high-income earner, or for holding inside a tax-deferred account, the tax exemption's value evaporates, and other income vehicles may be preferable. For any investor considering FTMU, the starting point is the prospectus and the fund's fact sheet, which detail the [current yield](/current-yield/), duration, credit quality, and top holdings. Comparing FTMU's yield and duration to other municipal funds — state-specific, shorter-duration, or higher-yield alternatives — frames where it fits in a portfolio strategy.
