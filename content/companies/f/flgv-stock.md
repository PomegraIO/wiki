*The **Franklin U.S. [Treasury Bond](/treasury-bond/) ETF (FLGV)** is a fund that holds a diversified portfolio of [bonds](/bond/) issued by the US government, giving investors liquid access to the full range of Treasury maturities without having to build or rebalance the holdings individually.*

## What exactly does FLGV hold?

FLGV aims to track the Bloomberg U.S. Treasury Index, which encompasses Treasury securities across the maturity spectrum — from short-term bills to long-term bonds stretching into decades. The fund does not concentrate in any single maturity; instead, it distributes holdings proportionally based on the market weight of each segment. This means the fund is tilted toward longer-dated Treasuries, because those represent the largest portion of the outstanding Treasury market by dollar value. The net effect is a portfolio with a [modified duration](/modified-duration/) somewhere in the intermediate-to-long range, depending on the [interest rate](/interest-rate/) environment and shifts in the Treasury [yield curve](/yield-curve/).

## Why hold Treasuries in an ETF rather than buying them directly?

An individual investor can buy Treasury bonds directly from the government with no middleman through the TreasuryDirect program, but FLGV offers something neither direct purchase nor a Treasury ladder can easily provide: instant [diversification](/diversification/) across the entire curve and continuous rebalancing. When one Treasury matures, the ETF reinvests proceeds automatically. If the composition of the index shifts — because older bonds mature or the weighting rules change — the fund adjusts its holdings to stay aligned. For someone who wants Treasuries but does not want to do the custodial work of managing dozens of individual securities, an ETF is cleaner and far cheaper than paying a financial advisor to do it. FLGV also remains liquid on the [stock exchange](/stock-exchange/) during market hours, so an investor can exit the position instantly rather than waiting for Treasuries to mature.

The trade-off is that FLGV charges a small annual fee (an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/)) where TreasuryDirect is free. For investors who plan to hold Treasuries for years, direct purchase may be cheaper; for those who want flexibility and automatic rebalancing, the ETF's convenience justifies the cost.

## How does interest rate movement affect this fund?

Because FLGV holds longer-dated bonds, its value rises when interest rates fall (bond prices move inversely to rates) and falls when rates rise. The size of those moves is captured in the metric called **duration** — a measure of how sensitive a bond or bond portfolio is to interest rate changes. A Treasury fund with a five-year duration means that if rates rise by one percentage point, the fund's [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) will decline by roughly five percent. This is not a loss if the investor holds until the bonds mature or rates decline again, but it is a real mark-to-market loss in the interim.

The flip side is that when rates are falling, a duration-heavy fund like FLGV delivers gains as bond prices rise. The performance over any given year depends enormously on the direction and volatility of interest rates, not on the underlying credit quality of Treasuries (which is unquestioned).

## What are the actual risks with US Treasuries?

The [credit risk](/credit-risk/) — the chance that the US government will default — is universally considered negligible. Treasuries are backed by the full faith and credit of the federal government and denominated in dollars that the government issues, making default almost unimaginable within a market economy context.

The real risks are interest rate risk (the portfolio value swings with rates) and, for a non-US investor, [currency risk](/currency-risk/) (if the dollar weakens, the value of Treasury holdings shrinks in their home currency). For a dollar-based investor, the material concern is [inflation risk](/inflation-risk/): if prices rise faster than the yield on Treasuries, the real (inflation-adjusted) return turns negative. This is a chronic concern when Treasuries offer very low yields, and it is why many investors pair Treasuries with other assets rather than holding them alone as a complete portfolio.

## How liquid is FLGV, and what does that cost?

FLGV trades on stock exchanges with tight [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) and high trading volume, so exiting a position is nearly instantaneous at a price close to the true value of the underlying bonds. The annualized expense ratio is very low — in the ballpark of 0.05–0.10%, depending on the specific fund structure, making it competitive with competing Treasury ETFs. This low cost is feasible because Treasuries are highly liquid assets, trading continuously in massive volume, and the index is simple and mechanical to replicate.

The fund distributes income (the [coupon payments](/coupon-payment/) from the bonds) regularly, typically monthly, so investors receive steady cash flow. Reinvesting those distributions or using them as spending money is a choice left to the investor.

## How is FLGV distinct from other Treasury options?

The Treasury market is deep and transparent, so competing Treasury ETFs from other sponsors — such as the Vanguard Total Bond Market ETF or the iShares core Treasury ETF — are functionally very similar to FLGV. The choice between them usually hinges on expense ratio, which moves in basis points, and personal preference for the sponsor. Franklin Templeton's version is a competent, straightforward implementation that does what it says it will do. An investor choosing between Treasury ETF options should compare costs first and then consider fund size and trading volume; the underlying holdings and returns will be nearly identical.

## How would a researcher evaluate this fund?

Start with the fund's official prospectus and fact sheet, available on Franklin Templeton's website, which lay out the exact index being tracked, the holdings, the expense ratio, and the ongoing yield. Compare the fund's duration against your own time horizon and interest rate outlook — if you think rates are near a bottom, the duration risk may be unattractive; if you think they will fall, longer duration is rewarded. Check the bid-ask spread on the exchange and the average daily trading volume to confirm liquidity matches your trading size. Finally, review how the fund's price has moved during rising-rate environments (such as 2022–2023) to see whether the duration sensitivity aligns with what the fund's profile suggests. Treasuries are straightforward enough that understanding FLGV requires understanding Treasuries themselves — and that foundation applies to almost any Treasury fund available.
