Nomura Transformational Technologies ETF (ticker FRWD) holds stocks in companies that are reshaping business and society through emerging technologies. Rather than betting on a single hot trend or picking tech stocks by gut feel, the fund uses a rules-based screening process to identify publicly traded companies working in artificial intelligence, cloud computing, robotics, digital payments, cybersecurity, biotechnology, and related fields. The fund's basic premise is straightforward: transformational technologies drive long-term returns, and a systematic approach to selecting companies involved in these shifts is better than chasing today's favorites.

## What the fund holds and why it matters

FRWD does not hold every tech stock or every large-cap company. Instead, it screens the global stock market using criteria designed to identify companies at the center of transformational shifts. The screening looks for firms where technology is the core business or a critical strategic advantage, and where the technology itself is creating value in measurable ways. This might be a software company selling enterprise cloud services, a semiconductor maker designing chips for artificial intelligence, a biotech firm developing diagnostic tools, or a fintech platform processing digital payments.

The result is a portfolio that might include large established software companies, smaller growth-stage tech firms, and non-tech companies that are transforming themselves through technology adoption. What ties them together is not sector or market capitalization but rather the role emerging technology plays in their business model and their competitive positioning.

## Costs and trading characteristics

FRWD charges an expense ratio in the range of 0.65 to 0.75 percent annually, which is reasonable for a thematic ETF with a proprietary screening methodology. The fund trades on NASDAQ with good liquidity and spreads typical of moderately sized equity ETFs. Holdings are published regularly and the fund's selection rules do not change arbitrarily, which means an investor can understand what drives the portfolio.

The fund rebalances periodically to maintain its tilts, which introduces moderate turnover. This is typical of systematic screening approaches and keeps costs predictable.

## The risks of thematic concentration

A thematic fund like FRWD carries specific risks worth understanding. First, it concentrates risk into a single theme: transformational technology. When investors are excited about tech innovation, the fund may outperform the broader market. When tech stocks fall out of favour or when valuations become extreme, FRWD may underperform significantly. This makes it a satellite holding, not a core equity position, suitable for investors with conviction about the long-term importance of technological change.

Second, rapid change in technology means today's transformational tech can become obsolete. A screening process that identifies important emerging technologies in one year might be holding companies threatened by newer innovations five years later. The fund's rules do attempt to address this by focusing on broader technology themes rather than betting on single platforms or companies, but the risk remains.

Third, many transformational technologies are concentrated in the developed world, particularly the United States. While the fund includes global holdings, geography will tilt toward the markets with the most active technology innovation and capital markets. This means currency exposure and the risk of underperformance if non-U.S. markets outpace the U.S.

Finally, thematic funds often carry higher valuations than the broader market, since investors pay premiums for exposure to growth themes. A reversal in sentiment toward expensive growth stocks hits thematic funds particularly hard.

## Who FRWD serves and how to research it

FRWD is designed for growth-oriented investors who believe emerging and transformational technologies will create disproportionate investment returns over long periods and who are comfortable accepting volatility to gain that exposure. It is not for conservative investors, income seekers, or those uncomfortable with concentration risk in the tech sector. The fund works best as a satellite position in a diversified portfolio, not as a core equity holding.

To research the fund, start with the prospectus and the index methodology from Nomura, which lay out the exact screening criteria. The fund publishes its top holdings and sector breakdowns, which give a sense of what categories of technology it emphasizes at any moment. An investor should review the historical performance of the fund during periods when technology stocks underperformed (the bear markets in 2000–2002, 2008–2009, and 2022) to understand how much drawdown they would have endured, and should form a view about whether the premium valuations embedded in tech stocks are justified by future growth. Past performance is not predictive of future results, but understanding how similar tech-focused funds behaved in prior cycles provides valuable context.
