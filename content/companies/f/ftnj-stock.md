*The **Franklin New Jersey Municipal [Income ETF](/income-etf/)** (FTNJ) is an exchange-traded fund holding [municipal bonds](/municipal-bond/) issued by New Jersey cities, counties, school districts, and authorities, offering New Jersey residents both federal and state income-tax exemption on the [bond](/bond/) interest.*

## Why does New Jersey get its own fund?

The answer lies in tax policy. A bond issued by a New Jersey municipality generates interest that is exempt from U.S. federal income tax. It is also exempt from New Jersey state income tax. For a New Jersey resident in a high federal and state tax bracket, that double exemption is valuable. A New Jersey municipal bond yielding 2.8% after all taxes might be worth more than a national municipal bond yielding 3.2% or a [corporate bond](/corporate-bond/) yielding 4%, once you factor in the investor's federal and New Jersey state tax burdens.

FTNJ lets a New Jersey investor capture that double benefit without hand-picking individual New Jersey bonds. Instead of researching the creditworthiness of Newark, the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey, or a New Jersey school district, the investor can buy a share of the fund and own a diversified slice of the New Jersey municipal market.

## What the fund owns

FTNJ holds municipal bonds issued by New Jersey entities: cities and towns, counties, school districts, utility commissions, port authorities, and other local agencies and quasi-governmental bodies. The fund focuses on [investment-grade bonds](/investment-grade-bond/), typically A-rated and above, from issuers with stable finances and reliable revenue sources. [General obligation bonds](/general-obligation-bond/) backed by property taxes sit alongside [revenue bonds](/revenue-bond/) backed by user fees or specific revenue streams.

The fund spans New Jersey's geography and economic base. Bonds from Newark, Jersey City, and Trenton — the largest cities — may appear alongside bonds from smaller municipalities and special districts. The portfolio avoids any single issuer dominance; holdings are spread across many credits so that one municipality's financial distress does not impair the whole fund.

## The yield-tax arithmetic

This is where FTNJ's appeal becomes concrete. Suppose a New Jersey resident in the top federal bracket and the top New Jersey state bracket is considering where to earn fixed income. A New Jersey municipal bond yielding 2.8% produces 2.8% tax-free to that resident. A national municipal bond yielding 3.1% still gets hit with federal and New Jersey state taxes. A taxable corporate bond yielding 4.2% gets hit harder still. For the resident, the after-tax yield on the New Jersey bond often exceeds the after-tax yield on the higher-yielding alternatives.

FTNJ makes that calculation simpler than buying individual bonds. A single retail investor buying a New Jersey municipal bond faces [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/), minimum purchase requirements, and the need to assess credit quality alone. An ETF pools thousands of investors and thousands of bonds, lowering costs and spreading [credit risk](/credit-risk/).

For a non-resident of New Jersey, FTNJ is rarely attractive. The New Jersey state tax exemption is lost, leaving only the federal exemption. Other funds might offer higher yields or better geographic [diversification](/diversification/) at a lower cost.

## Duration and interest-rate risk

FTNJ holds bonds across a range of maturities — some due in a few years, others due in ten or twenty years. The weighted-average maturity is typically six to ten years, giving the fund intermediate duration. When [interest rates](/interest-rate/) rise, bond prices fall; the fund's price falls with them. When rates fall, prices rise. For a New Jersey investor planning to hold FTNJ long-term and reinvest interest income, those price swings are academic — the fund will ultimately be held to maturity or replaced, and interim price fluctuations do not matter. For an investor who might need to sell soon, rising rates could force a loss.

## Credit quality in the New Jersey context

New Jersey municipalities vary in financial health. Cities like Montclair and suburbs of New York and Philadelphia have strong tax bases. Others face aging infrastructure, declining populations, or structural budget challenges. The fund's investment-grade filter keeps it away from the most troubled credits, but some holding can and do deteriorate during [recessions](/recession/) or through mismanagement. The fund's diversification across issuers limits any single default's impact, but a significant [credit event](/credit-event-sovereign/) in a large New Jersey city — or a recession that weakens many issuers at once — can impair the fund's returns.

## Who should own FTNJ

FTNJ is designed for New Jersey residents with high federal and state income-tax liability who want a portion of their portfolio in tax-exempt income. A retired New Jersey resident living on portfolio income, or a high-income professional, is the archetypal investor. Someone planning to remain in New Jersey long-term should find the fund most valuable; the double tax exemption justifies holding New Jersey bonds despite any yield or diversification cost.

A New Jersey resident planning to move away should reconsider FTNJ. Once you leave the state, the New Jersey tax exemption is gone, and the fund becomes less attractive. A national municipal fund might be preferable.

For a non-resident, FTNJ offers little advantage over a national or broader-based New Jersey does not offer a particularly high yield compared to other high-tax states, so residents of other states searching for maximum income should look elsewhere.

## Researching FTNJ

Start with the prospectus on the [Franklin Templeton](/fgdl-stock/) website. It details which New Jersey issuers the fund may hold and the credit-quality standards. The fact sheet shows the [current yield](/current-yield/), average maturity, and percentage allocation to each major issuer type — cities, schools, utilities, etc.

Monitor the fund's holdings regularly. Large holdings in specific municipalities should be reviewed for credit news: budget balancing, infrastructure deterioration, or management changes. If a holding city faces a fiscal crisis, the fund's price could fall sharply. Compare FTNJ's yield to that of other New Jersey muni funds and to national alternatives; wide differences might signal value or risk.

For New Jersey residents in the high-tax brackets, FTNJ offers low-cost, tax-efficient income in a familiar geographic context. For others, it is likely not the optimal choice.
