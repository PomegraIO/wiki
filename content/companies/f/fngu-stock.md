FNGU is a leveraged exchange-traded note issued by [Credit Suisse](/credit-suisse/) that amplifies the daily returns of the FANG+ index by a factor of three. The FANG+ index itself comprises ten large-cap technology and internet companies: Facebook (now Meta), [Apple](/aapl-stock/), [Netflix](/nflx-stock/), Google ([Alphabet](/googl-stock/)), Amazon, [Microsoft](/msft-stock/), [Tesla](/tsla-stock/), [Nvidia](/nvda-stock/), and the Magnificent Seven's two newer additions. FNGU uses daily rebalancing to maintain its 3X multiplier, meaning the note resets each trading day to aim at triple the index's one-day return. This structure makes it a tool for traders seeking leveraged exposure to a specific basket of mega-cap technology firms, not a buy-and-hold investment.

## What FNGU tracks and how it moves

The FANG+ index excludes the P in FANG (no [Berkshire Hathaway](/brk-b-stock/)) and focuses on the ten-stock list above. These are all multinational, profitable technology companies with [deep market](/deep-market/) presence and substantial trading volume. FNGU's goal is simple: if the FANG+ index rises 1% on a given day, FNGU aims to rise 3%. If the index falls 1%, FNGU aims to fall 3%. This multiplication happens daily, which means each trading session the note resets and begins fresh chasing a new triple of the index's return.

The appeal is mechanical: a trader who expects immediate, sharp gains in mega-cap technology can use FNGU as leverage without needing to borrow on margin. The leverage is built in. A $10,000 investment in FNGU captures the economic exposure of roughly $30,000 invested directly in the underlying index — minus fees and the cost of the leverage structure itself.

## How leverage decay works and why timing matters

The daily reset creates a phenomenon called volatility decay. On any given day, the math is clean: 3X multiplier, 3X the return. But over a week, a month, or a year, the path of returns matters enormously. If an index rises 10% then falls 10%, it ends where it started — a round trip with zero net return. A 3X leveraged note in the same scenario, however, would rise 30% then fall 30%, ending at 91% of where it began (because the 30% loss is calculated on the higher base). The longer an investor holds FNGU, the more decay erodes the returns relative to tripling the index's buy-and-hold return.

This is why FNGU is marketed for traders, not long-term investors. A trader making a directional bet on technology for a single day or a few days faces minimal decay. A holder planning to own it for six months or a year is almost certain to see decay drag down the final return compared with simply tripling the buy-and-hold gain of the FANG+ index. In strong, one-directional rallies the math works; in choppy, sideways, or reversing markets it works against you.

## Costs, credit risk, and liquidity

FNGU carries an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) of roughly 1% per year, which is higher than most plain-vanilla index ETFs but not unusual for leveraged products. This fee covers the issuer's costs of maintaining and rebalancing the note and managing the derivatives contracts that create the leverage.

As an exchange-traded note, FNGU also carries issuer [credit risk](/credit-risk/): the note is ultimately a debt obligation of Credit Suisse, not a basket of physical securities held in trust. If the issuer were to fail, note holders would have a claim on its estate alongside all other unsecured creditors. This risk is typically small for a major bank in ordinary circumstances, but it is not zero — it is the tradeoff for the simplicity of the leveraged structure. A conventional [leveraged ETF](/leveraged-etf/), by contrast, would hold actual securities and use options or futures for leverage, passing that risk to the derivatives counterparties rather than the fund sponsor.

FNGU trades on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) with reasonable liquidity given its size, though volume is far lower than the underlying FANG+ stocks themselves. Wide [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) can emerge in [thin markets](/thin-market/), particularly at the open or close of the trading day.

## Who should own FNGU and how to research it

FNGU is suitable only for traders with a specific, near-term directional conviction about the FANG+ stocks and a high tolerance for drawdowns. The document to read first is the prospectus filed with the SEC, which lays out the leverage mechanism, the daily reset process, the fee structure, and the full risk disclosures around volatility decay and issuer credit. The fund also publishes fact sheets describing the composition of the FANG+ index and the recent path of the note's value.

Any prospective buyer should model what happens to a leveraged investment over multi-week and multi-month [holding periods](/holding-period/) under different market scenarios. The moment-to-moment price of FNGU tells you nothing about the drift of decay; you must think in time horizons. And because leverage amplifies losses as much as gains, [position sizing](/position-sizing/) is critical — a trader should never commit capital they cannot afford to lose entirely, nor should they let a 30 or 40% drawdown push them into panic selling, as that crystallizes the loss exactly when they should be managing the position with discipline.
