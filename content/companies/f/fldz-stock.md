*RiverNorth Patriot ETF (FLDZ) is an exchange-traded [closed-end fund](/closed-end-fund/) that combines US large-cap equities with a thematic emphasis on American corporate heritage and operations, paired with systematic covered-call option strategies to generate additional income for shareholders.*

## The core holding strategy

FLDZ tracks a curated portfolio of large-cap US companies selected partly for market weight and partly for alignment with criteria the fund's managers frame as patriotic — companies headquartered and primarily operating within the United States with deep roots in American economic history. This is not a pure index replication; the fund applies active selection and thematic weighting rather than mechanically following the Standard & Poor 500.

The portfolio is equity-heavy, holding primarily [common stocks](/common-stock/) of companies across technology, industrials, financials, healthcare, and consumer sectors. The fund does not exclude international revenue or operations; most large US corporations earn substantial portions of their sales abroad. Rather, the filter is on incorporation, management location, and the company's primary strategic focus.

Large-cap companies dominate the holdings because they provide liquidity, stability, and the scale needed to support the fund's derivative strategies. Small and mid-cap stocks, whilst offering growth potential, often lack the option-market depth that FLDZ's income strategy requires.

## Income generation through covered calls

The defining feature of FLDZ is its use of covered-call option writing. Each quarter, the fund sells [call options](/call-option/) on a portion of its stock holdings at [strike prices](/strike-price/) above current market levels. Buyers of these calls pay the fund a premium for the right to purchase the shares at the strike price. If the stock price rises above the strike before expiration, the shares are called away and the fund realises a gain. If the price stays below the strike, the option expires worthless and the fund keeps the premium.

This strategy has three effects. First, it generates recurring [option premium](/option-premium/) income on top of [dividends](/dividend/) and capital appreciation, boosting the fund's yield. Second, it introduces a ceiling on upside if shares surge sharply — the fund's gains are capped once shares are called away. Third, it creates tax implications for shareholders: option premiums are typically treated as short-term capital gains rather than dividends, which can affect the tax efficiency of the fund relative to a pure buy-and-hold equity strategy.

[Covered calls](/covered-call/) work best in flat or moderately rising markets. They dampen losses in sharp declines because the premium received provides a partial cushion. In explosive [bull markets](/bull-market/), the capped upside becomes a drag on total returns compared to an unhedged equity position.

## Fund structure and costs

FLDZ is a closed-end fund, which means the number of shares outstanding is fixed. Investors buy and sell shares on an exchange like a stock, and the share price can trade at a premium or discount to the fund's underlying [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) depending on supply and demand. This is different from an open-end [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/), where shares are created and redeemed continuously at net asset value.

Closed-end funds often trade at discounts to net asset value because they appeal to a narrower investor base and typically have lower trading volumes than open-end competitors. The discount fluctuates over time and can create opportunities for value-conscious investors, but it also means the fund is not a transparent proxy for its holdings — the traded price may differ meaningfully from what a dollar's worth of holdings is actually worth.

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is meaningful to monitor. Income-generating closed-end funds using derivative strategies typically carry higher costs than passive equity [index funds](/index-fund/), though not dramatically so. The costs reflect active management, the cost of executing option trades, and the smaller asset base spread across overhead.

## Yield and distribution

Distributions are paid regularly, often monthly or quarterly, and the fund aims for a yield higher than the underlying large-cap market would deliver alone. This attractiveness to income-focused investors comes at the cost of the upside cap imposed by the covered-call strategy. Shareholders receive both the dividend income paid by the underlying stocks and the option premiums the fund captures.

Because the fund is tax-inefficient in some respects — option premium income is short-term capital gain — it is most suitable for tax-deferred accounts like IRAs or pension plans where distributions are not taxed annually.

## Key risks and constraints

The primary risk is opportunity cost. In years when large-cap US equities deliver strong capital appreciation, FLDZ will lag because its call options are exercised or prevent the fund from capturing the full upside. Over full [market cycles](/market-cycle/) this underperformance can be material.

[Liquidity risk](/liquidity-risk/) applies to closed-end funds trading at wide discounts. If the fund becomes unpopular or assets shrink, the [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) on the fund's shares themselves can widen, making it expensive to enter or exit a position.

Sector concentration is a secondary consideration. The fund's patriotic theme imposes implicit biases toward sectors that are numerically dominant in the US large-cap universe — technology and financials — and may underweight or exclude sectors like energy or commodities that some view as equally American.

The covered-call strategy introduces complexity and tax drag. Investors who do not understand that the premium income is taxed as short-term gains rather than eligible dividends may be disappointed by the after-tax return.

## How to research and evaluate FLDZ

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, available from RiverNorth Advisors and the fund's official website. These documents detail the selection criteria, the covered-call methodology, the current allocation by sector and company, and the tax treatment of distributions.

Compare the fund's total return (including reinvested distributions) to the [S&P 500 index](/sp-500-index/) over 1-year, 3-year, 5-year, and full-history periods. The comparison reveals whether the covered-call premium income and selection strategy have truly enhanced returns or merely redistributed them. Pay special attention to the fund's performance during sharp market declines, where the premium cushion should reduce the fund's losses relative to an unhedged index.

Monitor the fund's current discount or premium to net asset value, listed on the exchange data feeds. A large discount suggests the market is indifferent or pessimistic about the strategy; a premium suggests high demand. Neither guarantees future returns, but the discount-to-NAV trend can signal changing investor sentiment.

Finally, assess the fit within your portfolio. FLDZ is most attractive to investors in high tax brackets who hold it in tax-deferred accounts and value current income over growth. For younger investors or those in low tax brackets, a standard equity index fund may be more efficient.
