*The **[T. Rowe Price](/t-rowe-price/) Innovation Leaders ETF** (TNXT) is an [actively managed fund](/actively-managed-fund/) holding leading companies in technology, healthcare, and other sectors that T. Rowe Price's managers believe are driving structural innovation and are positioned for long-term growth, with an emphasis on large-cap, established leaders rather than early-stage or speculative firms.*

T. Rowe Price's definition of innovation is deliberately pragmatic. The fund does not chase hot IPOs or unprofitable pre-revenue startups. Instead, it focuses on mature, profitable companies with strong competitive advantages in areas where secular demand is growing — artificial intelligence, cloud computing, life sciences, biotechnology, digital commerce, semiconductor design, and similar domains. The fund's managers look for businesses that are both benefiting from innovation trends and, crucially, have the resources to lead rather than follow.

### Concentration in technology and healthcare

The fund's largest sector exposures are technology and healthcare, which together typically account for more than two-thirds of the portfolio. Within technology, TNXT tends to hold mega-cap cloud computing companies, semiconductor leaders, and software giants that serve as infrastructure for the digital economy. Within healthcare, the fund favors large-cap pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies with robust pipelines and pricing power, as well as medical-device makers benefiting from aging populations and procedure growth in emerging markets.

These two sectors are chosen not arbitrarily but because they align with multi-decade demographic and technological shifts: aging in developed countries driving healthcare spending, digital transformation across all industries driving cloud and enterprise software demand, and artificial intelligence driving semiconductor and semiconductor-equipment demand. A fund built around these sectors is implicitly betting that these trends will persist over the long term.

### Exposure to digitalization and automation

A secondary theme running through TNXT is enablement of business digitalization and automation. The fund holds companies providing the tools and platforms others use to run digital operations — cloud infrastructure, data analytics, cybersecurity, and enterprise software. These businesses benefit from multi-year digital transformation spending by corporations across sectors, and their customer bases include firms in every industry. This thematic breadth reduces the fund's dependence on any single [market cycle](/market-cycle/).

### International diversification within large-cap innovation

Although the fund is denominated in [U.S. dollars](/us-dollar/) and listed in the U.S., it is not purely U.S.-centric. T. Rowe Price's managers can and do hold foreign domiciled companies — particularly in semiconductors, life sciences, and fintech — that are leaders in their fields. This allows the fund to avoid missing opportunities in countries where innovation is genuinely driving the company, not just where it happens to be listed.

### Active management and concentration

TNXT is actively managed, so its holdings are selected by T. Rowe Price's portfolio team rather than tracked against an index. This typically results in greater concentration than a passive fund: the fund may hold 50 to 80 stocks rather than 100+, reflecting the managers' highest-conviction ideas. This concentration can amplify returns when the team's bets are right but can also increase volatility and the risk of significant underperformance if the team's views prove wrong.

The actively managed structure also allows the fund to shift the portfolio as new opportunities emerge and old ones fade. If a company that was once a leader in innovation stalls, managers can reduce or exit the position rather than being mechanically locked into it by an index definition. Conversely, if a company's innovation narrative changes for the worse, the fund is not forced to hold it to completion.

### Performance implications and volatility

Because TNXT focuses on growth and innovation rather than value or [dividends](/dividend/), it tends to have higher volatility than the broad market. The fund includes relatively young companies that trade on earnings growth expectations rather than current cash flow, so sentiment shifts and interest-rate moves can trigger sharp drawdowns. In periods when growth is favored and [interest rates](/interest-rate/) are falling, TNXT can significantly outperform; in periods when value is favored or rates are rising, the fund can lag or decline sharply.

The absence of a dividend is another implication: TNXT returns are dependent on capital appreciation, not yield, so it is best suited for investors with a long time horizon and the ability to tolerate multi-year periods of mark-to-market losses. For retirees seeking current income, the fund is a poor fit.

### Expense ratio and structure

As an actively managed fund, TNXT carries a higher [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) than a passive technology or growth [index fund](/index-fund/) would. The fee structure should be reviewed in the prospectus to verify whether the fund's historical excess return (outperformance of its benchmark or peer group) has been sufficient to justify the cost.

## How to research TNXT

Start by reviewing T. Rowe Price's prospectus and fact sheet, which outline the fund's investment philosophy, its definition of "innovation," sector and geographic allocation targets, and the types of companies eligible for the portfolio. Examine the fund's current top 10 holdings and sector breakdown to understand whether the current positioning aligns with your own view of innovation and growth.

Compare TNXT's performance to relevant benchmarks — such as the [NASDAQ Composite](/nasdaq-composite/) (which is technology-heavy) or a growth-oriented index — over trailing periods of three, five, and ten years. This comparison shows whether active management has added value. Watch the fund's turnover rate; high turnover (frequent buying and selling) can erode returns through trading costs and taxes if held in a taxable account.

Monitor T. Rowe Price's quarterly or semi-annual commentary on the fund, which typically discusses how the managers view major innovation trends and where they see opportunity. This narrative helps you understand the fund's bets and whether you agree with them. Finally, because the fund has no dividend, focus on total return (capital appreciation plus any distributions, which are typically small), not yield, when assessing performance.
