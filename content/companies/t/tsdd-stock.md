What does this fund do?

TSDD is an inverse leveraged exchange-traded fund issued by GraniteShares. It aims to deliver twice the inverse daily performance of Tesla stock. If Tesla stock falls 1% on a given day, TSDD should rise approximately 2%. Conversely, if Tesla rises 1%, TSDD should fall about 2%. The fund is structured specifically for daily rebalancing and is intended as a short-term tactical instrument, not a buy-and-hold position. Investors typically hold TSDD for periods of hours, days, or weeks, not months or years.

How does the daily-reset mechanics work?

A leveraged inverse fund does not simply multiply the longer-term inverse return by its leverage multiple. Instead, it resets daily. At the close of each trading day, the fund is rebalanced so that it holds the exact derivative positions (typically through equity index futures and options) needed to deliver -2x the next day's Tesla moves. This daily reset is crucial to understanding the fund's actual behavior. Over time, if Tesla stock trends in one direction while experiencing normal volatility, TSDD's cumulative return will diverge from -2x the cumulative Tesla return—often unfavorably for TSDD holders. This divergence, called volatility decay or path dependency, is the central risk of inverse leveraged products.

What is volatility decay, and why does it matter?

Suppose Tesla stock rises and falls repeatedly around a stable long-term level—a common pattern in choppy markets. TSDD resets every day, so on days Tesla falls, TSDD rises by -2x, and on days Tesla rises, TSDD falls by 2x. Over time, the day-to-day volatility of Tesla means TSDD loses money relative to a simple -2x inverse return, because the losses on up days compound faster than the gains on down days. In extreme cases, an inverse leveraged ETF can approach zero even if the underlying stock has not changed significantly in price. This is not a market anomaly—it is simple mathematics. A -2x daily reset product held across weeks or months in a volatile underlying is almost certain to underperform its theoretical -2x inverse return, often by a meaningful amount.

Who should own TSDD, and for how long?

TSDD is designed for sophisticated traders and portfolio managers using it tactically to hedge a long Tesla position or to profit from an expected near-term decline in Tesla stock price. The holding period should be measured in trading days or weeks, not months. An investor who believes Tesla will fall 10% and holds TSDD for the duration expects to profit if the decline happens quickly. An investor who buys TSDD expecting to profit from a months-long bear case in Tesla is exposing themselves to volatility decay and is almost certainly better served by a simple short position, a put option, or a non-leveraged inverse fund (though even those have complications). TSDD is a tactical tool, not an investment.

What are the costs?

The fund carries an expense ratio that should be checked in the prospectus and fact sheet. For leveraged and inverse products, the ongoing costs of rebalancing (buying and selling futures and derivatives every single day) are built into the fund's performance. The real cost is the opportunity cost of volatility decay—far larger than the stated expense ratio for most investors who hold TSDD longer than intended.

How should investors research TSDD?

Read the prospectus and fact sheet carefully, paying particular attention to the section on daily reset and limitations. Understand that TSDD is not a long-term short position on Tesla; it is a daily-rebalancing tactical bet. Compare the fund's actual historical returns against -2x the daily returns of Tesla stock over various time periods—one month, three months, six months—and observe how volatility decay erodes returns over time. Consider whether a simple short sale, a put-option position, or options strategies like put spreads might be more appropriate for the investment thesis. TSDD exists for traders executing specific near-term hedges, not for investors expecting Tesla to fall.
