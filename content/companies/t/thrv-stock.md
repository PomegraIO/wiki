> The goal is income that holds steady across changing markets by spreading the bet across many yield sources, not concentrating in bonds or stocks alone.

*Prospera Income ETF (NASDAQ: THRV) is an actively managed exchange-traded fund that pursues income by holding a diverse basket of yield-bearing instruments—fixed-income ETFs, dividend-paying equities, closed-end funds, and other registered investment companies. The fund aims to balance current income with capital preservation.*

## Multi-source income strategy

Unlike a traditional bond fund or equity income fund, THRV diversifies its income sources. The portfolio includes fixed-income ETFs (which themselves hold government and corporate bonds), equity funds focused on dividend payers, closed-end funds trading at various premiums and discounts to net asset value, and other income-generating structures. The manager adjusts allocations among these asset classes based on market conditions: when bond yields rise and look attractive, the fixed-income allocation may expand; when equity dividends become compelling, the equity portion may grow.

This approach appeals to investors who want to avoid concentration risk—the danger that if you own only bonds, rising interest rates hurt you; if you own only dividend stocks, falling equity markets hurt you. By holding multiple income streams simultaneously, the fund aims to smooth returns across different market environments. The premise is that when one source weakens, others may compensate.

## Structure and composition

THRV invests primarily in other registered investment companies rather than buying individual securities directly. This fund-of-funds structure offers flexibility—the manager can shift between a dividend-equity ETF and a fixed-income fund more easily than rebalancing thousands of individual stocks and bonds. However, this architecture introduces a layer of fees: THRV charges its own expense ratio, and the underlying funds carry their own, creating a cost stack.

The typical portfolio holds 35–45 securities at a time, with the ten largest representing roughly half of assets. This concentration is material and means THRV is not as diversified as a broad bond index or a total-market equity fund. The composition varies seasonally and with market conditions, so the exact mix of bonds, equities, and alternative income instruments shifts.

## Income generation and capital preservation

The fund distributes income from interest payments, dividends, and periodic capital gains realized by its underlying holdings. The manager explicitly targets capital preservation alongside income generation, recognizing that many income-fund investors depend on stable distributions rather than growth. The multi-asset approach is supposed to cushion volatility: when stocks fall, bonds may steady the portfolio; when rates drop, equity dividends may accelerate.

However, capital preservation is a goal, not a guarantee. The fund can lose value if bond yields spike sharply (pushing existing bond prices down) or if equity valuations compress. The active trading approach employed by the manager can also introduce short-term capital gains, which carry higher tax consequences than long-term holdings.

## Who benefits and research considerations

THRV suits investors who prioritize current income over growth, who want exposure to multiple asset classes but prefer a single fund rather than building a portfolio individually, and who value active management to adjust allocations as market conditions shift. It is less attractive for high-bracket taxpayers (the active trading generates ordinary-income-taxed gains) or for long-horizon buy-and-hold investors who can manage their own asset allocation.

Research the fund by reviewing Prospera's prospectus, fact sheet, and performance data. Compare the fund's realized yield—what it has actually distributed as a percentage of its value—against its investment objective and the composition of holdings. High turnover suggests active trading and may indicate tax inefficiency. Examine whether the underlying funds are diversified or concentrated, since THRV's true diversification depends on what those underlying vehicles hold. Finally, confirm that the fee structure is transparent and that the total cost of fees—THRV's expense ratio plus the embedded costs of underlying funds—justifies the convenience.
