*TSLS is an **inverse ETF** — a fund that aims to move opposite to Tesla stock. When Tesla gains, TSLS loses. When Tesla falls, TSLS rises. It is a 1x inverse, meaning TSLS moves roughly in line with Tesla's decline, neither amplified nor reduced.*

## Structure and positioning

Direxion's TSLS holds a position in inverse derivatives on Tesla — typically short futures, short calls, or synthetic short contracts — that is rebalanced daily to maintain 1x inverse exposure. The fund is designed to track the inverse of Tesla's daily returns with fidelity, not to amplify them as a 2x or 3x inverse product would.

The 1x structure means TSLS avoids some of the volatility decay that plagues leveraged-inverse funds. A Tesla up-down-up pattern still erodes TSLS value, but less severely than a 2x or 3x inverse would. Still, the daily reset mechanic means that over multiday holding periods, TSLS's actual return will likely underperform the simple inverse of what Tesla did, especially in whipsaw environments.

## Use cases

TSLS is used in two main ways. First, as a tactical short-term hedge: a trader or investor who holds Tesla in a portfolio can buy TSLS to offset downside over the next few days, without selling their long position or using margin. Second, as a standalone bet that Tesla will decline. Because TSLS is a 1x inverse, it is far less aggressive than a 2x or 3x inverse fund and may feel more suitable to traders with a moderately bearish view.

## The cost of inverse exposure

TSLS carries a higher expense ratio than an ordinary Tesla ETF — typically around 0.95% annually — because maintaining inverse exposure through derivatives is more expensive than simply holding stock. The daily rebalancing also incurs transaction costs that are baked into the fund's reported expense.

## Decay in practice

An inverse ETF that is held for months will almost certainly underperform a simple short sale of Tesla stock or a put option, due to volatility drag. A trader who expects a 20% decline in Tesla over six months would get a better return by shorting Tesla shares directly than by holding TSLS for that entire period. But for a trader expecting a 5% to 10% drop over a few days and wanting to avoid short borrow costs, TSLS offers a liquid, low-friction way to express that view.

## Liquidity and market reality

TSLS trades on the NASDAQ with reasonable daily volume, though less than a broad inverse-market ETF or a major leveraged product. During normal market hours, the fund should track the inverse of Tesla with a tight bid-ask spread. During market stress — when Tesla trading is heavy and options markets widen — TSLS spreads can blow out, and the daily rebalancing may lag.

## Research and documentation

Direxion publishes a detailed prospectus explaining the daily reset mechanics, the fee structure, and the situations under which TSLS might not track the inverse of Tesla as expected (such as trading halts in Tesla or circuit-breaker events in the derivatives markets). Anyone considering a multiday or longer holding period should read the prospectus carefully to understand the volatility decay risks, and anyone using TSLS as a hedge should calculate the duration over which the hedge is worth its cost.
