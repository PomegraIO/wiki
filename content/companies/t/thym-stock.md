*T. Rowe Price High Income Municipal ETF ([NASDAQ](/nasdaq/): THYM) is an actively managed exchange-traded fund that invests in [municipal bonds](/municipal-bond/)—the debt of states, cities, and their authorities—selected for high tax-exempt yield. The fund targets longer-term, lower-rated [bonds](/bond/), making it suitable for high-income investors who benefit from federal tax exemption on municipal interest.*

## Tax exemption: the core appeal

Municipal bond interest is exempt from federal income taxes and, if you live in the state of issue, from state and local income taxes too. This exemption is reflected in yields: a municipal bond paying 4 percent federal-tax-free is economically equivalent to a higher taxable yield for someone in the 37 percent federal bracket. The exact benefit depends on your marginal tax rate. A high-bracket taxpayer gets significant value; a low-bracket taxpayer gets little. THYM is fundamentally a vehicle for investors who will benefit from this exemption throughout their [holding period](/holding-period/).

## The portfolio structure

THYM holds 150–200 municipal bonds spread across state and local issuers. The [diversification](/diversification/) is broad—geography, issuer type, credit quality—so no single municipality dominates. The fund deliberately targets longer-term bonds (typical maturities of 10–20+ years) because longer bonds carry higher yields than shorter-dated bonds. The credit quality is intentionally lower than the highest-rated municipals: the fund invests in bonds rated low-to-upper-medium (roughly A to BBB, with some below-investment-grade) to capture the extra yield those issuers must pay. Safer, AAA-rated bonds pay very little; to deliver high income, the fund accepts [credit risk](/credit-risk/).

This is an explicit trade-off: higher yield in exchange for lower credit safety. The portfolio will suffer more in a municipal credit crisis than a fund holding only high-quality bonds would.

## Active selection and management

T. Rowe Price's municipal bond team brings deep expertise in bond analysis and issuer relationships. Unlike the Treasury market, where every participant sees the same prices and information, the municipal market is fragmented and less transparent. Individual city and school-district bonds are often lightly researched and illiquid. This opacity creates room for skill: a manager who knows the credit profiles of hundreds of issuers, who has relationships with bond dealers, and who understands the nuances of pension liabilities and revenue structures can identify which bonds offer excess yield for the risk.

The managers also make tactical decisions about duration—the average maturity of the portfolio. If [interest rates](/interest-rate/) are expected to fall, longer maturities lock in higher rates; if rates are expected to rise, shorter maturities cushion the damage. This active timing can improve or impair returns.

## Risks: credit cycles and interest-rate exposure

Municipal issuers face structural pressures: [pension obligations](/pension-obligation/) that grow regardless of tax revenue, aging infrastructure, demographic trends that erode the tax base, and economic downturns that shrink revenues. Some municipalities manage these pressures well; others face fiscal stress. During [recessions](/recession/) or credit crunches, even investment-grade municipals decline in price as investors demand higher yields. THYM's allocation to lower-rated bonds means greater vulnerability in such stress periods.

[Interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/) is material. If yields rise sharply, longer-term municipal bonds lose value immediately. The portfolio is duration-adjusted actively, but the manager cannot eliminate this risk—it is structural to longer-dated bonds.

## Who THYM suits and research approach

THYM is built for high-income, tax-conscious investors who want regular, tax-exempt distributions and are comfortable accepting below-investment-grade credit risk for higher yield. It is poorly suited for tax-deferred accounts (IRAs, 401ks), where the tax exemption is wasted, or for low-income investors who do not benefit from the federal exemption.

Research the fund via T. Rowe Price's prospectus, fact sheets, and yield data. Examine the credit-quality breakdown: what percentage is AAA, A, BBB, below-investment-grade? Study geographic diversification and issuer types (schools, water systems, highways, housing) to ensure you are comfortable. Track realized distributions against advertised yield—if distributions significantly exceed income, the fund is paying capital gains, which has different tax treatment. Review the fund's performance during past credit cycles (2008, 2020, 2024) to understand downside risk. Municipal bond research services like Morningstar MUNI can provide deeper issuer credit analysis if you want to audit the portfolio.
