*The **Cambria Trinity ETF** (TRTY) is an actively managed fund-of-funds that blends exposure to multiple asset classes — equities, fixed income, real estate, commodities, and currencies — inside a single holding. Rather than investing directly in stocks or [bonds](/bond/), it holds a portfolio of other Cambria-branded ETFs plus Treasury securities, aiming to deliver both income and capital appreciation without requiring investors to assemble and rebalance a diversified portfolio themselves.*

## The multi-asset toolkit

Trinity's defining feature is simplicity within complexity. Rather than asking an investor to pick a stock fund, a bond fund, a [real estate fund](/real-estate-fund/), and a commodity fund separately, Trinity pools them together. The fund's core holdings are other Cambria ETFs — funds focused on specific niches like [dividend](/dividend/) strategies, international equity, and structured income — plus Treasury securities. This stack provides exposure across traditional stocks and bonds while reaching into less-travelled territory like [real estate investment trusts](/real-estate-investment-trust/) and commodities.

The approach appeals to investors seeking [diversification](/diversification/) across multiple asset classes without having to build and maintain the allocation themselves. The fund's management team makes tactical shifts in how much weight each asset class receives, based on their assessment of market conditions and [relative valuations](/relative-valuation/). This active overlay is what distinguishes it from a static allocation: the blend is not fixed, and over time the weight toward equities or bonds can drift based on management's conviction.

## How the pieces fit together

The fund's 33 holdings are weighted unevenly. Cambria's own specialty ETFs form the meat of the portfolio, but Treasury securities also play a structural role — they provide ballast and income when equity markets weaken. The precise allocation changes, but a typical mix might emphasize Cambria's equity strategies (especially dividend and value plays) alongside fixed-income exposure, real estate, and smaller positions in commodities and alternative strategies.

Because Trinity is a [fund of funds](/fund-of-funds/), investors are paying two layers of fees: the fund's own 0.46% [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) plus the embedded fees within the underlying Cambria ETFs it holds. That double-layering means the true all-in cost is higher than the headline figure. However, the fee is competitive for an actively managed multi-asset vehicle, and the convenience of a single holding that rebalances automatically can offset the layered cost for investors who would otherwise build a similar allocation piecemeal.

## Income and capital appreciation as dual mandates

Trinity's prospectus identifies two objectives: generating current income and achieving capital appreciation. The emphasis on income reflects the fund's heavy use of dividend-paying stocks, [dividend-focused ETFs](/dividend-focused-etf/), and coupon-bearing bonds. This makes it suitable for investors in or near retirement who want a stream of payouts alongside the potential for the underlying asset values to rise. The flip side is that a fund chasing yield in a flat or falling market can see its share price decline — income yield cushions total return but does not eliminate price risk.

The capital appreciation goal keeps the fund from being purely income-focused. Management maintains equity exposure sufficient to participate in [bull markets](/bull-market/), which means Trinity is not a defensive "bonds and dividend stocks only" strategy. It sits somewhere in the middle of the risk-reward spectrum, neither a conservative allocation nor an aggressive one.

## Risks and the rebalancing challenge

The main risk is the broad one that cuts across any diversified portfolio: if all asset classes move lower together during a severe market downturn, no amount of diversification insulates the fund. Real estate can sell off alongside stocks during liquidity crunches; commodities and currencies add their own volatility. The fund's active management is meant to reduce these risks through tactical shifts, but active management is itself a bet — if the manager's market calls are wrong, the fund can underperform a simple passive allocation.

A secondary risk is the fund-of-funds structure itself. Embedded fees across multiple layers mean that Trinity must outperform its passive equivalents by more than its expense ratio just to break even. Over long periods, active management faces a statistical headwind: most active funds underperform their benchmarks before fees, and fees make the gap worse. Trinity's survival depends on its managers adding value through astute allocation shifts, which is possible but not guaranteed.

The final risk is concentration in Cambria's own ecosystem. By design, Trinity holds primarily Cambria-branded ETFs. If investors sour on Cambria's strategies or philosophy, there is limited alternative positioning available within this fund.

## How to research and monitor Trinity

Investors interested in Trinity should begin with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, available on Cambria's website. The prospectus lays out the fund's objectives, the Cambria ETFs it can hold, and how it is permitted to allocate capital. Because it is an [actively managed fund](/actively-managed-fund/), the quarterly holdings list is crucial — examining it reveals not just which Cambria ETFs are held but also their relative weights and any shifts since the previous quarter.

Key metrics to track include the fund's total return over rolling periods (one year, three years, five years), its expense ratio relative to comparable [multi-asset ETFs](/multi-asset-etf/), and the volatility of the underlying holdings. The [annual report](/10-k/) and shareholder letters, if published, often provide insight into the management team's thinking about [asset allocation](/asset-allocation/) and which market views drove recent shifts. Since Trinity is a fund of funds, comparing its performance to a simple balanced index — such as a 60% stock, 40% bond allocation or a diversified asset allocation index — can reveal whether active management is delivering value after accounting for fees.

Trinity trades on an exchange like any ETF, so the share price fluctuates intraday and can trade at a modest premium or discount to its underlying [net asset value](/net-asset-value/). Monitoring this spread is worth doing, as a persistent large discount can signal trouble or opportunity depending on the circumstances.
