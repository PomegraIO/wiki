The Truth Social American Security & Defense ETF is an exchange-traded fund that concentrates its holdings in publicly traded companies involved in the U.S. defense, aerospace, and security industries. It reflects a thematic investment approach—betting that revenues and valuations in defense-adjacent businesses will move higher as geopolitical tensions, military spending, and national security priorities capture policy attention.

## What the fund holds

TSSD's portfolio consists of mid-cap and large-cap defense contractors, aerospace manufacturers, and security technology companies. The fund's prospectus and fact sheet detail which specific companies qualify, but the universe typically includes firms that derive material revenue from military contracts, weapons systems, cybersecurity for government and critical infrastructure, satellite communications, missile defense, fighter-jet components, and related national-security equipment. The fund aims to capture not just traditional "big defense" firms but also smaller, specialized players in emerging security domains like cyber and space.

The fund holds a concentrated, selected set of securities rather than tracking a broad market-cap-weighted index. This means it has smaller weightings in companies where mere revenue in defense is incidental, and higher weightings where defense and security work is the primary business engine.

## Why this structure exists

Defense spending is often a counterintuitive place to look for stock returns because military budgets are a political outcome, not a market outcome. An ETF like this arose because some investors believe that rising geopolitical tension, increased defense budgets, and a policy shift toward reshoring and domestic manufacturing of defense goods create an identifiable category worth overweighting. The fund's creation reflects a thesis: that a period of rising defense spending and military prioritization in U.S. policy would flow through to equity valuations in defense stocks.

This is distinct from a broad market or sector index. It is a **thematic or political tilt**—a bet on a specific policy or geopolitical direction, not simply a reflection of which industries exist in the market.

## Costs and how it trades

Like all exchange-traded funds, TSSD has an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) that covers the fund's operating costs. The prospectus and fact sheet lay out this fee, which is paid annually as a small percentage of assets. The fund trades on a [stock exchange](/stock-exchange/) during market hours, so investors can buy and sell shares intraday at market-determined prices, not just at the daily close.

Liquidity varies with the total assets in the fund and the breadth of its investor base. A larger, better-known fund may trade with tighter spreads (smaller gap between bid and ask prices); a smaller or newer fund may have wider spreads and less natural liquidity.

## Real risks

The most obvious risk is **concentration**. Because the fund is selective and themed rather than broad, a downturn in defense spending or a reversal of hawkish policy would hit the portfolio hard. If a major geopolitical event resolves, or if a new administration prioritizes retrenchment over military expansion, the logic for overweighting defense stocks evaporates.

**Regulatory risk** is also real. Defense contractors operate under intense regulatory scrutiny, export controls, and government review. A change in arms-export policy, a scandal involving a major contractor, or a shift in foreign policy could shift demand for the companies the fund holds.

**Sector concentration** is a structural feature, not a bug. Because the fund holds only defense-related businesses, it does not offer [diversification](/diversification/) across the broader economy. It performs well if defense does; it lags if defensive equities underperform.

Finally, thematic and political-bet funds carry a **narrative risk**. If the thesis that animated the fund's creation—rising defense budgets, military prioritization, reshoring—does not materialize, the fund becomes a vehicle for a story that no longer holds.

## How to research this fund

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet on the issuer's website; these documents disclose the exact holdings, the selection criteria, the expense ratio, and the fund's objective. Then look at the top 10 holdings and read about the business of each—what fraction of revenue comes from defense, whether it is improving or declining, how dependent it is on U.S. government contracts, and what the political environment means for budgets.

Watch defense-spending bills and defense-budget news carefully. Major legislation (a surge in military appropriations, a reduction, or a policy shift) is material to the fund. Study the historical correlation between defense stocks and geopolitical events to understand the timing and magnitude of moves. Finally, compare the fund's performance to broad defense indices and competing defense-themed ETFs to see whether its selection strategy adds value or simply charges a fee for a narrower bet.
