*The **Pacer Data and Digital Revolution ETF** (TRFK) invests in companies powering the digital and data infrastructure buildout. The fund holds a mix of software, semiconductor, cloud, and cybersecurity firms — businesses that sell the tools and platforms for collecting, processing, storing, and securing data at enterprise and consumer scale.*

## Software and platform companies: the foundation

TRFK's core holdings are software vendors that serve enterprises. This includes infrastructure software (cloud platforms, databases, networking), application software (enterprise resource planning systems, analytics platforms, workflow automation), and software-as-a-service (SaaS) providers. These companies earn recurring subscription revenue from customers and typically have high gross margins (60–80%) because software scales without proportional cost increases.

The attraction to a digital-transformation fund is straightforward: companies are migrating workloads from on-premises data centres to cloud platforms, adopting artificial intelligence for analytics and decision-making, and automating workflows that were previously manual. Each of these shifts requires software licensing, cloud infrastructure spending, or both. A financial services firm moving core trading systems to the cloud might spend tens of millions on migration and ongoing cloud services; a manufacturer adopting predictive-maintenance AI might license analytics software for millions per year.

## Cloud and infrastructure: the pipes

Underlying the software layer is cloud infrastructure — the data centres and servers that actually store and process the data. TRFK typically holds shares of major cloud providers and the semiconductor makers that power them. Cloud providers earn revenue from compute time (renting virtual machines), storage (charging for data stored), and data transfer (charging per gigabyte moved). Like software SaaS, cloud revenue is recurring and high-margin once capacity is built.

Semiconductor makers benefit from this buildout twofold: artificial intelligence requires custom chips optimised for training and inference (running trained models), and data centres consume vast quantities of commodity processors. Companies designing chips for AI workloads or manufacturing processors for cloud infrastructure are material beneficiaries of the digital-transformation thesis.

## Cybersecurity and data protection

As enterprises collect and store more data, security becomes critical. Cybersecurity vendors sell tools to detect intrusions, prevent data theft, manage encryption, and monitor networks. TRFK holds companies across this stack: network security, endpoint protection (software that locks down individual computers and devices), identity and access management (controlling who can reach what data), and cloud-security specialists. These businesses also operate on recurring subscription models, often bundled into larger contracts with enterprises.

Data breaches are costly — both directly (regulatory fines, customer lawsuits) and reputationally. This makes cybersecurity a necessary spend for enterprises, and its growth is less cyclical than software spending in general. A [recession](/recession/) may delay some software purchases, but companies continue hardening security. TRFK's cybersecurity exposure hedges some of the cyclical risk in the broader tech sector.

## Data analytics and artificial intelligence

The most explicit AI exposure in TRFK comes from analytics platforms, large-language-model infrastructure, and enterprise AI applications. This includes companies that help businesses build and deploy AI models, vendors that provide data analytics to extract insights from business data, and firms selling AI-specific hardware or software. The AI category is the fastest-moving segment of TRFK's portfolio and carries the most hype and volatility.

Many companies in this category are growth-stage, with strong narrative arcs but still burning cash or early in profitability. The risk is that AI adoption may take longer or be less valuable than today's assumptions, or that the capability (ability to build and train useful models) becomes commoditised quickly, crushing margins for all but the largest players.

## Sector and concentration risks

TRFK is heavily weighted toward large technology companies with cloud, AI, or digital-infrastructure exposure. The fund skews toward North America (where most of these companies are headquartered) and is concentrated in the largest, most liquid names. This concentration is unavoidable given the fund's thematic focus — the companies that dominate cloud, AI infrastructure, and large-scale data analytics are concentrated in a handful of mega-cap firms.

Concentration brings both benefits and risks. Benefits: these firms have network effects, are difficult to displace, and have strong financials. Risks: a single downturn in enterprise spending or AI hype hits many holdings simultaneously. Unlike a diversified technology fund, TRFK has little hedge against a broad technology selloff.

## The cyclicality trap: when digital transformation slows

Enterprise software and cloud spending are correlated with overall business investment and technology budgets. In recessions or when corporate profits fall, companies defer cloud migrations, delay AI projects, and reduce software licensing. Many of TRFK's holdings saw dramatic pullbacks in 2022–2023 when rising [interest rates](/interest-rate/) and recession fears spooked investors and companies cut spending.

The flip side is that in strong growth environments, digital transformation accelerates. The fund is leveraged to the cycle — more volatile than the broader market. For investors seeking exposure to digital megatrends but with lower cyclicality, a broader technology fund may be preferable.

## How to research TRFK

Start with the prospectus and a recent fact sheet showing sector and top-holdings breakdown. Identify the largest positions and read their latest quarterly earnings reports to understand growth rates, margins, and management commentary on customer spending plans. Watch for mentions of "budget scrutiny" or "slower deal cycles" — these are early warnings of cyclical slowdown. Review the fund's rolling returns (1-, 3-, 5-year) to understand its volatility relative to the broad technology sector and the S&P 500. Finally, assess your own conviction on the pace of AI adoption and digital transformation — TRFK is a thematic bet, and its returns depend heavily on that trend continuing at or above current market expectations.
