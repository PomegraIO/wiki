TDV pursues a specific thesis: that some large technology companies have grown mature enough to pay [dividends](/dividend/) reliably and raise them year after year, much like utilities or blue-chip industrials historically have. The fund tracks an index of technology stocks in the S&P 500 that meet dividend-growth criteria — at minimum, they must have raised their dividend every year for the past 25 years or more. This screen naturally excludes growth-obsessed tech firms that reinvest all free cash and produce zero shareholder payouts; instead, it captures profitable, cash-generative software and semiconductor companies that have chosen to share returns with shareholders.

The roster typically includes familiar names: [Microsoft](/msft-stock/) (for its Office and cloud franchises), Cisco (networking equipment and software), [Intel](/intc-stock/) (before recent challenges in its core chip business), and a handful of other large established tech firms. The number of qualifying names is small — often only 20 to 30 companies clear the 25-year dividend-growth hurdle — which makes the portfolio concentrated by tech-sector standards. All holdings are large-cap; the smallest names by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) are often still in the tens of billions of dollars, so there is institutional-grade liquidity and analyst coverage.

The strategy trades on an observation: the technology sector has matured. The earliest generation of software and semiconductor makers — Microsoft, Cisco, Intel, Qualcomm — reached such scale and profitability that they began returning cash rather than reinvesting it all. Their ability to raise dividends annually reflects not just growth but also capital discipline and confidence in their franchise economics. For investors convinced that large tech has moved from a pure-growth story to a mature, profitable, cash-generative one, owning proven dividend-raisers concentrates that exposure without the drag of high-flying, unprofitable growth names.

The fund is issued by ProShares, a unit of Invesco, and trades on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/). Its [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is around 0.40 percent, modest for an actively managed or selectively screened strategy but higher than a passive total-tech-market fund. The fund rebalances once per year at year-end, when eligible securities are reviewed against the 25-year dividend-growth criterion and new qualifiers are added or losers are removed. Between rebalances, the holdings are static.

The key risks are concentration and dividend stability. The tech sector can be volatile, and a 25-stock portfolio, no matter how large the companies, will magnify that volatility versus a broader market index. The dividend-growth filter provides some insulation — companies that raise dividends annually are often durable and profitable — but it is not absolute. A severe downturn could force a technology company to cut its dividend, breaking its streak and removing it from the index. During the 2020 pandemic selloff and the 2022–2023 tech-sector rotation, dividend aristocrats in tech notably outperformed fast-growing unprofitable names, which demonstrated the hedging benefit; however, the fund still declined because its holdings are exposed to tech-sector weakness even if they are the stronger, more stable names in that sector.

An investor in TDV is betting that large, profitable, dividend-paying technology companies offer a middle ground: better growth than a utility or staple-company dividend aristocrat, but more stable returns and income than a pure growth-tech portfolio. To research the fund, review the prospectus for the exact index methodology and the 25-year dividend criteria, then examine the current holdings and their individual dividend histories. Comparing TDV's rolling returns to a broad technology ETF over cycles of rising and falling rates will show whether dividend discipline has proven a durable advantage or a drag. Watching for any holdings that approach dividend-cut risk is also worthwhile — a breach of the streak would result in a rotation out of the fund.
