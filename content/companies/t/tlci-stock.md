*The **Touchstone International [Equity ETF](/equity-etf/) (TLCI)** is a fund that holds stocks from developed economies outside the United States — principally Western Europe, Australia, and developed Asia. It is structured as a conventional ETF trading on the [New York Stock Exchange](/new-york-stock-exchange/), and it aims to replicate the return of its benchmark index before costs.*

## From regional focus to global reach

Touchstone is a subsidiary of the [Federated Hermes](/fhi-stock/) financial-services group, an asset manager with roots going back to the 1950s. Touchstone Investments itself emerged in the 1990s as Federated's mutual-fund division began to diversify beyond domestic-equity strategies. The creation of TLCI reflected a broader industry shift in the late 1990s and early 2000s: retail investors and financial advisors were starting to embrace international [diversification](/diversification/) as a core principle, no longer treating overseas stocks as an exotic tilt but as a permanent portion of a balanced portfolio.

The fund was designed to track the [Morgan Stanley](/morgan-stanley/) Capital International EAFE (Europe, Australasia, Far East) Index, one of the oldest and most widely used gauges of developed-market stocks outside North America. The EAFE was constructed in 1969 and became the de facto benchmark for international equity strategies across the industry. By the early 2000s, when index-tracking ETFs were consolidating as the preferred vehicle for passive exposure, Touchstone launched TLCI to provide low-cost access to that established benchmark.

## What the fund holds and why

TLCI owns a representative cross-section of the companies in the MSCI EAFE Index, which currently encompasses roughly 900 stocks from 21 developed nations. The heaviest concentrations are in the United Kingdom, Japan, France, Switzerland, and Germany — a weighting that reflects both the size of those economies and the [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) of their publicly listed companies. Within those countries, the fund holds industrial companies, financial institutions, consumer-goods makers, luxury-goods firms, pharmaceutical companies, and technology firms, all in proportion to their presence in the index.

This is a capitalization-weighted approach: the largest companies by market value make up the largest pieces of the fund. A company like [ASML](/asml-stock/) Holding from the Netherlands — a dominant maker of semiconductor manufacturing equipment — naturally receives a larger allocation than a smaller firm in the same sector. This weighting mechanism means TLCI's returns will closely track the index's returns before costs.

## Costs, liquidity, and the reality of tracking

TLCI charges an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) in the range of 0.35% to 0.50% per year, a cost that is deducted from the fund's [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) daily. For a fund of this type — a straightforward, low-maintenance, index-tracking product — this fee is moderate to slightly high by modern standards, though it remains substantially lower than the average actively managed international-equity [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/). The fee reflects both the cost of holding several hundred individual stocks and Touchstone's overhead as the fund operator.

As an ETF, TLCI trades on the open market throughout each trading day just as a stock does. Its trading volume is moderate; the fund has accumulated several billion dollars in assets under management, which means buyers and sellers can typically execute trades at prices very close to the fund's net asset value per share. The [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) — the difference between the best price a buyer will offer and the best price a seller will accept — is usually a few cents, a fraction of a penny in percentage terms.

## The pressures and limits of developed-market investing

International developed markets have had a complicated decade relative to the United States. The aggregate earnings growth of European and Japanese companies has lagged that of American firms; [central banks](/central-bank/) in those regions pursued ultralow interest-rate policies for far longer than the [Federal Reserve](/federal-reserve/); and the rise of large technology companies in the United States created a concentration of wealth and market-cap gains in American equities that had no parallel in developed Europe or Asia. As a result, holding TLCI meant accepting underperformance to the S&P 500 for much of the 2010s and 2020s, a divergence that tested the resolve of diversified investors committed to international exposure.

Currency is another real consideration. When TLCI holds stocks traded in euros, pounds sterling, [Japanese yen](/japanese-yen/), and [Swiss francs](/swiss-franc/), the fund is also implicitly taking a currency position. If the [US dollar](/us-dollar/) weakens against those currencies, the non-dollar portion of the fund's returns will be amplified; if the dollar strengthens, those returns are muted. This currency effect is not diversification — it is an additional source of volatility that a domestic US investor might not have asked for.

Geopolitical concentration matters too. Nearly a third of EAFE stocks are European, and Europe has faced recurring political and economic shocks — sovereign-debt crises, Brexit, and energy-market disruption from geopolitical conflict. Japan, the second-largest component, has struggled with low growth and demographic contraction for decades. Australia and Canada, which make up a meaningful portion, are commodity-dependent economies. An investor holding TLCI is implicitly betting on the resilience of these regions and the stability of their capital markets.

## Who this fund is for and how to research it

TLCI is suited for investors seeking straightforward, cost-efficient exposure to developed international equity markets as part of a broader, diversified portfolio. It works well as the international-equity sleeve of a [three-fund portfolio](/three-fund-portfolio/) (domestic equities, international equities, [bonds](/bond/)) or as a complement to a domestic-focused core holding. It is not for investors trying to time currency movements, exploit emerging-market growth, or construct a concentrated view on a specific foreign country.

To understand TLCI's performance and behavior, start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, available from Touchstone or through the fund's page on the NYSE. These documents lay out the fund's objectives, the composition of the underlying index, historical returns, and risk metrics. Compare TLCI's expense ratio and historical tracking error to rivals like iShares MSCI EAFE ETF (EFA), which is far larger and often has tighter costs. Monitor the fund's geographic and sector weightings to see how much of its return is driven by exposure to particular regions — a sharp move in the yen or the euro, for instance, will show up in the international portion of a portfolio well before it appears in absolute returns.

As with any single fund, TLCI's returns are not a prediction of future results; they reflect the accumulated returns of all the companies it holds, minus costs, over the periods in question. International diversification is a long-term commitment, not a tactical bet, and the fund serves that purpose best when held as part of a disciplined, rebalanced portfolio rather than as a strategic timing play.
