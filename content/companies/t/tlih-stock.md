Ten-League International Holdings Ltd, listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange under the ticker TLIH, is a diversified holding company with a sprawling portfolio that spans traditional Chinese medicine manufacturing, trading operations across Southeast Asia, and selective stakes in health-technology ventures. The company is rooted in decades of family business in TCM production and distribution, but over time it has evolved into a more broadly-focused regional vehicle — blending legacy herbal products with newer consumer-health exposure and opportunistic investments in the growing wellness sector.

## What is Ten-League, and where did it come from?

Ten-League traces its origins to family enterprises in Hong Kong and mainland China focused on the manufacture and trade of traditional Chinese medicinal products. The modern holding company structure emerged as the founders consolidated operations and began diversifying beyond core TCM manufacturing into broader trading and investment activities. Like many family-run Hong Kong businesses, Ten-League has navigated the transformation of Chinese and Southeast Asian markets, pivoting from pure manufacturing toward import-export operations and, increasingly, toward selective venture investment in health-adjacent technology.

The company maintains operational ties to facilities and suppliers across mainland China, Hong Kong, and Vietnam — a geographic spread that reflects both historical family connections and deliberate positioning within regional supply chains. That positioning places Ten-League at the intersection of several long-term trends: the rising global interest in traditional remedies, the expansion of Chinese health-product exports, and the hunt for cost-effective manufacturing partnerships within Asia.

## How does Ten-League actually make money?

The company's revenue streams cluster around three core activities:

**Traditional Chinese Medicine Products** — the original business and still a material contributor. Ten-League manufactures and distributes herbal extracts, powders, and finished medicinal products, primarily sold through wholesale channels to pharmacies, hospitals, and practitioners across Asia. These products carry higher gross margins than commodity herbal materials, but demand is steady rather than growing explosively.

**Trading and Distribution Operations** — Ten-League acts as a middleman for imported and exported health-related goods and materials, moving products between Chinese manufacturers and distributors in Southeast Asia and beyond. This segment generates revenue on volume and logistics markup, with lower margins than manufacturing but strong cash flow.

**Investment Portfolio and Licensing Revenue** — more recently, Ten-League has acquired minority stakes in health-tech startups and complementary wellness businesses. Some deals involve licensing traditional TCM formulations to manufacturers in higher-income markets; others are pure equity stakes in companies developing digital health tools or nutraceutical brands. These stakes are volatile and illiquid but offer upside if any portfolio company succeeds.

## What makes Ten-League distinctive?

The company's main advantages lie in **regional positioning and legacy relationships**. Ten-League has supplier networks and distribution footprints that took decades to build — relationships with ingredient farmers in rural China, established channels into pharmacy chains across Vietnam and Thailand, and trust among practitioners. These are moats that are difficult to replicate, especially for foreign entrants unfamiliar with local regulatory and cultural nuance around TCM products.

The **family control and long time horizon** is both a strength and a source of opacity. Because the company is not forced into short-term profit chasing, it can invest in supply-chain resilience, hold inventory countercyclically, and take patient stakes in emerging opportunities. But the lack of professional management disclosure and the concentration of decision-making power create governance risks that international investors find frustrating.

## What are the risks and pressures?

**Regulatory tightening on TCM and herbal products** is an ongoing threat. Governments in developed markets are increasingly stringent about the efficacy claims and ingredient transparency required for herbal products, which can force costly reformulation and limit marketing claims. China's own regulatory environment has tightened around TCM production quality and safety standards, raising manufacturing compliance costs.

**Currency and geopolitical exposure** is real. Ten-League earns revenue across multiple currencies (Chinese renminbi, Vietnamese dong, Thai baht) and operates supply chains that depend on stable relationships with mainland China. Shifts in US-China trade policy, semiconductor export controls, or regional tensions can ripple through operations and reputations.

**Illiquidity and limited disclosure** also weigh on the stock. Ten-League is listed but thinly traded, with limited English-language financial disclosures. The lack of transparency makes valuation difficult and leaves the stock vulnerable to rumor and mispricing.

**Competition from larger, better-capitalized herbal-products makers** is intensifying. As the global wellness market has grown, major pharmaceutical and supplement manufacturers have entered the TCM space with modern branding, distribution, and marketing. Ten-League's family-scale operations struggle to compete on scale.

## How a reader would research Ten-League

Start with the company's Hong Kong Stock Exchange filings (SEHK code: TLIH), which are published in English and Chinese. The annual report and interim disclosures lay out the breakdown by business segment and geography. Because Ten-League is a family holding company with a broad portfolio, the 10-20F filing (if the company ever lists on a US exchange) or the HK Exchange filings are the primary source of truth; earnings calls are less common and less detailed than at larger-cap firms.

Watch the trajectory of the TCM and trading segments separately — the former is mature and margin-pressured, while the latter is sensitive to regional economic cycles. Any announcement of new investment ventures or acquisitions is worth scrutinizing, because that is where the company's growth hopes lie. Currency impacts are material; compare earnings in reported currency versus standardized USD to see whether the company is growing organically or merely benefiting from currency swings.

Peers to follow include larger regional herbal-products makers and Southeast Asian trading conglomerates; comparing Ten-League's margins and growth to those groups gives context for valuation and competitive positioning. Finally, monitor regulatory changes in key markets — announcements from China's NMPA (National Medical Products Administration) or local health authorities in target markets can shift the cost structure and growth outlook materially.
