Lazard Next Generation Technologies ETF offers investors exposure to companies shaped by emerging technologies and the forces reshaping the digital economy. The fund takes an active, thematic approach to technology investing, assembling a portfolio designed to capture growth opportunities across sectors defined by innovation trends rather than traditional industry classifications. Unlike passive index trackers, TEKY employs a manager-directed research process to identify and weight holdings.

## The fund's founding and evolution

Lazard Asset Management established TEKY to provide individual and institutional investors a structured vehicle for navigating the rapidly shifting technology sector. The fund's inception reflects Lazard's long history of technology investing and active management. Where many technology funds track a fixed index or tech-heavy benchmark, TEKY was conceived as a more dynamic, research-driven alternative that could rotate exposures as technology themes evolved. This approach allows the fund to adjust its holdings as new trends emerge and older technologies mature or face disruption.

## What TEKY holds and how it decides

TEKY's holdings span software, hardware, semiconductors, telecommunications infrastructure, and other companies positioned within transformational technology shifts. The fund's managers conduct proprietary research to identify the themes most likely to drive returns — artificial intelligence, cloud computing, cybersecurity, semiconductor design, or the next wave of connectivity technologies. Rather than holding a fixed list of stocks, the portfolio is reconstituted as technology priorities shift.

The fund maintains a diversified set of positions, neither concentrated in a single mega-cap holding nor scattered thinly across dozens of marginal exposures. Its median market capitalization and sector weights vary year to year as the managers reorient the portfolio around their conviction themes. Some holdings are established, profitable technology leaders; others are smaller-cap firms or earlier-stage players in nascent categories.

## Structure, costs, and trading

TEKY is a standard open-ended exchange-traded fund domiciled in the United States, traded on a major exchange during normal market hours. It is not a leveraged or inverse product; it does not employ financial engineering or daily rebalancing mechanics. The fund carries an expense ratio reflecting active management fees — higher than passive index trackers but inline with other actively managed technology funds.

Like any ETF, TEKY can be bought and sold throughout the trading day at market prices determined by supply and demand. Shares are freely redeemable through the fund company. The bid-ask spread typically reflects the liquidity of the underlying holdings and the fund's asset base. Investors tracking TEKY should monitor its tracking error — the degree to which returns diverge from the fund's stated objective — as a proxy for how effectively the managers are executing their vision.

## Who TEKY is for and how to evaluate it

TEKY appeals to investors who believe active management adds value in technology investing and who want exposure to emerging technology themes without constructing a bespoke portfolio of individual stocks. It is appropriate for investors with a multi-year time horizon and a tolerance for technology sector volatility. The fund is less suitable for investors seeking broad, low-cost market exposure or those uncomfortable with a fund manager's discretion to shift holdings significantly.

An investor evaluating TEKY should review the fund's prospectus and fact sheet for the current holdings, sector composition, and expense ratio. Track its performance over multiple market cycles rather than over short periods; active funds can underperform their benchmarks or outperform them by wide margins over any single year depending on the managers' conviction and market conditions. Examine the tenure and track record of the portfolio management team, as technology investing success often hinges on the quality of the research organization. Finally, compare TEKY's returns, costs, and thematic focus against other actively managed technology or innovation-themed funds to assess whether its approach aligns with your investment thesis.
