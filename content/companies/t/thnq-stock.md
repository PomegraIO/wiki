*The ROBO Global Artificial Intelligence ETF (THNQ) is an exchange-traded fund designed to give investors broad exposure to the companies building and commercializing artificial intelligence technology — spanning semiconductor makers, software platforms, cloud providers, and specialized AI-infrastructure vendors. It is a thematic fund, meaning it selects holdings based not on a market-cap weighting or an index rule, but on the manager's view of which companies are genuinely central to the artificial intelligence boom.*

## What the fund holds

THNQ's portfolio spans the entire AI technology stack. Holdings typically include the semiconductor manufacturers that produce the processors, graphics chips, and accelerators needed to train and run AI models — companies like [NVIDIA](/nvda-stock/) that design the silicon without which modern AI cannot happen. The fund also owns cloud-infrastructure providers whose data centers host the computational power AI applications demand. Software platforms and the AI-tools vendors that package machine-learning libraries and model-hosting services make up another layer. And because AI is increasingly becoming an input to every other industry, THNQ may hold companies whose primary business is not AI but whose competitive advantage hinges on deploying AI internally — financial-services firms using AI for risk modeling, manufacturers using computer vision, or healthcare companies using machine learning to interpret medical data.

The fund is actively managed, meaning the portfolio is constructed and rebalanced by human decision rather than automatically tracking a fixed index. The managers screen the global [stock market](/stock-market/) for companies with meaningful exposure to artificial intelligence — either by revenue derived from AI products and services, or by strategic positioning as a core supplier to the AI ecosystem. This flexibility means the fund's holdings change as the AI landscape evolves, which is both a strength (the manager can pivot toward emerging opportunities) and a cost (active management comes with higher fees than a passive [index fund](/index-fund/) would charge).

## Why the fund exists and who it is for

Most conventional index funds weight holdings by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/), meaning the largest tech companies automatically dominate. A fund built on conventional indexing would capture some AI exposure simply because the biggest companies (like [Apple](/aapl-stock/) or [Microsoft](/msft-stock/)) are using AI, but the weighting would be driven by size, not by the company's AI intensity.

THNQ inverts that logic. It asks: which companies would cease to exist, or fail entirely, without artificial intelligence? The answer spans from chip designers to cloud operators to pure-play AI-software vendors — some tiny and fast-growing, some already massive but fundamentally dependent on AI's continued advancement. By constructing the portfolio around that question, the fund isolates the AI trend from the broader tech market. An investor might own a large-cap index fund (which gives her exposure to the whole market) and layer THNQ on top if she believes artificial intelligence will outpace the broader economy for years to come.

THNQ is a thematic bet, which means it works best for investors with a specific conviction — that AI adoption will continue to drive returns for a meaningful period — and a medium-to-long time horizon. Thematic funds are volatile and concentrated compared to broad indexes; they are not recommended for anyone who cannot tolerate significant drawdowns or who needs the money within a few years.

## Structure and costs

THNQ is a simple [equity ETF](/equity-etf/) that can be bought and sold on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) like any stock share. The fund is open-ended, meaning it can accept new money indefinitely, and shares can be created or redeemed by authorized [market makers](/market-maker-trading/) to keep the fund's price in line with the underlying portfolio value.

Directional's active-management model means the fund carries an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) in the range typical of actively managed equity funds — meaningfully higher than a passive AI-tracking index would cost, but lower than a traditional actively managed [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/) with similar strategy. The higher cost is paid in exchange for the manager's judgment in selecting holdings and rotating among them as the AI landscape shifts. Whether that active selection delivers value above and beyond passive indexing is a classic debate in finance; the past performance of any particular fund sheds some light on the question, but does not guarantee future results.

## Risks worth considering

Thematic funds concentrate around one idea, which amplifies both gains and losses. If AI adoption accelerates as many observers expect, an AI-intensive fund will outperform the broad market. If [capital flows](/capital-flows/) reverse — if investors decide AI will deliver less value than priced in — a concentrated AI fund falls faster than the overall market.

The semiconductor industry, which dominates many AI-exposure vehicles, is cyclical. Chip makers ride waves of surplus demand followed by oversupply and price cuts, and a THNQ holding with a peak valuation in a boom year can see earnings and stock price compress sharply during a downturn. The policy and geopolitical environment also matters: trade restrictions on chip exports to certain countries, changes to visa or immigration policy affecting tech talent, or regulatory crackdowns on AI development could all pressure holdings.

The other structural risk is thematic creep. As AI becomes a ubiquitous feature of most corporate businesses, the distinction between "AI stocks" and "all stocks" becomes blurrier. A company one year clearly earns its place in an AI fund might, a decade later, appear too mature or too diversified to be considered a pure-play AI business. That transition is natural and expected, but it means THNQ's current holdings are not a permanent list — active rotation is built into the model.

## How to research the fund

Start with the fund's prospectus and holdings list, which Direxion publishes and updates regularly. The prospectus lays out the investment objective, the screening criteria for inclusion, and the fund's risk factors. The current holdings reveal what the active manager sees as the highest-conviction AI plays today, though the portfolio will shift.

For longer-term context, watch the trajectory of key underlying companies — particularly the major semiconductor and cloud-infrastructure players that typically anchor an AI-exposure fund. Earnings reports from NVIDIA, [Advanced Micro Devices](/amd-stock/), Microsoft, Amazon, and [Alphabet](/googl-stock/) will telegraph the health of the AI infrastructure market. If those core holdings are reporting continued strength in AI-related revenue streams, the tailwind behind thematic AI funds remains intact. If adoption rates decelerate or pricing pressure emerges, the fund's performance will reflect it.

The fund's returns relative to the broader tech sector (typically tracked by QQQ or the NASDAQ-100) show whether the active manager's bets are paying off. Over a full [market cycle](/market-cycle/) — which includes downturns — a fund that outperforms its benchmark by a margin larger than its fee advantage has justified the higher costs; one that lags has not.
