*T. Rowe Price Financials ETF (NASDAQ: TFNS) is a passively managed exchange-traded fund that tracks a broad index of large-cap financial-services companies—commercial banks, insurance carriers, securities brokers, investment banks, and specialized financial intermediaries. It offers sector-level exposure to the entire financial system in a single, liquid instrument.*

## Holdings snapshot

The fund holds the major national banks (JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Citigroup), large regional lenders, and the insurance heavyweight carriers. Beyond banks and insurance, it captures broker-dealers, asset managers, credit card networks like Visa and Mastercard, and fintech-adjacent payment and lending platforms. The largest holdings typically represent 3–5 percent of fund assets each, so no single bank or insurer dominates. The fund rebalances quarterly and is cap-weighted, so moves track with which parts of the sector are largest at any given time.

Smaller regional banks and specialty lenders appear throughout the portfolio. The breadth is deliberate—a true sector fund, not a mega-cap bank fund disguised as one.

## Why it exists

Investors use TFNS for two reasons: to gain pure-play financials exposure without picking individual stocks, and to rotate sector allocations tactically. If you believe the financial sector will outperform or underperform the broader market, TFNS is cheaper and simpler than building a portfolio directly. The expense ratio is very low—well under 0.2 percent annually—because the fund is passively managed and the underlying holdings are liquid. Trading costs are minimal; the bid-ask spread is typically just a few cents per share.

## Risks: cycle, regulation, concentration

Banks are fundamentally cyclical. When the yield curve steepens, net interest margins widen and earnings rise. When the curve flattens or inverts, margins compress and loan losses loom. TFNS has no shelter from these swings—a severe credit cycle hits the entire sector at once. Regulatory risk is structural: capital requirements, resolution frameworks, conduct standards, and tax policy can all shift overnight and reshape the sector's profitability.

Concentration is modest but real. The largest banks and insurers—JP Morgan, Bank of America, UnitedHealth, Berkshire Hathaway—collectively drive much of the fund's movement. A stress event that hits the system simultaneously affects all of them, and there is no diversification escape. For most holders this is acceptable given the low fees, but it matters.

## How to use it

TFNS is bought by investors who want financials in their portfolio but prefer not to select individual banks or insurers. It is most useful as a core holding for those with a secular view on wealth concentration driving asset-management growth, or as a tactical trade when Fed policy or interest-rate expectations shift. It is not a proxy for economic growth (though it is correlated with it); it is a pure bet on the financial system's profitability and structure.

Monitor the fund against: the two-ten-year Treasury spread (margin driver for banks), credit spreads (loan-quality indicator), and major regulatory announcements. The T. Rowe Price website publishes the prospectus and full holdings list quarterly.
