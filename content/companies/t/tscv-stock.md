*The **Thrivent Small Cap Value ETF** (TSCV) is an [actively managed fund](/actively-managed-fund/) that hunts for undervalued small-cap stocks — companies with market values below roughly 20 billion dollars that trade below their [intrinsic value](/intrinsic-value/) according to fundamental metrics. By focusing on lesser-known, mispriced businesses with sound underlying operations, Thrivent's managers aim to capture the returns that come when the market eventually recognizes the value that was always there.*

> "Small-cap value is the market's attic: dusty, ignored, and occasionally hiding treasure."

## Why small-cap value matters

The small-cap value corner of the market is historically where individual security selection has mattered most. Large-cap stocks are researched relentlessly by thousands of analysts, pricing in most available information quickly. Small-cap stocks, by contrast, attract far less Wall Street attention. Analyst coverage is sparse, institutional ownership is lighter, and pricing inefficiencies persist longer. For an active manager with research capability and patience, this neglect creates opportunity.

The value element adds a second filter. TSCV does not simply buy any small-cap stock; it seeks those trading at discounts to earnings, cash flow, or book value — the classic value metrics. This combination, small-cap plus value, is traditionally high-risk but high-reward. Small companies are more volatile than large ones (they can grow fast or fail fast), and [value investing](/value-investing/) requires conviction to hold unpopular names while waiting for recognition.

## The fundamental approach

Thrivent uses fundamental analysis to populate TSCV's portfolio. This means the fund's managers study a company's financial statements, competitive position, management quality, and growth prospects to estimate what the business is actually worth, then buy when the stock price lags that estimate. The process is inherently subjective — different analysts may value the same company very differently — but it is grounded in the actual business economics rather than momentum or technical indicators.

The fund launched on November 17, 2025, a recent arrival in the small-cap value space. Despite its youth, it holds 60 different securities, providing meaningful [diversification](/diversification/) within the small-cap universe. The portfolio is actively tilted toward companies the management team believes are most mispriced relative to fundamental value. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) of 0.60% is reasonable for an actively managed small-cap fund, though passive small-cap value alternatives exist at lower cost.

## The active management bet

By choosing active management over an index, TSCV makes an explicit bet: that Thrivent's analysts can identify small-cap value stocks that will outperform before fees, and by enough to justify the higher cost. History shows this is a difficult bar. Most active small-cap funds underperform their benchmarks over long periods, dragged down by fees and the difficulty of predicting which forgotten stocks will regain favor.

The upside, if Thrivent's stock-picking proves sharp, is meaningful outperformance. Small-cap value has gone through long stretches of outperformance versus both large-cap and growth. In periods when value rebounds and small-cap stocks regain investor interest, a well-timed, fundamentally solid portfolio can shine. The downside is that if value continues to underperform or if Thrivent's fundamental research is not superior, the fund's higher cost will be a drag.

## Volatility and patience

TSCV will be more volatile than a large-cap index or a [balanced fund](/balanced-fund/). Small stocks move more sharply than large ones, and value stocks can remain depressed for years if sentiment turns against them. Investors in TSCV need to be prepared for drawdowns that could exceed 30 percent or more during [bear markets](/bear-market/), and for periods of underperformance versus growth-focused or large-cap alternatives.

The volatility is partly a cost of the opportunity; small-cap value does not reward the impatient. A portfolio of underfollowed, undervalued small companies can take years to deliver returns after purchase, because it takes time for the market to notice what an alert analyst has already spotted. Investors drawn to TSCV should have a multiyear time horizon and the emotional discipline to hold through extended periods of underperformance.

## Liquidity and concentration risks

A potential friction is liquidity. Small-cap stocks trade in lower volumes than large caps, which means the fund itself may have trouble buying or selling large blocks without moving prices. The fund's 60 holdings help mitigate this — wide diversification prevents any single name from dominating — but it is still a real consideration for large investors trying to enter or exit the fund quickly.

There is also a style-drift risk inherent in any active fund: if Thrivent's managers depart from the strict value discipline and begin chasing growth or momentum, the fund could behave quite differently from what its name suggests. Watching the quarterly holdings list and the manager commentary helps track whether the strategy is being executed as intended.

## Researching TSCV

The fund's fact sheet and prospectus detail the investment approach and the metrics used to define "undervalued." The quarterly holdings list is essential reading — it shows exactly which small-cap stocks are in the portfolio and their weightings. Comparing TSCV's returns to a passive small-cap value index (such as the Russell 2000 Value) reveals whether active management is adding value net of fees.

The expense ratio is another key number: in small-cap value, a few basis points of cost difference can compound into years of relative performance. Investors should also examine turnover — how often managers buy and sell holdings. High turnover can generate tax inefficiency and trading costs; low turnover suggests conviction and patience. Since TSCV is newly launched, tracking its progress over a few years of varying market conditions will be important before drawing long-term conclusions about manager skill.
