*The **[T. Rowe Price](/t-rowe-price/) Short Municipal [Income ETF](/income-etf/)** (TMNS) is an actively managed exchange-traded fund that holds predominantly short- to intermediate-term [municipal bonds](/municipal-bond/) — debt issued by states, cities, and other local authorities — seeking to deliver tax-free income to shareholders while limiting the principal risk that comes with longer [bond](/bond/) durations.*

T. Rowe Price, a asset-management firm based in Baltimore, manages TMNS as one of its municipal-income lineup. The fund targets investors in higher tax brackets for whom the yield available on municipal bonds — which are exempt from federal (and often state and local) income tax — makes more economic sense than taxable alternatives. By focusing on shorter maturities (typically bonds due within five to seven years), TMNS trades some yield for reduced price volatility: a bond that matures sooner is less sensitive to interest-rate swings than one due in twenty years.

The fund is actively managed, meaning T. Rowe Price's portfolio team selects individual municipal issuers rather than tracking an index. This creates a few implications. First, the fund's holdings and weightings can shift based on the managers' conviction about which states, municipalities, and project-backed bonds offer value. Second, because the portfolio is not mechanically rebalancing to a fixed benchmark, the fund can hunt for relative-value opportunities — undervalued bonds or issuers with improving credit conditions. Third, the fund carries higher operational costs than a passive municipal-bond [index fund](/index-fund/) would, though the hope is that active management delivers returns sufficient to justify those costs.

The core holding pattern is tax-exempt general-obligation bonds (backed by the full taxing power of a municipality), [revenue bonds](/revenue-bond/) (backed by a specific project's cash flow, such as an airport toll or a utility bill), and bonds backed by state and local authorities across the country. TMNS does not concentrate in a single state, which helps insulate it from idiosyncratic state fiscal stress, though positions vary by what the managers see as the most attractive risk-return opportunities at any given time.

Duration — the sensitivity of a bond's price to interest-rate moves — is the key technical constraint. The fund targets a duration shorter than the Bloomberg Municipal Bond Index to reduce the principal drawdown in a rising-rate environment. When [interest rates](/interest-rate/) fall, longer bonds outperform; TMNS trades that potential upside for lower volatility and a smaller peak-to-trough decline when rates move up. For someone prioritizing steady tax-free cash flow over capital appreciation, that trade-off aligns with the goal.

The real tension in a municipal-bond fund is [credit risk](/credit-risk/): issuers do occasionally default, and a deteriorating local economy or [pension obligation](/pension-obligation/) can threaten bond valuations before maturity. T. Rowe Price's managers are responsible for assessing which municipalities and projects are sound and which are not. Over time, their track record matters more than any promise that "municipals are safe" — the market for tax-exempt debt is large and deep enough that credit analysis rather than broad sector risk is where value is made or lost.

[Expense ratios](/expense-ratio/) on actively managed municipal ETFs typically run higher than index funds, reflecting the cost of research and portfolio management. Tax-exempt income itself is the fund's primary appeal, and a holder in a high federal tax bracket might find the after-tax yield attractive even after paying these costs. Someone in a lower tax bracket should compare the pre-tax yield of TMNS against the after-tax yield of a taxable bond fund to determine which is actually better for their situation.

TMNS trades on a major exchange like any ETF, so it can be bought and sold throughout the trading day at prices that fluctuate with the market, unlike traditional open-end municipal funds (which price only once per day). That liquidity makes it suitable for investors who might want to add to or reduce their position without the delay of fund-order settlement.

## How to research TMNS

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, available from T. Rowe Price's website, which lay out the fund's strategy, fees, and holdings. The prospectus will detail how the fund defines "short" duration (typically a range rather than a fixed maximum), its policies on credit quality and [diversification](/diversification/), and the tax implications of holding it in taxable versus tax-deferred accounts. (Municipal-fund holdings in a [traditional IRA](/traditional-ira/) are actually disadvantageous, since the tax-exemption is wasted inside an account that is already tax-deferred.)

The fund's top holdings list shows which municipalities and project types are favored at any moment. Monitoring the average duration and average credit quality over time reveals whether the managers are shifting toward or away from risk. Compare TMNS's yield and volatility to a passive municipal-bond index fund or a competitor fund like Vanguard's short-term municipal bond offerings to see whether active management is delivering relative value. Finally, watch the composition of the fund's cash flows: as shorter bonds mature, managers must reinvest in new securities, which means the fund's exposure is constantly refreshed.
