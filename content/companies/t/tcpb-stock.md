*TCPB is an exchange-traded fund that holds a diversified portfolio of [bonds](/bond/) across US government securities, corporate debt, and mortgage-backed obligations. The fund aims to capture income and price appreciation across a broad spectrum of the bond market, shifting its allocation based on which segments of fixed income appear most attractive.*

## Government and agency bonds

The foundation of TCPB's holdings includes US Treasury securities and debt issued by government-sponsored enterprises like [Fannie Mae](/fannie-mae/) and [Freddie Mac](/freddie-mac/). Treasuries are the baseline of all fixed-income investing — they carry no [credit risk](/credit-risk/) (the US government can always print money) but offer modest yields tied to [inflation expectations](/inflation-expectations/) and the [Federal Reserve's](/federal-reserve/) rate-setting. The fund typically holds both short-term Treasuries (which reset quickly to new rates) and longer-maturity pieces that lock in yields but are sensitive to rate changes.

Government-agency bonds backed by mortgages (agency mortgage-backed securities) represent a significant portion of TCPB's allocation. These securities are created when banks bundle pools of home loans into tradeable packages. The fund receives monthly principal and interest payments from homeowners' mortgage payments. Because the mortgages themselves carry credit risk (homeowners can default), but the agency guarantee backstops principal repayment, agency mortgage-backed bonds are higher-yielding than [Treasury bonds](/treasury-bond/) but still extremely safe.

## Investment-grade corporate debt

A meaningful share of TCPB is invested in [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/) — debt issued by large, established companies with stable cash flows and strong [credit ratings](/credit-rating/). Investment-grade corporate bonds span the BBB to AAA range, meaning companies like pharmaceutical giants, utilities, telecommunications firms, and industrial manufacturers whose bonds are considered low-risk. These bonds carry higher yields than Treasuries because there is some probability that the issuing company's business could deteriorate and it could fail to repay.

The "core plus" designation in the fund's name signals that while the bulk of holdings are investment-grade (BBB or higher), the fund reserves the right to hold a modest allocation to below-investment-grade, or "high-yield," corporate bonds. This adds yield but introduces credit risk. In a strong economy, TCPB will tilt higher on the yield spectrum, holding riskier names. In a downturn, the fund may reduce high-yield exposure to preserve capital.

## Credit exposure and economic sensitivity

The mix of investment-grade corporate bonds in TCPB creates sensitivity to the economic cycle. When corporate earnings are strong and default risk is low, corporate bonds trade at tighter spreads (lower yields relative to Treasuries), and their prices rise. When a [recession](/recession/) looms or spreads widen, corporate bond prices fall. The fund thus captures both the income from [coupon payments](/coupon-payment/) and the potential for price appreciation (or [depreciation](/depreciation/)) as credit conditions shift.

Thrivent's investment team rotates the fund's positioning based on their view of where value lies in fixed income. In periods of economic strength, they might increase corporate exposure. In periods of tightening credit, they might shift to Treasuries and agencies. This active management — selecting which parts of the bond market to overweight — is what distinguishes TCPB from a passive bond-index fund.

## Duration and interest-rate sensitivity

Bond prices move inversely to [interest rates](/interest-rate/): when the Federal Reserve raises rates, existing bonds paying lower coupons lose value. TCPB's price sensitivity to rate changes (called duration) typically sits somewhere in the middle of the bond spectrum — not as long as a 20-year Treasury bond, but longer than a floating-rate security that resets constantly. This moderate duration means the fund will decline when rates rise sharply but will also appreciate when rates fall.

The fund's duration can shift based on positioning. If the investment team expects rates to fall, they extend duration by buying longer-maturity bonds, betting that price appreciation will be large. If they expect rates to rise, they shorten duration to limit losses. This dynamic positioning is part of Thrivent's active-management approach and distinguishes the fund from a passive bond-index tracker with fixed duration.

## High-yield and emerging-market bonds

Within the "core plus" framework, TCPB holds some below-investment-grade corporate debt and potentially bonds issued by foreign governments and emerging-market corporations. These securities carry higher credit risk but offer substantially higher yields to compensate. The fund limits these allocations (they are "plus" to the core holding, not the center), but in years when credit is strong and investors are willing to take risk, TCPB will dial up exposure and capture the additional income.

Emerging-market bonds introduce [currency risk](/currency-risk/) as well: if a foreign government's currency weakens, the dollar value of its debt falls even if the bond itself does not default. Thrivent manages this currency risk actively, sometimes hedging exposure back to the [US dollar](/us-dollar/) and sometimes allowing the fund to benefit (or suffer) from currency moves.

## Yield and income distribution

TCPB distributes income monthly to shareholders, typically from the coupons paid by the underlying bonds. The yield varies based on the level of interest rates and the fund's positioning — in high-rate environments, yields are substantial; in low-rate environments, they compress. The fund's yield is typically lower than [high-yield bond funds](/high-yield-bond-fund/) but higher than a pure Treasury fund.

Total return includes both the yield income and any capital appreciation or depreciation from price changes. In years when rates fall, TCPB can generate strong total returns even if the yield itself is modest. In years when rates rise, the fund may deliver negative returns despite the coupon income, because the mark-to-market loss on existing bonds outweighs the income collected.

## Expenses and trading

As an ETF, TCPB trades on an exchange throughout the day at prices that fluctuate. The fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is typically 0.25% to 0.40%, reasonable for an actively managed bond fund but higher than passive bond-index ETFs. The daily trading feature makes it easy to buy and sell, and in normal markets liquidity is deep. During market stress or periods when bondholders are rushing for exits, ETF liquidity can tighten, and [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) can widen.

## Who TCPB suits and how to research it

TCPB appeals to income-focused investors seeking broad fixed-income exposure with modest upside from [credit spread](/credit-spread/) tightening. It suits conservative portfolio allocations where bonds serve as the ballast. It can also appear in total-return portfolios that blend growth and income across asset classes.

To research TCPB, start with Thrivent's fund fact sheet and prospectus, which detail the investment strategy and current allocation to Treasuries, corporate bonds, and high-yield debt. Review the fund's interest-rate duration and how it compares to a broad bond-index benchmark. Examine the fund's sector exposure within corporate holdings — concentration in banking, energy, or real estate creates different risk profiles. Monitor the fund's yield relative to alternatives (pure Treasury funds, high-yield funds, total-bond-market [index funds](/index-fund/)) to evaluate whether the "core plus" positioning justifies the expense ratio. Check the fund's performance through complete [credit cycles](/credit-cycle/) — both a strong period and a recession — to understand its downside behavior.
