*The State Street Galaxy Transformative Tech Accelerators ETF (TEKX) is a thematic ETF designed to capture exposure to companies benefiting from transformative technologies across multiple domains. Rather than betting on a single technology or industry, TEKX diversifies across several major innovation themes and the companies positioned as beneficiaries.*

## What is thematic investing and how does TEKX use it?

Thematic investing is a top-down approach that identifies a structural, long-term trend or technology expected to reshape the economy, then buys the companies positioned to benefit from that trend, regardless of their traditional industry classification. Instead of investing in "semiconductor stocks" or "pharmaceutical stocks," a thematic fund might invest in companies powering "artificial intelligence" which could include chip designers, software companies, cloud providers, and enterprises deploying AI.

TEKX applies this philosophy across multiple themes simultaneously. The fund identifies several transformative technology domains — artificial intelligence, cloud infrastructure, biotechnology, quantum computing, and others — and builds a portfolio of companies that are benefiting from or enabling these technologies. This is a different approach than either broad-market indexing (which owns everything) or single-industry investing (which owns only semiconductors or only software).

## Which technology themes does TEKX cover?

The exact themes evolve as the fund manager updates its view of which technologies matter most. Common themes in State Street Galaxy's universe typically include:

**Artificial intelligence and machine learning** — companies building AI chips (companies like Nvidia), offering cloud AI services, or operating as AI software platforms. The theme includes both the infrastructure layer (computing power) and the application layer (AI software and services).

**Cloud infrastructure and data centers** — companies providing cloud computing platforms, data-center operators, and network infrastructure supporting cloud workloads. This theme benefits from the ongoing shift of computing from on-premises to the cloud.

**Biotechnology and genomics** — companies using genetic sequencing, CRISPR gene editing, and computational biology to develop new drugs and diagnostics. This theme is long-term and speculative; many portfolio companies have not yet commercialized products.

**Advanced materials and semiconductors** — companies producing semiconductors for specialized applications, advanced materials for batteries or semiconductors, or other manufacturing inputs for next-generation technologies.

**Quantum computing and advanced computing** — companies researching quantum computers or advanced chip architectures. This is an early-stage theme; commercialization is years away for most players.

The fund may weight these themes differently at different times, and may add or drop themes as technology advances.

## How does State Street Galaxy select specific companies?

The fund's methodology typically combines quantitative screening with thematic judgment. The process might include:

Defining each theme in concrete terms — what technologies, products, and business models qualify?

Screening the market for companies with material exposure to each theme — typically those where the theme represents a meaningful portion of the company's revenue or R&D.

Applying quality and liquidity filters — avoiding micro-cap stocks, insisting on reasonable profitability or clear paths to it, and favoring stocks with sufficient trading volume.

Weighting the portfolio so that each theme contributes meaningfully but no single company dominates — this avoids making TEKX a de facto bet on a single mega-cap stock.

The result is a diversified portfolio of 50 to 100 companies across several themes, with regular rebalancing and theme-weighting adjustments as the fund manager's view of the technological landscape evolves.

## What are the costs and structure?

TEKX is a standard ETF, meaning it trades on an exchange, can be created and redeemed in large blocks, and is subject to SEC oversight. State Street, one of the world's largest asset managers, sponsors and manages the fund. The expense ratio is typically moderate — higher than a plain S&P 500 index fund, but lower than actively managed mutual funds, reflecting the systematic but not fully automated nature of the stock-selection process.

## What are the risks specific to TEKX?

The first risk is concentration within themes. If TEKX's AI theme consists mostly of Nvidia and a handful of chip companies, and AI sentiment shifts sharply, the fund can experience large drawdowns despite being nominally diversified. Thematic funds can mask concentration risk by distributing across companies, but if all those companies are exposed to a single narrative, the risk remains.

The second risk is theme obsolescence. Technologies and investors' enthusiasm for them wax and wane. Biotechnology was hailed as a transformative theme in 2015; valuations have since cooled, and returns have disappointed. Quantum computing may be a transformative theme years from now, but if commercialization takes longer than expected, exposure today is speculative.

The third is high volatility. Growth-oriented, technology-heavy portfolios experience larger drawdowns than the broad market. TEKX is not a defensive holding; in a severe recession or a tech sell-off, the fund can fall 40% or more.

The fourth is the high proportion of unprofitable or early-stage companies. Many biotechnology and quantum-computing companies in TEKX may not have meaningful revenue. These are bets on future breakthroughs, not established businesses. The failure rate is high.

The fifth is that State Street's thematic definition and weighting is discretionary and can change. Unlike an index fund, which follows a published rule, TEKX's holdings shift based on the fund manager's evolving view of which companies are "truly" transformed by technology. This brings skill risk: if the thematic framework is wrong, returns will suffer.

## Who is TEKX for?

TEKX is for investors who believe that artificial intelligence, quantum computing, biotechnology, and cloud infrastructure will reshape the economy over the next decade, and who are willing to bet on a diversified basket of companies exposed to these trends. It is ideal for investors with high risk tolerance, a long time horizon, and the ability to endure significant interim losses.

It is not suitable for conservative investors, those nearing retirement, or those uncomfortable with the volatility of speculative, often-unprofitable technology companies.

## How to research TEKX

Obtain State Street Galaxy's prospectus and detailed methodology document explaining which technology themes the fund currently emphasizes and how it selects companies within each. Ask for the fund's current holdings and review them critically: Are the companies actually benefiting from the stated themes, or is it a forced fit? How many are profitable, and how many are speculative? Compare TEKX's performance over a complete market cycle (at least one major downturn) to the Nasdaq and to other thematic technology ETFs. Check the fund's expense ratio and compare it to competitors. Read commentary from technology analysts and investors about whether each theme is genuinely transformative and whether the fund's allocation makes sense. Finally, assess your own conviction about the technology themes and your tolerance for 20% or greater declines.
