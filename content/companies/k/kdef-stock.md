*The **PLUS Korea Defense Industry Index ETF** (KDEF) is an exchange-traded fund that holds shares of South Korean companies engaged in defense, aerospace, and related technologies — a concentrated bet on a small, wealthy nation's military-industrial sector and its suppliers.*

## What is South Korea's defense industry?

South Korea's defense sector exists because of geography and history. The country faces North Korea directly across a demilitarized zone, and has for over seventy years. That ongoing security imperative has built a sophisticated, capital-intensive domestic defense industry. South Korean companies manufacture fighter jets, missiles, radar systems, naval ships, and ammunition — both for the Korean military and for export to allied nations. The sector also includes electronics, communications, and materials suppliers that feed into defense applications.

The industry is strategically important to the country, heavily regulated, and deeply intertwined with the government. Major defense contractors work under strict export controls, security clearances, and government oversight. Many are subsidiaries of larger chaebol — the massive conglomerated family groups that dominate the Korean economy — giving them access to capital and diversified operations beyond defense.

## Why own a Korea defense ETF?

An investor might hold KDEF for several reasons. First, betting on Korean geopolitical tension — the belief that security concerns will drive higher defense spending, or that tensions with North Korea could escalate and boost demand for Korean military hardware. Second, gaining exposure to a developed Asian economy's high-tech manufacturing sector, which can benefit from broader economic growth and export demand. Third, diversifying away from Western defense contractors by owning players in a different region.

The sector has outperformed periods when geopolitical risk was rising, North Korea conducted provocations, or when military modernization spending accelerated. Conversely, periods of reduced tensions or political movements toward reconciliation have pressured the sector. Like any theme-based ETF, KDEF is volatile and concentrated — it is not a way to own a diversified portfolio, but a way to make a specific directional bet.

## What companies are in KDEF?

The fund typically holds a roster of South Korean defense and aerospace contractors. The largest are subsidiaries of major chaebol — for instance, defense divisions of companies also engaged in electronics, heavy industry, or automotive. Mid-sized and smaller pure-play defense firms round out the index. The exact holdings appear in the fund's fact sheet and prospectus. The index methodology determines which firms qualify as "defense industry" based on revenue sources, licensing, and regulatory classification.

Because the universe of pure Korean defense companies is small, KDEF is concentrated. A handful of firms may represent a significant slice of the portfolio, meaning the fund's performance is driven by the largest holdings rather than evenly distributed across dozens of stocks. That concentration amplifies both gains and losses relative to a broader emerging-market or Asian equity fund.

## The geopolitical premium and risks

Unlike consumer-goods companies or tech firms, defense contractors ride geopolitical tides. A period of rising tensions on the peninsula, new military provocations by North Korea, or political shifts affecting security spending can lift the entire sector. Conversely, diplomatic breakthroughs, wars ending elsewhere, or budget pressures reducing defense budgets can depress the sector. This makes KDEF unusually sensitive to news flow and political developments.

There is also a liquidity and [currency risk](/currency-risk/). South Korea trades in won, not dollars. An investor holding KDEF is exposed to currency moves between the dollar and the won — if the won weakens, Korean stocks fall in dollar terms even if share prices in Korea stay flat. The fund may or may not hedge this currency risk; the prospectus states its approach.

Regulatory risk is real. South Korea controls defense exports tightly, and major geopolitical shifts — say, a formal peace settlement with the North — could reshape the sector's growth prospects or trigger government restructuring of the industry. Changes in U.S. or allied defense spending also matter, because Korean contractors export to American allies and depend on those relationships.

## Index structure and transparency

KDEF is a passive ETF tracking an index of Korean defense companies. The index is published and transparent, and the fund's job is to hold the index constituents with minimal deviation. This means you can easily see which companies the fund owns, check their fundamentals independently, and compare the fund's returns to its benchmark to verify that it is tracking efficiently.

The index includes companies on the Korean [stock exchange](/stock-exchange/) (KOSPI) meeting the defense-industry classification. As companies join or leave the index — due to corporate actions, [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) changes, or regulatory reclassification — the fund adjusts its holdings. This mechanical rebalancing is more transparent than an actively managed [sector fund](/sector-fund/) where a manager makes discretionary picks.

## Trading costs and expense ratio

The fund trades throughout the day at prices set by the market, with spreads between buy and sell prices determined by the depth of trading. A fund tracking a popular index in a liquid market generally has tight spreads. Korean equities are liquid, but a specialty [sector ETF](/sector-etf/) may have lower volume than a broad market fund, which can widen spreads and increase trading costs for large positions.

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) covers the fund's operations and any [currency hedging](/currency-hedging/). For a passive sector ETF, the ratio is typically modest, under 0.75 percent annually, reflecting low active trading. The prospectus discloses the exact fee.

## How to research and evaluate KDEF

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, which state the index methodology, current holdings, expense ratio, and currency-hedging policy. Visit the Korean stock exchange or major financial data providers to look up the fundamentals of the underlying firms — their earnings, valuation, and industry position.

Watch Korean economic indicators and defense-spending announcements, as these move the entire sector. Track North Korean news, because provocations or statements about military strength tend to correlate with upswings in Korean defense spending. Monitor geopolitical commentary and any discussions of inter-Korean relations or U.S. security commitments, as major shifts can reshape the sector's growth outlook.

Compare KDEF to other Asia-Pacific [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/) or broader Korean market ETFs to understand whether the defense-sector bet is outperforming or lagging the broader economy. Over long periods, the sector's returns will track both Korean economic growth and the cyclical nature of defense spending and geopolitical risk. A fund this specialized is best suited to investors with a deliberate view on Korean defense spending or Korean geopolitical risk, not as a core portfolio holding, but as a thematic satellite position.
