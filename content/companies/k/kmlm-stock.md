KraneShares Mount Lucas [Managed Futures](/managed-futures/) Index Strategy ETF (ticker KMLM) offers investors exposure to the managed futures space — a systematic, rules-based approach to financial markets that uses trends and momentum across a broad range of assets. The fund does not try to beat the market or predict specific price moves; instead, it replicates the returns of a published index designed to capture the returns of a diverse set of managed futures strategies. Managed futures have historically behaved differently from stocks and [bonds](/bond/) in many market environments, rising when traditional assets fall and vice versa, which makes them useful for a portfolio seeking [diversification](/diversification/).

## What the index measures

The Mount Lucas Managed Futures Index is built to approximate the returns a diversified group of professional managed futures traders might generate — traders who follow systematic signals derived from price trends and momentum across global markets. Unlike an index of company stocks, which measures the performance of businesses themselves, this index tracks *strategies* — rules-based algorithms that say "buy this when the trend is up" and "sell when it turns." The index includes exposures to roughly a dozen major commodity markets ([crude oil](/crude-oil/), [natural gas](/natural-gas/), gold, agricultural futures), [currency pairs](/currency-pair/) (dollar against major currencies), government bonds (US, European, Japanese), and equity indices. No single asset dominates; instead, the index spreads the money across these categories so that the overall portfolio can participate in upward trends in any of them.

The logic is intuitive: a trend-following approach can make money when any market is trending strongly up, regardless of what that market is. In a period when stocks are rising, it may catch some of that move. When stocks are falling but commodities are rallying, it can pivot toward commodities. When everything is falling but falling at different speeds, the signal might shift to shorter-term positions or move to cash-like safety. The historical appeal is that managed futures return something different from — and often opposite to — stock returns in extreme market environments, which makes the strategy valuable to a traditional portfolio as insurance.

## How the fund holds this exposure

KMLM does not directly hold [futures contracts](/futures-contract/) or employ real traders making discretionary decisions. Instead, it holds a transparent, replicable portfolio constructed monthly to mimic the performance of the index itself. The portfolio might consist of long and short positions across many different instruments — long [commodity ETFs](/commodity-etf/) and short equity indices simultaneously, for instance — or it might hold the underlying futures themselves in a synthetic wrapper. The actual holdings can shift as the underlying index changes, but the goal is mechanical: follow the index as closely as possible rather than augment or second-guess it.

This structure makes the fund accessible to retail investors who could not easily replicate managed futures strategies on their own. A typical investor cannot open a managed-futures trading account or negotiate access to commodity futures; KMLM provides that exposure in a transparent, tax-efficient wrapper that can be bought and held like any other ETF.

## The appeal and the tradeoffs

Managed futures have generated a loyal following among professional investors because they offer genuinely different return patterns from traditional assets. In severe downturns — 2008, early 2020, periods of rising [inflation](/inflation/) — the strategy has sometimes posted positive returns while stocks fell, providing real portfolio diversification. This is not guaranteed; the strategy can lose money like any other, especially in choppy, sideways markets where trends are short-lived or false, or when all assets rise or fall together for extended periods. But the long-term empirical record suggests that a diversified managed futures portfolio, held alongside stocks and bonds, can reduce overall portfolio volatility and smooth the ride through diverse [market cycles](/market-cycle/).

The cost of that diversification comes in several forms. First, systematic trend-following algorithms are not optimized for rising markets in general; they are optimized for *catching trends whenever they occur*, regardless of direction. This means a managed futures strategy may underperform during long [bull markets](/bull-market/) where simply holding stocks is the easiest path. Second, the strategy assumes that trends are real and tradeable — an assumption that breaks down in range-bound or choppy markets. Third, unlike equity indices where you are holding a piece of actual business earnings, a managed futures strategy is a *zero-sum* game: every dollar of gain to the fund comes at the expense of someone on the other side of a trade. The cost of execution and the fees embedded in the fund matter more than in a simple stock index.

## Costs and structure

KMLM trades like any other ETF — it can be bought and sold during regular market hours on [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/), and it does not have daily redemption mechanics like some managed-futures funds. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is low relative to actively managed futures strategies (which charge far higher fees), though marginally higher than the cheapest broad stock indices because executing the underlying strategy requires more trading and derivatives infrastructure.

Because the fund holds futures and other derivatives, it receives the tax treatment of a regulated futures contract (RFCs) under US tax law. This means that gains are taxed as 60% long-term capital gains and 40% short-term capital gains, regardless of how long the investor has held the fund — a favorable treatment if you are in a high tax bracket, though the precise impact depends on your other holdings and income.

## Who this fund is for

KMLM is designed for investors who believe that diversification beyond stocks and bonds is valuable and who want a low-cost, transparent way to achieve it. It is not a tactical tool for short-term trading — it is a strategic holding, meant to be paired with equity and bond indices in a broader portfolio. The managed futures space itself demands patience; the strategy will underperform in some years and outperform in others, and the best returns come from holding it through a full cycle.

Investors researching the fund should start with the prospectus and fact sheet from KraneShares, which breaks down the exact holdings and the methodology of the underlying Mount Lucas index. Because the fund holds leveraged instruments, it's important to understand the daily mechanics and whether they introduce any drift from the index over time. Published performance history across different market periods — especially how the fund performed during past downturns — gives a sense of what the "diversification" actually means in practice.
