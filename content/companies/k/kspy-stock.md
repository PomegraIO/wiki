## Dynamic hedging for volatile markets

KSPY is built on a simple premise: give investors S&P 500 exposure but manage the risk. Rather than holding the index straight, the fund implements a hedging strategy designed and managed by Hedgeye Asset Management, an options and risk-management firm. The fund tracks the Hedgeye Hedged Equity Index, which combines S&P 500 holdings with a layer of put and [call options](/call-option/) that shift based on market conditions.

The mechanical rule is elegant. Each trading day, Hedgeye measures where the S&P 500 sits relative to predefined ranges. Based on that signal, the index switches between three different options postures — effectively toggling between different hedging intensities. When the market trades in certain bands, one set of put and [call spreads](/call-spread/) is active. When it moves elsewhere, the strategy pivots. This daily recalibration means the hedge tightens and loosens dynamically rather than staying static.

## What the risk range does

Hedgeye's framework is built on a proprietary signal called the Risk Range, which analyzes the daily trading range of the S&P 500. The idea is that when volatility is high and trading patterns are wild, the market is showing fear and repricing. The hedge responds by tightening protection. Conversely, on calm days with narrow ranges, the strategy relaxes the hedge to let more upside through.

The three switchable approaches work through [put spreads](/put-spread/) (buying downside protection while selling further-out puts to fund it) and call spreads (capping some upside to offset the cost of protection). The exact strikes and spreads change based on the Risk Range signal, so the fund is neither fully hedged nor fully exposed at any moment. It exists in a managed middle, trading some upside to reduce downside volatility.

## The cost of hedging

Protection is never free. Because the fund holds options that offset some upside moves, returns typically lag the plain S&P 500 in strong [bull markets](/bull-market/). Over a measured time span, the drag from hedging costs and the cap on gains is visible when KSPY is compared side-by-side to an unhedged S&P 500 ETF. A reader examining historical data will see that in years when stocks rose sharply, KSPY captured a lower percentage of the gain. In years of decline or sideways action, the hedge meaningfully reduced losses.

The fund arrived in July 2024, so investors can examine how the strategy performed through various market environments since inception — from Fed rate-cut anticipation to earnings seasons to geopolitical shocks. The key question is whether the reduction in volatility and downside is worth the cost in upside capture.

## Who KSPY serves and how to research it

This fund suits investors who own meaningful stock allocations and wish to reduce portfolio volatility without abandoning equities entirely. It appeals to those nearing retirement, those with low risk tolerance, or those who expect a choppy market and want a smoother ride. It is not suitable for long-term growth investors with high risk tolerance, since the hedging drag compounds over decades.

To evaluate KSPY, examine the prospectus and fact sheet for the specific [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) and the detailed mechanics of how the three hedging approaches work. Track the fund's performance versus the S&P 500 across bull, bear, and sideways market environments. Review Hedgeye's methodology document to understand the Risk Range signal. Most critically, compare the outcome: Did the volatility reduction justify the return drag in real-world testing? The fund's short history means answers are still forming, but the framework is transparent and auditable.
