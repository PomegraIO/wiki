What exactly is Vietnam's [stock market](/stock-market/), and why does a specific fund focus on growth companies within it?

Vietnam is a rapidly developing Southeast Asian economy where stock-exchange trading has become more accessible and liquid in recent decades. The Ho Chi Minh [Stock Exchange](/stock-exchange/) and the Hanoi Stock Exchange together form Vietnam's equity market, though a cap on foreign ownership and periodic regulatory restrictions complicate the picture. KraneShares Dragon Capital Growth of Vietnam Index ETF (KPHO) is designed to give Western investors exposure to Vietnamese companies positioned for growth — specifically, a curated index rather than the entire market.

How does the fund choose which Vietnamese companies to hold?

KraneShares partnered with Dragon Capital, a Vietnam-focused investment firm, to build an index of Vietnamese companies meeting certain criteria. The index is not all 700-plus stocks trading in Vietnam, but a subset meeting screens for scale, liquidity, and growth characteristics. The idea is to exclude micro-cap names, restrict to shares that trade with reasonable volume, and tilt toward companies in expanding sectors: technology, consumer discretionary, healthcare, and financial services. This curation keeps the fund more liquid and more focused on genuine growth stories than a simple "all Vietnamese stocks" approach would be.

What businesses does the fund typically own?

Vietnamese equities in growth-oriented sectors include technology startups and established software firms, e-commerce and consumer-goods companies serving a rising middle class, financial-services businesses gaining market share, and manufacturing and export-oriented firms. Vietnam has attracted manufacturers seeking to diversify supply chains away from China, which can create growth opportunities. However, Vietnam's stock market remains dominated by a handful of names, so [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) is inherent.

What are the main risks of investing through KPHO?

Vietnam carries emerging-market risk: the economy and its regulatory environment are less stable than developed countries, corporate governance can be weaker, and [political risk](/political-risk/) is real. [Currency risk](/currency-risk/) is significant — returns are denominated in Vietnamese dong, so swings in the dollar-to-dong exchange rate directly affect returns to U.S. investors. There are also [liquidity risks](/liquidity-risk/) for some holdings; stocks that look liquid at the index level might be hard to move in size. Foreign ownership restrictions in Vietnam limit how much of certain sectors any non-Vietnamese investor can own, creating structural constraints on the fund's positioning. And growth forecasts for Vietnam's companies are often optimistic — the actual earnings delivery may disappoint, especially in downturns.

How is KPHO structured, and what does it cost?

KPHO is a traditional ETF that trades on U.S. exchanges in [U.S. dollars](/us-dollar/). It holds the underlying Vietnamese securities in a [custodial account](/custodial-account/). The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is typically in the 0.70–0.90% range annually, which is reasonable for a specialized emerging-market product but higher than a U.S. equity [index fund](/index-fund/). The fund is liquid for its size, meaning it can be bought and sold on the exchange with tight spreads, though the underlying holdings can trade in lower volumes.

Who typically holds Vietnam-focused ETFs like this one?

Investors with a thesis that Vietnam's economy and companies will grow faster than the global average and offer valuations still attractive relative to that growth. Long-term investors building emerging-market [diversification](/diversification/) across geographies. Traders making tactical bets on Vietnam's near-term performance or hedging China exposure with a Vietnam alternative. Investors specifically interested in Vietnam's structural advantages: a young population, a manufacturing base, and growing consumer spending.

What should a researcher investigate before buying?

Begin with KraneShares' fact sheet and index documentation, which should lay out the exact holdings, the sector weights, and the index methodology. Compare the fund's valuation metrics — price-to-earnings, price-to-book — against other Vietnam ETFs and against global emerging-market benchmarks to see whether you are buying at a reasonable price. Track the fund's performance against rival Vietnam offerings and against the broader emerging-market index to understand whether KraneShares' curation has added value. Study Vietnam's regulatory environment: foreign ownership caps, tax policy, and political risk. And understand your currency hedge: KPHO is unhedged, so a strengthening dollar will reduce returns even if Vietnamese stocks rise in local-currency terms. If you are concerned about that, compare KPHO to a Vietnamese ETF hedged to the dollar, though hedging carries costs of its own.
