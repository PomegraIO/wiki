KMLI is a leveraged exchange-traded product launched by KraneShares that aims to deliver twice the daily return of Mercado Libre stock (MELI). Like other daily-reset [leveraged ETFs](/leveraged-etf/), KMLI is built for short-term traders expecting sharp moves in Mercado Libre's share price over days or a few weeks, not for buy-and-hold investors. The structure and mechanics are identical to other 2x daily-reset funds — daily rebalancing, volatility decay in choppy markets, [expense ratios](/expense-ratio/) above standard [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/), and explicit warnings in the prospectus against long-term holding.

## Mercado Libre and why it matters for traders

Mercado Libre is Latin America's dominant e-commerce and digital-payments platform. Founded in 1999, the company has grown from an online marketplace — the Amazon of Argentina and Brazil — into a sprawling financial-technology business that includes payments, lending, and advertising. MELI stock is one of the few ways for investors to gain direct exposure to Latin American consumer tech growth.

The stock carries three key characteristics that attract traders to a leveraged product like KMLI. First, MELI is volatile. Latin American markets and single-company exposure can produce large daily price swings, especially around earnings announcements, regulatory shifts in Brazil or Argentina, or macroeconomic shocks in the region. Second, MELI earnings cycles and new-product launches create forecastable catalysts — moments when traders expect big moves. Third, MELI trades on [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) with reasonable liquidity, so a leveraged product linked to it has tight spreads and can be entered and exited throughout the trading day.

## The evolution of KMLI in the leveraged-ETF landscape

KraneShares is a Colorado-based ETF issuer founded in 2009 that specializes in thematic and exotic indices. The company is known for developing funds around emerging-market themes, special sectors, and increasingly, leveraged and inverse products targeting single stocks. KMLI arrived as part of KraneShares' expansion into the leveraged-single-stock ETF space — a wave of product launches beginning in the early 2020s that made 2x and 3x daily bets on individual stocks available to retail investors through ordinary brokerage accounts.

The leveraged-ETF category itself is older. Daily-reset 3x leverage on broad indices like the S&P 500 has been available for two decades. What is newer is KraneShares' model of offering 2x daily leverage on individual stocks, including ones with high volatility and emerging-market exposure like MELI. KMLI fits into a broader shift toward retail-friendly tactical trading vehicles.

## How KMLI works: mechanics and costs

KMLI rebalances at the close of every trading day. The fund holds a combination of MELI shares and cash (or derivatives, depending on the fund's current structure) sized so that a 1 percent move in MELI produces approximately a 2 percent move in KMLI.

Like all daily-reset leveraged products, KMLI suffers from volatility decay when MELI trades sideways or oscillates. A 5 percent up day followed by a 5 percent down day should leave MELI flat, but KMLI starting at 100 would rise to 110 (2x the 5 percent gain), then fall to 103.9 (2x the 5 percent loss applied to the 110 base), resulting in a 3.9 percent loss on zero net movement of the underlying.

KraneShares charges approximately 0.95 percent per annum, covering fund administration, the daily rebalancing operations, and profit. This is steep compared to a broad market index ETF (which might charge 0.05 percent) but standard for leveraged products. There are also implicit costs from the [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) and any financing charges if the fund uses derivatives or short positions.

MELI shares trade with tight spreads on NASDAQ because the stock is popular and liquid. KMLI inherits most of that liquidity, so a trader can typically enter and exit without significant slippage.

## Who trades KMLI and why

KMLI attracts experienced traders and [hedge funds](/hedge-fund/) betting on Mercado Libre in the near term. The fund is useful for hedging a short position in MELI (by owning KMLI, a trader can offset losses if MELI rises unexpectedly), for leveraging a conviction about an upcoming earnings beat or regulatory approval, or for playing macro moves in emerging markets or Brazilian currency swings that affect MELI's profitability.

Retail investors occasionally find KMLI through their brokerage's scan-and-sort tools and hold it thinking it is a way to "amplify my upside" on a stock they believe in. This misunderstanding often proves expensive. KMLI is not an amplified buy-and-hold play. It is a tactical, time-bound instrument whose expected value over weeks and months is negative relative to holding MELI stock outright, due to decay and costs.

## How to evaluate KMLI and do the research

Anyone considering KMLI should first confirm they understand daily-reset mechanics and read KraneShares' prospectus for the fund. The prospectus will detail holdings, the swap counterparty (if applicable), and explicit warnings that the fund is not suitable for buy-and-hold investors.

Beyond that, research KMLI through the lens of MELI fundamentals and near-term catalysts. Review Mercado Libre's quarterly earnings reports and guidance, track regulatory developments in Brazil and Argentina (which are material to the company), monitor currency movements between the peso and dollar (which affect pricing power), and watch for announcements on major product launches or [acquisitions](/acquisition/). A trader entering KMLI should have a concrete hypothesis about why MELI will move sharply in a specific direction over a short, defined window.

Compare KMLI's daily performance to twice MELI's daily performance to observe volatility decay in action. Over any period longer than a day, KMLI will typically underperform 2x MELI due to this decay, especially in choppy markets. If a trader is willing to hold for weeks, holding MELI stock outright is almost always preferable to holding KMLI.
