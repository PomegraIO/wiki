Kratos Defense & Security Solutions manufactures specialized systems and equipment for the U.S. Department of Defense, intelligence agencies, and space-industry customers. The company operates across three principal business segments: Unmanned Systems, which designs and builds autonomous platforms for the military; Space and Satellite Communications, which supplies equipment and solutions for military space operations; and Kratos Defense & Security Solutions engineered products for various government and commercial applications.

## Unmanned Systems and Autonomous Platforms

Kratos's Unmanned Systems division designs, manufactures, and supports unmanned aerial and ground platforms, primarily for military applications. These systems range from small-scale tactical drones used by forward units to larger, longer-endurance surveillance and strike platforms. The division serves both U.S. armed services and select allied militaries. Revenue in this segment is earned through initial platform sales, integration and customization work, maintenance contracts, and spare-parts revenue that continues across the operational lifetime of systems already deployed. Unmanned systems have become central to modern military operations, and Kratos positions itself as a supplier of specialized platforms and the engineering expertise required to integrate them into existing military architectures.

The economics of this segment are mixed. Initial system sales require engineering effort and manufacturing capacity but yield lump-sum revenue upon delivery. Aftermarket support — spares, repairs, software updates, field service — generates recurring revenue but at a lower absolute dollar value. Kratos's strategy in this segment is to expand the installed base of platforms and win long-term support contracts that tie the company to customers over many years. As with any defense contractor, sales are subject to government budget cycles and procurement timelines that can compress or extend across quarters.

## Space and Satellite Communications

The Space and Satellite Communications segment supplies equipment, systems integration, and support for military space operations and satellite communications. This includes ground stations, signal processing equipment, and specialized systems that support the military's satellite infrastructure. As governments worldwide have come to depend on satellite communications for intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance — and as that infrastructure has become a contested domain in military thinking — demand for advanced space-based systems and ground equipment has grown. Kratos supplies both commercial-style equipment and heavily customized systems built to military specifications.

Revenue in this segment comes from systems sales, engineering integration work, and long-term support contracts. Because space systems are expensive to develop and deploy, customers tend to develop lasting relationships with suppliers once a system is in operation. Kratos's role is typically to supply equipment or act as a systems integrator, bringing together components and software into a functional system that meets military or intelligence requirements. The profit margins and stability of long-term support contracts make this segment strategically valuable, even if individual system sales are lumpy and unpredictable.

## Business Model and Customer Concentration

Kratos depends almost entirely on U.S. government customers, with the vast majority of revenue coming from the Department of Defense, intelligence agencies, and related federal entities. This concentration creates both opportunity and risk. On the upside, government budgets for defense and national security are large and largely decoupled from private-sector [business cycles](/business-cycle/). On the downside, losing a single large customer or failing to win a major procurement can materially affect the company's growth trajectory. Government contracts are typically won through a formal bidding process and are often fixed-price, meaning Kratos must estimate costs accurately or see margins compress if development or manufacturing runs over.

The company's backlog — the dollar value of orders received but not yet delivered — is a key metric to watch. A healthy, growing backlog indicates future revenue visibility and customer demand. Government programs, once started, tend to continue, so a large backlog of recognized orders provides revenue certainty for months or quarters ahead.

## Technology, Competition, and Strategic Position

Kratos competes against established defense contractors and specialist unmanned-systems companies. Larger competitors like [Lockheed Martin](/lmt-stock/), General Atomics, and [Boeing](/ba-stock/) have deeper resources and broader portfolios but tend to focus on bigger programs and higher-cost systems. Kratos positions itself as a more nimble supplier of specialized platforms and space technologies, able to move faster than larger incumbents on smaller programs or new domains.

The key to Kratos's competitive position is specialized engineering talent and proprietary designs that customers value. The company invests heavily in research and development, which is the source of new products and the technical edge that wins contracts. Government contracting increasingly favors companies that can demonstrate innovation in areas like autonomous operations, AI integration, and advanced materials — domains where Kratos has invested.

## Risks and Pressures

The company faces multiple risks. Government spending is politically volatile; a shift in defense priorities, a budget sequester, or a change in administration's strategic focus can redirect money away from Kratos's core markets. Competition is intense, and winning a major contract requires both technical excellence and favorable economics. Supply-chain disruptions can delay deliveries and compress margins. Because Kratos relies on fixed-price contracts, cost overruns flow directly to the bottom line.

A regulatory risk worth noting is export control. Kratos manufactures technology that the U.S. government designates as sensitive to national security, so exports of hardware and technical data are restricted. This limits the company's addressable market to U.S. customers and approved allies but is a characteristic constraint on the entire defense-industrial base rather than a threat unique to Kratos.

## How to Research Kratos as an Investment

The company's annual 10-K filing (SEC CIK 0001069258) breaks revenue by segment and customer and outlines risk factors. Quarterly earnings calls provide color on backlog, new program wins, and the competitive landscape. Key metrics include backlog growth, gross-margin trends by segment, research-and-development spending, and the company's win rate on competitive bids. Government contract wins and cancellations — often announced separately from earnings — signal whether Kratos is gaining or losing competitive position. The defense industry is inherently tied to government budgets, so awareness of Congressional spending bills and Department of Defense budget highlights is necessary context for understanding Kratos's opportunity.
