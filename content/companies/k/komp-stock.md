*The **[State Street](/stt-stock/) SPDR S&P Kensho New Economies Composite ETF**, ticker KOMP, is a thematic equity fund designed to hold companies at the forefront of economic disruption and emerging technologies—fewer than two hundred holdings selected from sectors ranging from artificial intelligence and cloud infrastructure to genomics and clean energy.*

The fund is neither a simple index tracker nor a stock picker's showcase. Rather, it is a systematically managed portfolio of companies that fit predefined categories: technology paradigm shifts that investors believe will reshape economic activity over the next decade and beyond. It sits at the boundary between passive index investing (systematic, rule-based) and active stock-picking (discretionary, conviction-driven).

## Artificial Intelligence and Automation

The largest thematic strand in KOMP is companies positioned to benefit from artificial intelligence and related automation. This includes semiconductor manufacturers that design chips for AI ([Nvidia](/nvda-stock/), [Intel](/intc-stock/), AMD, [Broadcom](/avgo-stock/)), cloud infrastructure providers that host large language models (Amazon Web Services, [Microsoft](/msft-stock/) Azure, Google Cloud), software companies building AI tools and APIs, and businesses embedding AI into robotics, manufacturing, or autonomous systems. The premise is that AI is a general-purpose technology that will touch nearly every industry, and winners are companies providing the enabling infrastructure and software.

The risk is obvious: the AI boom may be hype-driven and narrower in real economic impact than near-term share prices reflect. Semiconductor cycles also turn, and expensive AI chips can become commodity products. [Concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) is real—the top five or six AI-infrastructure companies have outsized weight in this thematic strand, so KOMP is heavily exposed to a handful of mega-cap tech stocks.

## Cloud Computing and Digital Infrastructure

Cloud infrastructure, including data centers, edge computing, and the underlying software platforms, form another major thread. Companies like AWS, Microsoft, and Google dominate cloud computing, but KOMP also holds smaller picks that service cloud infrastructure—companies making microchips, cooling systems, networking equipment, and software tools that run inside cloud environments. The thesis is straightforward: the shift from on-premises computing to cloud and AI-powered data processing is still in its early innings, and the companies providing infrastructure will capture value for decades.

This thematic area has less binary risk than AI—cloud adoption is happening, not hypothetical—but it is also more mature. The larger players (Amazon, Microsoft) are already massive, which limits upside, though they continue to grow cloud revenues at double-digit rates.

## Cybersecurity and Digital Privacy

As economic activity moves online, the surface area for cyber attacks expands. KOMP includes cybersecurity software companies, identity-and-access-management vendors, and encryption specialists. The investment case is steady and defensive: these are critical tools that enterprises and governments will always need. The risk is equally steady: cybersecurity is a feature many companies are bundling in-house or buying from larger vendors, which can shrink the addressable market for specialist players.

## Genomics, Biotech, and Healthcare Innovation

A smaller thematic wing focuses on companies using advanced genetic sequencing, gene therapy, and computational biology to develop new drugs and diagnostics. This includes larger biotech firms, as well as smaller companies focused on specific therapies or targets. The upside is transformative—curing genetic diseases or cancer would be extraordinary—but biotech is high-risk, long-cycle, and deeply uncertain. Clinical trials fail, patent cliffs materialize, and regulatory approval is never assured.

## Clean Energy and Environmental Technologies

KOMP holds companies making solar panels, wind turbines, battery systems for electric vehicles and grid storage, and materials for energy-efficient buildings and vehicles. The investment case combines environmental necessity (climate transition is underway) with financial opportunity (clean energy is becoming cost-competitive with fossil fuels). The risk is policy dependence—clean-energy subsidies and mandates can be rolled back or changed, and commodity price cycles (for metals like lithium and cobalt) can squeeze margins. Overcapacity in solar and wind equipment has also repeatedly pressed margins in this sector.

## Blockchain and Digital Assets

A minimal thematic slice reflects holdings in companies related to blockchain technology and digital assets—both protocol-layer companies (crypto infrastructure) and application-layer companies building services on blockchain. This is the most contentious and volatile thematic area in KOMP. The opportunity is real ([distributed ledgers](/distributed-ledger/) may reshape financial infrastructure) but speculative. The regulation is still forming. Fraud and collapse (FTX, others) have highlighted the risks. Most traditional institutions remain deeply skeptical, which means the thematic bet is contrarian and unproven.

## The composite and rebalancing

KOMP bundles these themes into a single portfolio, which means it rides all the waves at once. A quarter where cloud stocks boom and biotech stumbles will deliver different results than one where AI falters but clean energy surges. The fund's managers rebalance periodically to maintain exposure across themes, which means selling winners and buying losers—a discipline that can pay off when themes cycle but can also lock in losses if a theme simply underperforms for years.

## Costs and the thematic premium

KOMP's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is typically 0.40–0.50 per cent annually, which reflects the active-management element (selecting companies within each theme) and the ongoing research required to identify theme beneficiaries. This is substantially higher than a plain [index fund](/index-fund/) but in line with other [thematic ETFs](/thematic-etf/). The higher cost implicitly assumes that the thematic screening adds value; if the themes simply replicate the mega-cap tech stocks that dominate the market already, the fund underperforms cheaper alternatives.

## Concentration and volatility

KOMP is concentrated: the top 10–15 holdings typically account for 30–40 per cent of the fund, with mega-cap tech (particularly AI and cloud leaders) dominating. This concentration is a double-edged risk. In periods when these names perform, KOMP outperforms. When they stumble, KOMP can decline sharply. The fund's volatility is typically higher than a broad-market index, which makes it a satellite holding rather than a core position for most investors.

## How to research and use KOMP

KOMP suits investors with a multi-year horizon, high risk tolerance, and conviction that the identified economic themes will reshape the economy and generate outsized returns. It is not appropriate for conservative portfolios, short time horizons, or those uncomfortable with the concentration in mega-cap tech and early-stage biotech inherent in a thematic fund.

To research, review KOMP's quarterly fact sheet, which discloses the current thematic allocation and top holdings. Understand that the fund is not a pure AI bet or a pure clean-energy bet—it is a diversified portfolio across multiple themes, which can make it harder to analyse but also hedges against a single narrative failing. Compare KOMP's trailing one-year, three-year, and five-year performance to broad-market indexes and to other thematic ETFs to assess whether the thematic approach is adding value or simply concentrating risk. Monitor the fund's turnover—high turnover (above 25 per cent annually) suggests the fund is actively trading to update theme membership, which increases costs and potential tax drag.

KOMP is best used as a satellite position in an growth-oriented portfolio, not as a core holding, and only for investors who understand and accept the risks of thematic concentration and can stay committed through periods of underperformance relative to the broader market.
