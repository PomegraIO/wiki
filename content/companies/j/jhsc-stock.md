*The **John Hancock Multifactor Small Cap ETF** (JHSC) is a rules-based equity fund that selects roughly 250 small-cap US stocks using a quantitative framework combining value metrics, profitability indicators, and price momentum, seeking to exploit [factor premiums](/factor-premium/) that academic research has documented in the small-cap universe.*

## The small-cap value proposition

Small-cap stocks — publicly traded companies with [market capitalizations](/market-capitalization/) in the low billions — occupy a distinctive niche in equity markets. They trade less frequently than large-cap names, so [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) are wider and ownership is more concentrated, creating friction for buyers and sellers but also potential informational inefficiency. Historically, small-cap stocks have delivered higher long-term returns than large-cap stocks, a pattern attributed partly to illiquidity compensation and partly to their concentration in cyclical, early-growth businesses that thrive during economic expansions. JHSC targets this segment, but rather than hold small-cap stocks indiscriminately, it applies a multifactor screening process to identify companies most likely to outperform on a risk-adjusted basis.

The fund is not a pure small-cap index tracker; it is a rules-based actively managed portfolio that selects from the investable small-cap universe according to quantitative signals. This approach sits between passive indexing and discretionary active management — cheaper and more transparent than stock-picking but more selective than simply holding every small-cap in proportion to market value.

## The three pillars: value, quality, and momentum

JHSC's screening framework evaluates small-cap candidates across three broad factor dimensions that academic research has associated with outperformance over long periods.

**Value factors** identify companies trading at low prices relative to accounting measures like earnings, book value, or sales. The logic is that cheap stocks as a group tend to outperform expensive ones, partly because investors overweight pessimism and partly because low valuations leave more room for positive surprises. For small caps, this effect is pronounced because small-cap stocks attract less research coverage, creating a greater chance that bargains remain overlooked.

**Quality factors** screen for profitability, financial stability, and operational efficiency — metrics such as [return on equity](/return-on-equity/), earnings stability, and debt ratios. High-quality small caps are firms that have proven they can generate consistent profits without relying on leverage or accounting gimmicks. This anchor balances the pure-value tilt; a company may be cheap because it is genuinely distressed, so quality filters avoid the worst value traps.

**Momentum factors** measure the direction and magnitude of recent price trends. Stocks with positive momentum — those that have outperformed over the past three to twelve months — continue to outperform in the near term at a measurable rate, a pattern observed across markets and time periods. Small-cap momentum has been especially strong, so this factor receives meaningful weight in the fund's stock-selection algorithm.

These three factors are combined using quantitative optimization to construct a portfolio that balances their contributions. A stock might be cheap but lack quality and momentum; the algorithm would assign it a modest position. Conversely, a high-quality company with strong recent performance and a low valuation receives a larger allocation.

## Cyclicality and the small-cap beat

The fund's performance is intimately tied to small-cap equity cycles. In late-stage expansions, when investors rotate from large-cap defensive stocks into higher-beta small caps, JHSC's concentrated exposure to the small-cap premium often outpaces broad market returns. Conversely, early [recessions](/recession/) trigger flight-to-quality, and small caps sell off faster than large caps, amplifying losses for the fund. The multifactor lens does not eliminate this cyclical sensitivity but can modulate it: during downturns, quality screens may overweight more stable small-cap names that hold up better, and value screens may raise positioning in names that have already fallen hard and trade at depressed multiples. A momentum screen might tell against the idea at that moment, flagging negative recent trends, so the three factors create tension and a balanaced portfolio.

In periods of technological disruption or sectoral rotation, the fund's factor tilt can significantly diverge from broader small-cap performance. The 2020–2022 period illustrated this: as investors rotated out of unprofitable, high-momentum growth stocks into cheaper, more profitable firms, the multifactor approach captured alpha relative to traditional market-cap-weighted indexes that were overweighted toward the momentum mega-caps.

## Portfolio composition and style drift

JHSC's roughly 250 holdings span all sectors of the small-cap market: industrials, financials, healthcare, energy, consumer goods, technology, materials, and others. The sector weights emerge endogenously from the factor screens rather than from a target allocation, so they shift as [relative valuations](/relative-valuation/) and profitability change. During an energy boom, if small-cap energy stocks offer compelling value and momentum metrics, the fund may tilt overweight there. During a tech rally, if small-cap tech stocks trade at premium multiples, the factor screens may underweight the sector, creating a deliberate underalignment relative to the benchmark small-cap index.

The portfolio rebalances on a fixed schedule, typically quarterly. Each rebalancing captures gains from outperformers and reallocates toward the screened signals of the moment. This rebalancing introduces turnover and transaction costs; small-cap stocks are less liquid than large caps, so trading costs are higher. JHSC's effective [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) includes trading costs implicitly, and high turnover periods (after steep market declines, for instance, when the factor signals shift abruptly) can create performance drag relative to less active approaches.

## Research and factor stability risks

Multifactor strategies depend on the persistence of the factors themselves. [Value investing](/value-investing/) has worked over long periods, but there have been stretches — the 2010s growth-stock surge, for example — where cheap stocks meaningfully underperformed. Momentum can reverse swiftly. Quality, while more stable, has suffered when leverage-heavy, stable-earnings firms fall out of favor. JHSC's three-factor approach diversifies across these risks, but it does not eliminate them.

The fund's methodology is published by John Hancock and the [index provider](/index-provider/), so investors can see exactly how stocks are scored and selected. This transparency aids accountability but also creates the risk of overcrowding: if large sums pursue the same multifactor signals across small caps, the premiums may compress. Small-cap liquidity is limited; large inflows can move prices faster than in large-cap markets.

## Fitting small-cap multifactor into a portfolio

JHSC is most appropriate for investors who believe small-cap equities will outperform large caps and who have conviction that factor-based selection adds value beyond market-cap weighting. It suits portfolios where large-cap index exposure already exists and small-cap allocation is sought as a diversifier. Because the fund concentrates on the small end of the equity market, it carries higher volatility and higher tax inefficiency (from turnover) than broad market indexes. As an individual security, JHSC trades on a [stock exchange](/stock-exchange/) at prices set by supply and demand, often closely tracking its underlying [net asset value](/net-asset-value/).

For [due diligence](/due-diligence/), investors should review the fund's factsheet to understand the current factor exposures, sector tilts, and the recent performance relative to the [Russell 2000 Index](/russell-2000/) and similar peers. The fund's [annual report](/10-k/) details the stock-selection methodology and highlights any changes to the screening algorithm or weighting scheme.
