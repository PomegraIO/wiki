*The **[Janus Henderson](/jhg-stock/) Small Cap Growth Alpha ETF** (JSML) is an exchange-traded fund holding a focused portfolio of small-cap US equities, with Janus Henderson's research team selecting companies that score highly on growth potential, profitability, and capital discipline. The fund operates as pure small-cap play — narrower and more concentrated than its larger sibling JSMD.*

**The ticket and pivot point.** JSML trades on [Nasdaq](/nasdaq/) under ticker JSML. Actively managed since May 2025, when Janus Henderson shifted from mechanical index-tracking to proprietary factor-based stock selection. That transition matters: the fund went from passive to discretionary, from tracking an index to beating it.

**Universe and scope.** JSML concentrates strictly on true small-caps — companies typically under 10 billion dollars in market capitalisation. That boundary separates it from JSMD, which bleeds into mid-cap territory. Smaller [market cap](/market-capitalization/) means larger opportunity set but also higher density of company-specific risk. Each position matters more.

**How it picks.** Three metrics, systematically scored across candidates. Growth — is the company expanding earnings faster than peers? Profitability — is it generating meaningful returns on capital, or burning cash? Capital efficiency — is it allocating capital wisely through reinvestment, [dividends](/dividend/), or buybacks? Companies scoring highly on all three make the portfolio. The system runs continuously; positions adjust as underlying metrics change. It is disciplined growth, not growth-at-any-cost.

**Portfolio construction.** Roughly 75 to 100 holdings. Industrials, technology, healthcare cluster near the top of sector weightings. A smattering of smaller financial and consumer names round the portfolio. Sector weights are fluid, following the factors rather than arbitrary mandates. Concentration is modest compared to true micro-cap funds but tighter than broad small-cap indices.

**Assets and liquidity.** Currently around 300 million dollars under management. Small by ETF standards but large enough to trade with reasonable [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/). Not so large that Janus Henderson is forced into increasingly mediocre names just to deploy capital — a constraint that affects larger funds.

**Costs.** [Expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) reflects research, trading, and systematic model maintenance. Higher than a passive small-cap index ETF but competitive among actively managed small-cap vehicles. The question is whether the fee is earned back through outperformance.

**The edge thesis.** Sell-side analyst coverage drops sharply below large-cap. Institutional money often ignores small-cap. That gap creates room for a skilled manager with proprietary models to find mispricings. Janus Henderson's systematic approach scales the process: the models filter thousands of candidates down to the best 75 to 100. Process over intuition.

**The risks.** Small-cap volatility is real. A poorly selected position can crumble fast. If the proprietary factors that predicted growth lose predictive power — if growth stops, profitability deteriorates, or management stumbles — the fund underperforms. Small-cap growth is also crowded at times; style headwinds can persist for years.

**Tracking error and performance.** Active management means divergence from the Russell 2000 Growth Index. Sometimes positive, sometimes negative. The fund's outperformance versus that benchmark over rolling periods is the true test of management skill.

**Tax and currency.** US-traded; no [currency hedging](/currency-hedging/). For taxable investors, active trading can spin off short-term capital gains, though the ETF structure typically minimizes distributions compared to [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/). Still, examine the prospectus for turnover and after-tax return history.

**Research checklist.** Study the holdings and sector positioning to gauge conviction. Compare performance to the Russell 2000 Growth Index over three, five, and ten years. Look at turnover — high turnover suggests trading costs and tax drag. Read the prospectus to confirm the factor definitions match your expectations. Check whether the expense ratio is stable or creeping higher. And be clear-eyed about drawdown history; small-cap [growth funds](/growth-fund/) experience sharp selloffs in risk-off markets, and that pain is structural, not a bug.
