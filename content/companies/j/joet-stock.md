*The Virtus Terranova U.S. Quality Momentum ETF (JOET) selects large-cap U.S. companies that exhibit both strong underlying business fundamentals—quality—and positive price momentum, combining two of the most persistent factors in [quantitative investing](/quantitative-investing/) into a single portfolio.*

## The technology and finance sector concentration

JOET allocates roughly 26% of assets to technology companies and 14% to financial services, making it a growth-leaning portfolio despite its large-cap structure. Within technology, the fund holds [Datadog](/ddog-stock/) (a cloud-monitoring platform), [Micron Technology](/mu-stock/) (semiconductor memory), and other mid-tier tech names that have demonstrated both operational strength and rising share prices. The financial services allocation includes regional banks and financial technology companies rather than the mega-cap money-center banks, a positioning that reflects the fund's emphasis on momentum and business quality rather than defensive value plays.

This composition matters because it makes JOET more cyclical and more exposed to economic growth than a simple cap-weighted index. In years when technology and financials thrive, JOET will outperform. In downturns when those sectors suffer, the concentrated factor exposure can work against you.

## The industrial and consumer cyclicals

Industrials represent roughly 22% of the fund, and JOET holds a mixture of companies in capital equipment, aerospace and defense, transportation, and specialized manufacturing. These are not utility-like defensive industrials but rather businesses with high operational leverage to economic cycles. The momentum factor screens for those whose business is accelerating and whose shares are moving higher.

Consumer cyclicals make up another 10%, including discretionary retailers and automotive-adjacent businesses. These companies benefit from economic expansion and tend to be hurt in [recessions](/recession/), but JOET's quality filter should exclude the weakest operators, leaving a portfolio of franchises that can survive downturns with less damage.

## The healthcare and utilities ballast

Healthcare accounts for 11% and provides some stability relative to the higher-beta sectors. Utilities and energy, though underweighted, round out the portfolio. The fund is not a defensive fortress; it is tilted toward economic sensitivity and growth. But the healthcare and staples allocation prevents the portfolio from being a pure-play equity risk bet.

## How the quality and momentum factors work together

JOET does not simply hold the companies with the biggest [earnings per share](/earnings-per-share/) or the most consistent returns. Instead, it uses a quantitative process developed by Terranova that looks for companies exhibiting two things simultaneously: first, quality—measured by metrics like [return on equity](/return-on-equity/), earnings stability, and [balance sheet](/balance-sheet/) strength—and second, momentum—rising price trends and favorable analyst revisions, suggesting the market has recently recognized the quality.

The combination is elegant in theory: you avoid value traps (cheap stocks that are cheap for a reason), and you avoid momentum traps (stocks rising on pure sentiment disconnected from fundamentals). A company that shows both high quality and positive momentum has passed a two-stage filter. In [bull markets](/bull-market/), factor combinations like this can outrun plain indices. In [bear markets](/bear-market/) or when the market reprices growth, they can underperform.

## The performance and volatility profile

JOET has delivered solid returns in recent years, with five-year annualized returns around 12% before fees and a beta near 1.04 relative to the S&P 500, meaning it moves slightly more than the broad market. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) of 0.29% is reasonable for a factor-based strategy, lower than [actively managed funds](/actively-managed-fund/) but higher than passive broad-market index trackers. The fund's top 10 holdings represent about 12% of total assets, which is reasonable [diversification](/diversification/) but signals that the quality-momentum filter does result in some concentration around the best-scoring names.

Holdings rotate based on the quantitative scoring; a company that exhibits strong quality and momentum this quarter may lag next quarter as its valuation rises and momentum wanes. This active rebalancing incurs trading costs and can generate tax inefficiency for taxable investors, though Virtus works to minimize unnecessary turnover.

## Factor tilts in different market regimes

The quality-momentum combination has advantages and disadvantages depending on the market backdrop. In an environment where rates are stable and economic growth is steady, quality and momentum tend to work together: strong companies grow their earnings, and the market bids them higher. In an inflationary environment where the [central bank](/central-bank/) is tightening, growth companies (which are over-represented in the quality-momentum space) can struggle, and value stocks and defensive sectors can outperform.

Similarly, in a market panic, even high-quality companies can get sold indiscriminately, and the momentum filter will have been wrong—it identified rising prices, not a floor. The factor provided no protection.

The fund works best for investors who believe the economy will continue to expand at a moderate pace, that profit growth will be steady, and that the market will reward quality and momentum at least in line with the long-term average. It is less suitable for defensive investors preparing for a recession or for true value believers convinced that growth stocks are overvalued.

## The underlying holdings and their character

The fund's composition shifts, but the bias is consistently toward companies with strong return on equity (meaning they generate profit efficiently relative to shareholder capital), low debt, and rising earnings and share prices. In the technology space, this means cloud infrastructure, fintech, and semiconductor companies with genuine competitive moats. In industrials, it means capital equipment makers and aerospace suppliers that are expanding. In consumer, it means selective retailers with strong brands and rising same-store sales, not discount players.

This character makes JOET suitable for growth-oriented investors who still want some assurance of business quality and not just momentum chasing. It is not suitable for conservative income investors seeking stable [dividends](/dividend/) or for investors convinced that momentum is overvalued and mean-reversion is imminent.

## How to research JOET

Start by reviewing the fund's prospectus and fact sheet from Virtus, which will detail the Terranova quality-momentum scoring methodology. Examine the current top holdings and their characteristics: are they companies with genuinely strong fundamentals, or are they growth stocks trading at elevated multiples? Compare JOET's returns to simpler benchmarks like the S&P 500 or a large-cap growth index over multiple [market cycles](/market-cycle/)—bull, bear, and sideways markets—to understand when the factor combination helps and when it hurts.

Track the fund's turnover rate and realized [capital gains distributions](/capital-gains-distribution/), which can signal tax efficiency for taxable investors. Finally, assess whether you believe the market currently overvalues or undervalues quality and momentum; if you think growth stocks are priced to perfection and risk/reward is poor, JOET is not the right tool, no matter how elegant the methodology. If you think quality companies with positive business momentum will continue to be rewarded, JOET is a straightforward way to gain that exposure.
