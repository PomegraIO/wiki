*The **Janus Henderson Small/Mid Cap Growth Alpha ETF** (JSMD) is an exchange-traded fund holding a concentrated portfolio of small- and mid-cap US equities selected by Janus Henderson's research team. The fund aims to identify growth-oriented companies trading at reasonable valuations and outperform a broad small-cap benchmark through disciplined stock selection and conviction-based holding decisions.*

## What does the fund actually invest in?

JSMD holds approximately 80 to 100 small- and mid-sized American companies, not the full universe of thousands that comprise the Russell 2500 index. The fund targets businesses in their growth phase — firms whose earnings are expanding at above-average rates — with profitable operations and disciplined management. The portfolio concentrates holdings in industrials, information technology, and healthcare, reflecting both the reality of where growth narratives tend to exist at smaller company scales and Janus Henderson's current conviction about those sectors.

The manager selects stocks using a systematic process that evaluates each candidate across three dimensions: the company's growth trajectory relative to peers and the broader market, its ability to generate profitable returns on invested capital, and the quality of its capital allocation to shareholders and reinvestment. These factors are not vague judgments but quantifiable metrics that feed into a proprietary scoring system. The system aims to identify small- and mid-cap names that excel across all three dimensions at once — a rare combination that avoids both expensive unprofitable growth stories and cheap value traps.

## Why is small-cap and mid-cap a sensible place for an active manager to find an edge?

Small- and mid-cap stocks occupy what many investors call an "inefficiency sweet spot." They are large enough to be liquid and followed by thousands of traders, but small enough that sell-side analyst coverage is far lighter than it is for mega-cap names. That gap between market liquidity and analyst attention creates room for skilled stock-pickers to find overlooked opportunities. A manager who correctly identifies a small-cap company poised for faster growth than consensus expects can position in a name before the market recognizes it, and that edge can compound across dozens of positions. The downside is that small- and mid-cap stocks are inherently more volatile and risky than large caps; a good idea selected poorly becomes a large loss.

## What are the main risks of owning JSMD?

The largest is that active management skill is not guaranteed and may not persist. Janus Henderson's stock-picking team must outperform not just the broader small-cap index but also the many other active managers competing for the same edge. When market conditions favour large-cap mega-tech stocks over small-cap growth — as has been true in certain periods — even excellent stock selection cannot overcome the sector headwind.

Concentration is a second risk. The fund holds fewer than 100 stocks, so a few poor stock-picking calls can meaningfully hurt performance. If the manager misjudges a company's earnings durability or the profitability trajectory of a large position, that mistake compounds across a small portfolio. Single-stock risk is real.

Tax drag is a third. Active trading can spin off short-term capital gains, which are taxed at ordinary income rates in taxable accounts. Over time, that tax leakage can erode the fund's net-of-tax performance significantly. An investor should examine the prospectus to understand the fund's historical turnover and the tax impact of active management.

## How should an investor evaluate whether JSMD is worth owning?

Start by comparing the fund's returns to the Russell 2500 Growth Index over rolling periods of three, five, and ten years. The central question is whether active management has added value after expenses and taxes. If the fund has materially outperformed over multiple full cycles, the edge may be real; if it has merely matched or trailed the index, active management is not earning its keep.

Review the fund's holdings and sector positioning to understand what Janus Henderson's team currently believes will drive returns. Check the prospectus for the portfolio's average market cap, price-to-earnings multiple, and earnings growth rate relative to the Russell 2500; these metrics reveal whether the team is tilting toward cheap or expensive growth, smaller or larger small-caps.

Assess your own conviction in Janus Henderson's small-cap growth research. Do you believe the team has identified a durable edge in finding growth companies before the market does? Can you tolerate the higher volatility that comes with small-cap ownership and concentrated stock selection? If the answers are yes, JSMD may merit a satellite position. If you doubt the skill or cannot stomach the drawdowns, a low-cost broad small-cap index fund is the safer choice.
