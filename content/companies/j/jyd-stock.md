Jayud Global Logistics Ltd. ([NASDAQ](/nasdaq/): JYD) is a provider of integrated cross-border logistics and supply-chain services headquartered in China. The company operates as an end-to-end facilitator of international goods movement, handling everything from warehousing and customs clearance to final-mile delivery, primarily serving e-commerce merchants and businesses that trade across borders.

## The company and its market

Jayud operates at a critical junction in global trade: the movement of goods from Chinese manufacturers and suppliers to buyers worldwide. The company bridges a fragmented logistics ecosystem by bundling warehousing, packaging, customs handling, shipping line coordination, and tracking into integrated offerings. Most customers are small to mid-sized e-commerce sellers and exporters who lack the scale or expertise to manage complex international shipments on their own.

The core service is a supply-chain platform that connects suppliers with international logistics infrastructure. Jayud maintains or partners with warehousing facilities, manages inventory, coordinates with freight forwarders and shipping lines, and handles the documentation and clearances required to move goods across borders. This aggregation model allows the company to offer competitive pricing by consolidating shipments and optimizing routes and vessel space.

Revenue comes from service fees charged to merchants and shippers — each transaction or shipment generates a commission or fixed fee depending on the service package. The business also earns margin on logistics operations themselves, such as warehousing fees and handling charges.

## The competitive landscape and positioning

China's logistics market is highly fragmented, with thousands of small operators competing on price, service quality, and reliability. Jayud's advantage lies in its integrated platform, which reduces the friction and risk involved in cross-border shipping. By centralizing multiple logistics functions, the company can offer faster turnaround times, more transparent pricing, and better visibility than a customer trying to coordinate a dozen separate providers.

The core tension in this business is straightforward: margins are thin in logistics, particularly in the price-sensitive e-commerce segment. Competitors can range from global carriers (FedEx, DHL, UPS) to smaller, localized freight forwarders. Global players bring scale and infrastructure but are less specialized for the e-commerce and SME market segment Jayud targets. Smaller, local competitors can undercut on price but lack the breadth of service or geographic reach. Jayud's opportunity is in the middle — offering better service and reliability than local operators, at better pricing than multinational carriers.

## Key business challenges

Jayud faces several structural pressures inherent to logistics. Shipping costs and capacity fluctuate with global shipping rates, fuel prices, and port congestion, all of which compress margins when unfavorable and require rapid repricing when favorable. Customers are typically highly price-sensitive, so winning volume often means accepting thin margins per shipment. The company also depends on the health of cross-border e-commerce trade, which can slow during economic downturns or when customer demand for imported goods weakens.

Regulatory risk is another ongoing concern. Customs requirements, tariffs, and trade policies differ by country and change frequently. Companies serving international trade must stay current with these rules to avoid delays, fines, or shipment seizures. Any major shift in trade policy — such as tariff increases or new customs procedures — can disrupt operations and customer costs.

The company also operates in a market where scale and technology investment matter increasingly. As logistics automation advances, larger players investing in warehousing technology, route optimization software, and real-time tracking have advantages smaller competitors cannot easily match.
