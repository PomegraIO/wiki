JGRW is an exchange-traded fund that tracks quality-focused growth companies — firms with strong [balance sheets](/balance-sheet/), durable competitive advantages, and a history of growing earnings without excessive debt. The fund targets investors who want exposure to growth stocks but prefer to own businesses with financial discipline and resilience, rather than trading on momentum or [multiple expansion](/multiple-expansion/) alone.

## What JGRW tracks and holds

The fund follows the Jensen Quality Growth Index, which screens for three characteristics: quality (strong balance sheets, low leverage, steady cash generation), growth (rising earnings and sales), and valuation (reasonable price relative to earnings). The index starts with large U.S. equities and applies these screens to narrow down to a subset that meets the quality bar. The result is a diversified portfolio of typically 100–150 holdings across sectors — technology, industrials, healthcare, consumer staples, financials — weighted by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) within the quality-screened universe.

Holdings typically include companies like [Microsoft](/msft-stock/), [Procter & Gamble](/pg-stock/), Visa, and similar franchises: businesses with pricing power, strong returns on capital, manageable debt loads, and a demonstrated ability to raise earnings year after year without depending on financial leverage. Because the index methodology prefers financial health, many cyclical or leveraged businesses are screened out.

## How it differs from the broad market

JGRW is more concentrated than a total-market ETF but far broader than a pure-growth or sector play. A total-market index holds every publicly traded U.S. company, including highly levered firms, unprofitable growth-stage companies, and businesses with weak competitive positions. The Jensen Quality Growth Index excludes companies that fail its quality bar, tilting the portfolio toward firms with fortress-like characteristics.

In practice, this means JGRW typically carries a slight growth tilt and a premium valuation compared to the broad market — investors pay for quality and stability. During periods when the market rewards momentum and cheap, cyclical stocks, JGRW may lag broad indices. During downturns or periods of risk-off sentiment, the fund's holdings tend to hold up better because their balance sheets and cash flows are stronger.

## Expenses and trading

JGRW trades as a standard ETF on a U.S. [stock exchange](/stock-exchange/), so it can be bought and sold intraday at prices set by the market. The fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is competitive for an actively screened equity fund, typically in the 0.4–0.5% range annually. The underlying index is rebalanced periodically, and the fund's turnover is modest relative to [actively managed funds](/actively-managed-fund/), keeping transaction costs and tax drag low for buy-and-hold investors.

## Who it suits and who it doesn't

JGRW appeals to long-term investors who want U.S. equity exposure but believe that focusing on companies with strong fundamentals and clean balance sheets reduces risk and improves odds of steady returns. It works well within a diversified portfolio as a core U.S. equity holding, particularly for those who are skeptical of momentum-driven valuations or who want to tilt away from excessive debt.

It is less suitable for those seeking pure momentum exposure, for value-focused investors who want to own deeply discounted or cyclical stocks, or for investors trying to time the market — the fund's quality tilt means it can underperform during periods when the cheapest and most leveraged firms lead. It is also worth noting that a quality screen tilts the portfolio toward larger, more established firms, so it offers less exposure to smaller or earlier-stage growth companies than a cap-weighted broad index would.

## Risks and considerations

The main risk is concentration within quality stocks. If the market rotates dramatically toward low-quality, leveraged companies — as it has at certain points in [market cycles](/market-cycle/) — JGRW will lag. The fund also carries duration to U.S. equities generally: in a severe market downturn, quality stocks fall too, even if they fall less than the broad market.

Style drift is another consideration. The index methodology is backward-looking — it screens for companies that have exhibited quality and growth to date, but past strength is no guarantee of future performance. A company screened in today may lose its competitive moat or see earnings growth stall.

## How to research JGRW

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, which disclose the index methodology in detail, the current holdings, and the expense ratio. The fund company's website typically provides holdings, sector allocation, and historical performance. The prospectus also lays out the index screen: how "quality" is defined, the specific thresholds for debt ratios and return metrics, and the rebalancing schedule. For context on how quality-focused strategies have performed historically, review academic research on quality factors and value-weighted quality indices — the literature is extensive. Compare JGRW's recent performance, turnover, and holdings to other quality-focused [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/) to assess whether the fund's specific screen and cost structure suit your preferences.
