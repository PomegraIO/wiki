*The **Defiance Drone and Modern Warfare ETF** (JEDI) is a thematic fund that holds companies across defense, aerospace, robotics, and surveillance — firms that make drones, missiles, radar, cybersecurity systems, and other hardware and software tied to modern military conflict. It is one way investors can gain exposure to the weapons and technology sectors without picking individual stocks.*

## What the fund actually holds

JEDI picks companies that make or rely on drone technology, unmanned systems, missile defense, radar, electronic warfare, and other hardware tied to modern warfare. The holdings are a mix. Some are massive defense contractors like [Lockheed Martin](/lmt-stock/) and Northrop Grumman that have enormous drone and autonomous-systems divisions alongside their traditional jet and missile business. Others are smaller specialists — companies that design payloads for drones, make surveillance software, or build components that go into unmanned aircraft.

The fund also includes robotics and autonomous-systems firms that serve military and civilian markets. The line between "drone company" and "defense contractor" blurs — a manufacturer of computer vision systems, for instance, may sell to both the Department of Defense and to commercial autonomous-vehicle makers.

JEDI usually holds between 50 and 100 stocks. The exact holdings change as the underlying index rebalances, typically quarterly or semi-annually. The fund is not a pure play on drones; it is a bet on the cluster of technologies and firms that touch modern military systems and surveillance.

## Why thematic ETFs exist and their appeal

Thematic funds like JEDI emerged in the 2010s as a way for investors to ride long-term trends — renewable energy, artificial intelligence, aging populations, water scarcity — without having to research and pick individual companies. The appeal is obvious: instead of trying to figure out whether one robotics startup will succeed, an investor buys a basket of companies across the entire theme and lets [diversification](/diversification/) do the work.

The risk is equally obvious: a theme can be overdone. Every hot trend attracts new investors, which inflates valuations. Drones and autonomous systems have been a hot theme for years, and that enthusiasm is baked into prices. Additionally, thematic funds often have higher turnover and higher fees than broad market or [sector ETFs](/sector-etf/), reflecting the work required to maintain a index that tracks an evolving theme.

JEDI appeals to investors who believe that spending on defense technology — driven by geopolitical tension, modernization mandates, and the shift toward unmanned systems — will continue to grow. It also appeals to those uncomfortable researching defense stocks individually but wanting exposure to that area of the market.

## The defense and drone market backdrop

Drone spending and unmanned-systems development have accelerated over the past two decades as countries modernize their militaries and the technology matures. The U.S. Department of Defense is a massive buyer, and other NATO countries and regional powers are investing heavily. Commercial drone markets — delivery, surveying, inspection — are also growing, though military and defense applications remain the dominant revenue driver for companies held in JEDI.

This spending is not cyclical in the traditional sense. Defense budgets are set by law and geopolitics, not by [recessions](/recession/) or [bull markets](/bull-market/). A severe recession might trim procurement, but sustained cuts are unusual because defense is politically sensitive. Conversely, escalating international tensions or new military threats can spike demand. The fund is less sensitive to consumer-spending cycles than most [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/) but more sensitive to political decisions and international relations.

## Real costs and real risks

JEDI's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is higher than a basic market index or even a sector ETF, reflecting the cost of curating and rebalancing a thematic index. That ongoing drag matters over decades of holding.

[Concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) is real. The largest holdings are the big defense contractors — firms whose drones and unmanned systems are just one part of a much larger business. If Lockheed Martin struggles for reasons unrelated to drones, the fund suffers. The smaller companies in the fund are far more exposed to defense spending; a cut in procurement can hammer them.

There is also the regulatory and geopolitical risk. Export controls, arms treaties, and political pressure on weapons sales can shift unexpectedly. A new administration or a peace settlement in a key conflict could reduce demand overnight. Investors holding JEDI should be aware that they are betting, in part, on the continuation of certain geopolitical conditions.

Finally, because JEDI is a thematic fund holding some smaller, less-liquid stocks, the [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) can be wider than a mega-cap ETF, and the fund itself has lower assets under management, which means lower trading volume. That translates to wider friction costs when entering or exiting.

## How to monitor and research JEDI

Start with Defiance's website for the fund's prospectus, holdings list, and index methodology. Look at the top 10 holdings to see which companies dominate the fund. Understand the split between the large, diversified defense contractors (which are stable but less explosive in drone growth) and the smaller specialists (which have more drone exposure but more volatility).

Watch major defense budgets and international news. Shifts in U.S. or NATO defense spending, conflicts, or policy toward arms sales directly affect the fund's holdings. Also monitor the valuations of the holdings — if the theme has gotten too popular, stocks may be priced for unrealistic growth.

JEDI is a reasonable vehicle for gaining diversified exposure to defense and drone technology without picking individual stocks, but it is not a buy-and-forget fund. It requires periodic attention to valuations, geopolitics, and the fund's performance relative to its benchmark and to broad defense indices.
