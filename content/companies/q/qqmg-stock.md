### What does QQMG track?

QQMG starts with the Nasdaq-100 universe — the 100 largest non-financial companies listed on the Nasdaq, predominantly technology, software, semiconductors, and digital-commerce firms — then applies ESG (environmental, social, governance) screening. The result is a filtered portfolio holding a subset of the 100, excluding companies that fail Invesco's ESG thresholds. The fund aims to deliver Nasdaq-100-like growth characteristics while tilting toward companies with demonstrably stronger environmental practices, social responsibility, and governance structures.

### How does Invesco's ESG methodology work?

Invesco ranks Nasdaq-100 constituents across a range of ESG metrics: environmental factors (greenhouse gas emissions, resource efficiency, climate risk disclosure), social criteria (labour practices, workplace diversity, community relations), and governance standards (board independence, executive compensation alignment, shareholder protections). The methodology is proprietary to Invesco, involving both quantitative scoring and qualitative judgment; the fund is not mechanical in the way a simple market-cap index is.

The practical consequence is selective inclusion. Large technology firms with mature ESG reporting and demonstrated commitment to renewable energy and diversity tend to remain in the portfolio. Companies with weaker ESG disclosure, contested environmental or labour practices, or business models fundamentally at odds with ESG priorities may be excluded or underweighted. Invesco reviews and rebalances the portfolio quarterly or semi-annually, so holdings shift as ESG scores change.

### How does QQMG perform relative to QQQ?

The performance difference is modest but not trivial. In years when the Nasdaq rallies broadly, QQMG may lag if excluded companies participate in the outperformance. Conversely, during periods when ESG-aligned companies outperform, QQMG may slightly lead. Differences typically range from a handful of basis points to low single-digit percentage points annually — visible to the attentive investor, though not dramatic.

The expense ratio of roughly 0.48% annually is higher than the base QQQ (which costs around 0.20%) but typical for funds requiring active ESG analysis and quarterly compliance reviews. The extra cost reflects Invesco's ongoing evaluation of holdings against ESG criteria.

### Who uses QQMG and why?

QQMG appeals to individual investors with philosophical commitments to ESG-aligned investing who want large-cap growth exposure. It is equally important for institutional portfolios — pension funds, university endowments, foundations — with explicit ESG mandates requiring exclusion of certain industries or companies. Such institutions may be prohibited from holding broad-market Nasdaq funds due to governance restrictions on fossil fuels, arms manufacturing, tobacco, or poor labour practices.

A crucial caveat: ESG standards are not uniform. What Invesco screens out, a competitor may accept. Investors comparing QQMG to other ESG Nasdaq-100 funds often find meaningful differences in holdings and philosophy. Additionally, ESG scoring remains contested; some view it as rigorous and forward-looking, whilst critics argue it relies on self-reported company data, has weak predictive power for financial returns, and does not reliably translate to actual environmental or social benefit.

### How to evaluate QQMG

Read the fund's prospectus to understand Invesco's exact ESG criteria and any hard exclusions (fossil fuels, tobacco, weapons). The quarterly fact sheet and holdings list show current inclusions and exclusions. Invesco's commentary explaining holdings changes reveals the fund's philosophy on ESG trade-offs: does it require divestment from entire sectors, or does it favour improvement and engagement within existing holdings? Understanding that philosophy determines whether the fund aligns with your own ESG priorities.
