Pacer Cash COWZ 100 (ticker QQWZ) is an exchange-traded fund that takes a tactical approach to exposure to the NASDAQ-100 index by rotating between full stock exposure and cash based on perceived [market risk](/market-risk/). Rather than holding the NASDAQ-100 continuously, the fund switches between the underlying index and Treasury-backed cash reserves according to a defined volatility rule. The objective is to participate in gains when conditions favour equities while reducing drawdowns during periods of elevated market stress.

## How the rotation works

The fund's signal comes from the NASDAQ-100 Volatility Index, commonly called the VQ. When volatility rises above a threshold, the QQWZ algorithm reduces stock exposure and moves a portion of assets into cash equivalents ([Treasury bills](/treasury-bill/) or similar instruments). When volatility recedes back below the threshold, the fund rotates back to full NASDAQ-100 exposure.

This approach aims to sidestep a real cost of traditional hedging tools like [put options](/put-option/) or [inverse ETFs](/inverse-etf/): daily decay. An inverse or [leveraged ETF](/leveraged-etf/) loses money every day from the mechanics of its construction, because it resets its exposure each session; a fund that holds nothing but long positions and cash has no such drag. The trade-off is different: the rotation strategy can lag in sideways or [flat markets](/flat-market/), or miss the first days of sharp rallies if the signal is slow to trigger.

## What it tracks and holds

The fund's core holding is the NASDAQ-100 Index itself, which consists of about 100 of the largest non-financial companies listed on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) exchange. The NASDAQ-100 is heavily weighted toward technology, consumer discretionary, and communications stocks; the top 10 positions often represent nearly half the index. When QQWZ is fully invested, it moves in close tandem with the NASDAQ-100. When rotated to cash, that holding is replaced by very short-term Treasury-backed instruments, which offer minimal yield but extreme liquidity and near-zero default risk.

The fund itself trades on the NASDAQ exchange like any other ETF, meaning investors buy and sell shares throughout the day at prices set by market supply and demand. The [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) can vary depending on market conditions and the overall volume in QQWZ shares.

## Costs and who it serves

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is comparable to that of a plain NASDAQ-100 ETF, typically in the range of 0.4–0.6 per cent annually, reflecting the modest cost of the underlying passive index and the tactical decision logic. This is meaningfully higher than a straight low-cost NASDAQ-100 ETF but lower than the costs of active management or regular options-based hedging.

The fund is best suited for investors who believe they can forecast volatility patterns with some reliability, or who simply want the peace of mind that comes from automatic downside protection without having to pay the daily cost of leverage. It serves tactical traders and more defensive long-term holders who find the trade-off between participation and downside mitigation appealing. It is less suitable for passive buy-and-hold investors who are comfortable with full market exposure, since the rotation logic adds both complexity and the risk of being in cash at the wrong times.

## Risks and limitations

The fund's main risk is whipsaw: a sharp reversal after the signal has moved to cash can force the fund to buy back in near the top. The volatility signal itself is backward-looking, derived from recent price movements, so it may lag genuine inflection points in market regimes. The fund also carries the risk of concentration — because the NASDAQ-100 is tech-heavy, both the equity sleeve and the broader [stock market](/stock-market/) exposure share similar drivers.

Additionally, in a prolonged low-volatility environment, the fund may stay in stocks continuously and offer no better returns than a plain NASDAQ-100 ETF, while still charging the full expense ratio. Conversely, in a volatile sideways market, frequent rotation between equities and cash could result in suboptimal timing.

## How to research QQWZ

Investors should examine the fund's prospectus to understand the exact volatility rule and the rebalancing frequency. Fact sheets published regularly by Pacer Financial outline the current allocation split between cash and equities. Comparing rolling-period returns to a plain NASDAQ-100 ETF will reveal whether the rotation logic has added or subtracted value over different [market cycles](/market-cycle/). The fund's trading volume and bid-ask spread should be checked; lower volume can widen trading costs.
