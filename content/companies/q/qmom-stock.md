## What is momentum in stock investing?

Momentum is the tendency for stocks that have been outperforming to keep outperforming, at least for a window of months. This is not a claim that past prices predict the future with certainty, but rather an observed pattern in market data: when you buy stocks that have recently beaten their peers and you avoid stocks that have lagged, you tend to capture some of that relative strength going forward. QMOM is built entirely on this insight. The fund uses quantitative algorithms to identify the stocks in the U.S. market displaying the strongest momentum signals, then holds them in a portfolio designed to harness momentum-driven returns.

## How does Alpha Architect's algorithm work?

The fund does not rely on a stock-picker's judgment. Instead, it applies transparent, published screening rules to the entire investable U.S. [stock market](/stock-market/). These screens measure price momentum—how much a stock has outperformed its peers over recent months, typically the past three to twelve months depending on the specific signal. They also measure earnings momentum—how much a company's earnings estimates have risen relative to expectations. The algorithm ranks all stocks on these combined signals, selects the top performers, and weights them in the portfolio. Holdings turn over regularly as new winners ascend and prior leaders fall back. This turnover is intentional; it keeps the portfolio disciplined to the momentum signal rather than clinging to yesterday's winners.

## What are the real risks of momentum investing?

Momentum is a factor that thrives in certain environments and fails in others. When the market is calm and winners steadily outperform, momentum works well because strong stocks stay strong. When the market reverses sharply or when value stocks suddenly lead, momentum collapses because former winners become losers without warning. Momentum portfolios are also crowded—many investors employ similar strategies—which amplifies both gains and losses when the factor reverses. Sector concentration is another structural risk. Momentum screens often load heavily into whatever sector has been winning recently—technology in [bull markets](/bull-market/), energy in commodity surges—and QMOM will inherit that concentration. A rotation away from the favored sector creates sudden losses.

The fund's turnover is higher than buy-and-hold strategies. This creates real trading costs and, in taxable accounts, tax consequences. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) reflects systematic rebalancing and operational complexity. Tracking error to a broad market index is large and intentional; QMOM is meant to outperform by capturing momentum, not to match the market.

## Who should consider QMOM?

This is a tactical, factor-driven strategy, not a core holding. Investors who believe momentum is a reliable pattern and have risk tolerance for its inevitable underperformance periods can use QMOM as a satellite position or as part of a multi-factor approach. The fund requires a longer [holding period](/holding-period/) than day traders use—momentum plays out over months—but shorter commitment than buy-and-hold-forever investors demand. Systematic investors building factor portfolios that combine momentum with value, quality, and [dividend](/dividend/) tilt often use funds like this. Active traders sometimes use QMOM as a momentum proxy when building tactical tilts.

## How does QMOM perform in different market environments?

In strong, trending markets where momentum persists, QMOM tends to outperform passive indices because it is holding the strongest stocks and constantly rotating into fresh strength. In choppy, range-bound markets with frequent reversals, QMOM churns—buying and selling frequently—and transaction costs erode returns without compensation. In [bear markets](/bear-market/), momentum is a liability. QMOM hits sell signals after stocks are already down, locking in losses that a patient holder would avoid. The factor amplifies both market strength and market weakness, which is the core tension in [momentum investing](/momentum-investing/).

## How should someone research QMOM before buying?

Start with Alpha Architect's index methodology documentation, which details the exact momentum signals used, the scoring system, and rebalancing frequency. Review rolling returns over multiple market periods, especially including at least one significant drawdown, to see whether momentum amplified both gains and losses as expected. Compare QMOM's returns to a plain U.S. stock index and to other momentum strategies to evaluate competitive positioning. Finally, ask yourself honestly: can you hold this fund through a 20 to 30 percent decline, or will you panic-sell exactly when momentum is most against you? The answer determines whether QMOM suits your temperament or whether a gentler factor strategy is more appropriate.
