Tancheng Group Co., Ltd. is a small online retailer of self-designed ornamental and cultural products, primarily jewelry and spiritual artifacts marketed to Chinese consumers. The company operates from Taiyuan in Shanxi Province and sells directly to customers through its own e-commerce platform, focusing on a narrow but distinctive product range centred on cultural symbolism and philosophical tradition. It trades on the over-the-counter market under the ticker QSJC.

The business is, in essence, a small specialty brand attempting to capture demand for spiritually or culturally meaningful gifts within China. Its portfolio includes silver-plated platinum pendants, Jue Cheng ornaments, Chinese zodiac pendants, and items inspired by Master Hongyi's Heart Sutra — a Buddhist philosophical text. The company markets these products as items infused with cultural meaning rather than as pure fashion, aiming to appeal to gift-buyers and personal collectors willing to pay a premium for perceived authenticity and spiritual significance.

## From Bigeon to Tancheng

Tancheng was incorporated in 2018 under the name Bigeon before rebranding to Tancheng Group Co., Ltd. in October 2022. The rebranding reflected a strategic decision to align the company name with its core product line and cultural positioning. The company is structured as a Nevada holding company that owns subsidiaries operating in the People's Republic of China, a common structure for foreign-listed Chinese businesses seeking U.S. public markets without navigating direct PRC stock listings.

The company's parent is Shanxi Qiansui Tancheng Culture Media Co., Ltd., suggesting a broader cultural-media ambition, though the publicly traded entity has remained narrowly focused on ornament sales.

## The online ornament business

Tancheng's revenue model is straightforward: it designs ornaments and cultural products, sources or manufactures them, and sells them to end consumers through an online store. The company appears to rely on dropshipping or a lightweight fulfillment model rather than maintaining large inventory, keeping capital requirements low. Profit margins on specialty cultural items can be reasonable if the company avoids heavy discounting and holds to a premium positioning.

The challenge for any small ornament retailer is customer acquisition and retention. The company sells in a crowded market where larger, established players have brand recognition and where e-commerce platforms dominate retail. To survive, Tancheng would need either a defensible niche (which its focus on spirituality and Chinese cultural symbolism provides to some degree) or exceptional operating efficiency.

## Scale and substance

Recent financial reports show the company operates at a very small scale. Revenue in recent periods has been minimal, with reports indicating annual revenue in the low six figures or below. The company has reported net losses, suggesting it spends more on overhead and customer acquisition than it generates in sales. This is typical of a micro-cap company in its early or stalled growth phase.

The lack of profitable scale is the central issue. Without robust revenue and clear paths to expansion, the company struggles to justify its public-company overhead or to invest in product development and marketing. Many small cultural or niche-product companies eventually either find sustainable demand and scale or fade into irrelevance.

## The public markets and risks

Tancheng's shares trade on the OTC pink markets, a venue for companies too small or too thinly traded for listing on major exchanges. The OTC pink sheets offer lower regulatory burden but also less investor protection and lower liquidity. Anyone holding QSJC shares faces substantial illiquidity risk — selling shares at will may be difficult.

The company is also exposed to currency risk, as it earns revenue in Chinese yuan but reports in U.S. dollars. Shifts in exchange rates flow directly into reported results. Additionally, any change in Chinese regulations around e-commerce, consumer goods, or foreign ownership of domestic operating entities could disrupt operations.
