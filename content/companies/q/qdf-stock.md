*The **FlexShares Quality [Dividend](/dividend/) [Index Fund](/index-fund/)** (ticker QDF) is an exchange-traded fund that holds U.S. large-cap stocks selected for both their [dividend yields](/dividend-yield/) and their underlying business quality — a middle ground between funds that chase yield indiscriminately and passive index funds that hold everything regardless of fundamentals.*

## What QDF does

QDF tracks the Northern Trust U.S. Quality Dividend Index, a rules-based selection of dividend-paying stocks from the large-cap universe. The fund's selection process starts by identifying companies that pay dividends, then filters for quality measures: strong [balance sheets](/balance-sheet/), stable earnings, and sustainable [payout ratios](/payout-ratio/). The result is a more curated dividend portfolio than a simple dividend yield sort would produce — focusing on companies likely to raise dividends over time rather than ones locked into high yields from depressed stock prices.

The fund is sponsored by FlexShares, the index ETF arm of Northern Trust, a major institutional asset manager. Unlike [actively managed funds](/actively-managed-fund/), QDF simply holds the stocks in its index and rebalances mechanically whenever the index methodology updates its constituents — typically quarterly. This passive structure keeps costs low and the fund's holdings fully transparent.

## Who owns what and how it trades

QDF holds roughly 300 dividend-paying stocks spanning all sectors of the S&P 500, weighted by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/). Technology, healthcare, and industrial companies are well-represented, as are the traditional dividend strongholds in utilities, energy, and consumer staples. The fund avoids any stock that fails the quality screen, so it excludes companies with deteriorating balance sheets or unsustainable dividend cuts, even if they temporarily offer very high yields.

The fund trades on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) under the ticker QDF, like any stock, with buy and sell orders executing throughout the trading [day. Trading](/day-trading/) volume is solid, meaning investors can enter and exit without paying wide spreads. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is low relative to actively managed dividend funds — a qualitative measure of value that reflects the fund's passive, rules-based approach. Dividends paid by the underlying stocks are collected and distributed quarterly to shareholders.

## The dividend and yield trade-off

QDF offers meaningful dividend income, though not the highest yields in the universe. A fund that simply ranked all dividend-paying stocks by yield and held the top tier would produce higher current income but at the cost of [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/), deteriorating balance sheets, and the possibility of dividend cuts when business conditions sour. QDF's quality filter trades some yield for sustainability — a lower current payout from businesses more likely to grow their distributions and maintain them through economic cycles.

This matters because a dividend that gets cut is worse than a lower starting yield that holds steady. A shareholder's total return depends on both the current payment and the capital appreciation of the fund's value, and a fund holding deteriorating businesses may preserve yield temporarily while the underlying stocks sink. The quality screen does not guarantee dividends will never fall, but it tilts the odds toward funds that raise their payouts more often than they cut them.

## Composition and style

The fund's holdings skew toward established companies with long dividend histories — consumer staples, telecom, utilities, and the larger financial institutions. This creates a value-oriented tilt: QDF tends to hold companies trading at more moderate valuations and slower growth rates than the broader market. That bias is structural, not accidental, because fast-growing technology companies rarely offer high dividends, and quality-screening rules naturally exclude risky or unproven businesses.

The consequence is that QDF's returns will lag when growth stocks dominate market performance, as they did for much of the 2010s and the period around 2024. QDF tends to outperform during periods when investors favor income, dividend growth, and lower-volatility stocks — typically during downturns or when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) are rising. Over longer periods, the comparison to a total-market index fund depends heavily on whether dividend growth and the quality screen's risk reduction can offset QDF's slower headline growth.

## Risks and tracking

QDF's main structural risk is tracking error — the gap between what the index does and what the fund actually delivers. Frictions like trading costs, cash drag from dividends awaiting reinvestment, and the fund's expense ratio shave a small amount off the index return every year. For a passive dividend fund this is usually minimal, but it exists. A second risk is that the quality screen itself can underperform; if the fundamentals that define "quality" in the current market diverge from what drives returns, the filtered index could lag broader alternatives.

The fund's dividend-focused mandate also means it underweights or omits the fastest-growing segments of the market. It will never capture the outsized gains of a technology bubble or a momentum rally in unprofitable growth stocks, which is the trade-off for reduced volatility and steady income.

## How to research QDF

Start with the fund's fact sheet and prospectus, available on the Northern Trust and FlexShares websites, which detail the exact index methodology, the current holdings, and the expense ratio. The prospectus explains the quality criteria — what constitutes a sustainable payout ratio and a healthy balance sheet by the fund's definition. Compare QDF's recent yields and total returns to simpler dividend-index alternatives and to broad market index funds to understand the [yield pickup](/yield-pickup/) and the growth sacrifice. Check the fund's top ten holdings to see which companies dominate; concentration in any handful of names can amplify sector risk if those companies face business pressures together. Track dividend growth over time to see whether the quality screen is holding up — do payouts rise steadily, or do they stagnate and cut? Monitor the fund's price relative to its [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) (NAV); a persistent discount or premium can signal liquidity or sentiment shifts.
