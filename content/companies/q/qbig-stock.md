Invesco Top QQQ ETF (QBIG) is a straightforward concentration play: it holds only the 50 largest stocks in the Nasdaq-100 index, which itself comprises the 100 most valuable non-financial companies on the [Nasdaq](/nasdaq/) exchange. By holding just the top half by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/), QBIG magnifies the returns and volatility of the Nasdaq's largest megacap growth stocks — a tighter, more aggressive cousin of a full-index exposure.

## Why concentrate a concentrated index?

The Nasdaq-100 is already a heavy concentration on US technology and growth stocks; it is not a diversified index in the traditional sense. QBIG narrows further. The logic behind holding only the top 50 is that the largest names in the Nasdaq-100 are the most liquid, most widely researched, and most dominant by market value — a "best of the best" approach. For investors who believe that megacap tech will outperform or who simply want maximal exposure to the largest growth stories, QBIG delivers that directionally with lower fee drag than buying individual stocks.

## What QBIG holds and how the composition shifts

At any given time, QBIG's top five holdings account for a substantial fraction of its portfolio — often 20 to 30 per cent — because the Nasdaq-100 is dominated by a handful of AI-era mega-monopolies in cloud computing, semiconductors, and software. Below the very largest, the fund owns other megacap growth names: payment processors, e-commerce, social media, consumer electronics, and cloud providers. The fund rebalances quarterly, so as companies grow or shrink in market value relative to peers, their weight in the fund adjusts. This can result in high turnover and trading costs if the index methodology is strict about rank ordering.

## Volatility and downside risk

Concentration on the top 50 names amplifies volatility. When the Nasdaq-100's largest stocks rally, QBIG rallies harder. Conversely, when sentiment turns against megacap growth — particularly during periods when the market reprices interest-rate expectations or becomes nervous about artificial intelligence hype — QBIG can fall faster and further than the full index. A 20 per cent decline in the broad Nasdaq-100 might translate to a 25 or 30 per cent decline in QBIG if the top 50 stocks underperform the rest of the index. Over shorter time periods — months or a couple of years — this amplified volatility is real.

Additionally, the fund carries [idiosyncratic risk](/idiosyncratic-risk/) from any single megacap. A major earnings miss, regulatory action, or strategic misstep in a handful of the fund's core holdings can ripple through the entire portfolio. Unlike a truly diversified equity fund, QBIG has no insulation from sector concentration or company-specific shocks.

## Sector concentration and what investors are implicitly buying

QBIG is predominantly a bet on software, semiconductors, and internet-based services — the tech sectors that dominate the Nasdaq's upper tier. It is not a balanced exposure to economic growth broadly, nor is it a hedge portfolio. It is a levered bet on the proposition that the most dominant US technology companies will create shareholder value faster than other segments of the market. This works well in periods when the market favours growth and technology; it struggles in periods of value rotation or [stagflation](/stagflation/).

## Trading and expense costs

QBIG is liquid during [regular trading hours](/regular-trading-hours/), with reasonable [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) for most order sizes. The fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is modest, though the quarterly rebalancing can occasionally incur trading costs that are partly passed on to shareholders. These costs are minor relative to the volatility swings in the fund itself but worth accounting for if you are a frequent trader or timing significant purchases and sales.

## How to research QBIG

Compare QBIG's performance over rolling 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year periods against the full Nasdaq-100 index or a broad Nasdaq-tracking ETF to understand the return pickup from concentration and its volatility cost. Examine the fund's factsheet to see the current top 10 holdings and their weights — this snapshot shows how much of your capital is concentrated in a handful of stocks. If three or four holdings represent 20 per cent of the fund, you are accepting material idiosyncratic risk. Finally, stress-test the portfolio by considering what a 30 per cent decline in your top 5 holdings would do to the fund's total return — the answer will clarify whether the [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) is acceptable to your investment timeframe and risk appetite.
