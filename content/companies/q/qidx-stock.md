> *Quality is consistency; growth without consistency is a bet, not an investment.*

This philosophy animates the **Indexperts Quality Earnings Focused ETF** (QIDX), a fund that tilts toward U.S. large-cap companies with predictable, durable earnings streams rather than the most spectacular growth charts. The fund applies a multi-factor screen focused on earnings stability, profitability, and reasonable valuation, then holds the resulting portfolio with minimal turnover. It is a fund for investors who believe that the best long-term returns come from owning profitable businesses growing at a sustainable pace, rather than chasing the most accelerating growth stories.

Indexperts is a smaller [index provider](/index-provider/) than the household names — not in the league of MSCI, S&P Dow Jones Indices, or FTSE Russell — but the firm has built a reputation for carefully constructed factor-based indices. QIDX's index selects from the top 500 U.S. companies by market capitalisation, screening for characteristics like [return on equity](/return-on-equity/) (how much profit a company makes on shareholder capital), earnings growth stability (how smoothly earnings have grown, rather than in fits and starts), debt levels (preferring companies with lower leverage), and [price-to-earnings ratio](/price-to-earnings-ratio/) (avoiding the most expensive valuations). The result is typically a portfolio of 100 to 200 large-cap holdings, materially fewer than a broad [S&P 500 index](/sp-500-index/) but far more than an active manager's typical concentrated bet.

The selection process is fully transparent and rules-based. There is no discretion, no committee deciding whether to include or exclude a stock on subjective grounds. The same logic applies each quarter when the index rebalances. This transparency is important: an investor considering QIDX can read the methodology, see exactly which stocks are in the fund, and understand why. Active fund managers often keep their selection process proprietary, making it impossible for investors to truly evaluate whether the strategy is sound or whether recent performance was skill or luck. QIDX's rules-based approach eliminates that ambiguity.

The earnings focus distinguishes QIDX from other quality-tilted funds. Many funds lean on return-on-equity or profitability alone, which can capture efficiency but might miss earnings sustainability. QIDX specifically privileges companies whose earnings have grown in a regular, predictable manner, filtering out firms whose profits swing wildly year to year. A pharmaceutical company with a blockbuster drug might show explosive earnings growth one year, but if that growth is lumpy and unpredictable, the fund may underweight or exclude it. By contrast, a software-as-a-service company with steady, predictable annual recurring revenue makes the cut more readily. The fund is essentially betting that smoothness, predictability, and moderate growth beat inconsistency and high growth.

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) typically runs 0.35 to 0.45 percent annually, putting QIDX in the lower tier of [actively managed funds](/actively-managed-fund/) but slightly above the cheapest broad passive indices. The premium reflects the bespoke index construction and the quarterly rebalancing process. Turnover is modest — perhaps 20 to 35 percent annually, well below active funds but higher than a pure market-cap-weighted index that only trades when companies are added to or removed from the index.

Liquidity is solid. QIDX trades on the [New York Stock Exchange](/new-york-stock-exchange/) with sufficient volume that retail and institutional investors can buy and sell without difficulty. The [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) is typically tight — under 0.05 percent — meaning the cost to enter or exit is negligible relative to the fund's value.

Performance will depend entirely on whether the fund's quality-and-earnings focus actually outpaces the broader market. During extended periods when pure momentum and growth dominate — when investors reward the fastest-growing companies regardless of profit margins or valuation — QIDX will lag a pure growth index. During periods when the market rotates toward value, earnings consistency, and dividend-paying stocks, QIDX can outperform. There is no guarantee which regime will prevail next quarter or next year, so the fund is best suited for long-term holders who genuinely believe that consistent, profitable businesses are the best long-term compounders, rather than traders trying to time rotations.

QIDX fits into a portfolio as a large-cap U.S. equity core, similar in role to an S&P 500 [index fund](/index-fund/) but with a deliberate tilt toward quality and earnings consistency. It pairs naturally with international equities, fixed-income holdings, or more value-tilted funds to build a balanced, diversified portfolio. On its own, it is a single exposure and does not provide the breadth necessary for a complete investment plan.
