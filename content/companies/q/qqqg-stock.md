The Pacer [Nasdaq](/nasdaq/) 100 Top 50 Cash Cows Growth Leaders ETF (ticker QQQG) is a US-listed exchange-traded fund that isolates a disciplined subset of the Nasdaq 100: the 50 largest companies that generate the most durable cash flow relative to their net income.

## A cash-flow lens on the Nasdaq 100

The Nasdaq 100 is a market-cap-weighted index of the largest 100 non-financial stocks on the Nasdaq exchange — a universe dominated by technology, consumer services, and biotechnology. Many Nasdaq 100 companies are valued on growth expectations or momentum rather than on the cash they actually generate today. QQQG applies a different discipline: it starts with the 100 largest companies on the Nasdaq, ranks them by [free cash flow](/free-cash-flow/) as a percentage of net income, selects the top 50, and weights them by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/).

> "A company with strong earnings that actually converts those earnings into cash is a better-constructed business than one burning cash or showing accounting profit while money walks out the door."

The principle is sound. The difference between reported earnings and actual cash flow matters a great deal over long [holding periods](/holding-period/). In practice, the fund overweights companies mature enough to generate cash and underweights younger, pre-profitable, or heavily-reinvesting technology firms. The result is a tilt away from the highest-growth names in the index. An investor in QQQG is not buying the Nasdaq 100 as a whole; they are buying a segment of it — one biased toward less-explosive but more-grounded businesses.

## Index composition and quarterly mechanics

The underlying index rebalances every three months, so the 50 positions can turn over as companies' cash-generation patterns shift relative to earnings. Because the fund draws from the 100 largest Nasdaq companies, holdings are exceptionally liquid and easy to trade. The largest positions will typically include megacaps like [Apple](/aapl-stock/), [Microsoft](/msft-stock/), [Nvidia](/nvda-stock/), and other technology leaders, though their inclusion or weight depends on how each ranks on the cash-flow metric at rebalance dates.

QQQG itself trades on NYSE Arca with the volume and tight [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) typical of large, simple ETFs. The fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is kept low by Pacer, reflecting the mechanical nature of the index construction — no active management, just a transparent sorting rule applied quarterly. The fund holds all 50 index members directly and does not use derivatives.

## Cost structure and suitability

The expense ratio is typical for plain-vanilla [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/) tracking major indices, generally under 0.30 annually. This matters because the fund competes with other Nasdaq 100 vehicles and general large-cap [index funds](/index-fund/), where cost differences compound over decades.

QQQG appeals to investors who want the Nasdaq 100 exposure but with a quality bias toward cash generation. It is also useful for anyone building a systematic portfolio tilted toward quality factors — the cash-flow screen is one mechanical way to filter for durable compounding. Because the fund still tilts heavily toward technology and large-cap growth, it is most appropriate for investors with a long time horizon and comfort with growth-stock volatility.

## Tracking and concentration risks

The fund's main risk is concentration: it holds only 50 names, all drawn from a universe already dominated by a handful of megacaps, so company-specific or sector downturns hit hard. Technology and consumer-services weakness cascades into QQQG more severely than into a broad equity index.

A subtler risk is the backward-looking nature of the cash-flow screen. The metric reranks quarterly based on past conversion. A company with weak free cash flow today may recover next quarter, or a strong cash generator may deteriorate; the index lags that deterioration by months. This is not unique to QQQG — all mechanical indices face this lag.

Tracking error between the fund and the index is usually minimal, in the range of 0.01 to 0.05 annually, because the fund holds all 50 index positions and rebalances when the index does. The prospectus and fact sheet lay out the index methodology, current holdings, and the precise cash-flow formula applied quarterly.
