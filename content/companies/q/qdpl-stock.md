*The **Pacer Metaurus US Large Cap [Dividend](/dividend/) Multiplier 400 ETF** (ticker QDPL) is an exchange-traded fund designed to multiply dividend returns through a structured options strategy — it holds dividend-paying stocks from the large-cap universe and uses options derivatives to increase the fund's effective exposure to dividend growth.*

### What does "dividend multiplier" mean?

QDPL is not a simple buy-and-hold dividend fund. Instead, it uses a rules-based approach called the Metaurus methodology, created by Metaurus Capital. The core idea is to collect dividends from the underlying stocks, then reinvest those dividends into out-of-the-money [call options](/call-option/) on the same or related stocks. Because options are leveraged — one option contract controls a large amount of stock value — the same dividend income can theoretically control a much larger position than buying shares outright. If those call options finish in the money when dividends are paid again, the multiplied exposure generates proportionally more dividend income, creating a compound effect.

The mathematics are attractive in theory: if a stock pays a 3% annual dividend and you can use derivative leverage to amplify your exposure fourfold, you might generate a 12% dividend stream. However, call options expire worthless or finish out of the money regularly, which erodes the multiplied exposure. The Metaurus framework is designed to manage this trade-off through a disciplined rolling strategy — as options approach expiration, the fund sells them and buys new ones, trying to maintain the target leverage continuously.

### Who operates the fund and what structure does it use?

Pacer Financial, a smaller ETF provider focused on [structured products](/structured-product/), sponsors QDPL. The fund is classified as a standard ETF, not a [leveraged ETF](/leveraged-etf/) or an ETN, but it functions similarly to a leveraged product because the options strategy can amplify gains and losses. It is not a daily-reset leveraged ETF (like the 2x or 3x QQQ products), which decays predictably in ranging markets. Instead, QDPL's leverage is floating and depends on how the underlying calls perform — less transparent but potentially more efficient if the [stock market](/stock-market/) trends steadily upward.

The fund holds physical shares of dividend-paying large-cap stocks, primarily companies in the 400 largest dividend payers by [market cap](/market-capitalization/), and combines that with active options trading. This hybrid approach means QDPL has both equity exposure and derivative risk. Trading volume is lower than in the largest dividend ETFs, which can matter for investors building large positions.

### How do the costs work, and what risks come with the strategy?

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) includes both the management of the stock portfolio and the ongoing cost of the options strategy — rolling call positions, slippage from [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/), and administrative overhead. Qualitatively, these costs are higher than a straightforward dividend ETF because of the options operations, though the fund disclosures lay out the exact ratio.

The central risk is that the multiplier does not multiply in all market environments. If large-cap dividend stocks rally sharply, the call options finish in the money and the fund benefits from the leverage. But if stocks fall or stagnate, calls expire worthless, and the fund's leverage unwinds, leaving shareholders with diluted returns. A market that drifts sideways is particularly punishing: dividends are still collected and paid out, but the call options lose value, eroding the multiplied exposure without the offset of capital gains.

Additionally, call option positions are subject to volatility decay — the erosion of option value as expiration approaches, independent of whether the stock price moves. In high-volatility environments, the cost of rolling options (selling old calls and buying new ones) rises sharply, which can significantly reduce net returns.

### Who should consider QDPL, and how do you research it?

QDPL is designed for investors seeking enhanced dividend income and comfortable with options mechanics. It is not a buy-and-forget tool like a standard dividend [index fund](/index-fund/). It works best in environments where dividend-paying stocks rise steadily and option-writing generates meaningful premium income without the underlying positions falling below [strike prices](/strike-price/). It is worst in severe downturns or in extended [flat markets](/flat-market/), where the multiplier erodes.

To evaluate the fund, read the prospectus carefully to understand the exact Metaurus methodology and how leverage is calculated and maintained. Review the fund's track record — not the hypothetical leverage assumptions, but the actual returns delivered. Compare those returns to a simple dividend ETF and to a standard large-cap index fund on a total-return basis over multiple years covering different market regimes. Check the fund's holdings to confirm they are indeed large-cap dividend payers and not concentrated in a single sector. Track how much of the fund's volatility and returns come from the dividend-collection strategy versus the call-option mechanics by comparing to a non-multiplied dividend fund.
