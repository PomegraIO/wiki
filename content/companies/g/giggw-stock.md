GigCapital7 Corp. trades in three distinct securities on the [Nasdaq](/nasdaq/), each carrying different economics and redemption terms. The warrants (GIGGW) are the leveraged component of the capital structure—contractual rights to purchase common shares at a fixed price, offering outsized upside if the combined company appreciates significantly. Understanding warrants requires looking at how GigCapital7's capital stack works, because the warrant terms are written into the [merger](/merger/) agreement itself and shape payoffs for every investor class.

The SPAC structure creates several distinct ownership tiers, each with its own path through the [business combination](/business-combination-purchase/) and its own risk profile. The ordinary shares (GIG) are straightforward equity, with full voting and [dividend](/dividend/) rights. The units (GIGGU) bundle one ordinary share with warrants, giving retail investors a single security that contains both pieces. And the warrants (GIGGW) separate from units allow investors to buy in purely for the leverage—paying less upfront for the right to buy shares at a set [strike price](/strike-price/) if the stock appreciates.

## Warrant mechanics and exercise terms

GigCapital7's warrants carry a strike price set when the units were first issued, typically a few dollars above the IPO price per share. The warrant agreement specifies that holders can exercise their right to purchase one ordinary share at the strike price at any point before expiration, which for GigCapital7 falls five years after completion of the merger. That exercise window—and the strike price itself—is not renegotiable; it is baked into the warrant certificate and applies to every holder equally.

When a warrant holder exercises, they pay the strike price in cash and receive one newly issued ordinary share. That new share carries the same rights as any other common share: voting power, eligibility for dividends, and [liquidation](/liquidation/) standing. The warrant itself is then worthless and retired. For the company, warrant exercise is a capital event: it brings fresh cash in but dilutes existing shareholders by increasing the share count.

The critical distinction is that warrants are not equity—they are [call options](/call-option/) created by the company on its own shares. From the company's perspective, an outstanding warrant is a future dilution obligation. From the warrant holder's perspective, it is leverage: if the stock price rises significantly above the strike price, the warrant's [intrinsic value](/intrinsic-value/) rises with it. A $3 warrant strike with stock at $8 carries $5 of intrinsic value, making leverage-seeking investors pay premium prices for out-of-the-money warrants in volatile situations.

## Dilution and shareholder protection

Because warrants create new shares upon exercise, they dilute existing shareholders' ownership percentages and [earnings per share](/earnings-per-share/) if exercised in large numbers. A company with 10 million shares outstanding and 5 million warrants will see its share count jump to 15 million if all warrants exercise—a 50 percent dilution. Sophisticated investors price this in, discounting share prices when warrant overhang is substantial.

GigCapital7's warrants carry one additional twist: a redemption feature that many SPACs include to force [early exercise](/early-exercise/) or retirement. If the ordinary share price closes at or above a trigger level (often 110 to 130 percent of the warrant strike price) for a set number of consecutive days, the company can force warrant holders to exercise or lose the warrant entirely. This feature protects the company from perpetual warrant overhang but penalizes warrant holders who did not anticipate redemption and hold shares they did not intend to buy.

## Investor profiles and warrant demand

Warrant investors fall into a few categories. Retail investors often buy units (which include warrants) for simplicity, treating the warrant as a bonus. Seasoned warrant traders view them as volatile instruments with asymmetric payoff profiles: they can appreciate rapidly on stock price jumps but expire worthless if the stock underperforms. And institutional investors sometimes use warrants as a hedge against equity positions or as a way to gain market exposure with reduced capital commitment.

The trading profile of warrants also differs from shares. Warrant prices are more volatile than share prices, moving faster on good or bad news because the leverage amplifies percentage swings. [Bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) on warrants are often wider, making trading costs higher. Warrant holders do not own a company; they own an option on it, which means they earn nothing if the stock stays flat and lose everything if it expires out of the money.

## The path through merger and beyond

When GigCapital7's merger with Hadron Energy closes, the GIGGW warrants do not disappear. They remain outstanding, still entitling holders to buy shares of the now-combined firm at the fixed strike price. The only change is administrative: the warrant certificate will refer to Hadron shares instead of GigCapital7 shares, but the economic terms are identical.

From that point forward, the value of GIGGW depends entirely on Hadron's stock performance. If Hadron shares trade above the strike price, GIGGW carries intrinsic value. If the stock falls below strike, the warrant is underwater and unlikely to be exercised until (or unless) the stock recovers. If Hadron enters financial distress or bankruptcy, GIGGW holders are last in the creditor queue and typically receive nothing.

## How to research GigCapital7's warrant structure

Warrant terms are documented in the company's prospectus and the specific warrant agreement, both filed with the SEC (CIK 0002023730). Read the terms carefully: the strike price, the [expiration date](/expiration-date/), the redemption feature, and any other conditions. Calculate the intrinsic value by subtracting the strike price from the current share price; anything positive means the warrant has value.

For comparison, look at other SPAC warrant trades and [implied volatility](/implied-volatility/) priced into warrant premiums. Track the company's announcements around any redemption calls—when a company forces warrant redemption, warrant holders must decide quickly whether to exercise at the set price or let the warrant expire. As the merger closes and Hadron begins trading, the warrants become tools for leveraged long investors betting on the underlying stock, with all the amplified risk that leverage entails.
