The T-REX 2X Long GME Daily Target ETF (ticker: GMEU) is a leveraged fund designed to move in lockstep with GameStop stock, but amplified. For every 1 percent move up in GME, GMEU aims to move 2 percent up. For every 1 percent move down, it aims to fall 2 percent down. This 2x leverage is achieved not by borrowing on margin in the traditional sense but by holding financial derivatives — primarily futures and swaps — that provide two-times exposure to the underlying stock. The fund is deliberately short-term; it resets daily, meaning the [leverage ratio](/leverage-ratio-forex/) is refreshed every trading day.

This is not a fund for buy-and-hold portfolios. That distinction is critical and worth understanding in depth. The daily reset mechanism is the key. Every single trading day, at the market close, GMEU rebalances its derivatives portfolio to maintain exactly 2x leverage relative to GME going forward. This rebalancing is mechanical and has a cost called volatility decay — a slow bleed of performance over time in sideways or choppy markets.

Consider a concrete example. Imagine GME opens Monday at 100 dollars and moves like this: Monday up 10 percent (to 110), Tuesday down 10 percent (to 99), Wednesday flat. A traditional long holder ends at 99, nearly even. GMEU's holder, over those same three days, experiences gains and losses amplified by 2x, but with the twist of daily resets. Monday, GMEU is up 20 percent. Tuesday, the leverage resets and the fund aims for 2x the next day's -10 percent move, a loss of 20 percent. Three days later, the effects of that amplification and reset compound in a way that often leaves GMEU trailing the underlying stock, even if the stock ends flat or modestly up.

This is the central reality of [leveraged ETFs](/leveraged-etf/): they are tools for tactical, directional bets over periods of days or weeks, not for decades of wealth building. A leveraged fund held for a month in a volatile market can easily underperform the underlying asset by 5 or 10 percent due to volatility decay alone. Over years, the underperformance compounds.

The attraction of GMEU is real in narrow circumstances: if you believe GME will move up sharply over the next few days or weeks and want to maximize that gain, 2x leverage is one way to amplify the profit. If you are an options trader or daytrader who is in and out of positions within days, the leverage helps. If you are a long-term investor expecting to own GME for years, GMEU is an expensive mistake — the daily resets, the 0.95 percent to 1.10 percent annual [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/), and the inherent volatility decay will quietly erode your returns.

GameStop itself is a single stock, not a diversified company, which adds another layer of risk. GMEU is therefore a bet on a single stock with 2x leverage applied daily. That is concentrated, speculative risk. A loss of 25 percent in GME turns into a 50 percent loss in GMEU. A holder of leveraged single-stock ETFs needs an unusually high risk tolerance and a clear exit plan.

Trading in GMEU is liquid enough for most investors to enter and exit the same day. The [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) is typically tight (0.05 percent to 0.15 percent) because volume is high. But the fund's value is deeply sensitive to volatility and daily rebalancing mechanics, making the intraday price sometimes unpredictable for passive observers. Options on GME itself, if available, may offer better precision for tactical bets.

GMEU is a reminder that leverage is not a free benefit. It amplifies both gains and losses and, over time, erodes value in choppy markets. Any investor considering it should understand the daily reset mechanism, run a volatility-decay simulation for the expected [holding period](/holding-period/), and ask themselves whether the 2x amplification for days or weeks is truly the right instrument, or whether a smaller position in the underlying stock or a [call option](/call-option/) might serve the same goal more efficiently.
