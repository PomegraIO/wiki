*[GETTY REALTY CORP /MD/](/gty-stock/) (ticker: GTY) is a [real-estate-investment-trust](/real-estate-investment-trust/) that acquires and leases [commercial real estate](/commercial-real-estate/) properties, with a concentration in convenience store and fuel station real estate. The company operates under a net-lease model, where tenants bear responsibility for property maintenance, insurance, and taxes.*

## The Net-Lease REIT Model and Passive Cash Flow

Getty Realty operates as a [REIT](/real-estate-investment-trust/), meaning the company owns properties and leases them to operating tenants, collecting rent in exchange. Unlike a full-service property manager, Getty uses a net-lease structure: the tenant (typically a convenience store operator or fuel station brand) handles all property maintenance, insurance, property taxes, and utilities. Getty's role is confined to property acquisition, lease negotiation, and rent collection. This arrangement is capital-efficient for the landlord—it minimizes operating expenses and shifts maintenance risk to the tenant—but it requires careful underwriting of tenant credit quality and lease covenants to ensure that rent payments are reliable.

The convenience store and fuel station sector is fragmented and competitive. Many sites are operated by individual owners or small chains, rather than major national brands. This means Getty must evaluate the creditworthiness of diverse, often smaller tenants. The quality of the tenant base directly affects the reliability of rent collection: if a tenant struggles, the property may fall vacant, requiring Getty to incur turnover costs and vacancy losses until a new tenant is secured.

## Real Estate Concentration and Site-Level Economics

Getty's portfolio is concentrated geographically in the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic regions, with significant holdings in New York, Pennsylvania, and Ohio. This regional concentration is both a source of stability and a source of risk. The stability comes from [deep market](/deep-market/) knowledge, established relationships with operators and [brokers](/broker/), and the ability to manage properties efficiently in familiar territories. The risk lies in economic shocks that disproportionately affect the region: localized [recessions](/recession/), shifts in population, or changes in commuting patterns can reduce the value of properties and the viability of tenants.

Site-level economics matter enormously in this business. A convenience store or fuel station in a high-traffic location with strong surrounding demographics generates healthy sales and can support higher rent. The same property in a declining neighborhood may struggle. Getty's underwriting process must evaluate traffic patterns, demographics, competing sites, and the operator's ability to generate sufficient revenue to cover rent. Properties in secondary locations or small markets may offer higher [dividend yields](/dividend-yield/) to compensate for risk, but they also entail higher vacancy and turnover risk.

## Tenant Credit Quality and Diversification

Getty's financial stability depends on the credit quality of its tenant base. If major tenants (whether large convenience store chains or independent operators) face financial difficulty, Getty risks non-payment and eventual vacancy. The company must therefore evaluate whether its tenant base is concentrated among a few large, strong operators or diversified across many smaller tenants. Concentration in a few strong tenants (e.g., a single large chain) can provide revenue stability but creates [counterparty risk](/counterparty-risk/): the loss of one large tenant materially impacts cash flow. [Diversification](/diversification/) across many smaller tenants reduces that single-tenant risk but increases the complexity of lease management and the sensitivity to widespread weakness in the operator base.

The convenience store industry is competitive and subject to secular change: changing consumer habits around fuel purchase patterns, the rise of delivery services, and the consolidation of the industry into larger chains all affect the viability of Getty's tenant base. During transitions—such as a shift from traditional fuel station economics to electric vehicle infrastructure—real estate operators face changing demand, and smaller, undiversified tenants may struggle to adapt.

## Capital Deployment and Acquisition Strategy

As a REIT, Getty must maintain disciplined capital allocation. The company acquires properties at yields that exceed its cost of capital and the dividend it must pay to shareholders. In a low-interest-rate environment, there is intense competition among REITs to acquire properties, which can drive valuations up and yields down, making it harder to find compelling [acquisitions](/acquisition/). Conversely, in high-rate environments, fewer competitors are active, and sellers may be more willing to negotiate, creating opportunities to acquire at higher yields.

Getty's acquisition strategy focuses on identifying convenience store and fuel properties that offer sustainable rent coverage (where tenant revenue is sufficiently above rent payments to allow for operational profitability and reinvestment). Properties with tight coverage ratios are riskier, as a small drop in tenant sales can quickly impair the ability to pay rent.

## Economic Sensitivity and Recession Risk

Getty's performance is tied to consumer [discretionary spending](/discretionary-spending/) and commuting patterns. During recessions, fuel consumption declines, convenience store traffic falls, and operators' profitability shrinks. This can lead to rent-collection challenges, vacancy, and write-downs in property values. Conversely, strong economic growth and high fuel prices can enhance tenant profitability and support higher rent. Getty is also sensitive to interest-rate cycles: as rates rise, the discounted value of future rent streams falls, which can depress REIT valuations despite stable underlying operations.

The shift toward electric vehicles and autonomous driving represents a longer-term structural risk. If fuel consumption continues to decline over decades, and if convenience stores increasingly depend on fuel traffic, the viability of traditional fuel station real estate is uncertain. Properties that are located primarily to serve fuel customers may eventually become stranded assets, worth less than their current basis.

## Dividend Policy and Capital Returns

Getty operates under REIT tax rules, which require the company to distribute a minimum of 90% of taxable income as [dividends](/dividend/) to shareholders. This creates a high [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/), which is attractive to income-focused investors, but it also constrains the company's ability to retain capital for aggressive growth or debt repayment. Getty's dividend is supported by the rent collected from tenants; if rent collection deteriorates, the company may face pressure to reduce the dividend or secure external financing, both of which are value-destructive to shareholders.
