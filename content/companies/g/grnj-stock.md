*The **Fundstrat Granny Shots US Small- & Mid-Cap ETF** (GRNJ) is an exchange-traded fund holding smaller and medium-sized US companies selected for financial quality and dividend-paying capacity, offering income and stability from a more narrow slice of the US equity market than large-cap peers.*

### What companies does GRNJ actually hold?

GRNJ owns publicly traded US companies below the largest tier — firms typically with [market capitalizations](/market-capitalization/) in the hundreds of millions to tens of billions of dollars. These are the businesses you might not recognize on sight but encounter often: regional banks, local utility companies, mid-sized industrial manufacturers, healthcare service providers, and small consumer brands. The fund holds dozens of such companies, selected not for growth potential but for financial strength and [dividend](/dividend/) payments. These are established, profitable businesses, not startups or turnaround situations.

### How is GRNJ different from buying a small-cap index fund?

A pure small-cap [index fund](/index-fund/) holds most or all publicly traded small and mid-cap companies, weighted by market value. That includes fragile firms, heavily indebted companies, firms with shaky profitability, and others that might be risky. GRNJ applies quality filters: it favors companies with high returns on equity, strong cash generation, sustainable [balance sheets](/balance-sheet/), and dividends that can be maintained. The result is a narrower portfolio of the strongest small and mid-cap firms, not a broad sample of all of them. You sacrifice some [diversification](/diversification/) to gain stability.

### Why would an investor choose smaller companies over large ones?

Large-cap stocks tend to dominate investment conversations, but small and mid-cap companies historically have returned more over long periods, partly because they carry higher risk but also because they have more room to grow. A small company that doubles in revenue is more plausible than [Microsoft](/msft-stock/) or [Apple](/aapl-stock/) doubling again. That growth potential attracts growth-oriented investors. GRNJ, however, does not rely on growth; it relies on quality and income. An investor in GRNJ is betting that smaller companies can still generate reliable dividends and hold their value, even if they do not grow into behemoths.

### Does GRNJ pay higher dividends than large-cap income funds?

Often, yes. Smaller companies, particularly those with limited reinvestment opportunities, tend to pay out a higher percentage of earnings as dividends. GRNJ's yield — the annual dividend payout divided by the fund's price — is typically higher than a large-cap dividend fund. However, small-cap companies are also more vulnerable to economic downturns, so those higher yields carry more risk. A [recession](/recession/) can force a small-cap company to cut its dividend, whereas a large, diversified conglomerate might absorb the downturn more easily. The higher yield is compensation for higher risk.

### What is the volatility like?

Smaller companies are volatile. Their stock prices swing more dramatically than large-cap stocks in response to economic news, earnings surprises, or sector trends. GRNJ's quality filters help — profitable, debt-light companies with reliable dividends tend to hold up better than speculative small caps in downturns. But GRNJ will still be more volatile than a large-cap dividend fund or an all-market index. If daily price swings trouble you, GRNJ is not the fund. If you can tolerate volatility in exchange for potentially higher long-term returns and income, it is worth considering.

### What sectors dominate GRNJ?

The Fundstrat methodology naturally tilts GRNJ toward sectors where small and mid-cap companies pay reliable dividends: regional financial institutions, utilities and utility infrastructure, energy, consumer staples, real-estate investment trusts, and healthcare services. Growth-heavy sectors like technology are underrepresented because small software or hardware companies typically reinvest all earnings and pay no dividends. This sector skew means GRNJ's returns can diverge sharply from the overall small-cap index during periods when technology or other growth sectors outperform.

### How much does it cost to own GRNJ?

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is modest, typical of index-based strategies. GRNJ does not employ active managers; it follows systematic rules, so costs stay low. Turnover (buying and selling within the portfolio) depends on how fast companies move in and out of the quality and dividend criteria, but in stable markets it is relatively low. Transaction costs are negligible given the liquidity of small and mid-cap stocks.

### How does GRNJ perform in different economic environments?

In expansions when the economy is growing and corporate earnings rising, smaller companies often outpace larger ones, so GRNJ can deliver strong returns. In recessions when companies cut costs and cancel dividends, GRNJ can struggle — its quality filters help, but they do not eliminate the downturn. Rising [interest rates](/interest-rate/) tend to hurt dividend-payers since higher [bond](/bond/) yields make stocks less attractive relative to fixed income. Falling rates boost dividend-payers. GRNJ is most attractive to investors with a multi-year horizon who can absorb interim volatility and do not need capital preservation month-to-month.

### How do I research GRNJ before investing?

Start with the fund factsheet and holdings list to see which companies you actually own. Read about small and mid-cap investing: what the risks are, what the historical returns have been, and how small-cap companies behave differently from large ones. Review GRNJ's performance versus a broad small/mid-cap index in multiple market environments. Understand the Fundstrat methodology: which quality metrics matter and why dividend sustainability is assessed as it is. Because smaller companies are less widely covered than large ones, analyst research is thinner — expect less information and more ambiguity in forecasting. That uncertainty is part of the reason for the quality filters: predictable, financially sound small caps are easier to hold confidently than speculative ones.
