Gabelli Equity Trust is a closed-end [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/) trading on the NYSE (ticker: GAB) that holds a concentrated portfolio of stocks chosen by Gabelli Asset Management. The fund exists to provide investors with professional stock selection in a single tradable security, with distributions paid to shareholders and the chance to buy or sell shares at market prices that may diverge from the underlying value of the holdings.

## What exactly is Gabelli Equity Trust?

Gabelli Equity Trust is a closed-end fund, which means it has a fixed number of shares that trade on the [stock exchange](/stock-exchange/) rather than one where you can redeem shares directly from the fund company. The fund was established to give investors access to a concentrated portfolio of stocks selected by the professional investment team at Gabelli Asset Management. The company typically holds 50-80 stocks, a portfolio narrower and more concentrated than a diversified [index fund](/index-fund/) but broader than a highly focused stock-picker's portfolio.

## How does the closed-end structure work?

The fund trades as a stock on the NYSE under the ticker GAB. When you want to own it, you buy shares at the market price — which may be higher or lower than the [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) per share (the underlying value of the stocks it holds divided by shares outstanding). If sentiment toward [closed-end funds](/closed-end-fund/) is positive, GAB might trade at a small premium to its net asset value. If sentiment is negative or if the market worries about the manager's performance, it might trade at a discount. This premium-discount dynamic creates opportunities: you can buy the fund at a discount and benefit if the discount narrows, or buy at a premium and face a headwind if it widens.

## How do distributions work?

Gabelli Equity Trust pays monthly distributions to shareholders, a common practice for closed-end funds seeking to provide regular income. These distributions can come from [stock dividends](/stock-dividend/) in the portfolio, from capital gains realized when the manager sells stocks at a profit, or from return of capital (distributing some of the fund's own principal back to shareholders). A high distribution yield looks attractive, but savvy investors check the composition: a distribution heavy on return of capital indicates the fund is slowly liquidating itself and the yield may not be sustainable long-term.

## What is the investment philosophy?

Gabelli Asset Management, the investment manager, employs a research-intensive approach focused on identifying stocks at attractive valuations. The team looks for securities that the market has mispriced or overlooked, with the goal of holding stocks that compound value over time. The concentrated nature of the portfolio (owning 50-80 stocks rather than 500) means each position is meaningful and the manager's stock-picking ability, or lack thereof, shows up clearly in results.

## What drives performance?

The fund's results depend on how well the Gabelli team selects stocks and on market conditions. If the market favors growth over value, a value-oriented portfolio may underperform. If the market is volatile, a concentrated portfolio may swing more sharply than a diversified index. Active managers like those at Gabelli accept this volatility in hopes of outperforming the index by enough to justify their [management fees](/management-fee/) and to reward shareholders for the risk they take.

## Why would an investor use it instead of buying stocks directly?

A closed-end fund like Gabelli Equity Trust offers [diversification](/diversification/), professional management, and the convenience of a single ticker. An investor who believes Gabelli's stock-picking adds value and who prefers not to manage a large portfolio directly might choose the fund. The downside is management fees, which reduce returns, and the closed-end premium-discount dynamic, which can work for or against you. An investor who buys the fund at a premium to its net asset value is paying extra for the manager; an investor who buys at a discount gets a discount on the underlying holdings.

## How should an investor evaluate Gabelli Equity Trust?

Start by comparing the fund's returns to its benchmarks over multiple time periods — one year, three years, five years, ten years. Does the fund's outperformance, after fees, justify the active management approach? Watch the net asset value per share over time to see if the underlying portfolio is compounding in value. Check the distribution composition to see what portion is from earnings versus return of capital. Monitor the premium or discount to net asset value — a persistent large discount might indicate the market has lost faith in the manager, while a premium indicates the opposite. Finally, understand your own time horizon: if you plan to hold the fund for many years, the long-term performance and fee structure matter most; if you are trading it shorter-term, the closed-end premium-discount dynamics become more significant.
