> A fund that walks the line between an equity holding and a gold hedge, by putting both in the same box.

WisdomTree's GDE is a hybrid fund that does something unusual: it merges a large-cap U.S. equity portfolio with short-term gold [futures contracts](/futures-contract/), all inside one wrapper. The result is leverage—not the kind that balloons at a single moment, but structural leverage baked into the design.

Here is how it works. The fund invests approximately 90 dollars of every 100 dollars of assets in a representative basket of large-cap U.S. equities. That basket is market-cap-weighted, so the largest companies like [Nvidia](/nvda-stock/), [Apple](/aapl-stock/), [Alphabet](/googl-stock/), and [Microsoft](/msft-stock/) carry the largest weights. Alongside that, the fund uses the remaining funds plus financial leverage to maintain gold futures exposure roughly equal to the stock exposure in dollar terms. The result: roughly 180 dollars of total exposure per 100 dollars of assets—about 90 in stocks and 90 in gold.

The appeal is rooted in a historical observation: gold and stocks have had a low historical correlation. When equities decline sharply, gold often rises or holds steady, providing a natural hedge. When stocks rally, gold may lag but the equity gains dominate. By holding both simultaneously, GDE offers investors a way to own stocks while tempering some of the downside risk through gold's uncorrelated behavior. The leverage amplifies both upside and downside, which is the tradeoff built into the structure.

Cyclically, the fund behaves in interesting ways. In a [bull market](/bull-market/) for equities with stable or falling gold prices, the equity component powers returns while gold adds drag. The leverage magnifies the equity wins. In a sharp equity correction where investors flee to gold, the fund can capture both the gold appreciation (which would normally be a hedge) and the equity decline (which is what you are hedging against). That is a loss in both directions—the worst case. But in a [stagflation](/stagflation/) scenario where equities decline and gold rallies sharply, the two components can partially offset each other, reducing the fund's downside relative to a pure-equity fund.

The fund's structure means it is highly sensitive to the day-to-day prices of gold futures. Gold itself is volatile—price swings of 10 to 20 percent within a few months are not unusual. A fund that holds a huge gold futures position will experience large percentage movements as those futures prices change. The leverage amplifies this further. This makes GDE a volatile, active-tilting fund rather than a passive hold-forever instrument.

The use of futures also introduces complications that simple [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/) do not face. Futures contracts roll regularly—the fund must continuously shift from one contract month to another, and the cost or benefit of rolling depends on the futures curve structure. In [contango](/contango/) (where [forward contracts](/forward-contract/) cost more), rolling incurs a cost. In [backwardation](/backwardation/) (where forward contracts cost less), rolling is actually a hidden gain. These mechanical forces affect the fund's performance in ways unrelated to the actual price movements of stock and gold markets.

There is also leverage decay to consider. In any fund using financial leverage, if stocks and gold move in opposite directions on any given day, the compounding of gains and losses can cause the fund to underperform what a simple formula would predict over longer periods. This is the same force that makes leveraged equity ETFs drift from their target over time. It is not a bug in the fund's operation; it is inherent to leverage itself.

The fund makes sense for sophisticated investors who understand leverage and want explicit exposure to a stock-plus-gold mixture. It is not appropriate as a simple core holding, and it is not a way to "get [diversification](/diversification/)" without active thought. Its volatility profile is substantially higher than either stocks or gold alone.

For research, investors should read WisdomTree's fund summary, which explains the exact allocation rules and gold futures mechanics. The prospectus covers the risks of leverage, the handling of rolling futures, and how performance can drift from a simple mathematical formula over time. Comparing the fund's returns to a 90-stock-plus-10-gold-equivalent portfolio shows how much leverage decay and futures mechanics matter in real performance.
