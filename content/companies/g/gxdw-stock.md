*The **Global X Dorsey Wright Thematic ETF** (NASDAQ: GXDW) is a fund-of-funds that invests in a collection of thematic ETFs instead of individual securities. Rather than picking stocks, the fund bets on broad structural trends — artificial intelligence, e-commerce, clean energy, demographics — and rotates capital among them based on a momentum-ranking system that identifies which themes are gaining strength.*

## Structure and strategy

GXDW operates as a meta-fund: it holds six underlying Global X thematic ETFs and rebalances them monthly according to Nasdaq Dorsey Wright's Relative Strength methodology. That methodology ranks themes by momentum, selecting the five with the strongest recent performance and weighting them accordingly. When a theme falls out of the top five, GXDW rotates the capital elsewhere.

This creates a dynamic portfolio that is not fixed to any permanent set of holdings. The fund might be heavily weighted toward artificial intelligence in one month and global infrastructure in the next, depending on how each theme's equity constituents have performed relative to peers. The idea is to ride trends while they have momentum and exit before they fade.

The fund has been active since October 2019, giving it a six-year track record in a period of rapid technological and structural change. It launched as Global X's vehicle for investors who wanted thematic exposure but lacked conviction about which specific themes would outperform.

## The underlying holdings

The fund's portfolio comprises only six securities — the top five Global X thematic ETFs ranked by momentum, plus some cash or cash equivalents. At any point, the holdings might include:

**Global X Artificial Intelligence & Technology ETF (AIQ)** — exposure to companies developing and deploying AI systems across hardware, software, and cloud infrastructure.

**Global X Social Media ETF** — companies monetizing social engagement, from platforms to advertising technology to creators' infrastructure.

**Global X E-Commerce ETF** — retailers and technology platforms enabling online commerce, logistics, and fulfillment.

**Global X U.S. Infrastructure Development ETF** — companies building and maintaining U.S. infrastructure projects, from utilities to engineering firms.

**Global X Millennials Consumer ETF** — companies serving the consumption patterns, preferences, and purchasing habits of younger demographics.

These examples change month to month as the ranking algorithm reorders them. The only constant is that GXDW holds the top five, weighted by their ranking scores, until the next monthly rebalancing.

## The momentum methodology

Nasdaq Dorsey Wright's Relative Strength system is not a mean-reversion strategy betting on comebacks. It is momentum-based: it assumes that themes showing strength will continue to do so until evidence suggests otherwise. The ranking looks at price trends, relative performance, and perhaps other technical factors — the exact proprietary formula is not disclosed to investors — and updates monthly.

This approach has a controversial implication: it frequently rotates capital from laggard themes to winners. In market rotations where laggards rebound sharply, this can create drag as GXDW sells just as a theme begins to recover. Conversely, in sustained trends where winners keep winning, GXDW rides them more efficiently than a static thematic allocation would.

The monthly rebalancing is frequent enough to stay nimble but not so frequent that transaction costs become punishing.

## Who it is for

GXDW appeals to investors who believe disruptive structural trends will drive returns over the next decade but lack conviction about the order or timing of those trends. Rather than committing to a single theme like AI or clean energy, GXDW lets the momentum system decide which themes are leading at any moment.

It also suits investors who view thematic investing as a satellite allocation — a smaller position meant to capture growth upside — rather than a core holding. The fund's simplicity (six holdings) and transparency (holdings change monthly, reported to investors) make it easy to monitor and understand.

GXDW is less suitable for buy-and-hold investors seeking stability. Monthly rebalancing creates turnover, tax drag in taxable accounts, and periods of underperformance when the fund exits themes just as they begin rallies. It is also inefficient for those with strong conviction in a single theme; a dedicated theme ETF would be more direct.

## The costs and trade-offs

The fund's expense ratio reflects its role as a fund-of-funds: it pays the fees of the underlying ETFs (each of which carries its own expense ratio in the 0.70%–1.00% range) plus a management fee. The all-in cost is typically in the 0.80%–1.20% range. That is higher than a broad passive index fund (0.03%–0.10%) but reasonable for a dynamically managed thematic strategy.

The real cost beyond the expense ratio is opportunity cost. By rotating out of a theme early, GXDW captures gains on the way up but misses the final leg when late-comers pile in. By rotating in to a theme late (when it has gained momentum), GXDW sometimes buys near peaks, only to see the theme stall. Over long periods, the realized cost of this timing is unpredictable and hard to hedge.

## Concentration and risk

With only six holdings (five thematic ETFs plus cash), GXDW is concentrated. The top ten holdings of the underlying ETFs constitute 99.9% of the fund's assets — meaning it holds a narrow slice of the thematic universe. This concentration delivers powerful upside when the highest-ranked themes deliver; it also means sharp underperformance if the momentum signal breaks and those themes fall abruptly.

The diversification claim — exposure to multiple themes — is only as good as the themes' correlation to one another. If all five themes move together during a market downturn, they will all decline together, negating the diversification benefit.

## How to research GXDW

Start with Global X's documentation on the fund structure, the Nasdaq Dorsey Wright Relative Strength methodology (published on Nasdaq's website), and the holdings of each underlying thematic ETF. Track GXDW's monthly rebalancing history over the past year to understand how frequently it rotates and what themes it favors. Compare GXDW's returns to the average return of all Global X thematic ETFs over the same period — if GXDW materially outperforms, the momentum rotation added value; if it underperforms, the timing cost money.

Examine GXDW during periods of thematic rotation (like 2022, when growth fell sharply) to see whether the momentum system effectively exited declining themes or held them during drawdowns. Past behavior is not a promise, but it reveals the system's real-world timing precision.
