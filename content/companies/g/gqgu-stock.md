*The **GQG US [Equity ETF](/equity-etf/)** (GQGU) is an [actively managed fund](/actively-managed-fund/) of large-cap American stocks, built by GQG Partners—an independent Australian asset manager with a philosophy of patient, [fundamental investing](/fundamental-investing/) focused on businesses trading below [intrinsic value](/intrinsic-value/).*

> The fund's bet is that careful stock-picking by experienced analysts beats the market, and that most investors would benefit from owning a disciplined selection of quality US companies rather than chasing momentum or style tilts.

## What GQG brings to the table

GQG Partners was founded in 2012 by Rajiv Jain, a veteran value investor, and operates with a culture of long-term, off-the-run thinking. The firm manages money for institutions and individuals globally. GQGU is its US equity vehicle—a window into how the firm approaches American stocks. The fund's philosophy rests on three pillars: buying quality businesses (those with sustainable competitive advantages, strong [balance sheets](/balance-sheet/), predictable cash flows), paying a discount to intrinsic value (waiting for a margin of safety), and holding for years, not quarters. It is the opposite of trend-following or tactical rotation.

The managers spend time learning individual companies. They visit management teams, study industry structures, model long-term competitive positioning. The fund's turnover is low relative to other active funds—holdings are retained unless the fundamental thesis breaks or the stock has appreciated so much that the margin of safety disappears. This patient, research-intensive approach produces high [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/): the fund may have only 40-60 distinct positions, not 100+. Each holding represents a genuine conviction that it is mispriced.

## Performance and the active-management bet

Active management in US equities is notoriously difficult. Most active funds underperform their benchmarks after fees over 10-year periods. GQGU's success depends on whether GQG's fundamental insights produce alpha—excess returns above what an [index fund](/index-fund/) would deliver. The fund's track record will show this clearly. If it has beaten the S&P 500 by 2-3 percentage points annually over a full decade while taking on similar risk, the active fee is justified. If it has merely matched the index, the fee—typically higher than a passive fund—is a drag.

Investors should examine performance across multiple time periods and market regimes. How did GQGU do in the 2020-2021 growth boom, when value lagged badly? How did it perform in the 2022 downturn? Did the discipline of owning quality at discounts provide downside protection? These context-specific questions matter more than a single number.

## The value-tilted anchor

GQGU is implicitly a value-tilted fund. It favors companies trading below book value, low price-to-earnings multiples, strong [free cash flow](/free-cash-flow/) relative to price. That tilt means the fund often looks cheap relative to growth-focused indices. In eras when value dramatically outperforms (2022-2023), GQGU looks prescient. In eras when growth crushes value (2016-2021), the fund looks stodgy. Investors must be comfortable with that cyclicality. GQGU is not a core equity holding for someone who wants broad market exposure with no style tilt. It is a deliberate bet that value and quality will outperform over the long haul.

## Concentration and downside risk

The fund's limited number of holdings means each position materially affects returns. If the fund's largest holding—say, a major bank or industrial manufacturer—suffers a devastating earnings miss, the impact on GQGU's [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) is material and immediate. Passive funds with 500 holdings in the S&P 500 spread that risk thinly. GQGU does not. Concentrated portfolios can outperform, but they also experience larger drawdowns. Investors uncomfortable with that volatility should add ballast—perhaps a core position in a broad index fund—around any GQGU allocation.

## Expense ratio and the fee picture

As an actively managed ETF, GQGU carries an annual [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) meaningfully higher than a passive S&P 500 ETF. That fee is the cost of the research, the management team, and the GQG organization. It is not a hidden drag if the fund is delivering alpha, but it is a hurdle that must be cleared. Over a 20-year [holding period](/holding-period/), a 0.5 percent annual fee difference compounds significantly. Verify the current expense ratio on GQG's website and compare it against competing active US equity ETFs or against the annual cost of a passive approach plus the expected return premium from active stock-picking.

## Cyclical positioning and market regime

The fund's value orientation means it often carries significant allocations to economically sensitive sectors—financials, industrials, energy, consumer discretionary—during periods when those sectors are unfavored. During booms, that positioning pays off; the economy is strong, companies earn profits, and value stocks reward investors. During slowdowns or liquidity crises, the position concentrates losses. GQGU is a bet that over long periods, a diversified portfolio of quality-value businesses will compound faster than the market. But that claim requires patience through multiple cycles.

## For whom is GQGU suitable

GQGU is built for long-term investors with conviction in fundamental stock-picking and comfort with value-style volatility. It suits those willing to hold for 10+ years, unconcerned with quarterly performance, and accepting that some years the fund will lag the S&P 500. It is less suitable for tactical traders, those seeking broad [diversification](/diversification/) with minimal style tilt, or investors uncomfortable with active risk. If GQGU forms part of a larger portfolio—say, 20-30 percent of an equity sleeve, with the rest in passive broad indices—it can add value through differentiation. If it is the entire equity holding, concentration risk is high.

## How to evaluate GQGU

Start with the fund's factsheet and [annual reports](/10-k/) from GQG Partners. Understand the current portfolio composition: which sectors, which countries of domicile, which sizes of companies? How many positions does the fund hold? What is the current price-to-book and [price-to-earnings ratio](/price-to-earnings-ratio/) relative to the S&P 500? Download 10 years of performance data and compare it to the S&P 500 Total Return, including during [bear markets](/bear-market/). Does the outperformance justify the fee? Finally, assess your own capacity to stick with GQGU through a period where value is deeply out of favor. If the thought of seeing the fund underperform the index for two or three years would cause you to bail, GQGU is not your fund.
