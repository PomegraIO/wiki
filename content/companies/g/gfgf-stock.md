*GFGF is a stock-picking ETF based on a simple premise: the investment ideas of talented, experienced money managers contain edge. By tracking what a panel of known investors actually hold in their portfolios, the fund makes a bet that consensus among skilled managers outperforms the broader market.*

## The origin of the guru-stocks idea

The concept of a guru-stock fund sits at the intersection of two observations. First, some professional investors — stock pickers with long track records and significant capital under management — do beat the market with consistency over extended periods. Second, their investment theses are often public: they appear on earnings calls, they publish letters to shareholders, they disclose their portfolio holdings in regulatory filings, and they speak to the financial press.

The idea emerged in the early 2000s that an investor could systematically track the holdings of multiple successful managers and create a portfolio that captures their collective judgment. Rather than trying to beat the market by finding an undiscovered gem, an investor could hold a diversified basket of the ideas that well-known gurus already liked. If each guru has an edge, the reasoning went, a portfolio of their crowded favorites might have an even larger edge.

GFGF follows this model. The fund identifies a panel of respected investment managers or investors whose strategies and ideas are public, examines their disclosed holdings or recommendations, and creates a portfolio that reflects the overlap — the stocks that appear most frequently in the gurus' portfolios.

## How the guru panel is assembled and updated

The specific methodology for selecting which investors qualify as "gurus" and how their holdings are weighted varies by fund and can change over time. Typically, the fund selects investors with:

- A long public track record of outperformance relative to benchmarks
- Significant assets under management or influence
- Fully transparent or regularly disclosed portfolio positions
- A distinctive investment philosophy or style that can be tracked

The fund then monitors their portfolio filings (quarterly or annual regulatory documents) and aggregates their holdings. Stocks that appear across multiple gurus' portfolios are included in the ETF with weights reflecting how many gurus hold them and the size of those positions. The portfolio is rebalanced periodically as the underlying guru holdings change.

## The crowded-trade risk and concentration

The guru-stocks approach inherently creates concentrated positions. If a stock appears in five different gurus' portfolios, it will have a larger weight than a stock held by only one. This concentration can amplify returns when the gurus' picks outperform — but it also concentrates losses when they miss. The portfolio is less diversified than a broad market fund by design.

There is also a subtle crowding risk. If GFGF becomes widely held, large inflows of capital following the same guru ideas can inflate the stocks that gurus like while they are being accumulated, creating a momentum effect that may not persist. When a crowded guru trade falls out of favor, the unwind can be sharp.

The gurus themselves are not immune to mistakes. Even successful long-term investors have periods of underperformance, miss major trends, or find that their proven approach stops working. A guru who crushed the market in a previous decade may simply get old, or the economic environment may shift in a way their approach cannot adapt to.

## Current shape and what to watch

GFGF's current composition should be checked against the fund's fact sheet or holdings list, which will show the largest positions and the gurus whose picks dominate. The fund's performance relative to the broader market reveals whether the collective wisdom of the panel is adding value in the current environment.

An investor considering GFGF should understand that the fund's returns depend heavily on whether the selected gurus are currently in favor. In eras when [value investing](/value-investing/) and old-school stock-picking style outperform, gurus with those philosophies will drive strong results. In eras when momentum and growth stocks dominate, a guru-focused fund may lag. The fund works best for an investor who has conviction that skilled active managers will outperform and who can tolerate periods of underperformance when the gurus' style is out of favor.

## Research and due diligence

Readers interested in GFGF should review the [fund prospectus](/fund-prospectus/) to understand which gurus are in the panel, how often the portfolio is updated, how holdings are weighted, and whether there are any exclusions or overrides to the methodology. The fact sheet should show the current top holdings and which gurus appear most frequently in the portfolio.

A useful exercise is to examine the fund's performance during the periods when the chosen gurus did well and when they underperformed, and to compare it to the performance of a simple equal-weight portfolio of those same gurus' disclosed holdings. The gap reveals whether the fund's methodology is capturing value or whether there are implementation costs that erode the gurus' edge.
