*The **Gotham Enhanced 500 ETF** — ticker GSPY — is an exchange-traded fund that holds a slimmed-down portfolio of roughly 300 stocks drawn from the S&P 500, selected using a quantitative screening process designed to favor durable businesses at reasonable valuations. It aims to outperform the broad index by applying a discipline that much passive indexing does not: explicit filters for financial health, [earnings quality](/earnings-quality/), and valuation discipline.*

## The philosophy behind the portfolio

Most [index funds](/index-fund/) simply buy every stock in their target index, weighted by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/). GSPY does not. Instead, it begins with the S&P 500 and applies a series of quantitative filters — rules derived from academic research and the fund managers' own analysis of what separates long-term winners from mediocre businesses. The screens look at balance-sheet strength, the quality and durability of earnings, return on capital, and valuation metrics like [earnings yield](/earnings-yield/) and book-to-market ratio. Stocks that pass the filters go into the portfolio; those that fail are excluded.

The result is a portfolio that is still broadly diversified across the index's most substantial companies — it overlaps heavily with the core holdings of the S&P 500 — but with a tilt toward quality and value. It will tend to own more companies that generate high returns on invested capital and fewer of those trading at extreme valuations. Gotham's philosophy is that markets occasionally misprice durability, and that a disciplined screen can capture that mispricing at a price the investor can afford.

## How it differs from plain indexing

A cap-weighted [S&P 500 index](/sp-500-index/) fund owns every constituent in proportion to its market value. That means the largest and often most expensive stocks get the largest weightings, simply by virtue of being the most valuable. GSPY, by filtering for quality and value characteristics, deliberately overweights smaller members of the index and underweights mega-cap glamour names. This is not a market-cap index; it is a hand-selected subset.

The practical consequence is that GSPY will sometimes materially lag the broad market when investors are willing to pay extraordinary premiums for growth and intangible brand value — the kind of environment that has favored the largest technology companies. In periods when the market reprices toward financial health, competitive advantage, and reasonable valuations, GSPY's tilts tend to add value. Over longer periods the outcome depends partly on whether the factors Gotham screens for — balance-sheet quality, earnings durability, return on capital — actually persist as sources of outperformance.

## Costs and transparency

As an ETF, GSPY trades throughout the day on a [stock exchange](/stock-exchange/) like any ordinary stock, and its [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) is transparent and updated in real time. The fund discloses its holdings daily, so investors always know what they own. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is modest — lower than most actively managed equity funds but higher than the cheapest cap-weighted index funds, reflecting the cost of the screening research and daily rebalancing. Still, the cost is narrow enough that even modest outperformance would make up for it; the question is whether that outperformance materializes consistently or only occasionally.

Inflows and outflows into GSPY create natural rebalancing as the fund buys and sells to stay invested, but the fund's size and the liquidity of its underlying stocks mean this is typically efficient. Investors can buy and sell GSPY at minimal spreads throughout the trading day.

## The real risks

GSPY's screening philosophy is sound, but it is not a guarantee. One risk is **[factor crowding](/factor-crowding/)** — if many other funds and strategies also pursue quality and value tilts, those factors may become crowded, and mean reversion can be sharp when the market turns. A second is **selection risk**: Gotham's quantitative model relies on the assumption that the factors it screens for will persist and will not be arbitraged away. If capital market efficiency improves or if business conditions change in ways the model did not anticipate, the outperformance could shrink.

A third, less obvious risk is **concentration drift**. Although GSPY starts with the S&P 500's broad base, the filtering process inevitably concentrates the portfolio — fewer than 300 of 500 stocks means some sectors or industries might be underrepresented if many of their constituents fail the quality screen. This can leave the fund exposed to style or sector bets it did not intend to make.

Finally, there is the **index overlap question**: because GSPY holds many of the same stocks as the plain S&P 500 index, much of its performance will move in line with the broad market anyway. Investors seeking a strong conviction tilt to quality and value might find that a factor-focused fund from a dedicated quant shop offers a clearer expression of that view. GSPY is better understood as a quality-tilted large-cap core holding than as a dramatic departure from traditional indexing.

## How to use and research it

GSPY is most useful for investors who believe that financial health and reasonable valuation matter — who want some of that discipline but within the comfort zone of the S&P 500's largest companies. It is not a substitute for the broad index, and it is not designed as a concentrated bet on a single factor. Rather, it is a hybrid: indexing's breadth with a modest tilt toward what Gotham's research suggests are the highest-quality businesses at the best prices the market is offering.

Prospective investors should review the fund's prospectus and the Gotham website for detail on exactly which screens are applied and how often they are refreshed. The published fact sheet breaks down the portfolio by sector and style characteristics, making it easy to see how tilted it is toward value, growth, momentum, or other dimensions relative to the market cap-weighted index. Looking at rolling multi-year performance relative to the S&P 500 shows whether the strategy is delivering the intended edge, and in which market environments it shines or struggles.
