GDXY pursues a similar option-income strategy to GDXW but through a different sponsor and with distinct structural choices. Sponsored by YieldMax, a firm focused on options-enhanced strategies, the fund seeks monthly distributions by systematically selling [covered calls](/covered-call/) against a carefully constructed portfolio of global gold mining companies. While GDXW focuses on weekly cycles, GDXY structures its option writing on a monthly basis, targeting large-cap, mid-cap, and select small-cap gold producers.

## The portfolio and the income engine

GDXY holds between 40 and 60 mining companies selected from both established producers and emerging developers. The fund weights positions based not purely on [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) but on a proprietary methodology that considers production scale, reserve quality, [dividend](/dividend/) history, and balance-sheet strength. This means larger, more established miners like Newmont and Barrick Gold may weigh heavily, but the fund is not purely a cap-weighted tracker like GDX.

Each month, the fund's managers sell [call options](/call-option/) on the entire portfolio, collecting premiums in exchange for capping upside if mining stocks rally. Unlike the weekly rhythm of GDXW, the monthly cycle matches the way many options-income strategies operate in institutional portfolios. One-month expirations are a natural sweet spot: premiums are meaningful but not tiny, and the management overhead is lower than weekly rebalancing. The collected premiums are distributed to shareholders monthly, creating a steady cash-flow stream.

The fund's strategy assumes that monthly volatility in mining equities will continue, that call strikes can be set to balance income with acceptable upside capture, and that the underlying miners will not experience a violent sustained rally that renders the income collection worthless. Over extended sideways or slowly rising markets, this machinery delivers high distributions. Over strong [bull markets](/bull-market/), it collages returns.

## Differences from GDXW

The most obvious difference is timing: GDXY's one-month call-writing cycle versus GDXW's one-week cycle. The practical consequence is that GDXY collects larger individual premiums (a full month's volatility premium rather than a week's) but less frequently. A monthly distribution means less income friction—fewer tax events, lower operational overhead, and potentially wider call strikes, giving more room for the underlying portfolio to appreciate before upside is capped.

The second difference is portfolio construction. GDXY explicitly includes large-cap integrated miners, accepting that the underlying holdings will be less volatile but more stable. This makes the fund somewhat less aggressive than GDXW, which may tilt more toward mid-cap and small-cap developers. For an investor seeking option-income exposure but less willing to accept the swings of junior-focused strategies, GDXY's composition is more conservative.

Third, the sponsor's proprietary approach to call-strike selection may differ. YieldMax and Roundhill (GDXW's sponsor) have different algorithms and market views on how far out-of-the-money to sell the calls. This affects both the probability of the shares being called away and the level of income relative to upside captured.

## The distribution story

GDXY's monthly distributions are typically in the 1 to 2 percent range per month, or 12 to 24 percent annualized, depending on market conditions and volatility levels. That is substantially higher than the [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/) of the underlying miners, which often sit in the 2 to 4 percent range. The difference is the [option premium](/option-premium/). During high-volatility periods (market panic, mining-sector concern), option premiums are fat, and distributions spike. During calm periods, premiums shrink, and distributions compress. The fund's distribution is not stable—it fluctuates with mining volatility.

## Tax and capital-gains considerations

Because the fund is writing and rolling options every month, realized gains and losses accumulate throughout the year. When a covered call is exercised (shares are called away), a capital gain is typically realized—the difference between the purchase price of the mining stock and the call strike. These gains are short-term if the underlying stock was held for less than a year, resulting in ordinary-income tax rates. In a taxable account, this compounds the tax hit: high distributions plus frequent short-term gains can result in a surprisingly large tax bill despite modest price appreciation.

In tax-deferred accounts, this structure is far less costly. A retiree in an IRA can collect high distributions without year-end tax surprises. For taxable investors, GDXY's efficiency is lower because of the tax drag.

## Risks and limitations

The core risk is identical to any covered-call strategy: **upside cap**. GDXY shareholders do not fully participate if gold and mining equities enter a powerful bull market. The fund is designed for sideways or modestly appreciating markets, not for strong advances.

Second, **distribution sustainability** is not guaranteed. If gold prices collapse, mining companies lose profitability, dividend payments fall, and mining volatility spikes. In a panic, option premiums may spike initially, but they collapse quickly as sellers pull bids and liquidity dries up. The fund's distribution could suddenly shrivel or even generate losses if the underlying holdings decline sharply.

Third, **[concentration risk](/concentration-risk/)**: even with 40–60 holdings, gold mining is a single-commodity, single-sector exposure. A sustained [bear market](/bear-market/) for gold or a structural shift away from gold investment (rising [real interest rates](/real-interest-rate/), strong dollar, crypto adoption) would impair the entire portfolio simultaneously. There is no [diversification](/diversification/) across asset classes, and the options strategy does not hedge this sector risk—it only moderates it somewhat by accelerating distributions in down markets.

## Who GDXY suits

GDXY appeals to investors seeking current income who are willing to cap upside in exchange for distributions that exceed what the underlying miners pay. It suits tactical positions—an investor who is bullish on gold but not betting on a dramatic rally in the next year or two. It suits taxable-deferred accounts more than taxable ones. And it appeals to those who understand options and are comfortable with the idea that the distributions they receive today are extracted from the potential gains tomorrow.

It does not suit buy-and-hold investors, growth-focused portfolios, or those betting on a gold bull market. And it is not appropriate for investors who are not comfortable with the options-pricing mechanics or who cannot tolerate the volatility of option-income strategies.

## How to research GDXY

Review the fund's fact sheet and prospectus, which detail the portfolio and the monthly distribution history. Compare one-year and three-year annualized returns against GDX and GDXW to see how the strategy performs across market conditions. Look at the rolling distributions over time—spikes and crashes tell you about the underlying volatility and the sustainability of income. Examine the largest holdings to understand the quality and diversification of the portfolio. And always ask whether the monthly distribution is enough to justify capping upside in a gold bull market scenario.
