*The **Twin Oak Endure ETF** (ticker SPYA) holds a portfolio of large and mid-cap U.S. stocks filtered for characteristics that have historically indicated durability and lower price swings. The fund uses quality and dividend-stability screens to build its index, aiming to deliver broad U.S. equity exposure without the highest-volatility names. It is designed for investors seeking stock-market returns with less day-to-day turbulence.*

## What makes a stock "enduring"

Twin Oak, the fund's sponsor, starts with the universe of large and mid-cap U.S. companies, then applies a multi-step filter. First, it ranks candidates by [dividend](/dividend/) consistency — companies that have paid stable or growing dividends over multiple years score higher than those with erratic or no dividend histories. Second, it screens for quality indicators: companies with strong [balance sheets](/balance-sheet/), steady earnings, and reasonable valuation multiples get preference over firms with leverage, volatile earnings, or stretched prices. Third, it excludes or downweights extreme outliers — the most volatile names and the highest flyers that tend to crash hardest. The result is an index that holds roughly 300–400 names across most of the S&P 500 categories but tilted toward firms that have historically been more stable.

This is not guaranteed to deliver lower volatility. The fund is still holding stocks, which move. But the screening methodology has historically tilted holdings toward companies less prone to 20–30% drawdowns in routine market corrections, which is the real appeal. An investor in SPYA is trading potential upside (stocks with the highest volatility sometimes deliver the highest returns) for reduced downside turbulence.

## How SPYA performs in different markets

When the broader market rallies steadily, SPYA typically lags slightly, because the most volatile, high-growth names often drive the big runs. When the market corrects sharply, SPYA tends to hold up better because it is tilted away from the names that get crushed fastest. Over a full [market cycle](/market-cycle/) that includes both rallies and corrections, the dividend-and-quality tilt has historically delivered competitive returns with lower peak-to-trough declines. This is valuable for investors with low risk tolerance or those approaching retirement and uncomfortable with dramatic swings.

The flip side is that screens like these can become crowded. If many investors are chasing dividend-paying, high-quality companies, those names can trade at premium valuations, making them less attractive as new purchases. This crowding effect is invisible to a fund holder but can create periods where dividend-quality strategies underperform the broader market by wider margins than history would suggest.

## Easy to understand and own

SPYA trades on [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) with strong daily volume and tight [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/). The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is low, typically in the 0.2–0.4% range per year, reflecting the passive indexing approach. The fund rebalances quarterly to stay aligned with its underlying index, which means holdings and weightings are relatively stable — the fund is not churning in and out of names constantly.

For an investor exploring SPYA, start by understanding what dividend-stability and quality screening mean in practice: read the fund's methodology document to see exactly how Twin Oak ranks and selects names. Then ask whether the higher stability appeals to your financial situation. If you are young, have a long time horizon, and can stomach big market swings, SPYA offers no advantage over a simple broad-market [index fund](/index-fund/) — and might actually underperform if volatility-tilted names rally hard. If you are near retirement, hold a large concentrated position that you cannot easily diversify, or have emotional difficulty watching your portfolio drop 25–30% in a correction, SPYA is worth considering despite its slightly higher cost and potential for lagging in strong rallies.
