Strive, Inc. is a Dallas-based corporate treasury and structured finance company that operates through two distinct segments: asset management services and cryptocurrency accumulation via preferred equity. The company has become known for an unusual capital structure that issues publicly traded preferred stock to fund large bitcoin purchases, creating a bridge between traditional financial markets and digital assets. What distinguishes Strive from conventional corporate treasuries is the transparency and frequency of its dividend distributions tied to bitcoin appreciation and the innovative use of liquid preferred equity as a mechanism for retail and institutional investors to gain leveraged exposure to bitcoin accumulation.

## Asset Management Operations

Strive's asset management segment operates through Strive Asset Management, LLC, a Securities and Exchange Commission-registered investment adviser. The subsidiary manages assets across approximately thirteen exchange-traded funds, collective investment trusts, and a direct indexing platform. The firm offers a range of products including specialized equity strategies and index-tracking vehicles, positioning itself within the broader landscape of institutional asset managers. This segment provides the operational foundation and regulatory standing that enables Strive to execute large, coordinated bitcoin transactions and justify the corporate treasury operations that follow. The asset management operations generate recurring management fees, but the strategic heart of the company lies in the capital raised through its preferred equity instrument rather than traditional advisory revenue.

## The SATA Preferred Equity Instrument

SATA is a Variable Rate Series A Perpetual Preferred Stock that trades publicly on financial exchanges, making it a liquid security accessible to retail investors. The instrument was designed to solve a capital-accumulation problem: how to raise large amounts of cash for bitcoin purchases without incurring debt at traditional interest rates. Instead of borrowing through corporate bonds or bank lines, Strive issues SATA at a stated annual dividend rate—initially 13 percent, with distributions paid daily rather than quarterly, which means the dividend compounds more frequently than conventional preferred shares. The daily payment structure creates a mechanically appealing feature: holders receive income even if they hold the shares for just a few trading days, attracting short-term traders alongside longer-term holders.

The perpetual structure means SATA has no maturity date and no obligation for Strive to repurchase the shares at face value. Dividends are paid in cash from corporate coffers, and the redemption scenario remains uncertain, which introduces equity-like characteristics despite the name "preferred." This hybrid quality—part debt-like stability, part equity-like permanence—is where Strive's moat rests. Investors who hold SATA do not directly own bitcoin, but their dividends and any eventual capital appreciation depend on Strive's ability to sustain and grow its bitcoin holdings without undue financial strain.

## Bitcoin Accumulation Strategy

The core function of Strive's structure is to accumulate bitcoin at scale while distributing the economic benefits through SATA dividends and the appreciation of the underlying bitcoin treasure. Proceeds from SATA offerings and other capital-raising activities fund large direct purchases of bitcoin in the open market. The company has demonstrated the ability to purchase such large volumes that it has occasionally exceeded the daily bitcoin mining supply in a single trading session, signaling the scale at which Strive operates. These purchases are held in custody arrangements, typically with regulated exchanges and custodians, to maintain security and regulatory compliance.

The strategy is openly acknowledged as a bet on bitcoin's long-term appreciation. Unlike a traditional corporation that might hold bitcoin as a hedge or treasury reserve, Strive positions bitcoin accumulation as the core value-creation mechanism. The moat here is not superior mining, technology, or operational efficiency—it is the structural ability to raise capital at favorable terms through preferred equity and deploy it faster and at larger scale than competitors using conventional debt or equity. As long as bitcoin continues to exist and trade on public markets, and as long as market participants believe Strive can manage the capital structure without catastrophic failure, the preferred shares have a claim on future bitcoin appreciation.

## Capital Structure and Risk

Strive's capital stack includes not only preferred equity but also various forms of debt and convertible securities that the company has retired over time as it has raised fresh capital. The company has used proceeds from SATA issuances to retire convertible notes and repay loans taken from cryptocurrency lending platforms. This rebalancing is crucial: as long as SATA capital is deployed to buy unencumbered bitcoin while debt is retired, the bitcoin holdings become increasingly equity-like rather than mortgaged.

The primary risk is straightforward: if bitcoin declines substantially in value, the bitcoin holdings are worth less, and SATA dividend sustainability becomes questionable. Strive has limited operating revenue outside the investment advisory segment, so a sharp crypto downturn without corresponding rises in assets under management in the advisory business could force difficult dividend cuts or capital raises at unfavorable prices. The perpetual nature of SATA also means there is no natural redemption pressure or forced maturity to force resolution of this risk—instead, holders face the risk of indefinite low or zero dividend payments if the underlying bitcoin fails to appreciate or declines.

## How to Research Strive

Start with the company's annual filings and quarterly reports on the SEC's EDGAR system using CIK 0001920406, which will detail the bitcoin holdings, the size of SATA issuance, the composition of assets under management, and the treasury operations. The quarterly earnings releases disclose the bitcoin purchase volumes, the market price at which acquisitions occurred, and any commentary on capital deployment plans. Watch closely for any indication that the company is shifting from bitcoin accumulation into other asset classes or scaling back purchase activity—such a shift would signal that management no longer views bitcoin appreciation as the dominant value driver.

For SATA specifically, track the daily dividend payments relative to the initial stated rate, the share price, and the implied yield. If dividends are cut or suspended, SATA holders will face immediate capital loss and likely refinancing challenges. Also monitor the regulatory environment: if regulators begin to restrict preferred equity issuance or treat SATA's perpetual structure more skeptically, capital-raising costs will rise. The viability of the entire model rests on the ability to continuously raise cheap capital through preferred equity to fund bitcoin purchases—if that valve closes, the strategy must pivot.
