*The **SP Funds [S&P Global](/spgi-stock/) REIT Sharia ETF** (SPRE) is an exchange-traded fund that tracks real-estate investment trusts (REITs) across developed markets, filtered through Islamic finance principles — screening out debt-heavy structures and companies whose revenue derives from prohibited activities, and including only those that meet Islamic (Sharia) standards for permissible property and financing.*

## What does SPRE hold and track?

SPRE is built on the S&P Global REIT Sharia Index, which selects large and mid-cap real-estate trusts worldwide that pass Islamic screens. The underlying index covers [commercial real estate](/commercial-real-estate/), residential properties, industrial facilities, healthcare real estate, and shopping centers across developed markets — essentially the global REIT universe, but filtered. A REIT qualifies for inclusion if its structure and revenue sources comply with Islamic principles: that means no interest-based debt above certain thresholds, no financing from prohibited activities (alcohol, gambling, pork), and revenue derived only from halal-compliant uses of the underlying properties.

The result is a diversified real-estate portfolio with a global footprint and a Sharia-compliant overlay. SPRE holds dozens of REITs and is rebalanced periodically to track the underlying index.

## Fund structure and costs

SPRE is a conventional exchange-traded fund — not leveraged, not inverse, not a note. It trades on a [stock exchange](/stock-exchange/) like a regular [equity ETF](/equity-etf/), with intraday pricing and no daily-reset mechanics. The fund is issued by SP Funds, which operates a suite of thematic and religious-screen ETFs.

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is moderate by ETF standards, reflecting both the cost of Sharia screening and the complexity of tracking a specialized index. Because it holds large, widely traded REITs, the fund is liquid — tight [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) and ordinary trading volume mean entry and exit costs are low. Distributions come from [dividend](/dividend/) income paid by the underlying REITs, typically quarterly or annually depending on the payout schedule.

## Why Sharia-compliant REITs?

Islamic finance prohibits interest (riba) and speculation on prohibited goods or services. A Sharia-compliant REIT must finance property purchases through structures that comply with those rules — often using equity partnerships, lease-based financing, or Sukuk (Islamic [bonds](/bond/)) rather than conventional debt. These constraints mean Sharia-screened REITs may not be borrowing as much as their conventional peers, though the screening criteria vary by fund and [index provider](/index-provider/).

For investors following Islamic principles, or seeking exposure to real estate through an Islamic lens, SPRE offers a way to own REITs without having to research each trust's financing and revenue sources individually. The screening is algorithmic and rule-based, applied consistently across the index.

## Real risks and limitations

Sharia screening is not a uniform standard — different Islamic scholars and different index providers apply different tests. SP Funds' screening criteria may be more or less strict than competitors, and that affects which REITs end up in the fund. An investor who disagrees with the index's interpretation of compliance may find the fund does not match their own Islamic principles.

Real-estate markets are cyclical, and REIT valuations fluctuate with [interest rates](/interest-rate/), occupancy, and economic growth. Because SPRE is a REIT fund, it carries real-estate-specific risks: concentration in any single property type or geography, exposure to construction costs and labor availability, and — especially for international holdings — [currency risk](/currency-risk/) if the fund holds REITs priced in foreign currencies.

The global [diversification](/diversification/) means SPRE faces geopolitical and regulatory risk across multiple jurisdictions, and some international markets have less transparent or developed real-estate markets than the developed economies that dominate the index.

## Who holds SPRE and how to research it

SPRE appeals to socially or religiously motivated investors seeking real-estate exposure that aligns with Islamic finance principles, and to institutions or funds that target Islamic-compliant portfolios. It is smaller and more specialized than broad REIT funds, so it attracts niche demand rather than mass-market flows.

To understand the fund, start by reading its prospectus, which details the Sharia screening criteria and how the S&P Global REIT Sharia Index is constructed. Review the index methodology from S&P Global itself — it explains the decision rules for which REITs qualify and which are excluded. Compare SPRE's holdings and performance to broad REIT funds (like those tracking the NAREIT index) and other Sharia-compliant equity funds to see how screening affects diversification and returns. And because REIT performance depends heavily on interest-rate expectations, monitor macroeconomic commentary on rates and credit conditions — rising rates tend to pressure REIT valuations across the board.
