*SCDS — the JPMorgan Fundamental Data Science Small Core ETF — is an active fund that invests in smaller American companies, chosen through a blend of traditional fundamental stock analysis and machine learning models that hunt for pricing inefficiencies.*

JPMorgan's approach to small-cap investing has evolved significantly over the past decade as computational power and data availability have grown. SCDS represents a deliberate bet by the firm that combining human fundamental analysts with algorithmic models yields better stock selection than either approach alone. The universe it operates in — companies with [market capitalizations](/market-capitalization/) typically between 2 billion and 10 billion dollars — contains thousands of potential holdings, most of them followed by only a handful of Wall Street analysts. This fragmentation is where data science can add value: by using text analysis on earnings calls, social-media sentiment, supply-chain disruption signals, and alternative data sources, the models can spot opportunities that traditional bottom-up analysis might miss.

The fund holds 40 to 60 stocks at any given time, which means it is relatively concentrated for a small-cap fund. That concentration reflects conviction — the managers believe their stock-picking process is accurate enough to take risk by holding fewer, larger positions rather than holding a representative sample of the entire small-cap universe. Concentration also limits the fund's appeal to risk-averse investors, but it is consistent with how serious active managers work.

The composition of the portfolio tilts the fund toward companies that display value characteristics: lower [price-to-book ratios](/price-to-book-ratio/), lower price-to-earnings multiples, and higher [dividend yields](/dividend-yield/) than the broad small-cap index. This is not a [growth fund](/growth-fund/). JPMorgan's data science models are explicitly trained to flag stocks that the market is mispricing on the value side — companies with solid fundamentals, recurring revenue, and reasonable [balance sheets](/balance-sheet/) that have fallen out of favour for cyclical or sentiment-driven reasons. When small-cap value is in a downturn, SCDS will suffer more than a broad small-cap index. When value is staging a recovery, it can outperform sharply.

The real risk to this approach is [model risk](/model-risk/). The data science engine powering the stock selection is, at its core, a statistical model trained on historical data. If the market's behaviour shifts — if the factors that drove outperformance in the past no longer work — the models can malfunction without warning. Additionally, small-cap stocks are inherently less liquid and less analysed than large caps, which means each pick carries more company-specific risk. A single position can crater on an earnings miss or a management change, and because SCDS is concentrated, a few bad picks can meaningfully damage returns.

The fund charges an active-management fee, which is higher than a passive small-cap [index fund](/index-fund/) but is not excessive. JPMorgan's scale and research infrastructure are genuine advantages; smaller asset managers often struggle to maintain the data science and human talent needed to run this type of hybrid strategy. The benefit of JPMorgan's resources is visible in the quality of the research infrastructure, but the benefit to returns is not guaranteed — active management in small caps is hotly competitive.

For investors, SCDS makes sense as a satellite position for someone who has a core small-cap index holding and wants to take a tactical bet on the idea that JPMorgan's combination of fundamental and data-driven analysis can beat the market. It is not a substitute for broad small-cap exposure, nor is it appropriate for investors seeking stability or predictability. Its performance is lumpy, its holdings are concentrated, and its fortunes depend on whether the models are working. Someone researching this fund should read JPMorgan's fact sheet carefully to understand the exact methodology, review the performance record in different small-cap environments (rising value, falling value, tech-heavy rallies), and assess whether the active fees are justified by the historical outperformance. Comparing SCDS's rolling three-year and five-year returns to a small-cap value index provides a useful benchmark.
