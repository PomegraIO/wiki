*The **iShares Systematic [Bond ETF](/bond-etf/)** (SYSB) is an exchange-traded fund that holds a diversified portfolio of [bonds](/bond/) selected according to a systematic indexing methodology rather than traditional market-cap-weighted allocation.*

## What exactly is a systematic bond index, and why does it matter?

A systematic bond index operates differently from the conventional approach of weighting bonds by the amount of debt outstanding. Traditional bond indices, like the Bloomberg Aggregate Bond Index, weight each issuer by the total value of bonds it has issued — so a government or corporation that has borrowed more gets more representation in the portfolio. A systematic approach instead applies a consistent set of rules to select and weight bonds based on characteristics like duration, credit quality, and issuer [diversification](/diversification/), aiming to avoid the passive over-concentration that can arise when a single issuer dominates.

SYSB's underlying index applies this systematic methodology to select bonds from across the U.S. Treasury, government-sponsored enterprise, corporate, and securitized bond markets. The result is a portfolio that, relative to market-cap-weighted indices, typically has more granular diversification — no single issuer dominates, and weightings reflect a disciplined screening process rather than the happenstance of who has borrowed most.

## What does the fund actually hold?

SYSB's portfolio includes U.S. [Treasury bonds](/treasury-bond/), agency debt, investment-grade [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/), and mortgage-backed securities. Treasuries typically form the largest slice, given their role as the backbone of the fixed-income market. The remainder is spread across corporates from sectors like finance, energy, utilities, and industrials, along with securitized debt backed by mortgages and other assets.

Because the index applies systematic rebalancing rules rather than following market weights slavishly, SYSB often carries a higher concentration in mid-tier issuers and a lower exposure to the largest borrowers compared to traditional cap-weighted bond indices. This can make it a useful diversifier for portfolios already holding conventional indices.

## What are the costs and how liquid is it to trade?

As a passive, index-tracking fund, SYSB carries a modest [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) — the annual fee charged by the fund, stated as a percentage of assets — in the neighbourhood of 0.10 to 0.15 per cent, though the exact figure should be checked in the fund's fact sheet. The net expense ratio may differ from the gross if the fund sponsor is temporarily waiving fees.

SYSB is an exchange-traded fund, so it trades on an exchange — in this case, the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) — at prices set throughout the trading day. Its size means trading volume is typically sufficient for retail and institutional investors alike to buy and sell shares without significant friction. The [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) (the difference between the price at which you can buy and the price at which you can sell) is usually measured in basis points — pennies relative to the share price.

## Who should consider this fund, and how would a researcher approach it?

SYSB suits an investor seeking broad, diversified exposure to bonds without the dominance of the largest borrowers that market-cap-weighting creates. It appeals to those who believe a systematic, rules-based approach to bond selection is more robust than reliance on the weights implied by outstanding debt volumes. Because it holds mostly investment-grade securities, it is not a vehicle for credit-risk seeking — it is fundamentally a core bond holding, not a satellite bet on high-yield credit or emerging-market debt.

A prospective investor should start by reviewing the fund's fact sheet and prospectus, available on the iShares website or through the SEC's EDGAR database. The prospectus explains the fund's methodology, holdings, and risks in formal legal language. The fact sheet provides a snapshot: current holdings, portfolio characteristics such as weighted-average duration and credit quality, recent performance, and the expense ratio. Checking the top ten holdings and noticing their diversification — Treasury dominance balanced with a scatter of corporates — gives intuitive feel for the portfolio's shape. Finally, comparing SYSB's expense ratio and returns to similar funds like the iShares Core Aggregate Bond ETF or the Vanguard Total Bond Market ETF offers perspective on whether this fund fills a useful gap in a portfolio or duplicates what a more conventional [index fund](/index-fund/) already provides.
