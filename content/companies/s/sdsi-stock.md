*The **American Century Short Duration Strategic [Income ETF](/income-etf/)** (SDSI) is an actively managed fixed-income fund that combines short-duration [bonds](/bond/), dividend-paying equities, and other income-producing assets in pursuit of a higher yield than traditional short-term bond funds, with less interest-rate sensitivity.*

> "Yield without taking the full risk of a long-dated portfolio."

That aspiration frames the fund's approach. By holding predominantly shorter-duration debt — bonds with years rather than decades until maturity — the portfolio minimises the risk that rising [interest rates](/interest-rate/) will markedly reduce the value of existing holdings. This low-duration positioning trades away some yield; SDSI compensates by adding equity exposure, preferred shares, and selective higher-yield debt to push the overall payout higher than a simple short-duration bond fund alone would generate.

## The income toolkit and positioning

SDSI's portfolio is a mix: short- and intermediate-duration [investment-grade bonds](/investment-grade-bond/) form the backbone, supplemented by a small equity allocation (often 10–20% of the fund), preferred shares, [floating-rate notes](/floating-rate-note/), and occasionally tactical holdings in higher-yield segments like [high-yield bonds](/high-yield-bond/) or emerging-market debt. The weighting is actively managed, meaning the fund managers adjust the mix based on their market outlook and interest-rate expectations, not a fixed index formula.

The short-duration focus is key. Duration measures the sensitivity of a bond's price to interest-rate changes; a bond with 3-year duration falls roughly 3% for every 1% rise in yield, while a 10-year bond falls roughly 10%. By keeping duration in the 3–5 year range, SDSI limits that interest-rate volatility whilst still positioning clients to earn more than money-market funds or ultra-short funds. The tactical equity and preferred-share overlay adds equity-like returns and higher [current yield](/current-yield/), at the cost of some volatility relative to a pure bond fund.

## Sponsor, structure, and costs

SDSI is managed by American Century, an investment firm focused on active equity and fixed-income management. The fund is structured as an open-end ETF, traded on a major exchange, combining the daily liquidity and tax efficiency of ETF wrappers with an actively managed strategy underneath. Shares can be created and redeemed in large blocks; the fund itself is liquid enough for retail trading.

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is typically in the 0.35–0.45% range, modest for an actively managed bond fund but higher than passive short-duration [bond ETFs](/bond-etf/), reflecting the cost of the active management and the small equity/alternatives overlay.

## Interest-rate sensitivity and concentration risk

The fund's main appeal — limited duration exposure — is also its main constraint. In a steep interest-rate decline, SDSI will capture only modest price appreciation because it has little interest-rate sensitivity to begin with. Investors seeking capital gains from falling yields should hold longer-duration bonds; SDSI is for those prioritising steady income and capital preservation.

The inclusion of equity and preferred shares introduces equity-market volatility. During sharp equity selloffs, the fund will underperform a pure bond allocation. The active management also introduces manager risk — if the portfolio managers make poor tactical calls on the direction of interest rates or the relative value of equities versus bonds, returns can lag peers. Over-concentration in preferred shares or high-yield debt in one active rotation could also amplify drawdowns if those segments sell off sharply.

## Who this is for and how to research it

SDSI is suited to conservative investors and income-focused retirees who need steady cash flow and are uncomfortable holding long-dated bonds in a rising-rate environment. It can also serve as a core fixed-income allocation for investors who want slightly more yield than money-market funds or short-term bond ETFs, without the full [interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/) of intermediate-duration portfolios.

Research begins with the fund's prospectus and latest fact sheet, which disclose the current portfolio composition, duration, and yield. Investors should review the managers' quarterly commentary to understand their current market positioning and the rationale for equity and higher-yield allocations. American Century publishes detailed fact sheets on their website, and the fund's year-to-date performance relative to simple short-duration bond indices and other actively managed income ETFs provides a sense of whether the active management is adding value or dragging on returns.
