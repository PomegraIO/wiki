**SSMG** (Virtus Silvant Small/Mid [Growth ETF](/growth-etf/)) is an actively managed exchange-traded fund run by Virtus using the Silvant small-cap growth strategy. Rather than tracking a fixed index passively, the fund's portfolio manager makes buy and sell decisions using a quantitative framework designed to identify small and mid-cap growth companies trading at reasonable valuations. The fund holds roughly 150–200 stocks, mostly in the $500 million to $5 billion market-cap range, and rebalances based on the manager's assessment of value and growth signals.

## The manager's approach: small caps with discipline

Unlike a passive fund that holds all stocks in a defined index, SSMG's manager actively selects which small and mid-cap growth companies to own. The Silvant strategy marries two historically useful ideas: small-cap stocks have historically outperformed large-cap stocks over long periods, and growth stocks (those with rising earnings or sales) have outperformed stagnant ones. But growth stocks can become expensive, priced for perfection, and vulnerable to disappointment. The Silvant approach filters for growth companies that still trade at valuations where there is room for upside if they execute.

This blend — [growth at a reasonable price](/growth-at-reasonable-price-garp/), often called GARP (Growth at a Reasonable Price) — attempts to avoid the two traps: buying stagnant cheap stocks that stay cheap because they deserve to, or chasing expensive growth stocks that disappoint when they stumble even slightly.

## The stock selection process

The manager screens the small and mid-cap universe for companies meeting criteria on growth (earnings revisions, sales growth, [return on equity](/return-on-equity/)) and valuation (price-to-earnings relative to historical norms, price-to-book, price-to-sales). The result is a narrower list of candidates than the entire small-cap market. From that list, the manager researches and selects the most promising names for the portfolio.

Because it is active, SSMG can concentrate on the manager's highest-conviction ideas or diversify broadly. In practice, the fund maintains meaningful exposure across sectors (technology, healthcare, industrials, consumer discretionary, etc.) without being locked into any single index's sector weightings. This gives the manager flexibility to tilt toward sectors where attractive small-cap growth opportunities are most abundant.

## Portfolio composition and turnover

The fund typically holds 150–200 stocks, with the largest positions representing perhaps 1–2% of the portfolio each and the smallest positions less than 0.5%. This is diversified enough to avoid single-stock risk but concentrated enough that the manager's best ideas drive returns. The top 10 holdings might represent 10–15% of the fund.

Because the manager is actively repositioning — moving out of stocks that no longer look cheap, into fresh opportunities — the turnover is higher than a passive fund. A turnover rate of 30–50% annually is common for actively managed [growth funds](/growth-fund/); it generates tax consequences in taxable accounts and contributes to the fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/).

## Costs and the active fee

SSMG carries an expense ratio typical of actively managed [small-cap growth funds](/small-cap-growth-fund/), usually between 0.70% and 1.10% annually. That is significantly higher than a passive small-cap fund (which costs 0.10–0.30%), reflecting the cost of the manager's research, trading, and administration. For an investor to come out ahead with active management, the manager's skill in picking winners has to exceed the fee by a meaningful margin — a high bar that not all active managers clear.

SSMG is an ETF rather than a traditional [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/), which provides some tax-efficiency advantages: the fund can exchange shares in-kind (without triggering capital gains) when investors redeem, passing less unrealised gain to remaining shareholders. This is a modest benefit but real in taxable accounts.

## Risk and sector exposure

Small and mid-cap stocks are more volatile than large-cap stocks. They also have less analyst coverage and smaller trading volumes, making them vulnerable to sharp swings on thinner demand. A growth-tilted portfolio amplifies that volatility — growth stocks tend to fall harder in downturns and rise faster in recoveries. An SSMG [holding period](/holding-period/) should be long-term; traders or investors uncomfortable with 25%, 30%, or larger drawdowns should avoid it.

The Silvant strategy's emphasis on valuation discipline offers some dampening: by avoiding the most expensive growth stocks, the manager theoretically reduces the crash risk from value destruction when growth fails to materialise. But this is a matter of degree, not elimination; SSMG will still swing sharply.

## Comparing to alternatives

An investor seeking small-cap growth exposure can choose between passive small-cap growth ETFs (which track an index and cost less but offer no manager flexibility), other actively managed small-cap funds (run by different managers with different records), or a portfolio of individual small-cap stocks (maximum control, maximum complexity). SSMG appeals to those convinced that active management adds value and willing to pay for it, and who trust Virtus's particular approach and track record.

## Performance and research

Before investing, review SSMG's performance versus passive small-cap growth indices and versus peer active funds over rolling one-, three-, five-, and ten-year periods. Manager tenure matters: if the current manager has been in place only a few years, that track record is limited and might not reflect how the strategy behaves in different market conditions.

Check the fund's fact sheet for the current top holdings, sector weightings, valuation metrics (price-to-earnings, price-to-book), and [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/) relative to its small-cap benchmark. These metrics reveal what the manager is emphasising and whether the portfolio looks expensive or cheap relative to history. The [annual report](/10-k/) discusses recent decisions and market outlook, offering colour on the manager's thinking.

Understand that active small-cap growth funds are inherently volatile and require patience; short-term performance swings can be wide. An investor considering SSMG should have at least a five-year time horizon and a stomach for double-digit annual drawdowns in bad years.
