SmartRent is a software and hardware company that sells property management and smart home automation platforms to the rental housing industry. The company was founded in 2017 and went public in 2021, and it now serves thousands of rental properties across multifamily communities (apartment complexes), [single-family rentals](/single-family-rental/), and student housing. Its platform combines proprietary cloud-based software with a carefully curated selection of connected hardware — thermostats, door locks, access systems, Wi-Fi routers, and occupancy sensors — to give property managers and residents an integrated, app-controlled view of building operations and tenant comfort.

## What SmartRent actually sells

The core product is a cloud platform that acts as a central nervous system for a rental property. On the property-management side, SmartRent's software tracks lease data, maintenance requests, rent collection, and tenant communications in a single interface. On the resident side, tenants access a mobile app that controls their thermostat, unlocks their door, grants access to guests, connects to community Wi-Fi, and initiates maintenance requests without a phone call.

The hardware layer is crucial to this vision. SmartRent has built its own line of thermostats, smart locks, and access-control devices, rather than simply integrating off-the-shelf products from rivals like Schlage or Ecobee. This vertical integration — owning both the software and a meaningful portion of the hardware — is the company's distinctive choice. It allows SmartRent to optimize the two layers together, ensuring that a resident's app stays synced with the lock, that the thermostat sends real-time data to the management system, and that all of these devices work reliably without the brittleness that haunts loosely coupled integrations.

The connectivity layer matters too. A property manager needs visibility into what is happening in hundreds or thousands of units simultaneously. This requires reliable network infrastructure. SmartRent has built proprietary Wi-Fi solutions and community networking tools to ensure that data from every device reaches the cloud reliably, even in older buildings with poor connectivity.

Beyond the core platform, SmartRent has evolved into adjacent areas. The company now offers **community Wi-Fi** — internet service provided directly by SmartRent to residents, a profitable recurring-revenue stream. It offers **asset protection and monitoring** — cameras and sensors that help property managers detect maintenance problems early and prevent break-ins. It provides **self-guided tours** — allowing prospective tenants to view vacant units on their own schedule, reducing the labour of leasing teams.

## The business model and economics

SmartRent's revenue comes from two broad sources: subscription fees for software and connectivity, and hardware sales.

**Subscription revenue** is the heart of the business. Property managers pay a monthly or annual fee per property or per unit managed. This revenue is recurring, predictable, and grows as the company adds more properties to its platform. The gross margins on subscription revenue are very high — perhaps 80–90 percent — because the incremental cost of serving one more property is minimal once the platform is built.

**Hardware revenue** is less predictable but more visible. When a property manager adopts SmartRent, the company typically installs new locks, thermostats, and Wi-Fi equipment, generating a one-time hardware sale. SmartRent profits on hardware but at lower margins than software, around 30–50 percent typically, because manufacturing and installation have real costs. Over time, as the installed base matures, hardware revenue plateaus — existing properties do not need new locks every year — while subscription revenue keeps growing.

A third stream, **ancillary services**, includes Wi-Fi subscriptions sold to residents and advertising or data products sold to partners. These are nascent but growing.

## Why property managers choose SmartRent

The appeal is operational simplicity and resident satisfaction. Before SmartRent and its competitors, property managers juggled separate systems for maintenance (work orders), communications (email, phone, text), access control (keys and pin codes), and resident reporting (paper complaint forms). Residents, meanwhile, had to call the office to request maintenance or ask for a guest code, and locked themselves out of their apartments regularly.

SmartRent consolidates these workflows. A resident locks themselves out, opens the app, and gets a virtual unlock instantly without calling anyone. A maintenance issue is reported through the app with a photo, routed automatically to the right technician, tracked until closed, and rated by the resident. A property manager can remotely monitor thermostats across an entire portfolio, identify units where heating is failing, and send a technician with confidence that the problem is real rather than a resident's complaint. These efficiencies free up staff to focus on leasing and customer service.

SmartRent's penetration among the largest multifamily operators — 15 of the top 20 — signals market acceptance. Large operators have the sophistication to integrate new platforms and the scale to capture ROI from reduced labour and vacancy loss.

## The competitive landscape

SmartRent competes against both established and emerging rivals. **AppFolio** and **RealPage** are older, larger property-management software vendors with installed bases in multifamily. **Yardi** dominates high-end enterprise properties. **Rently** and **Knock** compete on self-guided tours. Smart-home platforms like **Amazon Alexa for Business** and **Google Nest** for enterprise are adjacent competitors.

What distinguishes SmartRent is the combination of vertical integration (owning hardware and software together) and focus on the rental housing vertical. Older vendors like RealPage emphasize software breadth; smart-home companies emphasize consumer appeal. SmartRent has chosen to be specialist — optimizing specifically for property managers and renters, building hardware tuned to those workflows, and staying aligned with the industry's needs.

## Recent innovations and energy management

In 2025, SmartRent launched **SMRT IQ**, an AI-powered analytics layer that aggregates data across a property manager's entire portfolio and surfaces actionable insights — which properties have the highest turnover, which units are consuming excess energy, where maintenance problems recur. This is characteristic of software platform evolution: the value shifts from transactional features (turning a thermostat on) to analytical features (understanding why energy consumption is rising).

SmartRent has also expanded its energy management offerings, helping property managers reduce utility costs by providing real-time visibility into thermostat data and occupancy patterns. Older energy-management products rely on static reports or utility bill estimates; SmartRent's version taps live device data, enabling more precise interventions. This is valuable because utility costs are one of the largest controllable expenses for multifamily operators.

## Challenges and risks

[Execution risk](/execution-risk/) is real. Many proptech companies have stumbled on deployment — complex integrations with legacy building systems, unreliable hardware, software bugs that frustrate residents and managers alike. SmartRent is still young and must prove that it can scale its platform to thousands of properties without degradation.

Competitive risk is also present. Larger vendors could integrate or acquire competing smart-home platforms; property-management software vendors could build their own smart-home layer; or Amazon and Google could commercialize their platforms for rental housing. SmartRent's advantage is current momentum and vertical focus, not unassailable technology moats.

Customer concentration is a third concern. If a handful of large multifamily operators represent a significant portion of SmartRent's revenue, the loss of a major customer could hurt earnings. Growth depends on winning new large customers and expanding within existing ones.
