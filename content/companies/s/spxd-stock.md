## What is the underlying index?

SPXD tracks an index composed of all 500 stocks in the S&P 500, but rather than weight them by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) — a methodology that causes the largest companies to dominate — the index applies a diversified sector weighting scheme. Each of the eleven major equity sectors (technology, healthcare, financials, industrials, consumer discretionary, consumer staples, energy, utilities, materials, real estate, and communication services) receives a capped equal weight. Within each sector, individual stocks still compete by their size and performance characteristics, but no single sector can grow so large that it overwhelms the overall fund's movement.

The practical effect is a portfolio that holds the same S&P 500 companies but distributes the capital more evenly across economic functions. When technology has ballooned to an outsize share of market-cap-weighted indices, SPXD's approach keeps technology constrained to its target allocation, which means the fund holds relatively less in mega-cap tech names and more in underweight sectors like energy, utilities, or materials. Over periods when concentrated-sector markets severely outperform diversified ones, SPXD will lag. Over periods when breadth returns to the market, SPXD often leads.

## Who is Xtrackers, and what kind of fund is this?

Xtrackers is the ETF sponsor owned by Deutsche Börse and [BlackRock](/blk-stock/) (through iShares' relationship structures), operating globally and specializing in index tracking and factor-based strategies. SPXD is a traditional index-tracking ETF, not actively managed. The fund aims to replicate its index's returns as closely as possible, meaning the manager does not make discretionary bets on individual stocks — the holdings are determined entirely by the rules of the index methodology.

## How does it trade and what does it cost?

Like all ETFs, SPXD trades on an exchange during market hours at market prices determined by supply and demand. The fund's [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) depends on its trading volume and liquidity; as a relatively niche product compared to broad S&P 500 cap-weighted alternatives, the spread may be modest but wider than the tightest mega-cap [index funds](/index-fund/). The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/), detailed in the prospectus, is low because indexing requires minimal research and human decision-making — the portfolio simply replicates the index rule each day.

## What is the actual risk or advantage?

The core trade-off is simplicity: SPXD is not complex, but it behaves differently from a market-cap-weighted S&P 500 fund in ways that matter for returns. In periods when a few mega-cap stocks soar while the rest stagnate, SPXD underperforms the cap-weighted version. In periods when growth slows and leadership broadens across sectors and company sizes, SPXD outperforms. Neither outcome is guaranteed, and the relative performance depends entirely on market conditions and whether the cycle favors concentration or [diversification](/diversification/).

The fund also carries sector-tilting risks implicitly. If the diversified sector weighting consistently underweights the true growth drivers of the economy over decades, long-term return drag is possible. Conversely, if it provides natural rebalancing into lagging sectors that eventually recover, it acts as a stabilizing force.

## When would an investor choose SPXD?

Someone seeking S&P 500 exposure but concerned about [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) — either in tech specifically or in large-cap concentration broadly — might choose SPXD over a standard market-cap-weighted S&P 500 ETF. An investor pursuing a diversified equity strategy who prefers the U.S. market but wants to tilt toward equal sector representation would find the fund's methodology aligned with that goal. Conversely, an investor convinced that the largest-cap technology companies will continue to drive returns would likely view SPXD's tech underweighting as a drag rather than a benefit.

## How do I research it?

Start with the fund fact sheet and prospectus to confirm the exact index methodology and expense ratio. Compare SPXD's historical performance to a standard S&P 500 cap-weighted ETF (SPY, VOO) over periods of 3, 5, and 10 years to see how much difference the sector diversification has made. Look at the current sector allocations in SPXD and compare them to what they would be in a pure market-cap-weighted fund to understand the current tilts. Review the fund's flows and average daily volume to assess whether the bid-ask spread is acceptable for your trading size.
