**"Growth and profitability are not enemies—they are dance partners."** That observation frames the Simplify Propel Opportunities ETF (ticker SURI), which screens for US companies demonstrating strong, efficient growth. The fund is not a mega-cap momentum play; it is a systematic attempt to capture companies that are growing revenue faster than peers while deploying capital without reckless excess.

The strategy runs straightforward. Simplify Asset Management applies a rules-based screen to a broad universe of US-listed stocks, filtering for companies that meet thresholds on revenue growth, [return on invested capital](/return-on-invested-capital/), and other efficiency metrics. The holding list is rebalanced periodically—usually quarterly—and weighted by the scores these metrics produce. A company growing revenue at a double-digit clip while maintaining strong margins and reasonable debt levels gets a larger position than one where growth is stalling or returns are collapsing. The fund aims to sidestep the pure-momentum trap (chasing stocks that have already run) while also avoiding the deep-value watering hole where companies in secular decline trade cheap forever.

The portfolio skews small to mid-cap because many very large companies, by definition, have already achieved so much scale that revenue growth becomes a struggle. Mega-cap tech and financial giants may have excellent returns on capital, but growing the top line by 20 or 30 percent when you already control a huge market is harder than for a smaller, hungrier firm. SURI thus naturally holds fewer FAANG-sized names and more focused, ambitious mid-market companies across software, healthcare, industrials, and consumer sectors.

Performance depends entirely on whether that particular combination of screens—growth, profitability, capital efficiency—predicts future results. Historically, "quality growth" has worked in many market regimes, but it has also had painful drawdowns when the market prefers cheaper, struggling companies or when mega-cap growth momentum overpowers everything else. The approach is also subject to [factor timing](/factor-timing/): if the market suddenly rotates away from quality toward speculation, SURI can underperform for months or years, even if the underlying thesis is sound.

[Expense ratios](/expense-ratio/) are competitive. The fund is small enough that spreads can be wide, and trading volumes are lower than mega-funds, which can matter for large positions. Tax efficiency depends on the turnover from rebalancing; quarterly updates generate some realised gains, but the approach is not optimised for tax minimisation.

To understand SURI, review the fund's fact sheet and prospectus for the exact selection criteria and weighting method. Track the fund's style tilt (is it leaning growth, value, quality, or truly balanced?) by comparing its holdings to broad-market benchmarks. Compare the fund's returns to simple indices of mid-cap or small-cap stocks to see whether the methodology's screens have genuinely added alpha, or whether it is just tracking a size tilt. And watch the fund's sector concentration: if growth and efficiency screens are naturally converging on software and biotech while avoiding utilities and energy, that concentration may amplify both upside and downside in particular market regimes.
