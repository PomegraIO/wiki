*The **ProShares UltraShort Consumer Staples** ETF (SZK) is a leveraged short — a vehicle that amplifies and inverts daily movements in consumer staples stocks, delivering roughly negative three times the index's return on any given day.*

## The mechanics

SZK aims to move three times in the opposite direction of the Consumer Staples Select Sector Index, which tracks shares in companies that make and sell non-discretionary consumer goods: packaged food, beverages, personal care, household products, tobacco. [Procter & Gamble](/pg-stock/), Coca-Cola, Nestlé (via ADRs), Colgate-Palmolive, Mondelez, [General Mills](/gis-stock/) — the steady, defensive names that hold up when economies sag.

The fund accomplishes this through derivatives: swap agreements, inverse equity instruments, short positions held through [brokers](/broker/). It is not simply selling consumer staples shares — that would be a simple short. Instead, ProShares has structured a daily-reset mechanism. Each trading day, the fund adjusts its positions so that if the index falls 1 per cent, SZK aims to rise roughly 3 per cent; if the index rises 1 per cent, SZK falls 3 per cent. The reset happens at market close. This daily-reset design is critical: it means SZK's return over multi-day periods does not neatly scale the index's move. In a volatile market, the fund can suffer from "decay" — losing value even if the index ends a week where it began, because of the mathematics of compound moves in opposite directions.

## Who uses it and why

SZK appeals to traders and tactical hedgers, not long-term buy-and-hold investors. A fund manager holding a large position in consumer staples might use SZK as a short-term offset during periods of concern that staples are overvalued or the broader market is turning. An options trader might pair it with other positions to construct a specific risk profile. A portfolio manager might use a small position to bet that a particular earnings season will disappoint the staples cohort.

What it is not: a hedge for a staples-heavy [dividend](/dividend/) portfolio held over years. The daily reset and the compounding drag of leverage mean that SZK is a vehicle for views on near-term direction, not a hold-and-forget hedge. Someone genuinely worried about consumer staples' medium-term prospects would more rationally buy [put options](/put-option/) or reduce the position entirely, rather than deploy this fund.

## Costs and execution

SZK charges an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) of roughly 1 to 1.5 per cent annually — higher than an unlevered [equity ETF](/equity-etf/), because the fund must continuously rebalance its derivatives positions and pay the [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) inherent in swaps. It is liquid enough for institutional sized trades, though the bid-ask spread on the fund itself can be wider than that of core equity ETFs. Trading volume is respectable but not massive; large positions should be checked against the fund's size and typical daily volume before assuming it can be entered or exited painlessly.

## The real risks

Leverage and daily resets breed decay in range-bound or choppy markets. Persistent volatility with no clear directional move can erode the fund's value. Underlying index moves that reverse intraday can magnify the harm. The fund also tracks the Consumer Staples Select Sector Index, which is sector-specific — a narrower benchmark than the broad market. A trader betting on a broad market decline with this fund is making a concentrated bet on one sector.

Finally, leverage cuts both ways. If the staples sector rallies sharply, losses in SZK mount quickly. A 10 per cent rally in the index would, under the fund's design, produce approximately a 30 per cent loss. This is not a fund to hold unhedged through a [sector rotation](/sector-rotation/) into defensives.

## Researching and using SZK

Anyone considering SZK should read the fund's prospectus carefully. ProShares documents the daily-reset mechanism, the drag from volatility, and the risks of leverage plainly. The fact sheet shows recent performance, the underlying index's constituents, and the fund's holdings (mostly derivatives contracts with major banks). Comparing SZK's returns to negative three times the sector index's returns over recent weeks — accounting for the stated decay — gives intuitive feel for the mechanics.

This is an instrument for professionals or sophisticated traders with a specific directional thesis lasting days or weeks, not months. Holding it longer invites decay to erode returns even if the underlying view eventually proves correct. Allocating a disproportionate capital to it courts ruin if the trade moves against you. Used narrowly, it can be a useful tactical tool. Used carelessly, it is a bleed.
