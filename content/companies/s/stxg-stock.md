Strive 1000 [Growth ETF](/growth-etf/) (STXG) offers investors a straightforward path into large-cap growth equities through a single, liquid security that trades on a public exchange like any stock. The fund tracks a broad universe of American growth-oriented companies and aims to deliver returns that mirror its underlying index before costs — making it a core holding for portfolios that want exposure to established U.S. firms with above-average growth prospects. The fund is structured as a traditional open-ended exchange-traded fund and settles trades at the end of each day.

## What the fund holds and why

STXG targets the 1,000 largest publicly traded U.S. companies selected for growth characteristics. The holdings span the full spectrum of the large-cap universe: technology and consumer discretionary firms dominate by weight, but the fund also holds exposure to health care, industrials, financials, and other sectors. The index approach ensures the fund is diversified across many names rather than concentrated in a handful, which is a key reason investors use growth-focused ETFs as portfolio building blocks rather than tactical trades.

[Growth investing](/growth-investing/) as a category seeks companies that are expanding revenues and earnings faster than the market average, typically at the expense of current [dividend yields](/dividend-yield/). These firms — technology companies, premium consumer brands, and rapid-scaling operators — tend to reinvest profits rather than pay them out, which is why an investor in a [growth fund](/growth-fund/) should expect capital appreciation rather than income. The trade-off is real: when growth slows or [interest rates](/interest-rate/) rise, growth stocks can face sharper drawdowns than value-oriented peers.

## How the fund works for investors

STXG shares trade during regular market hours on a national securities exchange, meaning an investor can buy or sell a block of shares at any moment the market is open — not just at the day's close like a traditional [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/). The fund holds all the stocks in its index (or a representative sample, depending on the fund's operational approach) and distributes any [dividends](/dividend/) its underlying holdings pay to shareholders, typically on a quarterly or annual basis. These dividends are generally modest for a growth fund, since growth companies tend to retain their earnings.

The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) — the annual cost to own the fund, expressed as a percentage of assets — is a qualitative detail that matters for long-term investors, since even small differences in cost compound over decades. Investors should verify the current expense ratio through the fund's prospectus or fact sheet, as ratios can change and competitive pressure has brought many [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/) into the 0.03–0.10% range.

## Daily reset mechanics and tracking error

Because STXG is a traditional ETF (not a leveraged or inverse product), it has no daily reset and no built-in volatility decay. It aims to track its index continuously throughout the trading day. The only tracking error — the gap between the fund's return and its index's return — stems from the expense ratio and minor timing differences in when the fund purchases new securities or reinvests dividends. For a [passively managed fund](/passively-managed-fund/), tracking error is usually negligible.

## Who holds these funds and why

STXG fits into three common portfolio roles. Investors building a diversified stock portfolio often use broad, low-cost growth ETFs as a core holding to capture the U.S. equity market's performance. Financial advisors recommend them as a transparent, tax-efficient way to own a slice of American growth firms without stock-picking or paying active [management fees](/management-fee/). And [index funds](/index-fund/) — including retirement accounts and institutional portfolios — hold products like this because they offer scale, liquidity, and cost efficiency that individual stock ownership cannot match.

## Reading the fund's details and tracking your investment

A reader researching STXG should start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, both available from the fund sponsor's website or through a brokerage. The prospectus explains the fund's investment objective, the exact index it tracks, and the mechanics of how shares are created and redeemed. The fact sheet gives a cleaner summary: the current expense ratio, the top holdings, the sector weightings, and the fund's year-to-date and longer-term returns relative to its benchmark.

The underlying index itself is equally important to understand. The index methodology — how many stocks it includes, how they are weighted (equal-weight, market-cap weighted, or another scheme), and how often it rebalances — shapes what returns the fund can deliver. A fund tracking a different growth index may move quite differently from STXG even if both focus on the large-cap U.S. growth universe.

Watch the fund's year-to-date performance against its stated benchmark, which should be nearly identical after accounting for the expense ratio. If the fund begins to meaningfully lag, either its expense ratio has changed, there is a tracking issue, or the index definition has shifted. A fund's prospectus is the source of truth on all three counts, and it is worth reading at least once.
