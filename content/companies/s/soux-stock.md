*The **Defiance Daily Target 2X Long SOUN ETF (SOUX)** is a leveraged exchange-traded fund that seeks to deliver twice the daily return of the SonicShares Sound Index (SOUN). The fund rebalances daily to reset its [leverage ratio](/leverage-ratio-forex/), making it a short-term tactical instrument for traders betting on the sound and audio technology sector, not a buy-and-hold vehicle.*

## The Sound Index and thematic exposure

The SonicShares Sound Index is a curated basket of companies involved in audio, music, and sound technology. Unlike broad sector indices that capture entire industries, SOUN is a thematic index — it selects companies across multiple sectors united by a common focus: audio innovation and sound. This might include headphone makers, music-streaming platforms, podcast hosting companies, acoustic-technology firms, and hardware manufacturers focused on spatial audio or high-fidelity sound.

Defiance ETFs created SOUX to offer traders a way to amplify exposure to this specific theme without buying individual stocks. The fund is part of Defiance's broader suite of thematic and leveraged products targeting active traders with particular investment convictions.

## Daily leverage mechanics and their consequences

SOUX achieves 2x leverage through a combination of derivatives, cash collateral, and potentially borrowed funds. On any single trading day, the fund is designed to deliver approximately twice the daily percentage move of the SOUN index. If the index rises 2%, SOUX rises approximately 4%. If the index falls 2%, SOUX falls approximately 4%.

This works precisely because of daily rebalancing. Each trading day at close, the fund rebalances its leverage back to exactly 2x, resetting the ratio for the next trading session. This daily reset is the fund's defining feature — and also its trap for longer-term holders.

Volatility decay becomes severe quickly. If the SOUN index rises 10% over five days then falls 10% over the next five days (returning to its starting price), SOUX will have risen 20% then fallen 20% — ending below its starting point due to the compounding effect of leverage on daily moves. The more volatile the underlying index, the faster decay accelerates. The SOUN index, being focused on smaller and mid-cap audio and music companies, tends to be more volatile than broad market indices, which magnifies decay effects.

## The cost of leverage and rebalancing

SOUX's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) typically exceeds 1.10% annually, reflecting the cost of maintaining leverage. This includes the explicit [management fee](/management-fee/), the cost of derivatives hedging, daily rebalancing friction (the [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) incurred in daily trading to reset the leverage), and potentially borrowing costs if any of the leverage is sourced through debt.

For a fund designed for days-long tactical trades, the annual percentage cost is less relevant than the daily impact. A trader holding SOUX for two weeks faces the combined drag of volatility decay and daily compounding of these costs. Over such a horizon, decay can easily exceed 5% of position value in a volatile environment.

## Who SOUX is for and how it should be used

SOUX is for active traders who believe the sound and audio technology sector will outperform significantly over the next few days to a couple of weeks and who want to amplify that bet. A trader betting on a surge following an audio innovation announcement might use it. A [hedge fund](/hedge-fund/) running a thematic portfolio on the sound economy might deploy SOUX tactically.

SOUX is emphatically not for buy-and-hold investors, retirement accounts, or anyone holding for periods longer than a few weeks. Defiance's own disclosure and marketing emphasize the short-term nature of the product. A trader holding SOUX through normal market churn will experience returns that significantly lag 2x the index's actual move.

## Researching SOUX and the Sound Index

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, which list the exact holdings in the SOUN index (roughly 20-40 audio and music technology companies), the weights assigned to each, the expense ratio, and the mechanics of leverage. Understanding the SOUN index composition is crucial: which companies dominate it, which segments (wireless audio, streaming, podcasting, spatial audio) are represented, and how concentrated the index is.

Track the audio and music technology sector's fundamentals: are people buying headphones, increasing streaming subscriptions, adopting spatial audio, and investing in podcasting? Watch the macro environment — consumer spending, advertising trends in podcasting, and technology adoption cycles all affect the underlying index.

Monitor volatility levels. High volatility accelerates decay and increases risk. A trader using SOUX should expect outsized moves and be prepared for loss scenarios where two weeks of choppy markets erode returns even if the index is ultimately flat.

Most important: be clear about your holding timeframe before buying. If you intend to hold longer than a few weeks, SOUX is not the right tool. A spot audio technology ETF or individual stock purchases would better match your strategy.
