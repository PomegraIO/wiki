The VistaShares Target 15 S&P 100 Distribution ETF (ticker SIOO) arrived on the market in December 2025, representing one of the newest entries in the growing family of income-focused exchange-traded funds. VistaShares, the fund sponsor, launched SIOO as part of a broader strategy to offer investors exposure to mega-cap and large-cap U.S. equities bundled with a systematic income-generation overlay. The fund was designed to appeal to investors seeking steady distributions from the largest, most liquid American companies while also pursuing long-term capital appreciation.

The fund's core holdings track the S&P 100 Index, an index of the 100 largest constituents by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) within the larger S&P 500. These are the mega-cap companies that dominate U.S. equity market capitalizations — household technology names, financial institutions, energy majors, healthcare leaders, and consumer-staples giants. Unlike many passive S&P 100 products, SIOO is actively managed, meaning the fund's managers have discretion to deviate from strict index weighting and to make tactical positioning decisions.

The distinguishing feature of SIOO is its options overlay strategy, which is designed to generate the fund's signature 15% annualized income target. Rather than relying solely on the [dividend](/dividend/) income paid by the underlying S&P 100 companies, the fund uses section 1256 contracts — standardized equity options that receive favorable tax treatment under U.S. tax law. By writing (selling) [covered call](/covered-call/) options against its holdings or using other options strategies, the fund aims to accelerate income generation. The process works by the fund selling the right to buy its shares at a specified price, collecting premium income immediately; if shares are called away at the option's [strike price](/strike-price/), the fund realizes that gain; if shares are not called away, the fund retains both the premium and continued ownership. The strategy is engineered to target a 15% yield, paid monthly to shareholders, though the fund makes no guarantee it will achieve that target in all markets.

A secondary element of SIOO's approach is tax loss harvesting, a technique where the managers intentionally realize losses on select positions to offset gains elsewhere in the portfolio. In a long-term account, this can defer taxes; in a taxable account, it can reduce the net tax bill from the fund's distributions. Because the fund uses section 1256 options, those transactions receive 60/40 tax treatment (60% long-term capital gains, 40% short-term) regardless of how long the fund has held the option, which is more favorable than the ordinary income treatment many income strategies face.

The fund trades on the NYSE and carries an annual [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) of 0.59%, which is reasonable for an actively managed strategy but higher than a passive S&P 100 [index fund](/index-fund/). Because the fund is new, its track record is minimal, and the actual income it delivers over a full [market cycle](/market-cycle/) remains untested. The monthly distribution structure and the high stated yield target may attract income-focused investors but also carry meaningful risks.

The primary risks are that the 15% income target is aspirational, not guaranteed, and income can vary sharply depending on market volatility and options premiums. In a calm market environment where [implied volatility](/implied-volatility/) is low, options premiums decline and the fund may struggle to meet its income target. In a sharp downturn, covered-call strategies can underperform a passive S&P 100 fund because the calls limit upside if markets recover; the fund is willing to sacrifice some upside in exchange for the premium income it collects. The options overlay also introduces operational and [counterparty risks](/counterparty-risk/), though [FLEX options](/flex-options/) are exchange-traded and cleared, reducing these risks compared to over-the-counter structures. Additionally, the tax benefits of the 1256 treatment only apply to U.S. taxpayers; investors in tax-deferred accounts (like IRAs) do not benefit from the favorable tax positioning, and foreign investors may face complications.

For a reader investigating SIOO, the fund's prospectus is essential reading, as it explains the options strategy, the income target, and the mechanics of the monthly distributions. The fund's fact sheet provides the current portfolio composition, the expense ratio, and performance data (though performance data is limited given the fund's recent inception). The underlying index detail — the S&P 100 companies and their weights — can be found on the S&P Dow Jones Indices website. Monitoring the fund's actual monthly distributions versus the stated 15% target gives real-world insight into whether the strategy is working as intended. Finally, comparing the fund's total return (including distributions) to a passive S&P 100 fund over rolling periods will reveal whether the active options overlay and [tax-loss harvesting](/tax-loss-harvesting/) are adding value after fees.
