*The **VanEck Short Muni ETF** (SMB) is an inverse or "short" [bond](/bond/) fund that profits when [municipal bond](/municipal-bond/) prices decline. It holds short positions in short-term tax-exempt debt, making it a bet against the typical direction of the muni market — and a tool for experienced investors to hedge their exposure or express a view that muni valuations will compress.*

## What it does

SMB does not hold municipal bonds the way a traditional muni fund does. Instead, it takes short positions — essentially borrowing bonds, selling them, and profiting if they fall in value. For every dollar the underlying short-term municipal index drops, the fund typically gains. Conversely, when muni prices rise, SMB loses.

Most municipal bond investors buy bonds to collect tax-free interest over time. SMB is a fundamentally different animal. It is built for traders or hedgers who believe the muni market is overvalued, or for a bondholder who wants insurance against a decline in the value of their existing muni portfolio without having to sell it. The fund focuses on short-duration munis—bonds due to mature within a few years—which are less volatile than long-term municipals but still move with broader interest-rate trends.

## How it trades and costs

SMB trades on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) exchange under its ticker, meaning you can buy or sell shares at market prices throughout the trading day, much like a stock. The fund carries an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) that reflects the cost of maintaining short positions and rebalancing the underlying index positions — a structural expense that is higher than a comparable long muni fund would charge.

The fund's liquidity is moderate. It is not as heavily traded as the broad market index ETFs, so [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) (the difference between the buy and sell price) may be wider than investors accustomed to mega-cap funds expect. Smaller positions are easier to enter and exit cleanly; very large trades can move the market price.

## The use case and real risks

SMB appeals to a narrow but genuine group: professional investors, [market makers](/market-maker-trading/), and bond traders who believe muni valuations have stretched too far, or individual investors with substantial muni holdings who want to reduce their portfolio's duration risk without selling bonds or triggering tax consequences.

The core risk is that municipal bonds may not fall as expected. If [interest rates](/interest-rate/) decline or muni credit quality improves, SMB will lose value while traditional muni bonds gain. An investor who uses SMB as insurance is paying an ongoing cost—the expense ratio and any trading friction—for protection that may never be needed.

There is also leverage risk to be aware of. Because short positions require borrowing and selling, the fund's returns are amplified in both directions. A large rally in munis hits the fund harder than it hits a long-duration bond fund of equal notional size. The structure also introduces daily reset mechanics if the fund uses derivatives—each day the short exposure is "reset" to track the index, which can introduce tracking error over time in choppy markets.

Additionally, changes in municipal bond tax treatment—federal tax law, state tax policy, or the broader demand for tax-free income—can shift muni valuations in ways that work against the fund's positions. For example, if Congress expanded the muni-bond exemption, demand for municipal debt could surge, raising prices and losing money for SMB holders.

## How investors research it

Begin with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet on the VanEck website, which lay out the exact holdings, the methodology of the short position, the fee structure, and the tax implications. The prospectus will explain precisely which municipal index SMB tracks and how it constructs its inverse positions.

Next, monitor the underlying index—the Bloomberg Short-Term Municipal Bond Index or whichever index the fund tracks. If you want to know whether SMB should gain or lose value, you need to know where short-term munis are moving. Compare the fund's performance to that index over trailing periods to see if the fund is tracking accurately or if tracking error is accumulating.

Finally, stay alert to broader bond-market conditions: the [federal funds rate](/federal-funds-rate/), [inflation expectations](/inflation-expectations/), and any legislative changes to municipal bond tax treatment. SMB is a tactical position, not a core holding, and it makes sense only within a deliberate strategy to hedge or express a specific market view.
