The **SMART Small Cap ETF** (SSCP) is a passively managed exchange-traded fund that tracks the NASDAQ-100 Smart Beta Small Cap index. It offers investors exposure to smaller publicly traded US companies selected through a quantitative, rules-based process designed to favour certain financial characteristics associated with outperformance. The fund is sponsored by Invesco and holds a basket of roughly 100 small- and mid-cap stocks.

## What does SSCP actually hold?

The fund tracks an index that applies a "smart beta" methodology to US small-cap equities. Rather than weight companies by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/) alone — the way a traditional index works — the SMART index applies rules that tilt toward companies meeting certain criteria: lower valuation multiples, higher profitability, stronger growth, or lower volatility, depending on the weighting scheme. The result is a portfolio of perhaps 100 stocks, mostly in the $500 million to $2 billion range by market cap, spanning industrial, technology, healthcare, and consumer sectors.

Because the fund is rules-based and passive, there is no active manager looking at individual companies or trading frequently. The holdings simply follow the mechanical signals the index produces, which typically means they shift only when the rules demand rebalancing — usually quarterly or semi-annually.

## What is the cost of owning it?

The fund carries a low [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) typical of passively managed [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/) — usually under 0.50% annually. That means on a $10,000 holding, the annual cost is roughly $50 or less. The fund trades on an exchange with reasonable liquidity for a niche strategy; [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) are usually tight, particularly during market hours, so an investor can buy or sell without paying an outsized transaction cost.

## Why small caps using a rules-based tilt?

Small-cap stocks are more volatile and less widely followed by institutional analysts than their large-cap peers, which can create mispricings. A factor-based approach to selecting among them — focusing on value, quality, growth, or volatility characteristics — aims to capture those mispricings more systematically than either a random sample or an active manager's judgment.

The SMART methodology relies on the premise that certain quantifiable attributes correlate with long-term returns. A classic example: cheaper stocks (lower price relative to earnings) have historically outperformed expensive ones, particularly among smaller companies. By rotating toward cheaper small caps, the fund tilts toward exposure that has rewarded long [holding periods](/holding-period/) in many historical windows — though there are extended periods when the tilt underperforms, especially if the broader market is rising faster than the value factor.

## Who should consider this fund?

SSCP suits investors seeking a small-cap allocation to their portfolio and believing that quantitative factor tilts (particularly value, quality, or growth) offer better odds than market-cap weighting. It is less suitable for traders seeking high daily liquidity, given the small-cap universe itself is thinner than large-cap; it is also not appropriate for investors uncomfortable with higher volatility, as small-cap stocks swing more sharply than large-cap stocks in both directions.

The fund is often used as a core small-cap holding within a diversified portfolio, or as a satellite bet for investors who believe the factor tilt will outperform. It competes with broader small-cap options — many [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/) and ETFs that track the Russell 2000 or similar market-cap–weighted small-cap indices — but appeals specifically to those convinced that factor weighting adds value.

## How to research SSCP

Begin with the fund's fact sheet and prospectus, available from Invesco's website or the fund's page at major [brokers](/broker/). The prospectus details the index methodology and the rebalancing schedule. The fact sheet shows the current top holdings, sector breakdown, valuation metrics, and year-to-date performance — helpful for understanding what you actually own.

Compare SSCP's returns against the broader small-cap indices (the Russell 2000) and against competing factor-tilted small-cap funds to see whether the tilt has added value in recent years. Because factor performance is cyclical — sometimes value outperforms growth and vice versa — a single year of returns tells little; a five- or ten-year window is more revealing. The fund's expense ratio, though low, should also be weighed against other small-cap options to ensure the tilt justifies any cost premium. Finally, review the [index provider's](/index-provider/) ([Nasdaq's](/nasdaq/)) description of how the SMART methodology is constructed and rebalanced.

As always, small-cap positions in a portfolio tend to perform best when held with a long horizon and an understanding that volatility and periods of underperformance are routine features of the asset class.
