Streamex Corp. is a technology company that has pivoted from an earlier identity to focus on tokenization and digitalization of real-world assets, with emphasis on the commodity complex. The company operates a technology platform designed to allow institutional investors to buy, hold, and trade tokenized versions of physical commodities and commodity-linked financial instruments — gold, silver, copper, royalties, streams, and eventually oil and gas. The ambition is to build an on-chain commodities capital markets infrastructure that mirrors the structure and liquidity of traditional commodity markets but settles and operates via blockchain technology.

The company operates in a regulatory space that sits between traditional finance and emerging cryptocurrency infrastructure. Tokenization means converting ownership rights to a real-world asset — such as a bar of gold, a stream of cash flows from a mining royalty, or future production from an oil field — into digital tokens issued on a blockchain. Those tokens can be bought, sold, and held by investors in a wallet rather than stored in a vault or held in a brokerage account. The legal and tax mechanics are complex: tokenization does not change the underlying asset's legal status, but it changes how that asset is traded and settled.

Streamex's primary offering has been tokenized gold, marketed under the product name it operates. The company sells tokens backed by physical gold stored in vaults, marketed to institutional investors and accredited individuals who want exposure to gold without storing bullion themselves. The business model combines infrastructure (the blockchain platform), curation (identifying which assets to tokenize and on what terms), custody (arranging for physical storage or cashflow verification for the underlying assets), and marketing (selling the tokens into the market).

The appeal to investors is liquidity and accessibility. Traditionally, owning a precious-metals streaming asset — a contractual right to receive a portion of future gold production from a mine in exchange for advance capital — requires a large minimum investment, a relationships with investment banks or specialized advisors, and patience in a relatively illiquid market. Tokenization aims to lower the minimum investment size, allow fractional ownership, and offer daily trading rather than buy-and-hold-only options. The appeal to asset originators — mining companies, producers, investors with commodity assets — is access to new capital pools and faster execution.

The regulatory environment is still forming. The [Securities and Exchange Commission](/securities-and-exchange-commission/) has not issued a definitive framework for commodity tokenization, meaning Streamex and its peers operate under legal uncertainty. The company is likely subject to securities laws to the extent its tokens are deemed securities, commodities regulations to the extent it involves commodity derivatives, and banking regulations if it holds customer assets. State-level money transmission licenses may apply. Taxation is murky: whether token holders owe capital-gains tax only upon eventual sale, or upon each daily trading event, remains an open question depending on the token's legal character.

Streamex entered the blockchain space at a moment when cryptocurrency asset classes were attracting institutional capital while remaining heavily scrutinized by regulators. The 2023 failures of FTX and other crypto platforms created caution around unproven blockchain businesses and custody arrangements, despite Streamex using traditional precious-metals vaults rather than proprietary custody. That scrutiny affects customer [acquisition](/acquisition/) and the willingness of traditional institutional investors to allocate capital.

In May 2026, Streamex announced acquisition of a 9.9% equity stake in Empress Royalty Corp., a mining-royalty company. This move signals a strategic intention to deepen relationships with commodity originators and potentially integrate upstream into asset sourcing. Empress Royalty holds royalty interests in producing mines; partnering with Empress could give Streamex easier access to high-quality streaming and royalty assets to tokenize.

The company's roadmap through 2026 and 2027 includes expanding its tokenized commodity offerings beyond gold. Planned products include tokenized silver with yield, copper with yield, royalties and streams, and a pilot program in tokenized oil and gas starting in 2027 or 2028. Yield in this context means the tokens will pay monthly or quarterly cash distributions from production royalties or commodity purchases, mimicking the cash-flow characteristics of owning the underlying assets. This is a significant expansion: integrating oil and gas into the platform would require solving new regulatory challenges specific to energy trading and potentially interacting with the U.S. [Commodity Futures Trading Commission](/commodity-futures-trading-commission/).

Operationally, Streamex had accumulated roughly $45.85 million in cash as of the first quarter of 2026 with zero debt, suggesting the company has been funded through prior capital raises and has not required [debt financing](/debt-financing/). That cash position funds operations, product development, and customer acquisition. A development-stage technology company in an emerging market category faces the strategic question of whether to rush to market scale or maintain discipline on spending while building product and regulatory relationships. Streamex's cash burn and capital allocation strategy will be critical to whether the company reaches profitability before capital is exhausted.

The competitive landscape includes both traditional finance incumbents exploring blockchain and pure-play fintech tokenization platforms. Institutional investors are evaluating multiple providers; the market may not bear many winners. Regulatory clarity, either through SEC and CFTC guidance or through hostile enforcement, will likely force consolidation. Companies that build strong relationships with miners, institutional investors, and regulators may survive. Those that burn through capital on features the market does not value may not.
