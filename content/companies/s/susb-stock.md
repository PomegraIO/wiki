SUSB is a passive [bond](/bond/) fund sponsored by [BlackRock](/blk-stock/) that tracks an index of short-term investment-grade corporate debt. Maturity range: 1 to 5 years. Universe: U.S. dollar-denominated bonds. Filter: issuers with positive ESG characteristics in aggregate. The fund holds many individual bonds — the portfolio is typically broad, with no single issuer taking up a large weight.

The bond market naturally favors passive tracking more than stocks do. [Corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/) have no earnings surprises or sudden news shocks; they have coupons (interest payments) and maturity dates that are fixed in advance. Holding a representative sample of bonds across the index is straightforward and low-cost. SUSB's approach aligns perfectly with this logic: it replicates a published index of ESG-screened short-term corporate bonds, minimizing trading and letting yield and maturity distributions do the work.

The 1–5 year maturity band is conservative. Bonds in this range have lower price swings than longer-dated bonds when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) move. If rates rise, a 5-year bond loses value, but less sharply than a 20-year bond would. Conversely, upside from falling rates is capped — longer bonds see bigger price gains. Short-term corporates are therefore a middle ground: modestly higher yield than money-market funds or short-term Treasuries, but less [interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/) than longer bonds.

Investment-grade rating means the bonds come from companies with low default risk — roughly, those rated BBB or higher by the major rating agencies. The ESG screen removes issuers with severe environmental controversies, poor labor practices, or governance failures. This screen is applied at the portfolio level, meaning the index targets bonds from companies that exhibit positive ESG characteristics in aggregate, not necessarily that every single issuer is pristine on every dimension.

Cost is very low. SUSB's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) — the annual fee to hold it — is a fraction of a percent, well below actively managed bond funds. Liquidity is good: bonds are traded over-the-counter, but a fund holding hundreds of bonds can trade large blocks without much friction. The fund pays distributions monthly, common for bond funds, which return a blend of coupon income and any realized gains or losses.

Key risks. [Credit risk](/credit-risk/), though low, is present: even investment-grade issuers occasionally default or are downgraded. Interest-rate risk is moderate but real; if the [Federal Reserve](/federal-reserve/) raises rates sharply, all bonds suffer, but short-term corporates suffer less than longer-dated ones. ESG screens are themselves imperfect; a company flagged as ESG-conscious today might face sudden scandal or regulatory action tomorrow. Tracking error — the difference between the fund's return and the index it tracks — should be minimal, typically under 0.10% annually.

The fund suits conservative income investors who want exposure to corporate-bond returns but lack the expertise to build a bond portfolio and want ESG alignment as a value-signaling mechanism. It is not a growth vehicle; it is a steady-income, capital-preservation tool. For researchers, the index prospectus and methodology, available from BlackRock's website, detail exactly which bonds are included and the ESG criteria applied. Monthly fact sheets list top issuers and sector weights. Trailing yields and durations are published regularly, giving you a snapshot of current portfolio characteristics.
