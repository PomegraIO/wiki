ProShares Ultra Cloud Computing (SKYU) is a 2X leveraged exchange-traded fund that aims to deliver twice the daily return of an index of cloud computing and infrastructure stocks. The fund is issued by ProShares, one of the largest sponsors of leveraged products in the United States, and trades on an exchange like any stock.

The mechanics rest on derivatives rather than direct stock ownership. SKYU uses equity index futures and total-return swaps to construct twice-daily exposure to its underlying cloud index without actually holding the stocks. This derivative-based approach is more efficient than borrowing cash to buy additional shares would be. Every day at market close, the fund rebalances its positions to ensure that the next day's return profile is precisely 2X the index's return. This daily reset is essential to the fund's construction but creates a mathematical problem called volatility decay.

The underlying index includes the major cloud infrastructure and software-as-a-service companies: Amazon, [Microsoft](/msft-stock/), Google, [Salesforce](/crm-stock/), [ServiceNow](/now-stock/), [Datadog](/ddog-stock/), and peers. The index is market-cap weighted, so the largest companies dominate. The sector spans cloud servers and storage, productivity platforms, cybersecurity, and analytics — all businesses dependent on enterprise digitization and cloud adoption.

Volatility decay is the core structural issue. Suppose the cloud index rises 10 percent on day one, then falls 10 percent on day two, returning to its starting value. SKYU, rebalancing daily, rises 20 percent on day one (2X the plus 10), then falls 20 percent on day two (2X the minus 10). The arithmetic: $100 × 1.20 × 0.80 = $96. SKYU ends at $96 even though the underlying index is back to $100. The fund lost money while the index broke even. This erosion compounds during choppy or sideways markets. High volatility accelerates the decay.

During sustained rallies in cloud stocks, the leverage amplifies gains. A 10 percent index rally translates to roughly 20 percent for SKYU. During sustained declines, losses are amplified in kind: a 20 percent index drop causes roughly a 40 percent fund loss. The fund works well in directional markets. It bleeds value in choppy, sideways, or volatile-but-flat conditions.

SKYU carries an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) reflecting the cost of derivatives and daily rebalancing, typically around 0.95 percent annually or higher. This drag matters little over days or weeks but accumulates significantly over months and years. The fund trades during market hours with reasonable liquidity on normal trading days. During market stress, when volatility spikes and fear dominates, liquidity can evaporate and [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) can widen sharply.

The fund is designed for short-term tactical use — days or weeks — not buy-and-hold investing. An investor wanting long-term exposure to cloud computing should buy an unlevered cloud ETF or individual cloud stocks directly. Suitable investors are active traders who expect the cloud sector to rally significantly in the near term and who want to amplify that upside through leverage. They should size positions carefully, understand that losses compound as fast as gains, and have a clear exit plan.

To research SKYU, start with the prospectus and fact sheet, which detail the index composition, the leverage mechanics, the daily reset process, and the fee structure. Historical data showing performance across different market regimes — rising, falling, volatile, sideways — illustrates the volatility decay effect in action. Comparing the fund's one-week, one-month, three-month, and one-year returns against the unlevered cloud index shows how much decay has cost in each environment. Examining which specific companies dominate the underlying index reveals whether the cloud exposure aligns with the investor's thesis. Finally, checking the [implied volatility](/implied-volatility/) of equity derivatives and the structure of the underlying futures curve informs whether now is a tactically opportune or dangerous time to enter a leveraged position.
