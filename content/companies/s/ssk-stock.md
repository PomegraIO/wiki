> "Staking lets you earn yield on cryptocurrency holdings by participating in network consensus. SSK packages that into a regulated ETF."

**SSK** (REX-Osprey SOL + Staking ETF) is an exchange-traded fund designed to give investors exposure to the Solana cryptocurrency and the staking income it can generate. Rather than holding bare Solana tokens in a self-custodied wallet, investors buy shares of SSK to hold Solana through a regulated fund structure, and the fund earns staking rewards by participating in the Solana network's proof-of-stake consensus mechanism. Those rewards are reinvested or distributed, adding a yield layer on top of any appreciation in Solana's price.

## What is staking, and why does it matter?

Proof-of-stake blockchains like Solana operate differently from proof-of-work networks such as [Bitcoin](/bitcoin/). Instead of miners solving computational puzzles to earn rewards, proof-of-stake networks rely on validators — participants who lock up their tokens to help secure the network and validate transactions. In exchange for that locked capital and the work of validating, validators earn new tokens issued by the network and fees paid by users. This income is called staking yield or staking rewards.

For an investor, staking means putting your tokens into a validator's pool (or operating your own validator, which requires technical expertise) and receiving a share of the network rewards. Solana's staking yield has historically ranged from roughly 5% to 8% per year, depending on how many tokens are staked network-wide and what rewards the protocol issues. That yield is attractive because it arrives regardless of price movement — your tokens earn whether Solana rises or falls.

## How SSK differs from holding Solana directly

An investor could buy Solana tokens from an exchange and stake them through a validator or service. SSK offers a structured alternative: the fund holds Solana, operates or delegates to validators to stake the tokens, and reinvests the staking rewards back into more Solana. By holding it through an ETF, an investor avoids managing private keys, selecting validators, understanding custody, or worrying about technical slashing (the penalty validators face if they misbehave). The trade-off is an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) — typically 0.50% to 1.00% annually — and the loss of direct custody control.

## The risks peculiar to crypto and staking

Solana's price is volatile. Cryptocurrency markets move sharply in both directions, often on news or sentiment rather than fundamental economic data. A holder of SSK gains that volatility exposure — the fund's value can swing 20%, 30%, or more in short periods. This is far riskier than holding a diversified stock or [bond](/bond/) portfolio.

Staking introduces additional risks. If the Solana network suffers a major outage, slashing event, or loss of developer interest, the value of staked tokens could plummet and staking yield could evaporate. Regulatory risk is also material: governments are still deciding how to treat cryptocurrency, staking, and the taxation of staking rewards. A major regulatory change could damage staking incentives or Solana's broader ecosystem.

## Who considers a Solana staking ETF?

SSK suits investors who believe in Solana's long-term viability as a blockchain platform, want exposure to its token price appreciation, and want to earn the additional return that staking provides — all within a regulated, custody-free structure. It appeals less to those uncomfortable with extreme volatility or those convinced cryptocurrency is overvalued.

It is also useful for retirement-account holders who want crypto exposure but cannot easily hold crypto in some account types; an ETF is more portable and compatible with traditional [custodians](/custodian/) than directly held tokens.

## Costs and tax treatment

The fund carries an expense ratio covering the cost of operating validators, custody, and administration. Staking rewards, once earned and reinvested, are treated as ordinary income for tax purposes in most jurisdictions, even before the tokens are sold. This can create a tax liability annually if you receive staking distributions, even if the price of Solana itself falls — an unusual feature compared to traditional equities, where you have no tax liability until you sell.

Consult a tax professional if staking income is a new consideration for you; the treatment varies by country and is still evolving.

## Comparing alternatives

A direct holder of Solana tokens can also stake through services like Marinade Finance or Lido to earn staking yield, though this introduces additional [counterparty risk](/counterparty-risk/) (if the staking service fails or is hacked, the holder is exposed). Investors who already hold Solana might prefer those options to save on the ETF's fee. Those starting from scratch and wanting maximum simplicity and regulatory clarity favor an ETF.

## How to evaluate SSK

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet to understand the current staking yield being earned and the expense ratio eating into it. Review Solana's developer ecosystem and network health — these determine whether the blockchain will remain valuable over time. Monitor Solana Foundation announcements and on-chain metrics (transaction volume, validator participation, token price) to gauge the network's trajectory.

Understand that buying SSK is an explicit bet on Solana's long-term relevance and price appreciation. It is not a diversified cryptocurrency exposure; it is a single-token bet. Consider whether your risk tolerance matches the volatility, and whether your portfolio can afford a small, high-risk allocation without materially affecting your financial goals. Cryptocurrency remains a frontier asset; approach it as such.
