*The **VictoryShares Small Cap [Free Cash Flow](/free-cash-flow/) ETF** (ticker SFLO) is a rules-based, [passively managed fund](/passively-managed-fund/) that holds a diversified portfolio of small-capitalization U.S. companies ranked and weighted by their ability to generate free cash flow — cash left over after a company pays for essential operations and capital maintenance.*

## Strategy and construction

SFLO tracks the [Nasdaq](/nasdaq/) Victory Small Cap Free Cash Flow Index, which applies a quantitative screening process to the universe of small-cap U.S. stocks. The index methodology ranks companies primarily on their generation of free cash flow — a metric that strips away accounting discretion and shows real cash available for [dividends](/dividend/), debt reduction, [acquisitions](/acquisition/), or reinvestment. Rather than weighting all eligible stocks equally or by [market cap](/market-capitalization/), the fund gives larger weights to companies with stronger free cash flow profiles, creating a portfolio biased toward small-cap firms that generate surplus cash relative to their size.

The index screens out stocks with negative or minimal free cash flow, effectively filtering for companies that are already profitable and self-sustaining in operating cash terms. This systematic approach aims to reduce exposure to unprofitable growth companies that burn cash, a feature appealing to investors who view cash generation as a sign of true business health in the small-cap space, where accounting quality and business durability vary widely.

## What it holds and how it behaves

The portfolio typically holds between 80 and 100 stocks, each a small-cap company that meets the index's free cash flow criteria. Because the index rebalances periodically and weights positions by their relative cash-generation strength, the fund is concentrated enough to feel like an active bet on small-cap quality, yet transparent enough that investors can review the exact holdings and understand the rules driving selection. Holdings span sectors — industrials, technology, consumer, healthcare — wherever small companies are generating significant free cash.

Small-cap stocks are inherently more volatile than large-cap peers, and SFLO carries that volatility. However, the focus on cash-generating businesses rather than momentum or sentiment-driven names often dampens that volatility somewhat relative to broad small-cap indices, particularly during periods when investors grow skeptical of unprofitable firms. The fund's performance is driven by whether its holdings' free cash flow strength translates into stock price appreciation — a thesis that depends on the market recognizing and rewarding cash generation over longer periods.

## Costs and tradability

SFLO carries an annual [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) in the low-0.3% range, in line with other passive small-cap [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/). The fund trades on a major U.S. exchange with typical daily volume, so transaction costs are minimal for ordinary investors. The underlying index is rebalanced semiannually or periodically enough to avoid excessive turnover while keeping the portfolio aligned with the free cash flow methodology.

## Risks and who this is for

The primary risk is concentration in small-cap equities. These companies have less financial cushion, smaller analyst coverage, and more vulnerable competitive positions than their large-cap counterparts. A downturn in small-cap sentiment can depress SFLO regardless of the free cash flow quality of its holdings. Additionally, the free cash flow metric itself can be volatile — a company with strong cash in one year may see it decline sharply if capital expenditures rise or working capital swells.

Because the fund screens for current cash generation, it will miss high-growth small caps that are reinvesting all free cash back into the business, a common trait of successful technology startups and emerging industrials. Investors seeking [diversification](/diversification/) into small-cap profitability — and willing to accept the risks inherent in that segment — might use SFLO as part of a broader portfolio rather than a core holding. The fund is best suited for those who view small-cap free cash flow as a legitimate quality and durability signal, have a medium- to long-term time horizon, and understand that small-cap equity returns are neither predictable nor smoother than those of larger firms.
