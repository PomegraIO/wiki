*The [State Street](/stt-stock/) SPDR S&P 600 Small Cap Value ETF (SLYV) is a [passively managed fund](/passively-managed-fund/) holding small-cap U.S. companies selected for value characteristics — lower [earnings multiples](/earnings-multiple/), higher [dividend yields](/dividend-yield/), and stronger book-value ratios compared to growth peers. With roughly 460 holdings and $4 billion in assets, SLYV is one of the larger value-focused small-cap vehicles.*

## The value subset

SLYV carves out the value stocks from the S&P SmallCap 600 universe using S&P's proprietary screens. A company qualifies for value status if it exhibits lower price-to-earnings, price-to-book, and [price-to-sales ratios](/price-to-sales-ratio/) alongside higher [dividend](/dividend/) yield relative to peers in its market-cap band. The result is a portfolio of smaller, more mature or cyclical companies — regional manufacturers, healthcare services providers, financial firms — that trade cheaper than the market values them.

The fund is market-cap-weighted, so larger small-caps (those near $1 billion) represent bigger slices than smaller holdings. This weighting structure means the fund is not equally concentrated; the top five holdings together represent roughly 4–5% of assets, keeping any single name from outsized influence.

## Holdings and sector tilt

Top holdings have included [Eastman Chemical](/emn-stock/), Match Group, LKQ Corporation (automotive parts), [Molina Healthcare](/moh-stock/), and Jackson Financial — a mix of industrials, healthcare, consumer, and financials. Unlike SLYG (which leans toward growth sectors like technology and healthcare growth), SLYV's value mandate pulls it toward cyclical and income-generating sectors. Financials, industrials, and consumer staples tend to be overweight in SLYV relative to the overall market; technology is underweight.

Annual rebalancing and index methodology changes rotate companies in and out. A small-cap company can grow into mid-cap range and exit the index; another hits hard times, has its valuation crushed, and becomes newly eligible for the value slice. This turnover is built into the index construction, not driven by active trading on SLYV's part.

## Comparing SLYG and SLYV

SLYG and SLYV together approximate the entire S&P SmallCap 600 Index by sector and company characteristic. Over any given year, one dramatically outperforms the other depending on whether the market favours growth or value. In years when growth leads, SLYG rises faster; when value rebounds, SLYV catches up. A holder of both experiences less volatility than holding either alone, at the cost of neither spectacular gains nor dramatic declines. Investors must decide whether they are making a bet on value outperformance or seeking broad small-cap exposure; that choice determines whether to hold SLYV alone or pair it with SLYG.

## Value small-cap volatility and yield

SLYV's value focus brings higher dividend yield than SLYG, often in the 2–4% range depending on market conditions, but slower price appreciation and lower volatility in absolute terms. That said, small-cap value stocks are still volatile; SLYV is not a low-volatility fund. The trade-off is real: investors accept the volatility of small stocks but position the fund toward companies that may be out of favour, hoping that value characteristics — low multiples, strong [balance sheets](/balance-sheet/), cash-generation capability — eventually lead to outperformance. This is a bet that the market will eventually recognize these companies' worth.

Tax consequences matter for SLYV in taxable accounts. The dividend yield means tax drag from regular income; high turnover in the underlying index (which can be substantial when index membership changes) generates capital gains. Holding SLYV in tax-deferred accounts (IRAs, 401(k)s) eliminates this friction.

## How to research SLYV

Review SLYV's fact sheet and holdings list on State Street's website and Morningstar. The S&P SmallCap 600 Value Index methodology is public and maintained by S&P Dow Jones. Compare SLYV's annual returns and dividend yield against competitors like the Vanguard Small-Cap Value ETF (VBR) and the iShares Russell 2000 Value ETF (IWN), which track similar but not identical universes. Check the fund's tracking error (the annual difference between SLYV's return and the index return), which should be minimal. Review the fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) relative to peers, and note any changes in turnover year to year that might indicate unplanned redemptions or index methodology shifts. For sector exposure and forward dividend estimates, examine quarterly reports published by State Street. Compare the fund's tax efficiency by looking at distributions year-over-year; if distributions spike, it may signal large [index reconstitutions](/index-reconstitution/) or significant redemption activity that forced taxable sales.
