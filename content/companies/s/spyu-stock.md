*The **MAX S&P 500 4X Leveraged ETN** is not a [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/) or a traditional ETF. It is a debt instrument — a note issued by Société Générale that tracks the [S&P 500 index](/sp-500-index/) but amplified by a factor of four, meaning it aims to deliver four times the index's daily return. It is built for traders betting on rapid S&P 500 moves, not for long-term investors.*

## From creation to maturity

The fund was created and issued by Société Générale, a major French bank, and trades on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) exchange like a regular security. Unlike a conventional ETF, which technically has no expiration, this note has a hard maturity date: October 30, 2043. On that date, the issuer will redeem the outstanding notes, paying holders their final value based on the S&P 500's level at that time. Until then, the notes trade in the open market, and their price can drift from their theoretical value based on supply and demand, the creditworthiness of Société Générale, and wider market sentiment.

That maturity date is a two-decade away for most investors, but it exists. Société Générale is obligated to repay the note's value at maturity; if the issuer were to fail, note holders would become unsecured creditors, a significant risk that distinguishes this product from a traditional fund.

## The 4x leverage mechanism

The note is designed to deliver four times the S&P 500's daily return. If the S&P 500 rises one percent on a given day, the note aims to rise roughly four percent. Conversely, if the market falls one percent, the note aims to fall about four percent. This is accomplished through derivatives — principally [futures contracts](/futures-contract/) and swaps — that concentrate the economic exposure.

Leverage is not free. The note charges fees to cover the costs of holding and rebalancing derivatives positions, and these drag on returns. The larger drag, though, is the mathematical effect of daily reset compounding. Because the note resets its leverage to 4x at the end of each trading day, it compounds over time, creating volatility decay — a phenomenon where the note's return over longer periods diverges from four times the index's buy-and-hold return. In a sideways or choppy market, that decay accelerates. The note is thus built for short-term trades and rapid market moves, not for holding through quiet periods.

## Who this is for and the real risks

This product is designed for traders and sophisticated investors with high risk tolerance who believe the market is about to move sharply in one direction and want to magnify their bet. A small percentage move in the S&P 500 becomes a substantial percentage swing in the note, which appeals to those seeking outsized gains in a compressed timeframe.

The list of genuine risks is substantial. First, the daily-reset leverage means that in a choppy market environment, the note underperforms four times the buy-and-hold S&P 500 return — sometimes significantly. A market that rises and falls repeatedly at the same levels can see the note lose value even as the index ends where it started. Second, the note carries issuer [credit risk](/credit-risk/) — if Société Générale were to encounter serious financial trouble, note holders would face potential haircuts or default, unlike owners of a conventional ETF backed by actual assets. Third, a catastrophic market decline could wipe out the note's value entirely, while the S&P 500 itself cannot fall below zero. Fourth, the note's liquidity, while generally adequate, can tighten during market stress, making it harder to exit at favorable prices.

## Structure and research

The note is technically debt, so it appears on the issuer's [balance sheet](/balance-sheet/) as a liability. Investors considering it should review Société Générale's financial stability and [credit ratings](/credit-rating/). The prospectus details the leverage mechanism, daily reset process, fees, and the maturity date. Historical performance data is publicly available and will show the compounding drag in different market regimes.

Because this is a time-bound instrument with a 2043 maturity, investors should think carefully about the time horizon. Someone holding this note intending to pass it to heirs as a long-term investment is likely to be disappointed; the leverage decay and issuer risk make it a tactical tool, not an estate asset. Most serious uses involve active trading or hedging strategies that benefit from the amplified daily moves.
