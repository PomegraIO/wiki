The BNY Mellon High Yield ETF (BKHY) offers liquid, low-cost access to the high-yield (or junk-bond) segment of the US corporate debt market. For investors willing to take on credit risk in exchange for higher current income, it provides transparent diversification across hundreds of speculative-grade issuers in a single security that trades on an exchange like a stock.

## What does BKHY track and hold?

BKHY seeks to track the performance of the Bloomberg US Corporate High Yield Bond Index, a broad, market-weighted benchmark of non-investment-grade corporate bonds issued by US and foreign companies. High-yield bonds are debt instruments rated below investment grade (typically BB and below by Standard & Poor's, or Ba and below by Moody's) — the companies that issue them either cannot access investment-grade capital markets or choose not to, because the coupon they must offer investors is higher. BKHY's portfolio holds hundreds of individual bonds across dozens of industries: energy, telecommunications, healthcare services, retail, manufacturing, and financials. The fund holds the actual bonds rather than futures or swaps, so an investor buying BKHY receives a proportional slice of the underlying debt securities.

## Why hold high-yield bonds?

High-yield bonds offer substantially higher current yield than investment-grade debt or US Treasury bonds, reflecting the greater risk that an issuer could default or be unable to pay interest on time. A high-yield issuer might be a mature company in a cyclical or pressured industry, a leveraged-out firm carrying heavy debt loads from a buyout, or a younger business still building its credit profile. The trade-off is explicit: investors accept greater default risk and volatility in exchange for a meaningful income stream. In a stable economic environment, the risk premium works in the bondholder's favour. In a recession or credit crunch, high-yield spreads widen sharply and prices fall. BKHY is designed for investors who understand this cycle and want broad exposure to the entire high-yield market rather than picking individual credits.

## Who issues BKHY and what does it cost?

BNY Mellon is one of the world's largest asset managers and custodians, with decades of experience managing fixed-income portfolios for institutions. The fund carries an expense ratio that is competitive within the high-yield ETF category, making it an accessible entry point for retail and institutional investors alike. High-yield bonds trade on decentralised over-the-counter markets with wide bid-ask spreads, but BKHY's size and liquidity mean that shares trade on the exchange with tight spreads — generally far tighter than buying individual bonds directly. This liquidity premium is one of the key reasons to own the ETF rather than a basket of individual bonds.

## What are the real risks?

Credit risk is the primary driver of returns and losses. When the economy slows or a sector faces disruption, high-yield bond prices tend to fall because investors demand higher yields to compensate for rising default risk. A single large default or a wave of defaults can drag the entire fund down, though diversification across many issuers and industries limits any single company's impact. High-yield bonds also carry interest-rate risk: when US Treasury yields rise, bond prices fall, and BKHY will mark down in tandem. Because high-yield bonds already trade with wide spreads, the combined effect of rising rates and widening credit spreads during a rate-hiking cycle can be painful.

The fund also carries what is sometimes called "spread risk" — the risk that the gap between high-yield and Treasury yields widens without any change in the Treasury rate itself. This is a pure credit-cycle phenomenon and is endemic to high-yield investing.

## Who is BKHY for?

BKHY suits investors with medium to long time horizons who want fixed-income exposure beyond investment-grade bonds and who understand credit cycles. It is commonly used in balanced portfolios as a satellite holding — a smaller position that adds yield without dominating the risk profile of the entire portfolio. Active traders and those hedging portfolios sometimes sell BKHY or high-yield ETFs outright to reduce risk when credit conditions look fragile. Institutional investors also use BKHY as a core holding for dedicated high-yield sleeves.

## How to research BKHY as an investor

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet on the BNY Mellon website, which detail the underlying index methodology, the top ten holdings, sector weightings, and the weighted average credit rating of the portfolio. The prospectus also sets out the fund's fees. Pay attention to the yield distribution over time — high-yield funds that report unusually elevated yields relative to peers may be reaching for return in ways that increase risk. The Bloomberg website publishes live data on the underlying index: credit spreads (the difference in yield between high-yield and Treasury bonds), default rates, and sector positioning. When spreads are near historic lows, high-yield valuations are typically stretched; when spreads are wide, credit has often already sold off and better entry points may have passed. Finally, the US Federal Reserve and major financial media track high-yield debt metrics and credit conditions closely — understanding the broader credit cycle is essential to knowing when holding BKHY is opportune and when it is hazardous.
