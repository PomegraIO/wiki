> *The fund is rooted in a conviction that structural currency devaluation is a defining feature of modern monetary policy.*

BPRO is an exchange-traded fund issued by Bitwise Asset Management that takes a thematic approach to investing. Rather than passively tracking an index or actively picking individual stocks, BPRO is built around a macroeconomic thesis: that fiat currencies (dollars, euros, yen, and others) are being systematically debased through monetary expansion and [central bank](/central-bank/) intervention, and that investors should position for that reality. The fund holds a diversified mix of assets believed to benefit from or preserve value in an environment of currency debasement — which typically includes commodities, inflation-linked securities, and alternative assets.

## What does "currency debasement" mean as an investment thesis?

Currency debasement refers to the erosion of a currency's purchasing power through increases in the money supply or other central bank actions. Central banks expand the money supply when they conduct [quantitative easing](/quantitative-easing/), lower [interest rates](/interest-rate/), or engage in other expansionary policies. If the money supply grows faster than the supply of real goods, prices rise and each unit of currency buys less. Investors concerned about debasement fear that nominal returns (the numbers on a statement) will feel large while real returns (what you can actually buy with that money) will be muted by [inflation](/inflation/). Those who share this view often allocate to assets expected to hold or appreciate in real terms — commodities like oil, gold, and agricultural products; inflation-indexed [bonds](/bond/); and real assets — rather than bonds or cash, which decline in value if inflation erodes the currency.

## What does BPRO hold?

The exact holdings depend on Bitwise's interpretation of which assets best hedge debasement risk. Historically, such funds have allocated to commodities ([crude oil](/crude-oil/), [natural gas](/natural-gas/), metals, agricultural futures), commodity producers (mining companies, energy firms), inflation-linked securities (TIPS in the US, similar instruments in other countries), real estate and infrastructure, and sometimes alternative assets like cryptocurrency. The portfolio is designed to be diversified across multiple inflation hedges so that if one asset class underperforms, others offset it.

## The debasement thesis and its critics

Investors who embrace the debasement thesis point to decades of central bank money printing, persistently low [real interest rates](/real-interest-rate/), and the risk that accumulated [public debt](/public-debt/) (in the US and elsewhere) will be partially "inflated away" — repaid with cheaper dollars. They argue that conventional bonds and money-market funds offer insufficient protection in such a world because their returns are fixed in nominal terms while inflation eats away purchasing power.

Critics counter that central banks have considerable independence and that many have demonstrated commitment to inflation targets over long periods. They also note that debasement concerns have been periodically raised over decades yet, in many developed economies, inflation has remained moderate for long stretches. Betting heavily on debasement — via commodities or alternative assets that are historically volatile and prone to their own boom-bust cycles — can backfire if inflation remains contained and those hedges underperform for years.

## How BPRO fits in a portfolio

BPRO is typically used as a satellite position within a diversified portfolio — a concentrated bet on a specific macro scenario rather than a core holding. An investor might own a broad [index fund](/index-fund/) as the anchor, then add BPRO as a hedge against [stagflation](/stagflation/) (slow growth plus rising inflation) or as an expression of conviction that [monetary policy](/monetary-policy/) is unsustainably expansionary. Because commodity-heavy portfolios and inflation hedges are volatile and often move opposite to traditional stocks and bonds, they can dampen portfolio swings in certain environments, though they can also lag for years if inflation stays dormant.

## Costs and considerations

BPRO is traded on a US exchange as a standard ETF, so it offers [intraday liquidity](/intraday-liquidity/) and transparency. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) reflects the cost of active management and rebalancing across the underlying assets. The fund's prospectus and fact sheet detail the specific [asset allocation](/asset-allocation/) and the rebalancing frequency. Investors should monitor whether the portfolio weighting aligns with their own view of debasement risk and whether the assets held are providing the inflation protection they expect. Watch the fund's returns in both inflationary and disinflationary environments to assess whether the thesis is playing out as intended. Compare BPRO's performance against a simple commodities index or inflation-linked bond fund to see whether active management adds value.
