## The setup

BOEU is a leveraged fund issued by Direxion. It aims for 2X the daily move of [Boeing](/ba-stock/) stock. Boeing up 1%, BOEU targets +2%. Boeing down 1%, BOEU targets -2%. The mechanism: Direxion borrows capital, buys Boeing shares on margin, rebalances daily to maintain the 2X ratio. It is a blunt instrument — single-stock leverage with no hedging, no portfolio [diversification](/diversification/), no soften-the-blow features.

## Daily rebalancing in practice

Each trading day close, the fund adjusts its Boeing holdings to reset leverage. This is mechanical and necessary to keep the promise of 2X daily tracking. What it means in practice: on big Boeing moves, the fund's actual return will trail 2X the theoretical move because of how the math compounds. Volatile sideways action erodes value fastest. A stock that swings 5% up, then 5% down in consecutive days — which to a simple shareholder feels like flatness — will drag BOEU down meaningfully through the combination of rebalancing and decay.

## The volatility decay tax

Over weeks or months, decay accumulates. A Boeing move up then down (which nets zero) leaves BOEU down by a real, measurable amount. The longer you hold, the more this compounds against you unless Boeing is in a sustained bull move. Most leveraged funds disclose decay explicitly in their materials. For BOEU, the prospectus and fact sheet lay out the mechanics. The lesson: this is not an investment. It is a trade.

## Performance profile

BOEU shines on sharp upside moves. A 10% Boeing rally over a few days is a 19–20% gain for BOEU (less decay). On a 10% drop, BOEU falls 19–20%. The leverage cuts both ways at scale. Hold through chop, and both decay and the fund's own operating costs (around 0.95% annually in [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/)) quietly eat into nominal gains.

## Who uses this

Day traders, swing traders, and directional bettors with a multi-day Boeing thesis. Not for core portfolio exposure. Not for retirement accounts where the time horizon makes decay a near-certainty. Not for investors uncomfortable with losing the entire position in a worst-case crash.

## Research and monitoring

Prospectus is essential. Understand the daily reset explicitly. If you own BOEU, track decay versus 2X the underlying Boeing move to see the cost directly. Set exit criteria before entry — a profit target or a stop loss — because leverage amplifies both winners and losers, and conviction drifting into a losing position becomes expensive fast. Check Boeing's business fundamentals and news flow before trading, since a single negative announcement can cut the stock sharply and magnify through the leverage into a painful day.

## The structural reality

Leverage decays predictably. The fund's only advantage to Boeing stock itself is the 2X magnification on upside moves. That advantage vanishes and reverses if the stock stalls or chops. The volatility decay formula is well understood and can be modeled — you can actually predict roughly how much decay will accrue given a stock's [historical volatility](/historical-volatility/). If that math does not favor you, the trade is not attractive. This is a tactical vehicle for tactical thinking only.
