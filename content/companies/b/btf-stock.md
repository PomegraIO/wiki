## What the fund holds

The CoinShares [Bitcoin](/bitcoin/) and Ether ETF keeps things simple: it holds Bitcoin and Ether in roughly equal proportions, approximately 50% each by weight, and rebalances periodically. Both are physical holdings, meaning the fund actually owns the underlying cryptocurrencies rather than buying [futures contracts](/futures-contract/) or synthetic derivatives that track them. The digital assets are held in custody by professional crypto-focused [custodians](/custodian/), with safeguards against loss, theft, or operational failure—though the cryptocurrency landscape is younger and less regulated than traditional finance, and custody risk remains a structural consideration.

## Exposure without friction

For institutional and retail investors accustomed to brokerage accounts, the attraction is friction reduction. Buying Bitcoin or Ether directly requires opening an account on a [cryptocurrency exchange](/cryptocurrency-exchange/), learning unfamiliar security practices (safeguarding private keys, managing two-factor authentication for a novel platform), and navigating tax and accounting complexity. An ETF wrapper eliminates that friction: you buy BTF shares through your regular brokerage, settle in Canadian or [U.S. dollars](/us-dollar/) depending on the listing, and hold it in an RRSP, TFSA, or taxable account just as you would any [equity ETF](/equity-etf/).

This accessibility has lowered the barrier for institutional investors—pension funds, endowments, and asset managers—who previously faced operational and fiduciary hurdles to direct crypto ownership. It has also brought crypto exposure within reach of unsophisticated retail investors who might otherwise never own these assets.

## Rebalancing and the 50-50 split

The fund maintains a roughly equal weight between Bitcoin and Ether, which is an explicit strategic choice. Bitcoin is the larger and older of the two cryptocurrencies—often described as digital gold, a store of value with a fixed supply capped at 21 million coins. Ether is the native token of the [Ethereum](/ethereum/) network, a programmable blockchain that supports smart contracts and decentralized applications; Ether's supply is not capped in the same way, and its value derives more directly from network activity and demand for execution.

A 50-50 allocation is a convenient balance but carries hidden assumptions: it gives equal weight to these two very different theses and ignores the correlation between them (which is imperfect but substantial—in crypto market panics, both tend to fall together). Rebalancing quarterly means the fund will occasionally sell whichever of the two has appreciated more and use proceeds to buy the laggard. Over time, this disciplined rebalancing should dampen volatility, though in extreme bull or [bear markets](/bear-market/) it is a small effect.

## Custody, security, and counterparty risk

The fund's security depends entirely on the professional custody arrangement. CoinShares outsources custody to recognized custodians with insurance and audit-verified security protocols. But this is a meaningful [counterparty risk](/counterparty-risk/): if the custodian fails, mishandles private keys, or succumbs to a sophisticated attack, the fund's holdings are at risk. The cryptocurrency industry has experienced high-profile custody breaches and exchange collapses, and even well-managed custodians operate in a less-settled regulatory environment than traditional banks.

Insurance and bonding help but do not eliminate risk. An investor holding BTF is trusting not only the fund sponsor but the custodian and any insuring entities behind it. This is not unique to crypto ETFs—traditional ETFs also rely on custodians and settlement infrastructure—but the stakes feel different when the underlying asset is a bearer instrument whose private keys cannot be recovered if lost.

## Volatility and the boom-bust cycle

Bitcoin and Ether are volatile assets. Price swings of 10% to 20% in a single week are not uncommon; crashes of 50% or more from peak to trough have occurred multiple times. This volatility stems from the fact that these assets have no cash flows, no intrinsic yield, and no traditional valuation anchor (like earnings or book value). Price is driven almost entirely by sentiment, adoption narratives, and macroeconomic factors ([interest rates](/interest-rate/), liquidity, regulatory sentiment).

In booms—periods of risk appetite, loose credit, and tech-driven enthusiasm—Bitcoin and Ether appreciate dramatically, often outpacing any fundamental metric. In busts—regulatory crackdowns, credit crunches, or [sentiment reversals](/sentiment-reversal/)—they fall sharply and hold the losses for extended periods. An investor holding BTF should expect to experience years of strong returns and years of significant drawdown with little predictability as to which phase is coming next.

## Tax treatment and regulatory environment

The tax treatment of ETF shares depends on jurisdiction. In Canada, BTF is treated as a regular security for capital-gains purposes; in the U.S., tax treatment is complex and evolving. [Dividend](/dividend/) or distribution treatment, foreign withholding taxes, and the status of cryptocurrency itself under tax code are still being litigated and clarified by regulators globally.

More broadly, the regulatory environment for crypto and crypto-exposed funds is in flux. Some jurisdictions have welcomed crypto ETFs; others have restricted or banned them. Changes in regulation can affect the fund's ability to operate, custody arrangements, or the tax efficiency of the structure.

## How to research a crypto ETF

Start with CoinShares' detailed fact sheet, which covers the fund's composition, rebalancing rules, and expense breakdown. Review the custody agreement and the identity of the custodian, then research that custodian's history and insurance coverage. Compare BTF to competing Bitcoin and Ether ETFs to understand differences in structure (physical holdings versus derivatives), fees, and trading liquidity. Understand the underlying volatility of Bitcoin and Ether themselves—their historical drawdowns, correlation to equities and macro factors, and the regulatory risks specific to cryptocurrency. Most importantly, treat a crypto ETF as a small, high-risk, high-volatility satellite position within a broader portfolio, not a core holding, unless you have a high tolerance for loss and a long time horizon.
