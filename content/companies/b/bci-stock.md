## What the fund tracks and holds

BCI provides exposure to a diversified basket of commodity [futures contracts](/futures-contract/) selected from the Bloomberg Commodity Index. The index includes energy commodities ([crude oil](/crude-oil/), [natural gas](/natural-gas/), unleaded gasoline, [heating oil](/heating-oil/)), precious metals (gold, silver), industrial metals (copper, zinc, aluminum, nickel), and agricultural commodities (corn, wheat, sugar, cocoa, soybeans). The fund uses a systematic, rules-based weighting methodology that aims to rebalance regularly and maintain exposure across the commodity complex rather than concentrate on a single category.

Unlike equity funds, which hold shares directly, BCI does not hold physical commodities or direct commodity positions. Instead, it gains exposure through futures contracts — standardised agreements to buy or sell a commodity at a future date at a set price. The fund continuously rolls these futures positions as contracts approach expiration, maintaining ongoing exposure to the underlying commodity prices.

## The K-1 tax structure

Many [commodity index funds](/commodity-index-fund/) have historically reported using K-1 forms (Schedule K-1 of Partnership Return of Income), which are complex tax documents that require recipients to report their share of partnership income, gains, losses, and credits across many line items. This imposes a significant administrative and tax-planning burden on individual investors and their accountants. The "K-1 Free" designation in BCI's name signals that it uses a different structure — typically a grantor trust — that reports instead on 1099 forms, simplifying tax filing for most individual investors. This structural choice has made the fund attractive to taxable investors who might otherwise face complications from K-1 reporting.

## Commodity exposure as an asset class

Commodity futures offer investors a way to gain economic exposure to raw materials and energy without taking physical delivery or holding the underlying assets. Commodities are often viewed as a diversifier within a portfolio because their price movements frequently diverge from stocks and [bonds](/bond/). During periods of [inflation](/inflation/) or supply disruption, commodity prices can rise when traditional financial assets struggle, providing a hedge. Conversely, in deflationary periods or [recession](/recession/), commodities may decline sharply.

The Bloomberg Commodity Index represents a attempt at broad commodity [diversification](/diversification/) — holding multiple commodities reduces [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) on any single input or market shock. However, commodity futures investments carry distinct mechanics and risks relative to other asset classes.

## Volatility decay and rolling costs

Commodity futures prices exhibit a phenomenon called [contango](/contango/): when futures contracts far in the future trade at higher prices than near-term contracts. As a fund rolls from an expiring contract to a further-out contract, it is often selling a lower-priced contract and buying a higher-priced one, which reduces returns over time. In steep contango environments, this rolling cost can be significant. Conversely, when the market is in [backwardation](/backwardation/) (far-out contracts trading lower), rolling can add value. These mechanics are inherent to commodity futures investing and differ from simply holding the spot price of a commodity.

Commodity prices themselves are volatile. Economic cycles, geopolitical events, supply shocks, and weather can all drive sharp moves. An index-based approach mitigates concentration risk but does not eliminate commodity volatility.

## Costs and how the fund trades

BCI trades on exchange during standard market hours like any stock, so investors can buy and sell shares at live prices throughout the trading day. The fund carries an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) that covers the sponsor's costs of managing the fund, maintaining the indices, and handling the daily rolling of futures contracts. The exact cost is specified in the fund's prospectus and fact sheet.

As a fund holding commodity futures rather than equities, its trading characteristics differ from [equity ETFs](/equity-etf/). [Bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) may be wider during low-volume periods, and the fund's value moves directly with commodity futures prices, which trade on their own schedule (separate from equity market hours in many cases).

## Who the fund is for and how to research it

BCI is typically used by investors seeking direct commodity exposure within a portfolio — either as an inflation hedge, as a tactical allocation to commodities during periods of expected supply tightness, or as part of a diversified [asset allocation](/asset-allocation/). The K-1 tax simplification makes it particularly appealing to individual investors in taxable accounts.

Anyone considering BCI should begin with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, available through abrdn's website and the SEC. The prospectus details the exact composition of the Bloomberg Commodity Index, the weighting methodology, and the fund's trading and rolling procedures. Review the fund's holdings and weightings to understand which commodities dominate the portfolio. Monitor the fund's performance during different economic regimes — rising inflation, recession, and equity market stress — to assess how it correlates with your existing portfolio. Finally, compare the fund's expense ratio and the magnitude of its rolling costs to alternative commodity exposure vehicles (physical [commodity ETFs](/commodity-etf/), commodity equity funds, or direct futures trading) to ensure it fits your cost expectations.
