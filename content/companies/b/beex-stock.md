*The **Beehive ETF** — ticker BEEX — is a thematic, actively managed exchange-traded fund that focuses on companies whose businesses center on bees, pollination services, beekeeping, honey production, or agricultural technologies that support bee populations.*

## What does BEEX actually own?

BEEX does not track a broad market index. Instead, the fund's managers actively select and hold individual companies whose primary business involves bees or bee-related services. This could include beekeeping equipment manufacturers, honey processors and retailers, commercial crop-pollination service providers, companies developing bee-health monitoring technology, and biotech or agrochemical firms focused on solutions that protect bee populations. The portfolio is custom-built and actively managed rather than replicated from a published index, allowing managers to rotate holdings based on company performance and market conditions. Because the universe of purely bee-focused public companies is limited, the fund may also include broader agricultural or specialty-chemicals firms with significant exposure to bee-health products.

## Why concentrate on bees at all?

Bees pollinate crops worth tens of billions of dollars annually, making them economically essential to global agriculture. Bee-colony health has become a mainstream concern because population declines—linked to pesticide exposure, parasites, disease, and habitat loss—threaten agricultural output and food security. This combination of ecological urgency, economic stakes, and regulatory momentum around bee protection has created a growing category of publicly traded companies addressing the opportunity. For investors with conviction that bee-health businesses will expand over the long term, BEEX offers diversified exposure to the theme without the need to research and pick individual securities.

## How does BEEX trade, and what are the risks?

The fund trades on an exchange during market hours like any stock, and can be entered or exited at market prices. Its [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) reflects the cost of active thematic management and research; most [thematic ETFs](/thematic-etf/) charge meaningfully higher fees than passive broad-market funds because maintaining the theme and monitoring a curated list of holdings requires ongoing analytical work. The fund's liquidity—how tight the [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) is and how readily shares can be traded—depends on its total assets and trading volume; smaller thematic funds often have wider spreads and less trading frequency than mega-cap index ETFs.

Concentration is structural to the fund's design. Because BEEX is limited to a narrow industry, it faces outsized exposure to bee-related shocks: pest outbreaks, disease, regulatory changes in pesticide policy, shifts in agricultural economics, or technological disruption in beekeeping methods can disproportionately affect the portfolio. Many holdings are smaller or more volatile companies than large-cap diversified firms, amplifying the fund's swings. Valuation risk is also present: thematic funds often attract inflows when their theme is fashionable and attract outflows when enthusiasm fades, causing share prices to disconnect from fundamental value.

## What should an investor watch?

Tracking trends in agriculture, bee science, and regulation is essential. Monitor pesticide regulatory changes, advances in pollination technology, commodity prices for honey and pollination-dependent crops, and the profitability of the companies the fund holds. The prospectus and fact sheet reveal current holdings, turnover rate, and performance versus any stated benchmark. Recognize that smaller thematic funds can be volatile and their returns are highly sensitive to whether the particular theme is in favor with investors at any given moment.

This fund suits investors with strong conviction in the long-term economic opportunity around bee-health businesses and a willingness to tolerate higher [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) and volatility in exchange for targeted exposure. For most investors, holding BEEX alongside more-diversified equity or thematic positions is prudent to manage the risk of the fund's narrow focus.
