*The JPMorgan BetaBuilders U.S. Small Cap Equity ETF, trading under the symbol BBSC, provides direct exposure to the Russell 2000 Index — a broad measure of roughly 2,000 smaller publicly traded U.S. companies. It is structured as a passive, low-cost fund designed for investors seeking uncomplicated access to the small-cap segment of the equity market.*

## What the fund tracks

BBSC follows the Russell 2000, the standard benchmark for U.S. small-cap stocks. The index includes companies by market capitalisation roughly between $300 million and $2 billion (though these thresholds shift as markets move). The fund holds a representative portfolio of those constituents, aiming to replicate the index's performance before fees.

Small-cap stocks occupy a particular niche in the U.S. equity universe. They are far smaller than the mega-cap technology and financial names that dominate the S&P 500, and often lack the institutional analyst coverage and media attention of their larger peers. Many remain domestically focused businesses without significant international operations. They carry greater idiosyncratic risk — individual company performance matters more than broad market movements — but collectively they represent real economic activity: regional manufacturers, smaller financial institutions, local retailers, service companies, and niche industrial businesses.

## The fund's mechanics and costs

BBSC is a traditional exchange-traded fund, not leveraged or inverse. It trades on a U.S. stock exchange (the NASDAQ under the symbol BBSC) and can be bought and sold throughout the trading day at market-determined prices, unlike mutual funds that settle at end-of-day net asset value. The fund's expense ratio is quoted qualitatively as low, reflecting the streamlined cost structure of passive indexing.

Because it tracks a broad index of 2,000 constituents, BBSC holds substantial positions across many stocks. Liquidity in the fund itself is reasonable; the Russell 2000 constituents are actively traded, and the fund's size provides sufficient assets to execute trades without meaningful friction.

## Where the structural risk lies

The Russell 2000 reconstitutes its membership annually, typically in late June, which can create trading activity and temporary price pressure as funds mechanically add and drop constituents. Any stock that grows beyond the index's market-cap bands rolls out automatically; likewise, rising smaller stocks roll in. For a fund like BBSC, these reconstitutions are routine but do introduce seasonal trading costs.

Small-cap stocks themselves are more volatile than large-cap benchmarks. A small business is more exposed to economic downturns, supply shocks, and shifts in market sentiment than an entrenched mega-cap firm. Recessions historically hit small-cap performance harder. For that reason, BBSC is typically seen as a complementary holding to large-cap exposure rather than a core portfolio position, and it tends to underperform during market stress.

Concentration is less of a concern than with narrower thematic funds, but the Russell 2000 does overweight certain sectors — historically, finance and industrials have been substantial portions. A downturn in those sectors ripples through the index more sharply than it would through a diversified market-cap-weighted large-cap benchmark.

## How to research this fund

Prospective investors should review the fund's prospectus and most recent fact sheet, available from JPMorgan's website, to confirm the current holdings, expense ratio, and any policy changes. The Russell 2000 Index itself is published and updated by FTSE Russell, and observing how that benchmark performs versus the S&P 500 or Total Stock Market Index over different time periods — bull markets, recessions, and recovery phases — offers insight into when small-cap exposure adds or detracts from a portfolio. Reading the fund's annual report reveals realised tracking error (the actual performance gap from the index) and explains any significant deviations.

BBSC is appropriate for investors who wish to own a slice of U.S. small-cap companies through a low-cost, transparent vehicle, and who understand that small-cap performance is cyclical and often leads the market down before it leads it up.
