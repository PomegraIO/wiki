*The **Siren NexGen Economy ETF** (ticker: BLCN, on NASDAQ) is an actively managed exchange-traded fund that invests in publicly traded companies whose businesses depend on or benefit from blockchain technology and digital economy infrastructure. Unlike passive index funds that track a preset list of holdings, Siren's managers actively select companies they believe are best positioned to profit from blockchain adoption.*

## What is BLCN and how is it structured?

Siren NexGen Economy ETF holds companies engaged in blockchain technology development, cryptocurrency infrastructure, and digital finance. The fund captures both pure-play blockchain companies — cryptocurrency exchanges, mining operations, blockchain software developers — and traditional firms that have incorporated blockchain into their business: payments processors, data-centre operators, telecommunications companies. The portfolio reflects Siren's thesis that blockchain and related digital-economy technologies will drive economic value over the coming decade.

The defining feature is active management. Rather than mechanically tracking a preset index, Siren's managers review the landscape continuously and select which companies merit inclusion and how heavily to weight each position. This means the portfolio evolves as market conditions and technology adoption patterns shift. It also means higher costs.

## What does BLCN cost and what does the manager claim?

BLCN's expense ratio sits around 0.68 to 0.75 per cent annually, substantially higher than a passive blockchain index fund. On a one-million-dollar position, that translates to roughly six thousand to seven thousand dollars per year in fees before any trading costs. Active management imposes this premium on the expectation that the manager's skill — superior stock picking, timing, and sector allocation — will generate returns in excess of the fee. The investor's burden is to verify whether that claim holds.

The empirical record on active management is sobering. Most active mutual funds and ETFs underperform their passive benchmarks over extended periods once fees are accounted for. However, blockchain is a nascent sector where the competitive landscape remains fluid, regulations evolve rapidly, and deep expertise may allow managers to spot opportunities others miss. Whether Siren's team possesses such skill is an open question that only historical performance, carefully tracked, can help answer.

## What risks does BLCN face?

The fund's central risk is that blockchain technology fails to deliver the transformative impact its advocates expect. If blockchain remains a niche technology, if regulators prohibit or heavily restrict its use, or if alternative technologies prove superior, the fund's holdings would all suffer simultaneously. This is a binary, ecosystem-wide risk that diversification cannot eliminate — all the fund's companies share the same underlying bet.

A second risk is sectoral concentration. The blockchain ecosystem is still young and relatively small compared to the broader economy. Siren's managers might accumulate positions in mining companies and exchanges, which currently dominate the ecosystem, while underweighting less-developed blockchain applications. If mining becomes less profitable or exchanges face regulatory pressure, the fund's returns would suffer disproportionately.

The third risk is the moat question. Most companies the fund holds — exchanges, mining operations, blockchain developers — operate in markets with low barriers to entry. An exchange needs to offer trading functionality, but so can any competently run startup. Mining is a commodity business where cost and efficiency are paramount; no single miner commands durable pricing power. Blockchain developers face intense competition from other software firms. Traditional competitive moats — network effects, brand loyalty, switching costs, cost advantages — are either weak or absent in the blockchain sector. The fund's returns therefore depend less on durable competitive advantages and more on whether the broader blockchain ecosystem succeeds.

## Who should own BLCN and how to research it?

BLCN suits investors who believe blockchain technology will reshape finance, commerce, and digital infrastructure over the next decade, and who are willing to pay higher fees for active management focused on capturing that transformation. It is not suitable for conservative investors, those with short time horizons, or those uncomfortable with substantial volatility. Regulatory shocks can cause sharp, unexpected drawdowns.

Prospective investors should read Siren's fund prospectus and the most recent fact sheet to understand the current holdings and the manager's stated criteria for selection. Review the fund's historical performance carefully: how have returns compared to a simpler passive blockchain index fund or a broad technology index after all fees? Has the active management genuinely added value? Study the top ten holdings and research why Siren chose them — does the reasoning reflect genuine insight into competitive advantages, or are the selections generic technology bets?

Monitor regulatory developments affecting blockchain, cryptocurrency, and digital finance continuously, as policy shifts can reshape the investment case overnight. Examine earnings reports and investor calls of the fund's major holdings to assess business momentum and management confidence. Accept that blockchain is fundamentally a young sector and that Siren's views on which companies will prevail are informed but unproven. Own this fund only if you have conviction that blockchain technology will expand substantially in economic importance and that you can tolerate the volatility inherent in that bet.
