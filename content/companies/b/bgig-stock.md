*The **Bahl & Gaynor Income [Growth ETF](/growth-etf/)** (ticker BGIG) is an exchange-traded fund that seeks to capture both current [dividend](/dividend/) income and long-term capital appreciation by holding stocks with histories of rising dividends and earnings growth.*

## The dividend-growth premise

[Dividend-growth investing](/dividend-growth-investing/) occupies a distinctive middle ground between pure income generation and capital appreciation. Rather than simply holding the highest-yielding stocks, this strategy targets companies that demonstrate both an established history of paying dividends and a pattern of raising that dividend over time. A company that has increased its dividend for 10, 20, or 25 consecutive years signals several things: stable and growing profitability, management confidence in future cash generation, and shareholder-friendly capital allocation.

BGIG implements this philosophy through an index that selects stocks on [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/) and dividend-growth history. The result is a portfolio of predominantly mature, profitable businesses that are less volatile than the broader market yet offer more growth potential than a pure income strategy would capture.

## Holdings and sector composition

The fund's portfolio typically includes 40 to 80 stocks drawn from established U.S. industries. Utilities, consumer staples, healthcare, and industrials often represent significant portions of the portfolio because these sectors contain many dividend growers with durable competitive advantages. Large-cap companies dominate; small-cap and mid-cap dividend growers appear but are less common because dividend-raising histories are easier to document and sustain for mature, established firms.

The fund's methodology rebalances to maintain exposure to the highest-yielding dividend growers, meaning the composition shifts as companies initiate, cut, or accelerate their dividend policies. This turnover is typically moderate compared to [actively managed funds](/actively-managed-fund/).

## Income plus appreciation

The dual objective — income now and growth later — means BGIG performs differently across [market cycles](/market-cycle/). In periods when [bond](/bond/) yields are high or [recession](/recession/) fears spike, dividend-growth stocks often outperform because their yields become relatively attractive and their earnings stability provides reassurance. In [bull markets](/bull-market/) driven by high-growth technology and speculative sectors, BGIG may lag because dividend-growth stocks tend to be older, slower-growing businesses. Over full market cycles, the combination of compounding dividend growth and stock price appreciation can be meaningful, even if the fund underperforms explosive single-sector rallies.

## Expense ratio and costs

The fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is typically a low percentage of assets, reflecting the passive-index nature of the fund and the relatively straightforward nature of dividend-growth selection. Trading costs are modest for a diversified equity fund, though [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) and liquidity depend on the fund's size and trading volume on the exchange.

## Tax efficiency and dividend treatment

As a dividend-paying fund, BGIG distributes income from the stocks it holds, usually quarterly. Dividends are taxed as ordinary income in taxable accounts, making the fund less tax-efficient than a growth-oriented fund or a tax-managed equity strategy. For tax-advantaged accounts like IRAs and 401(k)s, tax treatment is irrelevant. Investors preferring to minimize taxable distributions should consider [tax-loss harvesting](/tax-loss-harvesting/) or holding the fund in a retirement account.

## Risks and limitations

BGIG is an equity fund and carries [market risk](/market-risk/). Dividends are not guaranteed; even long-time dividend payers can reduce or suspend dividends during downturns or operational challenges. The fund's skew toward defensive sectors and mature companies means it may underperform during technology-led rallies or periods of aggressive growth. Interest-rate increases can depress dividend-stock valuations as higher bond yields make dividends less attractive on a relative basis.

## Suitability and research

BGIG appeals to income-focused investors seeking both [current yield](/current-yield/) and long-term growth, particularly retirees or those nearing retirement who want exposure to equities without the volatility of pure-growth stocks. It is less suitable for investors in the accumulation phase who can tolerate volatility and do not require current income.

To evaluate the fund, review its prospectus and holdings list to verify the dividend-growth characteristics and sector weightings. Compare its yield, total return, and expense ratio against competing dividend-growth funds and broad equity indices. Check the dividend-growth record of the underlying index to confirm that constituents have a meaningful history of dividend increases.
