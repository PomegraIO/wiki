## The company and its founding

WonderFi Technologies, a Canadian-listed company, operates platforms where cryptocurrency traders buy, sell, and borrow digital assets. The company was born from the 2021 [merger](/merger/) of Coinsquare and Bitbuy — two established Canadian crypto exchanges — and subsequently expanded through [acquisitions](/acquisition/) of other crypto platforms and DeFi infrastructure. The business is built on the proposition that crypto markets are durable, that traders will need places to execute those trades, and that Canada, with a relatively clear regulatory framework for crypto, is a stable home for such platforms.

The company's core revenues come from trading fees, interest on cryptocurrency held in custody, and DeFi platform incentives. When someone buys [Bitcoin](/bitcoin/) on a WonderFi exchange, the company takes a small slice. When customers hold crypto on the platform, WonderFi can lend that crypto to others and keep a spread on the interest. These models are not unique to crypto — stock [brokers](/broker/) have done the same for decades — but crypto trading has historically offered much higher fee spreads because the market is less mature, less competitive, and less regulated than traditional securities.

## The structural vulnerability

The WonderFi story turns on one axis: the survivability of crypto as a legitimate asset class and the regulatory environment that governs who can trade it. The company made its bet early in that story, when crypto was mostly unregulated and retailers were desperate for access. As the market matured, two pressures arrived simultaneously.

The first is regulatory tightening. Canada is moving toward clearer rules for crypto exchanges and custody providers, which is good for long-term legitimacy but creates a higher compliance bar. The United States, larger than Canada, has oscillated between near-total hands-off and aggressive scrutiny, depending on which agency is in the lead and what crisis just occurred. Europe has introduced more prescriptive regulation. Each new rule raises compliance costs and narrows who can use the platform. A truly restrictive regulatory regime — one that bans crypto outright or makes it prohibitively expensive to trade — would destroy the value of these platforms overnight.

The second is competition at scale. WonderFi competes against Kraken, a well-funded US exchange with a larger user base; Coinbase, which is publicly listed and has institutional credibility; and increasingly against traditional brokers like Fidelity and Schwab that have begun offering crypto trading as a side service. As crypto becomes less of a fringe curiosity and more of an accepted asset class, traditional financial institutions with lower-cost infrastructure and trusted brands can move in. They do not need to build a crypto company from scratch — they just need to bolt crypto trading onto their existing platform.

## Revenue sustainability and structural risks

WonderFi's revenues are sensitive to two things: the price of Bitcoin and other major cryptocurrencies, and the volume of trading those higher prices generate. A market crash cuts both trading volume and the value of assets the company can lend out. A multi-year [bear market](/bear-market/) — as happened in 2022 — can reduce revenue by half or more and force the company to cut costs sharply. That is painful but survivable if the company has a strong [balance sheet](/balance-sheet/) and believes the recovery is coming. It is existential if the company over-leveraged itself during the boom.

The third vulnerability is the model itself. Crypto trading, custody, and lending all depend on customer deposits. If large numbers of customers lose confidence and withdraw their assets simultaneously, the company can face a [liquidity crisis](/liquidity-crisis/). If a major counterparty — another crypto firm or a bank WonderFi deals with — fails, WonderFi can be caught holding defaulted debt or frozen assets. The broader crypto market has seen several of these cascades: the failure of FTX, the contagion that hit Genesis and BlockFi, the bank runs that destroyed customers' deposits. WonderFi managed these storms intact, but the risk is endemic to any platform that relies on customer trust and interconnectedness within the crypto ecosystem.

## Path forward

WonderFi is betting that crypto is durable, that Canadian and global regulators will settle on a sustainable framework, and that the company can be profitable enough at a lower-volatility size to survive downturns. The company has retreated from its highest-ambition growth targets and focused on profitability and balance-sheet health. It is a plausible strategy — crypto is here to stay, and fintechs that survive regulatory waves often become quite valuable — but it is still a bet on both the future of an asset class and the continued tolerance of the financial system and its regulators.
