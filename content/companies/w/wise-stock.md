Themes AI ETF (Trades as WISE, NASDAQ) pools companies positioned to benefit from generative artificial intelligence—a broad brush stroked across the infrastructure, software, and enterprise layers of the AI stack. The fund tries to capture exposure to the runaway growth narrative in AI without requiring investors to pick individual winners in a fast-moving, still-consolidating space.

The selection problem is thornier than it first appears. Generative AI is not a single company or sector. The infrastructure layer includes chip designers (making the GPUs and specialized processors that train and run large language models) and cloud providers. The software layer includes frontier model builders, APIs and platforms wrapping those models, and tools for fine-tuning and deployment. The enterprise layer includes traditional software companies embedding generative AI features and entirely new startups solving specific problems with AI. Themes attempts to cast a net wide enough to catch shifts in which part of the stack proves most profitable while focused enough to avoid pure tech-sector indexing.

The fund's holdings likely skew toward established publicly traded companies with meaningful AI exposure rather than pure-play startups. That means household semiconductor names, cloud infrastructure giants, enterprise software incumbents adding AI copilots, and perhaps smaller public companies whose entire business is AI-native. The exact composition shifts as the fund's managers reweight positions based on AI adoption, earnings contribution, and valuation.

Holdings diversity is both a strength and a weakness. A diversified bet across the stack reduces the risk that one player captures all the value, but it also blurs the fund's identity. If the fund holds both the semiconductor maker selling chips to AI labs and the enterprise software company using those chips, the net exposure to each step is diluted. And if legacy software companies' AI features prove less valuable than expected while pure-play AI startups that go public rocket up, a fund holding a mix will lag the dedicated winners.

Valuation risk is acute. Generative AI remains unproven at delivering durable profits at scale. Many companies trading at high multiples on the promise of AI integration have not yet demonstrated that AI will meaningfully grow revenues or margins. A market correction on AI enthusiasm—triggered by slower-than-expected adoption, regulatory obstacles, or commoditization of AI capabilities—could hit the fund hard. Because many AI-exposed stocks are growth-oriented and richly valued, they tend to suffer sharply in rate-hiking or recession scenarios.

Currency and geographic exposure matter. The fund likely holds some international companies, particularly semiconductor leaders from Taiwan, South Korea, or the Netherlands. That brings currency hedging decisions: will gains or losses from foreign stock movements be amplified or dampened by currency swings?

Expense ratios for thematic ETFs tend to exceed those of broad index funds, reflecting the active work of constructing a thematic basket and rebalancing as the landscape shifts. Some thematic funds charge well into the 0.5-0.8% range, which is meaningful drag over a decade.

Tracking the fund requires following not just individual company earnings but the broader AI adoption curve. How fast are enterprises deploying generative AI? Are costs per inference falling? Which software companies are seeing material uptake of AI features? Are regulatory clouds gathering? The prospectus and fact sheet provide holdings and strategy; investor updates and earnings calls of major holdings offer colour on the AI deployment pace. Technology-focused research firms and generalist publications tracking AI trends provide context for whether WISE's bet is well-positioned or late to a trend already priced in.
