## From advisory firm to ETF sponsor

Warren Street Wealth Advisors operates as a research-driven wealth-management firm serving institutional and high-net-worth clients. The firm built its reputation through disciplined equity analysis, portfolio construction, and a commitment to fiduciary standards. WSGE represents the firm's first foray into the ETF structure, a decision reflecting both the growth of active ETFs as a vehicle for managing wealth and the broadening investor appetite for access to skilled, actively managed equity portfolios at lower costs than traditional separately managed accounts. The December 2025 launch marks a moment when the firm determined that offering its investment philosophy and approach to a broader audience — through a transparent, tradable fund — aligned with its long-term mission.

## Dual-strategy structure: core plus factor

The fund combines two complementary approaches. The core allocation draws on Warren Street's fundamental equity research and stock-selection discipline, identifying companies across geographies and sectors that meet the firm's quality and valuation criteria. Overlaid on this core is a strategic factor-based component, meaning the fund intentionally tilt its exposures toward characteristics — such as value, momentum, quality, or dividend yield — that have historically delivered outperformance over long periods. The factor tilt is not passive or mechanical; it remains subject to the portfolio managers' oversight and can be adjusted based on market conditions and the firm's outlook.

This hybrid approach attempts to balance two competing goals. The core piece captures alpha — returns from skilled stock selection and market timing. The factor piece adds consistency and mathematical backing: factors are well-documented sources of excess return in academic literature, and systematic factor exposure can reduce the role of luck in short-term performance. By combining both, the fund aims to capture neither pure alpha-hunt (which depends entirely on manager skill and can be unreliable) nor pure factor indexing (which forgoes the upside of security selection).

## Geographic and sector diversification

The fund invests in equities across the developed world — North America, Europe, and the Asia-Pacific region — as well as emerging markets. It does not restrict itself to large-cap names; the portfolio holds small and mid-cap companies as well. This breadth offers genuine diversification: a slowdown in U.S. growth does not cripple the fund if European or emerging-market equities are performing well, and sector concentration is naturally limited by holding companies across health care, financials, industrials, consumer goods, energy, technology, and other industries. The specific weightings between regions and sectors fluctuate as the managers' views change and as the factor tilts respond to valuation shifts.

## Active management and its trade-off

Active management means the fund does not mechanically track an index. Portfolio managers make discretionary decisions about which stocks to own, in what sizes, and when to adjust. This provides the potential to outperform an index, but it also introduces tracking error — periods when the fund diverges from its benchmark — and the risk of underperformance if managers' views prove wrong. The 0.80% expense ratio is moderate for an actively managed equity fund, reflecting the ETF structure's cost efficiency compared to traditional mutual funds. However, it is higher than a passive global equity index fund, so investors are paying for the managers' expertise and discretion.

## Who this is for and how to research it

WSGE is suitable for investors who believe skilled active managers can add value over time and who prefer the transparency and tax efficiency of the ETF structure over traditional mutual funds or separately managed accounts. It works well as a core global equity holding for diversified portfolios or for investors who want simple, global exposure without holding multiple geographic or sector-specific funds.

Prospective investors should request fact sheets and performance reports from Warren Street Wealth Advisors or from Morningstar and other fund-tracking platforms. Understand the current factor tilts and how they differ from a broad global index; this shapes the fund's performance in different market environments. Review the manager biographies and investment philosophy to assess whether their approach aligns with your own views on active management and factor investing. The fund is young, so historical performance is limited; look instead at the track record of Warren Street's advisory business and the managers' prior funds, if any exist.
