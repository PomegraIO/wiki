*The **Nuveen ESG [Small-Cap ETF](/small-cap-etf/)** — trading as NUSC — is an exchange-traded fund that holds a basket of small-cap U.S. stocks that have passed environmental, social, and governance (ESG) screening. It aims to give investors broad exposure to the smaller end of the American equity market while excluding companies that fail on a defined set of sustainability criteria.*

## What the fund owns and why

NUSC tracks an index of small-cap companies — those with [market capitalizations](/market-capitalization/) in the range typical of small-cap equity. The fund is actively managed within defined ESG parameters: it screens holdings for environmental practices (carbon footprint, resource efficiency), labour and governance standards, product safety, and community relations. The screening removes tobacco companies entirely and filters out businesses with poor ratings on sustainability metrics.

The result is a portfolio of roughly 300–500 small-cap stocks spread across all sectors, though with fewer or lighter positions in tobacco, weapons, and fossil-fuel extraction than a traditional small-cap [index fund](/index-fund/) would carry. Small-cap stocks as a category are volatile — they can move far more than large-cap peers over a year — but they also offer growth potential that mature large-cap businesses sometimes lack.

## Who runs it and how it trades

Nuveen (a unit of the Dutch insurance company Aegon) sponsors the fund. NUSC trades on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) under the ticker symbol NUSC, settling in [U.S. dollars](/us-dollar/), and can be bought and sold during regular market hours like any stock. The fund holds its assets in a standard ETF wrapper — meaning it can be transferred between [brokers](/broker/), does not charge a redemption fee, and passes through [dividends](/dividend/) that small-cap stocks pay out.

The fund's operating expenses are quoted as a percentage of assets per year (the [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/)). Because the fund is actively managed — a team makes decisions about which ESG-screened small caps to own, rather than simply tracking a published index — the fee is higher than a passive index fund's, though still modest by active-management standards. Holding charges are deducted from fund assets automatically and appear in the price you pay.

## Why small cap plus ESG?

Small-cap stocks have historically outperformed large-cap stocks over long stretches, though with considerably higher volatility. The logic is simple: a smaller company has more room to grow, so patient investors in that growth have sometimes been rewarded handsomely. But picking individual small-cap winners is difficult for most investors, so a diversified fund offers a way to gain exposure to the category without betting on any single company.

Adding an ESG filter narrows the universe — fewer companies qualify — and introduces a value judgment. Some investors see ESG screening as a hedge against regulatory and [reputational risk](/reputational-risk/): a company with poor environmental or labour practices faces increasing pressure from regulators and from customers. Others view it as a values-based choice, accepting lower returns if necessary in order to invest in businesses that align with their ethics. The fund is designed for investors who want both broad small-cap exposure and that ESG constraint.

## Real risks to understand

Small-cap stocks are inherently more volatile than large caps: a 20% drop in a year is not unusual, and individual stocks can decline far faster. NUSC magnifies that by concentrating in a subset of the small-cap universe — the ESG screening itself is a source of tracking error (deviation from a broader small-cap benchmark), so the fund's performance can lag or outpace its peers depending on whether ESG-screened stocks are in or out of favour that year.

The fund is also only as good as its ESG methodology. ESG ratings vary widely across providers, and reasonable people disagree on what constitutes genuine environmental or social progress versus mere marketing compliance. A company might pass one fund's ESG screen and fail another's, introducing an element of judgment that is not present in passive index funds.

Liquidity is generally not a constraint — NUSC trades millions of shares daily — but very small-cap positions within the fund itself can be thinly traded, which means the fund's annual rebalancing (buying and selling to maintain target weights) can sometimes move prices slightly.

## How to research NUSC

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet on Nuveen's website, which spell out the exact ESG criteria used to screen holdings, the current top 10 positions, and the expense ratio. The prospectus also lists historical performance (which, as always, is not a guarantee of future returns), the fund's turnover rate (how often it trades), and its yield.

Compare NUSC's returns, volatility, and expense ratio to other small-cap and small-cap-ESG ETFs that might serve the same purpose. Check the fund's annual flows — if it is growing or shrinking, that tells you whether investors are leaning in or out. And monitor commentary from the fund sponsor about the screening criteria; if Nuveen changes what it considers ESG-compliant, holdings will shift, and returns may too.

Like any equity fund, NUSC is subject to [stock market](/stock-market/) risk: if U.S. small-cap stocks fall broadly, so will the fund, regardless of how excellent its screening process is.
