*NVDS is a leveraged inverse exchange-traded fund that attempts to move 1.5 times in the opposite direction of NVIDIA stock on a daily basis. It is designed for traders with a near-term bearish view of the company, using daily reset mechanics to amplify short exposure at a fixed leverage ratio. Like all leveraged and inverse products, NVDS is built for tactical trading measured in hours or days, not long-term holding.*

Tradr is the fund sponsor. When NVIDIA rises 1 percent in a single trading day, NVDS is engineered to fall roughly 1.5 percent. When NVIDIA falls 1 percent, NVDS aims to rise 1.5 percent. This daily reset is the mechanical heart of how the fund works: each day at the market close, portfolio managers adjust the fund's holdings of derivatives (primarily futures and swaps referencing NVIDIA) so that tomorrow's performance will match the target ratio, regardless of what happened today. That daily reset is necessary but costly — it forces the fund to buy back losers and sell winners in a mechanical, sometimes unfavorable way, which over longer periods produces a drag that exceeds what simple leverage math alone would suggest.

The fund itself is a traditional ETF structure, meaning it holds a real portfolio (in this case, mostly derivative instruments pegged to NVIDIA's price movement) and trades on an exchange rather than trading directly with the issuer. NVDS trades on the NYSE Arca with modest liquidity — much lower volume than flagship leveraged products from larger sponsors like Direxion or Invesco. The primary cost beyond the expense ratio is the daily reset decay, which is the more insidious expense. If NVIDIA trades sideways or choppy — up one day, down the next — the fund will leak value even if NVIDIA ends the period exactly where it started. This decay accelerates in volatile periods, making the fund especially unsuitable for holding through market turbulence.

The expense ratio for single-stock leveraged products runs high relative to broader market ETFs — typically in the 0.7% to 1.0% annual range, reflecting the ongoing cost of maintaining the derivative hedges and rebalancing mechanics. That stated ratio is only the visible cost. The silent cost is compounding decay. A simple example: if NVIDIA trades up 2 percent on Monday and down 2 percent on Tuesday (ending flat), NVDS will be down roughly 3 percent on Monday and up 3 percent on Tuesday. The compounded result is a loss, not breakeven. That mathematical leakage accelerates the more volatile the underlying stock is.

NVDS is meant for traders with a clear short-term bearish view of NVIDIA who want to amplify that bet. It might be used as a tactical hedge by someone holding a large NVIDIA position and expecting a near-term pullback, or as a directional day-trade or swing-trade vehicle. It is emphatically not a position to hold for weeks, months, or years; the daily reset decay will erode value faster than any sustained decline in NVIDIA could offset. Prospective users should understand that NVDS moves 1.5 times the daily move of NVIDIA — not 1.5 times the multi-month move — and that sideways or choppy markets are the silent killers of leveraged products.

The prospectus and fact sheet, available on the fund sponsor's website or through major brokers, lay out the mechanics, costs, and risks in full. Anyone serious about using leveraged or inverse single-stock products should read the fund documents and understand the reset mechanics before deploying capital. The underlying benchmark is simply NVIDIA Inc., trading on the NASDAQ under ticker NVDA, a publicly traded semiconductor designer focused on graphics processing units and data-center computing.
