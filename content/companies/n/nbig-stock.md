*The **Leverage Shares 2X Long NBIS Daily ETF** (NBIG) is a [leveraged ETF](/leveraged-etf/) that seeks to provide 200 percent of the daily return of Nebius Group N.V. stock (NBIS). Unlike margin trading, it offers defined risk: an investor's maximum loss is their entire initial investment, not more. The fund rebalances daily to maintain its 2x [leverage ratio](/leverage-ratio-forex/).*

## What does this fund actually track?

NBIG tracks Nebius Group (NBIS), a cloud and technology infrastructure company. The fund aims to move twice as much as Nebius stock moves, every single trading day. If Nebius rises 1% in a day, NBIG tries to rise 2%. If Nebius falls 1%, NBIG tries to fall 2%. The word "tries" matters, because leverage is never perfect; trading costs and rebalancing create small tracking differences.

The leverage is reset every market close. Each day, the fund looks at its [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) and rebalances its positions to ensure it owns enough Nebius stock (or derivatives) to deliver 2x exposure the next day. This daily reset is crucial to understanding the fund's behavior over longer time horizons.

## Why would someone own a leveraged single-stock ETF?

Leverage amplifies both gains and losses. A trader who is very bullish on Nebius can capture twice the upside without opening a margin account, managing collateral, or worrying about a [broker's](/broker/) [margin call](/margin-call-forex/). If Nebius soars 30%, NBIG targets 60% (before costs). That appeal is obvious.

The defined-risk structure is the other draw. Margin trading on individual stocks is risky: you can lose far more than you invested if the stock collapses and the broker demands collateral. NBIG eliminates that [tail risk](/tail-risk/). You cannot lose more than 100% of what you invested; the fund's worst outcome is zero. That certainty appeals to traders who want aggressive upside but do not want to manage margin accounts.

NBIG charges a 0.75% annual [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/), lower than many leveraged ETFs and reflecting Leverage Shares' focus on cost efficiency.

## What happens over longer holding periods?

Here is the critical subtlety: daily leverage resets are not the same as constant leverage. If Nebius doubles in value in one day, NBIG targets 4x returns that day. But the rebalancing that happens at market close means the leverage is then recalibrated. Over weeks and months, this daily reset has a mathematical cost called volatility decay or geometric drag. If an underlying stock is volatile—up 5% one day, down 5% the next—it ends where it started. But a 2x leveraged version of that sequence does not end where it started; it ends lower, because of the way compounding works with leverage reset daily.

Concretely: if Nebius goes +10% then -10% over two days, you break even. But NBIG goes +20% then -20%, which compounds to a loss. This is not fraud or mismanagement; it is a mechanical feature of daily-reset leverage. The longer you hold NBIG, the more this decay matters, especially if the underlying stock is volatile.

NBIG is a trading vehicle, not a buy-and-hold holding. It is suited to traders with short [holding periods](/holding-period/)—days to weeks—and a strong conviction that Nebius will move significantly higher with limited volatility. It is not suited to buy-and-hold investors or those unsure of near-term direction.

## What are the real risks?

Nebius stock volatility is the primary risk. If Nebius trades sideways or falls, so does NBIG, but twice as much. An investor can lose significant capital quickly. If Nebius falls more than 50% in a single day, NBIG loses 100% of value (the maximum loss). This is rare but mathematically possible in the event of a corporate disaster or error.

Leverage creates another risk: if the fund's NAV falls far enough, the rebalancing becomes challenging or impossible to execute properly. Though Leverage Shares is experienced at managing this, extreme market dislocations could impair the fund's ability to maintain leverage precision.

[Liquidity risk](/liquidity-risk/) is low for the ETF itself (it trades on [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) with tight spreads), but the underlying derivatives or short-term hedges the fund may use to implement leverage could face liquidity stress during market panic.

Finally, there is [basis risk](/basis-risk/): NBIG may not track 2x Nebius performance exactly due to trading costs, rebalancing slippage, and fees. Tracking error is typically small but is not zero.

## Who should and should not own this

NBIG suits sophisticated traders with a bullish short-term view on Nebius, capital to risk, and an understanding of leverage mechanics. It is unsuitable for buy-and-hold investors, those avoiding leverage, those with low risk tolerance, or those holding the fund for longer than weeks to months.
