### What does NUMV track?

NUMV holds mid-sized companies that pass an ESG quality filter and trade at valuations below the overall market average. Whereas a [growth fund](/growth-fund/) seeks companies with expanding earnings, a [value fund](/value-fund/) seeks those trading at a discount to their intrinsic worth, betting that the market has mispriced them. Add an ESG layer, and the fund targets companies that are both undervalued *and* score well on environmental, social, and governance criteria — the thesis being that disciplined capital allocation and responsible management tend to overlap with long-term value.

### How does the fund select companies?

The screening process operates in stages. First, NUMV identifies mid-cap companies — typically those in a defined market-capitalization band well above small-caps but below the mega-cap tier. Second, it applies ESG criteria, excluding companies that score poorly on environmental standards, labor practices, governance structure, or other non-financial factors. This pre-screen reduces the eligible universe significantly. Third, it ranks the remaining companies by valuation metrics — [price-to-earnings ratio](/price-to-earnings-ratio/), price-to-book, price-to-sales — and selects those trading cheaply relative to historical averages and peers. The result is a portfolio of 50 to 150 stocks, smaller and more concentrated than a broad market index.

### Why value and ESG together?

The pairing reflects two separate investment philosophies. [Value investing](/value-investing/) assumes markets sometimes misprice stocks, and patient investors can profit by buying overlooked or out-of-favor companies at a discount. ESG integration assumes that companies with sound environmental practices, fair labor relations, and strong governance tend to be better-managed over the long run and carry less hidden risk. In theory, a company that is both cheap *and* well-managed is an especially attractive opportunity. In practice, the two screens sometimes pull against each other — a deeply discounted stock may be cheap for a good reason, and an ESG-exemplary company may carry a premium valuation that limits upside.

### Portfolio characteristics

The fund tilts toward sectors like industrials, consumer staples, healthcare, and occasionally utilities — businesses where balance-sheet quality and operational discipline show up in value metrics. The fund typically under-weights sectors like technology that have high valuations relative to book value, even if they score well on ESG criteria. The top holdings are usually less household-name companies than those in a large-cap value fund, which means less media coverage and potentially greater opportunity for repricing. They also mean higher single-stock volatility and less trading liquidity in the underlying securities.

### Costs and expense structure

As a passive fund tracking an index methodology, NUMV's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is low — typically around 0.4 percent annually or less. The fund trades as a listed ETF with reasonable trading liquidity, so entry and exit costs for buy-and-hold investors are usually minimal.

### Real risks and considerations

Mid-cap stocks swing more violently than large-cap peers, and value stocks have underperformed growth over much of the past decade, a period when investors favored high-growth technology over cheap industrials and utilities. If that preference persists, the fund's value tilt will be a headwind. The ESG screen, meanwhile, may exclude promising companies simply because they operate in sectors perceived as environmentally intensive or socially controversial, even if those individual firms are improving their profiles.

[Concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) is non-trivial — the portfolio is smaller than a broad-market index, and the screening process weeds out a large share of the eligible universe. This means individual stock movements matter more than in a diversified mega-cap fund.

The index methodology itself introduces risk. If the ESG criteria applied by Nuveen fail to identify genuinely responsible companies, or if the valuation metrics they use are poor predictors of future price appreciation, the fund can underperform a plain mid-cap index for extended periods.

### How to evaluate NUMV

Read the prospectus and fact sheet carefully. Understand exactly which ESG factors Nuveen weights most heavily and why, and whether their ESG ratings align with your own values. Review the valuation methodology — are they using price-to-earnings, price-to-book, or a blend? — and compare the fund's current valuation multiples to those of a non-ESG mid-cap value index. Check the sector breakdown and largest holdings to confirm the fund isn't inadvertently tilted toward areas you want to avoid. Finally, compare NUMV's historical return against a benchmark mid-cap value index and a broader ESG mid-cap fund to gauge whether the value tilt is adding value or subtracting it in recent performance.
