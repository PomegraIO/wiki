*The Tradr 2X Long NVTS Daily ETF (NVTX) is a leveraged exchange-traded fund designed to track twice the daily return of [Nvidia](/nvda-stock/) stock, allowing investors to gain amplified exposure to one of the technology sector's most influential semiconductor companies without holding shares directly.*

## What does NVTX hold?

NVTX is a single-stock leveraged product, not a diversified fund. It does not hold Nvidia shares directly. Instead, it uses derivatives—primarily swap agreements and [futures contracts](/futures-contract/)—to synthetically create a position that aims to return twice the daily return of Nvidia's stock. If Nvidia rises 1% on a given day, NVTX is designed to rise approximately 2%. Conversely, if Nvidia falls 1%, NVTX targets a 2% decline. The fund rebalances every trading day to reset this leverage back to exactly 2x, which is the mechanism that creates the "daily reset" label.

## Who should own NVTX, and why?

Leveraged single-stock ETFs appeal to a narrow audience: traders with high conviction in the near-term direction of Nvidia stock, seeking outsized participation in daily moves. They are not vehicles for [buy-and-hold investing](/buy-and-hold/). NVTX is useful for someone who believes Nvidia will outperform over weeks or a few months and wants to use less capital to capture that view than would be required to buy Nvidia shares themselves. It trades throughout the day on a [stock exchange](/stock-exchange/), offering liquidity that a [synthetic position](/synthetic-position/) might not otherwise provide.

## What is leverage decay, and why does it matter?

This is the critical limitation of all [leveraged ETFs](/leveraged-etf/). Because NVTX resets to 2x leverage every single day, it does not deliver precisely 2x the *cumulative* return of Nvidia over longer periods—it delivers 2x the daily return, and those daily 2x returns compound in ways that amplified funds do not.

Consider a simple example: if Nvidia returns +10% one day and then −10% the next day (for a net return of −1%), Nvidia itself is down 1%. But NVTX, targeting 2x daily, goes +20% (the first day) and then −20% (the second day). That sequence compounds to −4%, a worse outcome than 2x the −1% that would be −2%. This effect—called volatility decay or path dependency—means leveraged ETFs tend to lag their mathematical targets as volatility increases. Over years, the lag can be severe; over weeks, it may be minor.

## What are the costs?

NVTX charges an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) (disclosed in its prospectus) that covers the cost of rebalancing and the swap or futures agreements that create the synthetic leverage. Because it is a leveraged product tracking a single stock, the ratio is higher than a broad [index fund](/index-fund/), typically in the range of 1.0% to 1.5% annually. On top of the fee, investors bear the [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) when buying or selling the fund on the exchange, which can be material for thinly traded securities.

## How volatile is NVTX?

By design, NVTX's daily moves are twice as large as Nvidia's. If Nvidia has a standard daily volatility of around 3%, NVTX will move roughly 6% per day in normal conditions. Over longer [holding periods](/holding-period/), the cumulative volatility becomes extreme. This makes NVTX suitable only for active traders with ironclad stop-losses and clear exit plans, not for passive investors.

## What are the real risks?

Beyond volatility decay, the core risk is directional error. If you own NVTX believing Nvidia will rise and it falls instead, you experience not just the loss that Nvidia shareholders suffer, but double the loss. A 20% drop in Nvidia translates to approximately a 40% loss in NVTX, a magnitude that can quickly wipe out an account.

There is also structural risk. If Nvidia stock suffers an extreme gap down—a sudden plunge with no continuous price path—the fund's ability to rebalance and reset may be tested or even temporarily suspended. Swap counterparties used to create the synthetic exposure also carry [credit risk](/credit-risk/), though major issuers mitigate this through collateral agreements.

Tax efficiency is another weakness. The constant rebalancing and use of derivatives trigger frequent taxable events inside the fund, even in accounts that do not trade. This makes NVTX most sensible in tax-deferred accounts (IRAs, 401(k)s) where those events do not cascade into annual tax bills.

## How to research NVTX

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet from the issuer, which disclose the expense ratio, the rebalancing methodology, and the underlying strategy. The prospectus describes the use of swaps and how performance will differ from a simple 2x multiple of Nvidia returns, especially over longer periods. Look at the fund's historical price chart and compare it to 2x Nvidia's cumulative return over the same time window—the gap illustrates volatility decay in action. Nvidia's own quarterly earnings and product announcements move the stock sharply, so understanding Nvidia's competitive position in data-center processors and generative artificial intelligence is essential to any conviction in NVTX as a short-term position.
