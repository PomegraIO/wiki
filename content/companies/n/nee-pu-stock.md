What company is behind this security?

[NextEra Energy](/nee-stock/) Inc is an electric power and energy infrastructure company, one of the largest utility and renewable energy operators in North America. The company operates through two main business platforms: Florida Power & Light Company, a regulated utility serving the state of Florida, and NextEra Energy Resources, an independent power producer and renewable energy company that operates wind, solar, geothermal, and hydroelectric facilities across North America and other markets. The parent company, NextEra Energy, is the holding company that oversees both regulated utility and merchant power generation operations.

Why does NextEra issue preferred and equity units?

Like other large utilities and power companies, NextEra Energy uses [preferred stock](/preferred-stock/) and structured equity units as a source of long-term financing. These instruments rank junior to [bonds](/bond/) (debt) but senior to [common stock](/common-stock/) in bankruptcy priority, and they carry fixed or variable distributions paid to holders. Utilities favor preferred financing because it allows them to raise capital while preserving common equity ownership and maintaining a conservative [leverage ratio](/leverage-ratio-forex/). Power companies in particular, including NextEra, use preferred securities to fund infrastructure spending: upgrading transmission lines, building renewable energy capacity, and maintaining regulatory compliance. A preferred unit issued by NextEra or one of its subsidiaries is a contractual claim on future cash flows, backed by the company's revenue from selling power to utilities and customers, and by the regulated returns that state regulators allow the utility to earn.

What distinguishes NextEra's preferred units from its common stock?

Holders of preferred units receive a stated distribution rate — a fixed percentage paid quarterly or as specified in the prospectus — whereas common stockholders receive [dividends](/dividend/) only if the board declares them and only after all prior claims (debt and preferred) are satisfied. The distribution on a preferred unit is senior: if NextEra faces financial stress, preferred distributees are paid before common stockholders receive anything. This seniority makes preferred units less volatile than common shares but also less likely to participate in extraordinary upside. Most of NextEra's preferred units are cumulative, meaning if a distribution is skipped, it accrues and must be paid before any distribution to junior securities.

What is the underlying business risk?

NextEra's regulated utility arm (FPL) faces regulatory risk: the Florida Public Service Commission sets the rates that FPL can charge and the rate of return it is permitted to earn on invested capital. If regulators cap returns below the company's cost of capital, the business becomes less attractive. The renewable energy arm (NEER) faces merchant power risk: it owns power plants that sell electricity into wholesale markets and under long-term contracts. If wholesale prices fall sharply or major customers renegotiate contracts, revenues decline. Both arms face commodity price exposure ([natural gas](/natural-gas/) affects fuel costs and wholesale power prices) and weather risk (drought or cold snaps affect power demand and wind/solar generation). Preferred units holders are not exposed to all of this with the same intensity as common shareholders, but a company-wide stress does ultimately affect cash available for distributions, and the seniority of the preferred claim provides limited protection if the parent company faces genuine distress.

How are preferred units structured and tradeable?

NextEra and its subsidiaries have issued multiple series of preferred units over the years, each with distinct terms, distribution rates, and prospectus details. Some are issued directly by NextEra Energy Inc; others are issued by NextEra Energy Capital Holdings, a subsidiary created to hold preferred securities and related debentures. The security often pairs a purchase contract (the right to exchange for common shares under certain conditions) with an undivided ownership interest in a debenture, creating a composite instrument. These units trade on the [New York Stock Exchange](/new-york-stock-exchange/) under unique tickers, each corresponding to one series. The structure is complex and requires reading the prospectus and SEC filings to understand the specific conversion mechanics, distribution rate, call provisions, and maturity terms for any given unit.

How do you evaluate whether a NextEra preferred unit is a good investment?

Start with NextEra Energy's annual Form 10-K (SEC CIK 0000753308), which discloses each preferred series, its terms, and its status. Compare the stated distribution rate to current prevailing rates on new preferred issues; if significantly higher, the security may have been issued years ago at higher rates and is now trading at a discount. Watch the company's [credit ratings](/credit-rating/) and financial health: utilities are generally stable, but deterioration would eventually affect preferred distributions. Understand the specific terms: when does the issuer have the right to call (redeem) the unit? Is the rate fixed or floating? What triggers conversion to common shares? Model the company's cash flow under various scenarios, including [recession](/recession/), regulatory setback, and energy-price volatility, to assess the cushion available to support preferred distributions. The preferred unit is less risky than the common stock but is not risk-free; it is a senior claim on an underlying business, and that business can face structural challenges.
