*The Xtrackers RREEF Global Natural Resources ETF (NRES) is an [actively managed fund](/actively-managed-fund/) that invests in publicly traded companies extracting and producing natural resources — metals, energy, agricultural commodities, and timber — across global markets. As an actively managed ETF rather than a passive index tracker, NRES employs a team of portfolio managers who select holdings based on commodity price cycles, supply dynamics, and company fundamentals.*

NRES is sponsored by DWS Group, the asset management arm of Deutsche Börse, which operates the Xtrackers brand of exchange-traded products. The fund traces its origins to the RREEF Real Assets platform, acquired by DWS in the early 2000s. Unlike index-tracking ETFs that hold a fixed basket of stocks weighted mechanistically by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/), NRES' managers actively rotate holdings and adjust sector weights in response to their views of economic cycles, commodity price momentum, and relative value across natural resource companies.

The fund's investment universe spans four broad resource sectors. Mining companies extract metals (gold, copper, zinc, lithium) and minerals ([iron ore](/iron-ore/), potash, phosphate) for sale to manufacturers and industrial users worldwide. Energy firms produce [crude oil](/crude-oil/), [natural gas](/natural-gas/), and coal for power generation and transportation. Agricultural companies operate farming operations, grain trading, or supply seeds, fertilizers, and equipment. Timber [real estate investment trusts](/real-estate-investment-trust/) own forest land and harvest logs for construction and pulp. The portfolio typically maintains exposure across all four groups, though the exact allocation shifts as managers reassess which commodities face supply constraints or demand growth.

The case for natural resource funds rests on two broad economic principles. First is the cyclical thesis: as economies accelerate, raw material demand rises, commodity prices climb, and resource companies enjoy expanding margins and rising profits. In downturns, the reverse occurs sharply, making resource stocks acutely sensitive to economic growth. This makes NRES a bet on near-term economic momentum rather than long-term secular growth. Second is the [inflation](/inflation/) angle: when prices rise broadly, commodity prices often rise alongside inflation, and companies producing fixed volumes of output see nominal revenues and earnings expand. This inflation sensitivity makes natural resource stocks valuable during periods when [bonds](/bond/) and traditional equities face headwinds.

In practice, this means NRES shines during periods when the macroeconomic wind is at investors' backs — strong growth, rising commodity demand, or accelerating inflation. It can struggle during sustained periods of commodity oversupply, weak growth, or [deflation](/deflation/). The fund's value within a portfolio is as a diversifier: a mix of stocks and bonds can underperform during [stagflation](/stagflation/), but resource companies often rally when growth slows if inflation persists, because their prices rise faster than costs. Similarly, during periods of currency weakness, commodity prices (quoted globally in dollars) become more attractive to foreign buyers, benefiting U.S. producers.

NRES trades on major U.S. exchanges like a stock, with [intraday liquidity](/intraday-liquidity/) and market-set prices that may diverge slightly from the fund's underlying [net asset value](/net-asset-value/). [Authorized participants](/authorized-participant/) arbitrage these small gaps, keeping spreads tight. The fund can be held in retirement accounts or taxable accounts, and [fractional shares](/fractional-shares/) are available through most [brokers](/broker/). The active [management fees](/management-fee/) are material — higher than those of passive commodity or resource index ETFs — which matters more in this asset class than in broad equity markets, given commodity prices' inherent volatility and the fund's reliance on manager skill to capture cycles.

A reader researching NRES should consult its prospectus and [annual reports](/10-k/), which detail the current [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/), top holdings, and sector weightings. The fund's composition shifts daily with stock price movements, and market participants can track the fund's current positioning through financial websites that publish top holdings and sector breakdowns. Natural resource investing carries longer periods of underperformance when commodity prices are depressed or excess supply is abundant, and large [capital flows](/capital-flows/) in and out of the sector can amplify volatility. NRES functions best as a satellite position within a broader, diversified portfolio rather than as a core holding.
