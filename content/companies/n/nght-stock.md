> The fund pairs explosive upside with defensive stability.

*NGHT is an exchange-traded fund that holds a balanced portfolio of [Bitcoin](/bitcoin/) and U.S. Treasury securities, designed for investors who want exposure to cryptocurrency without the full volatility of owning Bitcoin outright. It merges the 24/7 [price discovery](/price-discovery/) of digital assets with the safety of government debt, rebalancing the two holdings according to a predetermined target.*

## The dual-asset concept

NGHT sidesteps the all-or-nothing choice between pure Bitcoin and pure [bonds](/bond/) by holding both simultaneously. Bitcoin believers who fear a 30 percent crash can pair digital upside with Treasury ballast. Traditional investors curious about cryptocurrency can gain exposure without committing 100 percent of a position to an asset they only partially trust.

The "AfterDark" branding captures an essential difference: Bitcoin trades 24 hours a day, seven days a week, never closing like [stock exchanges](/stock-exchange/). [Treasury bonds](/treasury-bond/) trade in normal market hours but are backed by the full faith of the U.S. government and centuries of stability. NGHT wraps these opposites into a single rebalanced holding.

## How the fund holds Bitcoin and Treasuries

The fund's prospectus specifies exactly how Bitcoin exposure is achieved. Some versions hold Bitcoin directly through a [custodian](/custodian/), giving holders indirect ownership of actual coins. Others use Bitcoin futures or other derivatives to gain price exposure without managing the security and custody of digital assets themselves. Each approach has trade-offs: direct Bitcoin carries custody costs and security responsibility; futures are cash-settled and liquid but introduce daily mark-to-market swings and potential tracking error.

Treasuries are typically straightforward—the fund holds bonds directly or holds Treasury ETFs, collecting interest and receiving principal repayment on maturity. The fund rebalances these two positions back to its stated target allocation at intervals disclosed in the prospectus. Frequent rebalancing keeps allocations tight but incurs trading costs that drag on returns. Infrequent rebalancing reduces costs but allows drift away from the intended balance.

## Volatility and the correlation problem

Bitcoin and Treasuries often move in opposite directions. When equity markets panic and investors flee to safety, Bitcoin may sink alongside stocks, but Treasuries typically rally. This negative correlation provides genuine [diversification](/diversification/) benefit in normal market environments. A 60-Bitcoin-40-Treasury fund will swing less violently than owning Bitcoin alone.

The real risk emerges during systemic crises. If a financial panic is severe enough—a banking failure, a currency crisis, or a severe [recession](/recession/)—both risk assets and Bitcoin may decline sharply while investors flee to the dollar itself rather than bonds. In that scenario, Treasuries might not rally enough to offset the Bitcoin losses, and the fund's diversification promise breaks. NGHT is designed for normal regimes and moderate shocks, not for extreme tail events where all risky assets corrode together.

A second volatility risk is rebalancing slippage. If the fund's target is 50-50 and Bitcoin rallies sharply, the Bitcoin portion grows to 60 percent of the fund. The fund must then sell Bitcoin and buy Treasuries to restore balance. Selling a rising asset to buy a modest-yielding bond is a cost paid by the fund—not unusual, but real.

## Costs and trading access

The fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) reflects the costs of holding, custodying, and rebalancing both assets. Bitcoin custody is more expensive than bond custody, so NGHT's expense ratio typically runs higher than a pure Treasury fund or a standard [equity ETF](/equity-etf/). The exact ratio is disclosed in the prospectus and fact sheet.

NGHT trades on major U.S. exchanges during normal market hours, like any ETF. Investors buy and sell shares at market prices. The fund's [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/)—the cost paid to enter and exit—is typically modest for a specialized fund but wider than a mega-cap ETF.

## Who should own it

NGHT suits Bitcoin believers who want to dampen the ride. If owning 100 percent Bitcoin feels emotionally or strategically untenable because of volatility, mixing in Treasuries via NGHT offers a compromise without owning separate funds.

It also appeals to traditional investors who see potential in Bitcoin but prefer not to own it outright—they gain exposure within a professionally rebalanced vehicle rather than managing the technical and psychological aspects of crypto ownership themselves.

NGHT is not for pure Bitcoin investors who believe the asset will massively outperform. Holding bonds alongside dilutes upside in a [bull market](/bull-market/). It is also not for bond-only investors; those are better served by dedicated Treasury or bond [index funds](/index-fund/). And it is unsuitable for investors uncomfortable with cryptocurrency concepts or those who view Bitcoin as wholly speculative.

## How to research NGHT

The fund's prospectus and fact sheet from Nicholas Investments are the essential starting point. They specify the exact Bitcoin holding mechanism, the Treasury maturity profile, the target weighting, the rebalancing frequency, the expense ratio, and performance history.

An investor should also honestly assess their own view on Bitcoin and their tolerance for its volatility. Because the fund blends two very different asset classes, understanding what you own and why is critical. Compare NGHT's performance and expense ratio to owning separate Bitcoin and Treasury funds alongside each other. Sometimes buying two funds with independent management and rebalancing is clearer than owning a blended fund, depending on your goals and liquidity needs.
