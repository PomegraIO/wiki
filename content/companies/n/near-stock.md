*The **iShares Short Duration [Bond](/bond/) [Active ETF](/active-etf/)** (NEAR) is an exchange-traded fund that holds a portfolio of shorter-maturity bonds selected through active management, aiming to deliver current income while moderating exposure to interest-rate swings.*

## What bonds are here, and why duration matters

NEAR is not a passive index tracker. Instead, BlackRock's iShares team actively selects individual bonds for the portfolio, focusing on securities with maturities typically ranging from one to five years. That short time horizon is the fund's defining feature. A bond's duration — the average length of time before its principal is repaid — is the chief lever that determines its [interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/). When [interest rates](/interest-rate/) rise, bond prices fall; longer-duration bonds feel that pain more sharply. A five-year bond's price might drop 5 per cent when rates climb a percentage point, while a thirty-year bond could lose 15 per cent or more. By staying short, NEAR trades some of the income a longer bond would yield for protection against rate moves.

The bonds themselves are typically investment-grade — rated as relatively safe by credit-rating agencies — and may include US Treasuries, corporate issues, and securities backed by mortgages or other assets. The active management means a human team (or teams) rather than a rules-based index definition decides which bonds to buy and sell. That approach can add value if the managers spot cheaper securities or avoid ones about to be downgraded, but it also means the fund's performance depends on skill rather than simply matching the market.

## An income weapon for an uncertain-rate world

The appeal of NEAR lies in its specificity. An investor who wants current income but believes interest rates will rise or remain volatile faces a choice: hold longer bonds and endure larger price swings, or hold cash and earn minimal interest. NEAR occupies the middle ground — it yields more than a money-market fund or [Treasury bills](/treasury-bill/) yet does not punish you as savagely if the [Federal Reserve](/federal-reserve/) raises rates. That positioning makes it useful for income-seeking portfolios, particularly for investors who cannot stomach the volatility of longer-bond funds.

Because it is actively managed, NEAR's exact holdings and strategy can shift. The fund might overweight [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/) in a particular sector, reduce exposure to one mortgage type, or favour shorter Treasuries in anticipation of rate moves. That flexibility is a strength when the managers read the market correctly and a weakness when they do not. Unlike a purely passive fund where you know exactly what you hold, you are placing some confidence in the fund's team.

## Costs and how NEAR fits into a bond portfolio

Actively managed bond funds typically charge higher [expense ratios](/expense-ratio/) than passive alternatives — NEAR's annual fee is a modest fraction of assets, reflecting the cost of research and trading. That fee comes from fund assets before returns are distributed, so it reduces what you ultimately receive. For comparison, a passively indexed short-bond fund might charge less, but NEAR's active approach aims to compensate through superior security selection.

NEAR trades on an exchange like a stock, so you can buy or sell it during market hours at prices that move throughout the day. That liquidity makes it easy to enter or exit the position, though you do pay a [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) — the small gap between the price you pay to buy and the price you receive to sell. For most investors, that spread is negligible on a fund this large.

## The real risks to watch

Interest-rate risk persists even with short duration; rates can still hurt, just less severely. [Credit risk](/credit-risk/) is real too — the companies or governments that issued the bonds can default, though investment-grade status means that risk is relatively contained. [Reinvestment risk](/reinvestment-risk/) is subtler: as bonds mature and cash flows in, you must reinvest those proceeds at whatever rates prevail at that moment, which may be lower than what the maturing bonds were earning. If rates fall sharply, reinvestment becomes painful.

The active-management gamble is itself a risk. If BlackRock's team misreads the market — favoring bonds that later underperform, or avoiding ones that later appreciate — the fund will lag a simpler, cheaper passive alternative. That is the trade NEAR takes: you pay slightly more in fees and accept the possibility of underperformance in exchange for the opportunity of a skilled manager spotting value.

## How to evaluate NEAR

Start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet on BlackRock's iShares website, which detail the investment objective and permitted bond types. Watch the fund's average duration, which tells you directly how much interest-rate sensitivity you are accepting. Compare NEAR's returns and yield against other [short-duration bond funds](/short-duration-bond-fund/), both active and passive, to assess whether the managers are justifying their fees. As with any fixed-income fund, understand that price moves daily as market interest rates fluctuate, and a rising-rate environment can temporarily depress the share price even though the bonds inside are performing according to plan.
