The **Neuberger Short Duration [Income ETF](/income-etf/)** (NBSD) is an actively managed, fixed-income fund that constructs a diversified portfolio of shorter-duration [bonds](/bond/)—predominantly investment-grade government and corporate debt, with a minor allocation to higher-yielding securities below investment grade. The fund seeks to generate reliable current income while limiting the interest-rate sensitivity that comes with longer-maturity bond portfolios.

## Duration as the core lever

Duration measures how much a bond's price moves when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) shift. A five-year duration bond falls roughly 5 percent in price for every 1 percentage point rise in interest rates, while a two-year duration bond falls only 2 percent. NBSD's focus on short duration means it is far less exposed to interest-rate movements than a traditional broad bond fund. This makes NBSD suitable for investors concerned about rising rates or those unwilling to accept large mark-to-market losses if the environment turns unfavourable, yet still seeking income above what money-market funds or savings accounts offer.

The fund does not target a fixed duration—Neuberger Berman's managers actively adjust the maturity mix within a reasonable band as their outlook evolves. If they expect rates to rise further, they might shorten duration further. If they see value in intermediate bonds, they might extend slightly. This active discretion is what distinguishes NBSD from a purely passive short-term bond [index fund](/index-fund/).

## What the portfolio holds

NBSD's 923 holdings span multiple bond categories: US Treasury and agency securities, investment-grade [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/), mortgage-backed securities, asset-backed securities, [collateralized debt obligations](/collateralized-debt-obligation/), and [collateralized loan obligations](/collateralized-loan-obligation/). Roughly 84 percent of assets are in domestic bonds, with the remainder in foreign sovereigns and corporate issuers. The 0.35 percent [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is exceptionally low for an actively managed bond fund, suggesting that Neuberger Berman is confident enough in the strategy's alpha potential to run it with minimal overhead.

Within this broadly diversified framework, Neuberger Berman's analysts assess [credit risk](/credit-risk/). The fund's policy permits up to 20 percent of assets in non-investment-grade (speculative or "junk") bonds, but the managers seek issuers in relatively stable financial condition, targeting credits whose ratings may improve rather than deteriorate. The [current yield](/current-yield/) of 4.84 percent reflects the weighted average coupon of the holdings plus any gains from tactical trading and credit selection.

## Why Morningstar rates it highly

NBSD carries a 4-star Morningstar rating over the 3- and 5-year periods and a 3-star rating over 10 years, based on risk-adjusted returns relative to peers in the short-term bond category. This places it in the upper quartile of performance for short-duration funds. The fund has navigated interest-rate volatility and [credit cycles](/credit-cycle/) effectively, delivering income to shareholders while protecting them from the worst downsides of longer-duration bonds during recent rate-hiking cycles.

## The risks and trade-offs

NBSD is not risk-free. Short-duration bonds still carry credit risk—if a issuer defaults or its [credit rating](/credit-rating/) falls sharply, the fund's value declines. The fund's willingness to hold up to 20 percent in below-investment-grade bonds amplifies this risk slightly, though the managers aim to own higher-quality names within that spectrum. [Interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/), while muted compared to long-term bonds, is not eliminated; rising rates still result in mark-to-market losses on existing holdings.

[Liquidity risk](/liquidity-risk/) is generally low, given the diversified holdings and the fund's significant assets under management. However, in severe market dislocations—when trading in bonds becomes strained and [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) widen—NBSD could face wider intra-day trading ranges. The fund also offers no capital appreciation potential; returns depend almost entirely on the income collected and any modest price gains from credit tightening or declining rates.

## Researching NBSD before investing

Prospective investors should request NBSD's fact sheet and most recent [annual report](/10-k/) from Neuberger Berman, which detail the exact holdings, credit ratings, maturity ladder, and sector weights. Comparing NBSD's yield and trailing total returns to peers—such as the [Bloomberg US Aggregate Bond Index](/bloomberg-aggregate-bond-index/), the BBloomberg US Intermediate Credit Index, and other short-duration active funds—provides context on relative value. Checking the fund's holdings for any concentrated positions, unfamiliar issuers, or exotic structures (especially collateralized loan obligations) offers comfort that the portfolio is truly diversified. Finally, reviewing the prospectus for the fees, potential tax impact of distributions, and any liquidity provisions ensures a clear understanding of the fund's structure before commitment.
