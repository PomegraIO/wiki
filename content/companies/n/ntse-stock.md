### What exactly is NTSE?

NTSE is an exchange-traded fund managed by WisdomTree that invests primarily in large-cap emerging-market stocks, with a meaningful allocation to U.S. Treasury [futures contracts](/futures-contract/). It trades on NYSEArca and holds approximately 90 percent of assets in emerging-market equities, weighted by [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/), with the remainder in Treasury futures. The fund trades at a [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) of roughly 45 dollars per share, though this changes daily with market movements.

### Which companies does NTSE actually own?

The largest holdings are technology and semiconductor firms dominating emerging markets: [Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing](/tsm-stock/) Company, Samsung Electronics, and SK hynix together comprise nearly 40 percent of the fund. These are not small companies; they are global giants that happen to be incorporated and traded in Taiwan, South Korea, and other emerging economies. The fund concentrates risk in the technology sector, which accounts for roughly half of all holdings, making it less diversified than a broad emerging-market index would be. Financial services and consumer cyclical stocks round out the portfolio, though in smaller amounts.

### Why does it hold Treasury futures if it is supposed to be an emerging-market fund?

The Treasury futures holdings (roughly 10 percent of assets) serve multiple purposes in the fund's structure. They provide ballast—Treasury returns often move opposite to stock returns during market stress, so the futures offer some portfolio [diversification](/diversification/) beyond what stocks alone would provide. They also reflect WisdomTree's model-based portfolio construction philosophy. Rather than holding individual foreign government [bonds](/bond/) or international fixed-income securities, the fund uses liquid U.S. Treasury futures to achieve bond-like exposure while maintaining operational simplicity. The fund is not a bond fund; the Treasury piece is tactical, designed to soften volatility without requiring the fund to own foreign fixed-income securities or deal with currency complications on the bond side.

### What does "efficient core" actually mean?

It means the fund uses a quantitative model to identify which emerging-market stocks offer the best combination of valuation, quality, and other systematic factors, rather than picking stocks based on fundamental research into individual companies. The model tilts the portfolio toward stocks scoring well on dimensions like [earnings quality](/earnings-quality/) and reasonable valuations. The "core" part indicates the fund aims at broad emerging-market exposure (as opposed to a concentrated bet on one country or sector), even though the actual holdings lean technology-heavy because that is where emerging-market quality and scale happen to reside.

### Why is it concentrated in Asia and semiconductors?

Taiwan, South Korea, and other Asian economies dominate global semiconductors and consumer electronics. NTSE's concentration reflects the reality that emerging-market large-caps are heavily skewed toward technology and manufacturing concentrated in Asia. If an investor wants diversified emerging-market exposure across many regions and sectors, NTSE is not the right choice; a traditional broad emerging-market [index fund](/index-fund/) would serve better. NTSE is more of a "efficient emerging-market large-cap technology" fund in practice, even if that is not its formal name.

### What are the risks?

The obvious one is sector concentration: roughly half the fund is technology, and technology stocks are cyclical. A downturn in semiconductor demand or a sharp drop in semiconductor valuations would hit NTSE hard. [Currency risk](/currency-risk/) is also present, though WisdomTree does not explicitly hedge foreign-exchange exposure, so NTSE rises when the companies' home currencies strengthen against the dollar and falls when they weaken. Political and economic risk in emerging markets—regulatory changes, capital controls, geopolitical tensions—also apply, particularly given the concentration in Taiwan and South Korea. The fund is smaller than major competing emerging-market ETFs (assets under management near 58 million dollars), so liquidity during stressed markets may be thinner than investors expect.

### How would someone research NTSE before investing?

Read WisdomTree's fact sheet and prospectus to understand the current composition, the Treasury futures allocation, and the fund's performance versus broad emerging-market indices over several years. Compare NTSE's returns and holdings to a traditional emerging-market index ETF to see whether the model-based "efficient core" approach adds value. Check the fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) (relative to competitors) and understand what you are paying for active management. Look at the 10-K filings of the largest holdings—Taiwan Semiconductor, Samsung, SK hynix—to understand the health of the semiconductor cycle. Finally, consider whether you want concentrated technology exposure or broader geographic and sectoral diversification, which would point toward a different fund entirely.
