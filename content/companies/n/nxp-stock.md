Nuveen Select Tax Free Income Portfolio is not a company in the sense of a business that makes or sells things. It is a closed-end fund, a financial structure that pools money from investors and buys and holds a portfolio of securities. In this case, the portfolio is [municipal bonds](/municipal-bond/) — debt issued by state and local governments — chosen specifically because the interest income they generate is exempt from federal income tax. For investors in high tax brackets, that tax exemption is the whole appeal.

## How a closed-end fund differs from an open-end mutual fund

When you buy shares in an open-end [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/), the fund sells new shares to you and buys back old shares from departing investors on demand. The fund's assets grow and shrink with inflows and outflows. A closed-end fund, by contrast, raises capital once at inception and then closes. The fund issues a fixed number of shares, and those shares trade on an exchange like regular stocks. If you want to own the fund, you buy shares from someone else who is selling them, at a price set by market supply and demand.

That distinction matters. An [open-end fund's](/open-end-fund/) share price is always anchored to its [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) — the total value of the [bonds](/bond/) and cash it holds, divided by the number of shares outstanding. A closed-end fund's share price can trade above or below that net asset value, depending on investor appetite. If investors are eager to buy municipal bonds but do not want to buy them directly, they flock to tax-exempt [closed-end funds](/closed-end-fund/) and bid up the share price above the underlying value. If sentiment turns, the fund can trade at a discount.

## What the portfolio holds

Nuveen Select Tax Free Income Portfolio's holdings are municipal bonds from across the United States — debt issued by states, cities, counties, and special authorities like school districts or transportation agencies. These bonds finance roads, schools, water systems, and other public infrastructure. The fund holds hundreds of bonds, spread across many issuers and geographies, to avoid [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) — if one city or state enters financial trouble, it does not devastate the portfolio.

The bonds in the portfolio are typically of investment-grade credit quality, meaning they are considered unlikely to default. Municipal bonds have historically had low [default rates](/default-rate/); cities and states rarely stop paying bondholders, in part because the reputational damage is severe and in part because the debt is often senior to other government spending. Still, not all municipal issuers are equally safe. Some cities and states face structural budget problems, declining populations, or rising [pension obligations](/pension-obligation/) that create genuine [credit risk](/credit-risk/).

## Why the tax exemption matters

The interest income from municipal bonds is exempt from federal income tax, and often from state income tax if you buy bonds from your home state. That exemption is valuable to investors in high marginal tax brackets. An investor facing a 37% federal tax rate would have to earn a taxable yield of roughly 4% to generate the same after-tax income as a municipal bond yielding 2.5%. For a wealthy investor, that is a powerful incentive to hold municipal bonds rather than taxable alternatives.

The tax exemption is also a subsidy from the federal government, designed to encourage states and municipalities to borrow cheaply for public works. It means municipal bonds typically offer lower yields than taxable [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/) of similar credit quality. The trade-off is acceptable for high-income investors; for lower-income investors, it is not, which is why [municipal bond funds](/municipal-bond-fund/) are concentrated among the wealthy.

## How the fund generates returns

Nuveen Select Tax Free Income generates returns in two ways. First, the bonds in the portfolio pay interest, which is distributed to shareholders as [dividends](/dividend/). These dividends are tax-exempt (or state-tax-exempt, depending on the bond holdings). Second, the share price of the fund itself can rise or fall based on market conditions and investor sentiment. If [interest rates](/interest-rate/) fall, the value of existing bonds rises, boosting the portfolio's net asset value and potentially the share price. If sentiment turns against municipal bonds, the share price can fall even if the portfolio is performing well.

The fund is managed actively: Nuveen's portfolio managers buy and sell bonds constantly, trying to optimize yield, manage credit risk, and rotate the portfolio as conditions change. The fund charges a [management fee](/management-fee/) (typically 0.3% to 0.5% annually) taken out of the fund's assets before income is paid to shareholders.

## The leverage question

Many municipal bond closed-end funds, including this one, use financial leverage — they borrow money at short-term interest rates and use the proceeds to buy additional long-term bonds. The bet is that the spread between the yield on the bonds and the cost of borrowing is positive, amplifying returns. When markets are calm, this leverage boosts dividend income. When credit markets seize up or short-term rates spike, leverage can become a problem: the cost of rolling over borrowed money rises, squeezing returns or forcing the fund to sell bonds to raise cash.

Investors in leveraged closed-end funds earn higher distributions in benign environments but face amplified losses in stress scenarios. The leverage is disclosed in the fund's prospectus and in regular reports, but it is a factor that distinguishes municipal bond closed-end funds from open-end municipal funds or direct municipal bond ownership.

## The risks

The primary risk is credit risk: an issuer whose bonds the fund holds could face financial distress and delay or fail to pay interest or principal. A [recession](/recession/) could hit local tax revenues hard, forcing difficult budget choices. A changing state or federal tax code could eliminate or reduce the federal tax exemption for municipal bonds, sharply reducing their attractiveness and value.

Interest rate risk is also present: if rates rise, existing bonds (which pay fixed interest) become less valuable because new bonds offer higher yields. The fund's net asset value falls, and so likely does the share price. Leverage amplifies this risk.

Finally, there is [liquidity risk](/liquidity-risk/): if sentiment toward municipal bonds or closed-end funds sours sharply, the fund could trade at a steep discount to net asset value, and the board might struggle to reinvest proceeds from maturing bonds at acceptable yields.

## How to research Nuveen Select Tax Free Income Portfolio

The fund's net asset value and current share price are updated daily; when those diverge, you are seeing the closed-end fund premium or discount in action. The fund's quarterly reports and [annual reports](/10-k/) detail the portfolio composition, the maturity profile of the bonds, credit quality, and geographic [diversification](/diversification/). The distributions history shows whether dividend payments are growing or shrinking — a sign of underlying portfolio performance.

Compare the fund's yield, [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/), and leverage to other [tax-exempt bond](/tax-exempt-bond/) funds to assess value. Watch the ratio of the share price to net asset value: is the market rating this fund at a significant premium or discount? For investors considering the fund, the critical question is whether the after-tax yield justifies the credit and [interest-rate risks](/interest-rate-risk/), and whether you are getting a bargain on a fund trading at a discount to its underlying assets.
