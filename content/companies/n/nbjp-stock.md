*The **Neuberger Japan [Equity ETF](/equity-etf/)** (NBJP) is an exchange-traded fund that grants investors direct equity exposure to a diversified portfolio of Japanese companies across all [market capitalizations](/market-capitalization/), selected for their perceived quality and growth prospects by Neuberger Berman's active management team.*

## What is NBJP and how does it differ from Japan index funds?

NBJP is an actively managed ETF launched in late 2024 by Neuberger Berman Investment Advisers. Unlike passive [index funds](/index-fund/) that simply track a broad Japanese market benchmark, NBJP employs a fundamental bottom-up stock-picking approach, with managers choosing individual companies they believe offer durable growth and strong quality characteristics. The fund normally invests at least 80 percent of its net assets in equity securities tied economically to Japan, which can include Japanese-domiciled companies and foreign firms with significant Japanese exposure or earnings.

The fund holds approximately 64 securities, with the top 10 holdings comprising roughly one-third of the portfolio. This moderate concentration reflects an intentional bet on the managers' best ideas rather than the comprehensive breadth of a broad market index. The portfolio spans all company sizes—large-cap multinationals, mid-sized industrials, and smaller regional players—rather than narrowing to a single tier.

## How does NBJP generate returns and income?

NBJP's returns come primarily from equity price appreciation, with income generated through [dividends](/dividend/) paid by the underlying holdings. The fund has a trailing yield of approximately 2.2 percent, which is typical for Japanese equities and reflects the dividend culture in Japan, where many large corporations distribute modest but steady payouts to shareholders. Because the fund's focus is growth through stock selection rather than income generation, [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/) is a secondary feature rather than the primary source of return.

Active management introduces the possibility that NBJP's returns will diverge from a Japanese market index, either outperforming or underperforming depending on the skill of the stock-picking process and market conditions. Investors are implicitly betting that the managers' fundamental analysis of company quality will translate into better-than-index results over an investment cycle.

## What are the costs and structure?

NBJP trades on an exchange like any stock—it can be bought and sold during market hours at intra-day prices rather than only at end-of-day [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) like traditional [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/). The gross [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is 1.92 percent, though Neuberger Berman has negotiated waivers that bring the net expense ratio down to 0.50 percent. This fee includes the cost of active management, research, and portfolio rebalancing. The lower net ratio makes NBJP more competitively priced than many actively managed mutual funds tracking Japan, though it remains higher than passive Japan index ETFs.

The fund provides daily portfolio transparency, meaning investors can see the complete list of holdings and weights at the end of each trading day. This transparency allows shareholders to understand exactly what companies and sectors they own, and to monitor the portfolio for any drift in strategy over time.

## What are the real risks for NBJP investors?

As a Japan-focused equity fund, NBJP carries all the risks inherent in a single-country concentration. Japanese equities are sensitive to movements in the yen—when the yen weakens against the dollar, returns for US-based investors are diminished, and when it strengthens, returns are enhanced. Investors holding NBJP therefore have exposure not just to Japanese corporate performance but also to currency fluctuations.

The fund is also subject to the specific economic and regulatory environment of Japan, including interest-rate decisions by the [Bank of Japan](/bank-of-japan/), domestic demographic trends (Japan has an aging, shrinking population), and geopolitical tensions in East Asia. Unlike a globally diversified portfolio, NBJP offers no buffer against Japan-specific shocks.

Active management introduces performance risk—the fund's returns may lag behind a simpler Japan index fund if the managers' stock-picking proves inferior, or if active trading incurs costs that exceed the added value generated. Investors should scrutinize the track record of the management team and compare NBJP's long-term returns to a Japan equity benchmark before committing capital.

## How would a researcher evaluate NBJP?

A prospective investor should begin by reviewing Neuberger Berman's factsheets and the fund's most recent [annual report](/10-k/), which detail the portfolio holdings, sector allocations, and historical returns versus a Japan benchmark such as the MSCI Japan Index or the Nikkei 225. Morningstar, Bloomberg, and major financial data providers publish detailed analytics on NBJP, including performance statistics, risk metrics, and peer comparisons to other Japan equity funds.

Given the fund's relative newness (launched in 2024), track record is limited. The focus should be on the credentials and philosophy of the management team—NBJP is overseen by three portfolio managers with strong tenures at Neuberger Berman—and on how closely the fund's holdings and sector exposures align with the stated strategy. An investor might also monitor the fund's assets under management; a very small fund runs a higher risk of closure or [merger](/merger/) if it fails to attract sufficient capital. Finally, understanding the tax efficiency of the fund (how much of performance is eaten by internal gains realization) matters for holders in taxable accounts, as daily tradability makes NBJP somewhat less tax-efficient than a typical buy-and-hold mutual fund structure.
