NN Inc manufactures precision metal parts and assemblies for customers across the automotive, aerospace, medical device, and industrial sectors. The company operates through a network of facilities under two main banners: its historic NN business (based in Tennessee and specializing in metal parts) and Precision Castparts Aerospace division (acquired to broaden the company's exposure to high-margin aerospace work). NNBR trades on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) and represents a play on industrial outsourcing and the consolidation of North American contract manufacturing.

## What business is NN Inc in?

NN Inc is a contract manufacturer. Its customers—large automotive makers, aerospace primes, medical-device companies, industrial OEMs—outsource the production of precision metal components. NN Inc machines, casts, and assembles those parts according to the customer's design and specifications. The company does not design the parts; it manufactures them. Its value proposition is reliable quality, on-time delivery, reasonable cost, and the ability to scale production up or down as the customer's demand fluctuates.

The work is unglamorous and highly competitive. Thousands of shops across North America do similar work. Competition centers on price, quality, lead time, and the willingness to invest in new tooling and machinery to support a customer's production ramps. Margins are modest in absolute terms, but a well-run contract manufacturer can achieve solid returns by optimizing utilization of assets (machines and labor) and maintaining a steady flow of orders across a diversified customer base.

## How does the company make money?

NN Inc's revenue comes from selling manufactured parts. Each part carries a cost of goods sold (raw materials, labor, machine time, [depreciation](/depreciation/)) and a gross margin. The company earns money on the total volume of parts shipped, minus operating expenses (sales, general overhead, facility costs not directly traceable to a specific customer), and any financing costs on debt. The business is capital-intensive—tooling, equipment, and facility buildout require upfront investment—but once tooling is in place, the marginal cost of additional units drops. A customer that commits to high-volume production over multiple years is therefore attractive to NN Inc.

Most orders come under long-term supply agreements where the customer commits to purchasing parts over a period of years at an agreed-upon price. That gives NN Inc some visibility to future revenue but also locks the company into prices for years, limiting its ability to pass along cost increases. If the company has priced a multi-year contract too aggressively, margins can compress if raw material costs, labor, or energy prices rise faster than expected.

## Why did NN Inc start acquiring companies?

For most of its history, NN Inc remained a relatively small regional manufacturer. But in the 2010s, under new ownership and management, the company shifted strategy: instead of growing organically by winning more customers, it would acquire other manufacturers and consolidate operations. The rationale is straightforward. Many small and mid-sized machine shops are family businesses or private equity-owned operations that are profitable but lack scale, modern tooling, or access to large OEM customers. An acquirer with capital and a sales force can buy such shops at reasonable multiples, replace inefficient management, invest in equipment, and funnel customers to consolidated operations, improving overall returns.

NN Inc made a series of [acquisitions](/acquisition/) in the late 2010s and early 2020s, including the Precision Castparts Aerospace division, which brought aerospace work—typically higher-margin than automotive—into the portfolio. The goal was to build a larger, more diversified contract manufacturer capable of competing for bigger programs and absorbing customer [concentration risk](/concentration-risk/) more effectively.

## What risks does NN Inc face?

Contract manufacturing is cyclical. When automotive and aerospace volumes are strong, demand for NN Inc's parts is strong and margins expand. During [recessions](/recession/) or demand slumps—such as the 2020 pandemic shutdowns—volume and margins compress quickly. NN Inc's ability to quickly right-size its cost structure (lay off workers, idle equipment, reduce overhead) determines how badly a downturn hits profitability.

Customer concentration is a perpetual risk. If one customer represents a large share of revenue (say, 20 percent or more) and that customer reduces orders or exits for a competitor, the impact is material. NN Inc's customer base includes major automotive and aerospace primes, companies with substantial leverage over suppliers on pricing and payment terms.

Raw material costs—steel, aluminum, other metals—fluctuate with commodity markets. NN Inc tries to pass through increases to customers via formula-based pricing in contracts, but not always with perfect timing or success. A sharp spike in material costs before the company can raise prices to customers squeezes margins.

Acquisition integration is another risk. When NN Inc buys a manufacturing operation, integration missteps—retaining the wrong management, shuttering facilities too aggressively, failing to cross-sell new customers to acquired operations, or overpaying—can destroy value. The company's track record of acquisitions will ultimately determine whether the consolidation strategy delivers expected returns.

## How to research NN Inc

Start with the company's annual 10-K filing (SEC CIK 0000918541) and quarterly reports, which break down revenue by end market (automotive, aerospace, medical, industrial) and by customer concentration. Watch for quarterly commentary on capacity utilization, pricing trends, raw material costs, and new customer wins or losses. Industry reports on automotive production and aerospace orders provide context for NN Inc's expected demand trends. [Credit ratings](/credit-rating/) and debt covenants matter because NN Inc carries debt from acquisitions, and deteriorating business conditions could create covenant pressure. The company's strategy around future acquisitions and integration efficiency is worth tracking; management commentary on these topics appears in earnings calls. As with any cyclical manufacturer, NN Inc's stock is sensitive to macroeconomic outlook and sector-specific demand signals, not to long-term earnings-per-share growth.
