*The **[Nuveen Municipal Income](/nmi-stock/) ETF** (NUMI) is a passive [bond](/bond/) fund that holds a diversified portfolio of investment-grade municipal securities issued by U.S. states, cities, and other local authorities — offering income that is typically exempt from federal income tax and often from state tax as well.*

[Municipal bonds](/municipal-bond/) are loans to local governments. A city issues a bond to finance a bridge or school; a state issues one to build or maintain a highway; an airport authority issues one to renovate a terminal. Investors who buy these bonds lend money and receive interest payments over the life of the security. What makes municipal bonds distinctive is the tax treatment: the interest paid on most municipal bonds is exempt from federal income tax, and often exempt from the income taxes of the state where the bond is issued. This tax advantage makes a 3 percent municipal bond yield roughly equivalent to a 4 percent or higher yield on a taxable bond, depending on the investor's tax bracket.

NUMI bundles these securities into a single, continuously rebalanced portfolio. Instead of buying individual bonds and managing defaults or reinvestment, investors own shares of the fund and participate in the combined income and price movements of hundreds of underlying bonds.

## The universe and the screen

The fund targets investment-grade municipal bonds — those rated in the top four categories by ratings agencies, indicating a low statistical likelihood of default. The fund typically holds municipal securities across all major sectors: general-obligation bonds (backed by the issuer's broad taxing power), [revenue bonds](/revenue-bond/) (backed by a specific project or revenue source like a toll road or utilities), and sometimes more specialized municipals issued by airports, universities, or housing authorities.

The portfolio is weighted toward longer-duration bonds, meaning the average maturity is several years out. This gives investors exposure to [interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/) — if rates rise sharply, the market value of the fund's holdings falls, and vice versa. In exchange, longer-duration bonds offer higher yields, which appeals to income-seeking investors willing to accept that volatility.

Nuveen's process involves continuous screening for credit quality, [diversification](/diversification/) across issuers and sectors, and sometimes tilts based on valuation. The fund avoids speculative-grade municipals, which eliminates some higher-yielding but riskier securities from consideration.

## The tax edge and who benefits

For a high-income taxpayer, the tax exemption on municipal bond interest is the entire reason to own them. A bond yielding 3 percent tax-free can be more valuable than a 4.5 percent bond on which you pay 35 percent tax. For investors in lower tax brackets, the advantage shrinks or disappears — a retired person with minimal federal tax liability might be better served by a taxable bond fund with a higher yield.

This is why municipal funds are often held in taxable accounts, not tax-sheltered retirement accounts where the tax advantage is wasted. It is also why residents of high-tax states sometimes own funds holding bonds from their own state, capturing both federal and state tax exemption.

## Risks and default rates

Municipal defaults are rare but not unknown. A handful of spectacular defaults — Detroit, Stockton, Puerto Rico — capture headlines and worry investors. In practice, historical [default rates](/default-rate/) on investment-grade municipals are very low, but they are not zero, and they tend to cluster during [recessions](/recession/) when local governments see tax revenue evaporate. An economic downturn could pressure some of the fund's holdings.

Interest-rate risk is the more immediate concern. If the [Federal Reserve](/federal-reserve/) raises rates, the market value of existing municipal bonds falls, and the fund's price falls along with them. Conversely, when rates fall, the bonds rise in value. Someone who needs the fund's assets in the near term should be aware that intermediate-term price swings can be meaningful.

There is also [reinvestment risk](/reinvestment-risk/): as bonds mature, the fund receives the principal back and must reinvest it in a new environment. If rates have fallen, the new bonds will carry lower yields, meaning income from the fund declines.

## Cost and liquidity

NUMI trades as a listed ETF on an exchange and carries a low [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/), typically under 0.3 percent annually — very competitive for a managed bond fund. The fund is usually liquid, with tight [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/), making it easy and cheap to enter and exit.

## How to research municipal bonds and funds

Anyone considering NUMI should start with the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, which spell out the credit quality distribution (what percentage is AAA-rated versus A-rated), the sector breakdown, and the average maturity. Compare NUMI's yield to that of other municipal ETFs and to a taxable [bond ETF](/bond-etf/), then calculate which is better for your tax bracket. Review the fund's largest holdings to see which states and local governments dominate the portfolio. And understand your own tax situation — if you are in a low tax bracket, the tax exemption may not be worth the typically lower yields municipal bonds carry relative to taxable alternatives.
