*The **Miller Value Partners Leverage ETF** (ticker MVPL) is a leveraged vehicle designed to amplify the returns of a value-oriented equity strategy. Using derivatives and borrowed capital, the fund aims to deliver approximately twice the daily return of its underlying value index — a tool for investors with high risk tolerance and a short-term time horizon, not a buy-and-hold retirement holding.*

## The leverage mechanism and daily reset

MVPL achieves its 2x leverage through a combination of long stock positions, index futures, and swaps — financial instruments that allow the fund to control more assets than its cash balance alone permits. Each day, the fund rebalances to maintain a consistent 2x exposure ratio to its underlying value strategy. This daily reset is the crux of how the fund works and why it behaves very differently from owning the underlying value stocks outright.

The daily-reset mechanism means that MVPL is mechanically optimized to track 2x the underlying index's daily performance, not its monthly or annual performance. On a day when the index rises 2 per cent, MVPL aims to rise roughly 4 per cent. On a day it falls 2 per cent, MVPL falls roughly 4 per cent. But this compounding over extended periods creates a divergence called **volatility decay** — the fund's long-term return can lag significantly behind twice the long-term return of the underlying index, especially in choppy or sideways markets.

## Volatility decay and the compounding trap

To illustrate: suppose the underlying value index returns 0 per cent over a year, zigzagging up and down with a lot of daily turbulence. MVPL, rebalancing daily to stay 2x leveraged, will likely finish below zero because it locks in losses on down days and only reaps half-strength gains on up days — a mathematical quirk of leveraged daily-reset vehicles.

Conversely, in a strongly uptrending market with few reversals, MVPL captures nearly the full 2x benefit. The fund works best in trending conditions and worst in choppy, sideways, or declining markets where volatility is high but net direction is unclear.

This characteristic means MVPL is fundamentally not a long-term buy-and-hold tool. Investors who deploy it do so with explicit short or medium-term bets — often a few weeks to a few months — timing a specific market environment.

## Leverage and margin risk

Because MVPL borrows to achieve 2x leverage, it carries **margin risk**. If the underlying value stocks fall steeply, the fund's borrowing costs can mount, and in extreme scenarios, daily losses could exceed the fund's capital. Regulatory guardrails prevent that — the fund is structured to avoid true bankruptcy — but the point remains: leverage amplifies not just gains but also losses, and during market stress, leveraged positions may face forced [liquidations](/liquidation/) or sharp tracking divergences.

The fund's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is higher than unlevered alternatives, reflecting the costs of borrowing capital and managing derivatives. Investors must weigh whether the potential for amplified returns justifies both the higher annual costs and the leverage risks.

## The investor profile

MVPL is designed for a very specific type of investor: one with sophisticated [market timing](/market-timing/) skill (or luck), a short time horizon, and genuine risk tolerance. It suits traders taking a directional bet — those convinced that value stocks will outperform significantly over the next few weeks or months and willing to risk loss if they are wrong. It is not appropriate for buy-and-hold investors seeking exposure to value stocks as a long-term equity allocation. Those investors are better served by the unlevered MVPA or traditional value [mutual funds](/mutual-fund/).

Regulatory rules recognize this distinction. Many brokerages categorize MVPL as a product suitable only for experienced investors, not for retail accounts without options trading approval.

## Tax inefficiency

[Leveraged ETFs](/leveraged-etf/) realize capital gains more frequently than their unleveraged peers because the daily rebalancing locks in small losses and gains repeatedly throughout the year. Even if the fund's net annual return matches its index target, the realized gains subject to taxation may be substantially higher — a drag on after-tax returns, especially in taxable accounts. Tax-sheltered accounts (IRAs, 401(k)s) are more appropriate for leveraged funds, though most retirement plans discourage or prohibit their use anyway, citing the unsuitability for long-term retirement savings.

## Sizing the bet and position management

Investors using MVPL typically employ [position sizing](/position-sizing/) discipline: holding a small fraction of total portfolio capital in the leveraged position, with clear entry and exit rules. A common approach is to allocate no more than 5–10 per cent of speculative capital to leveraged instruments, with tight stop-losses if the market moves against the thesis. Treating MVPL as a "set and forget" holding — loading it into a retirement account and holding it for twenty years — is a recipe for severe shortfall relative to the unleveraged alternative, due to volatility decay.

## How to research MVPL

Before using MVPL, study the fund's prospectus carefully, paying special attention to the section on how leverage is achieved and the daily reset mechanism. Simulators and back-tests abound online; run historical scenarios to see how a 2x leveraged value portfolio would have performed during periods of sideways markets and volatility spikes.

Compare MVPL's historical returns against the unleveraged MVPA or a simple value-stock benchmark, measured over periods of varying length. Observe the divergence — it should grow wider in choppy periods and narrower in trending ones. This exercise clarifies whether the fund's leverage is a feature or a liability for your anticipated [holding period](/holding-period/).

Finally, be absolutely clear on your time horizon before buying. MVPL is a tactical tool for weeks or months; it is not a structural portfolio building block.
