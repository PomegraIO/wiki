Roundhill Meme Stock ETF (MEME) is a thematic exchange-traded fund that tracks an index of stocks identified as having attracted significant community-driven retail investor attention, typically on platforms like Reddit and Twitter. The fund is sponsored by Roundhill Investments.

The underlying index selects stocks that have experienced outsized retail interest — elevated trading volume on retail-focused [brokers](/broker/), discussion volume on social platforms, and sustained community attention. This is distinct from traditional stock-selection methods based on valuation, growth, or sector. The index is designed to capture the basket of names that have been subjects of community enthusiasm at any given point in time.

[Index rebalancing](/index-rebalancing/) presents a real question: meme status is transient. A stock that is heavily discussed today may fade from attention in weeks, replaced by a new name capturing the community's focus. The index methodology attempts to capture this churn by tracking sentiment and trading activity rather than selecting names that are expected to be there forever. This means MEME's holdings turn over meaningfully, sometimes dramatically.

The fund's trading costs reflect both the volatility of its holdings and the trading costs involved in frequent rebalancing. Holdings in MEME are volatile by construction — a stock does not attract meme status by being a stable, dividend-paying utility. Most are small-cap or mid-cap names with low analyst coverage and high short interest, characteristics that can amplify both upside and downside swings.

The risks are severe. Volatility is extreme relative to the broader market. Many holdings are thinly traded, so large positions can experience meaningful [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/). The index can experience sudden, large positions in highly speculative names near the peak of their popularity, which can create difficult sell situations as the initial enthusiasm fades. There is no valuation discipline — the index captures attention, not value. A retail-investor bubble in a stock does not mean the company is worth what the market is paying, only that collective attention has pushed the price there.

Investors holding MEME should understand they are not diversifying into a genuine asset class but rather making a concentrated bet on the continuation of a particular pattern of retail enthusiasm. The fund's performance is highly dependent on whether new memes emerge regularly and whether prior favorites sustain community support long enough to remain in the index.

To research MEME, examine the current holdings and the index methodology on Roundhill's website. Check the turnover rate to understand how frequently the basket is changing. Compare the [historical volatility](/historical-volatility/) and returns to broad market indexes and to other small-cap funds. Watch the [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) and trading spread. Monitor community platforms informally to understand which names are currently subject to enthusiasm, to grasp the real-time input to the index. Any investor considering this fund should approach it with realistic expectations about volatility and the speculative nature of the underlying theme.
