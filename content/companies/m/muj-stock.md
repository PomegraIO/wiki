MUJ is a closed-end investment company that invests primarily in a portfolio of [municipal bonds](/municipal-bond/) issued by New Jersey municipalities and authorities. As a closed-end fund, it operates differently from an open-ended mutual fund: shares trade on an exchange like stock, the fund has a fixed number of outstanding shares, and it can trade at a premium or discount to its underlying [net asset value](/net-asset-value/). The fund's appeal lies in delivering tax-exempt income to shareholders through municipal bond holdings while concentrating on a single state's debt market, thereby allowing investors to tailor exposure to New Jersey-specific credit conditions and tax benefits.

## Formation and Early Strategy

MUJ was founded to serve investors seeking exposure to high-quality municipal debt in New Jersey. The fund emerged during an era when closed-end bond funds proliferated, offering a structured vehicle for retail investors to access professional bond management and [diversification](/diversification/) that would have been difficult to assemble individually. The fund's original mandate reflected a straightforward thesis: quality New Jersey municipal bonds deliver tax-exempt income while remaining safer than lower-grade credits, and professional management could navigate the complexities of municipal credit analysis across issuers ranging from school districts to toll authorities.

[BlackRock](/blk-stock/) Advisors has managed the fund since its inception, positioning it within a broader family of municipal bond [closed-end funds](/closed-end-fund/). The BlackRock brand brought institutional credibility and resources — analytics, credit expertise, and relationships with municipal issuers — that differentiated the fund from smaller, non-affiliated competitors. The fund's intra-state focus was deliberate: by concentrating solely on New Jersey bonds, MUJ allows shareholders in the state to benefit from the exemption of state income tax on municipal interest, creating a tax advantage for the intended audience.

## How the Fund Makes Money and Distributes It

MUJ generates revenue almost entirely from the interest income earned on its bond holdings. The portfolio typically consists of revenue bonds and [general obligation bonds](/general-obligation-bond/) issued by New Jersey municipalities, educational authorities, utilities, and transportation systems. The fund buys these bonds in the [secondary market](/secondary-market/) or at issuance, collects the coupons (interest payments), and passes the after-cost net income to shareholders as monthly distributions.

The fund's total return comes from two sources: the coupon income collected, which flows directly to shareholders, and any capital appreciation or depreciation as bond prices fluctuate. When [interest rates](/interest-rate/) rise, bond prices fall, pressuring net asset value. When rates fall, existing bonds rise in price, creating gains. As a closed-end fund, MUJ also experiences premiums and discounts to net asset value based on supply and demand for its shares on the exchange, which can create trading opportunities or risks for buyers and sellers.

The fund operates with leverage — it borrows money to amplify its bond holdings — a feature that increases both the income stream and the risk exposure. The leverage is structured carefully to stay within legal limits and risk tolerances, but it means that a sharp downturn in bond prices will hit leveraged returns harder than an unleveraged fund would experience. This amplification works both ways: in favorable markets, it enhances distributions; in stressed environments, it exaggerates losses.

## Municipal Credit in New Jersey

New Jersey's municipal debt market reflects the state's economic diversity and fiscal challenges. The state includes wealthy communities, major urban centers, declining industrial regions, and significant [pension obligations](/pension-obligation/). MUJ's portfolio must navigate this spectrum: some New Jersey credits are exceptionally strong, while others face structural deficits or demographic headwinds. The fund aims to hold quality issuers, avoiding the deepest credits but accepting some economic sensitivity rather than holding only the lowest-risk municipals.

The revenue sources behind New Jersey bonds vary widely. School districts fund operations partly through property taxes and state aid, creating exposure to property-tax base volatility and state budget politics. Toll authorities depend on traffic, sensitive to economic cycles. Utility authorities depend on user fees. General obligation bonds back the full taxing power of the issuer, while revenue bonds back specific income streams, making the latter more sensitive to business conditions.

New Jersey itself maintains an intermediate credit rating, not top-tier but solid, reflecting the state's economic base balanced against high public-employee pension liabilities and historical budgeting pressures. Individual municipal credits can diverge significantly from the state rating: a wealthy suburb might trade near AAA territory while a distressed urban district could trade much lower. MUJ's job is to identify and hold credits where quality justifies the yield, avoiding value traps and concentrating on names likely to service debt on time.

## Risk and Duration Management

The fund's main risks fall into several categories. [Interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/) is primary: if rates rise sharply, the market value of held bonds declines, pressuring the fund's net asset value. Duration — a measure of interest-rate sensitivity — is material. The fund typically holds intermediate-to-longer maturity bonds to capture yield, meaning significant duration risk. Rising rates can trigger a substantial drawdown in NAV.

Credit risk is the second major category. If a New Jersey issuer enters a fiscal crisis, faces a ratings downgrade, or defaults on debt, MUJ's portfolio suffers. While the fund targets investment-grade credits, the municipal market can surprise: allegedly strong issuers have faced sudden budget crises, and pension obligations have proven larger than anticipated.

Leverage amplifies both duration and credit risk. If NAV declines, the leverage ratio grows (the amount borrowed is fixed while the asset value shrinks), potentially forcing the fund to sell bonds at unfavorable prices or restricting its ability to maintain leverage levels. In severe stress, leverage becomes a liability.

Liquidity risk affects secondary-market New Jersey municipal bonds unevenly. Some bonds trade frequently; others are thinly traded. During market dislocations, [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) widen and the fund may find it hard to rebalance or meet redemptions without price concessions.

There is also a structural risk unique to closed-end funds: the discount-to-NAV problem. If shares trade at a significant discount, a shareholder selling shares realizes a loss below NAV even if the fund's investments are unchanged. Conversely, a fund at a premium creates an opportunity for the fund to issue new shares and invest the proceeds, but premiums can disappear.

## How Investors Can Research the Fund

Anyone interested in MUJ should begin with its annual report and semiannual reports, which detail the holdings, the credit quality distribution, the interest-rate maturity profile, and the use of leverage. The portfolio composition — the specific municipalities and bond types held — reveals the fund's actual credit exposure.

Key metrics include the fund's premium or discount to NAV (is it trading cheap or rich?), the distribution yield (what monthly payout is the fund currently paying as a percentage of share price?), the expense ratio (what percentage of assets the fund consumes in management and operational costs), and [leverage ratios](/leverage-ratio-forex/) (how much is borrowed relative to equity).

Tracking New Jersey municipal credit news is essential: fiscal stress in a major city, state budget actions, pension developments, or demographic shifts in key communities all ripple through the portfolio. The fund's monthly distributions also signal conditions: stable or growing payouts suggest the portfolio is performing well, while cuts indicate stress.

For comparison, a shareholder can look at other New Jersey municipal-focused closed-end funds, regional [tax-exempt bond](/tax-exempt-bond/) mutual funds, or the broader municipal bond index to understand whether MUJ is delivering competitive risk-adjusted returns. The tax benefits of municipal bonds apply uniformly, so differentiation comes from credit selection and expense management.
