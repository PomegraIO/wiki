*The **Direxion Daily META Bull 2X ETF** (ticker METU) is a leveraged exchange-traded fund engineered to deliver approximately twice the daily percentage return of Meta Platforms stock. If Meta rises 2 percent in a trading session, METU targets a 4 percent gain. If Meta falls 3 percent, METU aims to fall 6 percent. The leverage is synthetic — achieved through derivatives rather than borrowed cash — and recalibrated every trading day. It is a tactical instrument for traders with short-term directional conviction, not a buy-and-hold vehicle.*

## The mechanism: daily reset and the volatility drag

METU achieves its 2x target through derivatives — futures contracts, total return swaps, and options on Meta stock. At market close each day, the fund's managers rebalance these positions to reset leverage exactly to 2x for the next trading session. This daily rebalancing is not neutral. In a volatile market, it creates a cost called "volatility decay," which quietly erodes long-holding returns.

The math is unforgiving. Suppose Meta is $100. METU rises 20 percent on day one (Meta +10 percent). Meta then falls 10 percent on day two to $99. METU should fall 20 percent from its new level, landing at a net loss. The outcome: Meta is down 1 percent overall, but METU is down roughly 4 percent. Both instruments captured the volatility, but the daily reset and re-leveraging compounded the drag. The longer the hold period, the choppier the market, the worse this decay becomes. In calm, directional markets (Meta rises 30 percent on a steady climb), decay is minimal. In range-bound or whipsaw environments, it can be substantial.

## Who uses it and when

METU appeals to traders — not investors. The goal is a defined, near-term bet: Meta stock will rise over the next days or weeks, and leverage will amplify that gain. It is a speculation tool that allows investors to double down on a directional view without taking a margin loan from a broker.

The fund is inappropriate for anyone holding beyond a few weeks, uncertain of direction, or unable to tolerate the volatility. Buy-and-hold investors should own Meta stock directly. Those unconfident in Meta's near-term direction should own neither. Those unable to bear a rapid loss should not touch leverage.

## Structure, costs, and liquidity

METU trades on an exchange like any ETF, with tight bid-ask spreads and good intraday liquidity. The expense ratio reflects the cost of rolling derivatives positions and is higher than a plain Meta ETF, but modest in percentage terms. No margin interest accrues — the leverage is embedded in the fund structure, not external borrowing.

## Downside and tail risk

A 10 percent fall in Meta becomes a 20 percent fall in METU. A 20 percent crash becomes 40 percent or worse (volatility decay can amplify losses further). In extreme scenarios — a 50 percent collapse in Meta stock — METU could lose 80 to 100 percent of value or more. This is rare, but leverage makes it possible.

## Monitoring and research

Read Direxion's prospectus closely. Backtest METU's returns against simple 2x leverage on Meta over historical periods where Meta was choppy — the gap between the two reveals the decay cost. Check one-week, one-month, and three-month returns for METU versus 2x Meta returns; note the widening gap as the hold period extends. If your conviction is days to a few weeks out, decay is manageable. If weeks to months, own Meta directly. Lastly, stress-test your own directional thesis. Is this a conviction about Meta's trajectory, or just leverage attraction? Leverage amplifies both right and wrong bets at equal speed. Clarity on the underlying thesis separates traders from gamblers.
