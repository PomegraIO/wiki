*The State Street My2027 Municipal Bond ETF (MYMG) holds municipal bonds selected to mature around 2027. Combines tax-exempt income advantage with concrete maturity date.*

> "The appeal of a municipal bond is that you get to keep more of what you earn, if your tax rate justifies it."

That core truth shapes MYMG construction. Municipal bonds issued by government entities to fund public works. Federal government exempts interest from income tax. Often state and local tax exempt too. Valuable to high-income earners. MYMG holds hundreds of bonds across multiple states and issuing authorities, spreading credit risk so single municipality distress does not imperil fund.

## Building the 2027 portfolio

MYMG selects bonds maturing around 2027—one year later than MYMF 2026 target. Longer maturity window means year more of interest-rate risk and more time for unexpected credit problems. Investor needing cash in 2027 rather 2026, extra year becomes material. Bonds chosen from investment-grade and near-investment-grade universe; fund does not concentrate in speculative or distressed issuers.

Fund composition shifts over time. Older bonds mature, replaced with new issuances carrying 2027 dates, maintaining target maturity window. Not actively managed in traditional sense—MYMG follows formula. Not static basket. Turnover occurs at measured pace as maturities align.

## Tax advantages and cost structure

Tax exemption on municipal bond interest is entire economic point. For married couple filing jointly with income above 190,000 dollars, federal tax rate is 35 percent. Three percent tax-free muni yield equivalent to roughly 4.6 percent on taxable income for 35 percent federal bracket—munis beat taxable bonds for that investor. Someone in 24 percent federal bracket, three percent muni equivalent to just 3.95 percent taxable, less compelling.

Expense ratio typically 0.30–0.45 percent annually, reasonable for fund and far less than individual investor would pay buying 200+ municipal bonds. Fund trades NASDAQ with tight spreads relative to individual bonds. Tax advantage evaporates if held in tax-deferred account like 401k or IRA. Munis pointless in that context.

## The credit landscape

Municipal bonds historically safer than corporate bonds of equivalent maturity. Default rates on investment-grade municipal debt very low. However, MYMG holds bonds across range of credit qualities from near-perfect issuers to others with ratings downgrades. Severe recession, local fiscal crisis, or unexpected pension liabilities could trigger credit event, depressing fund value.

Pension obligations quiet risk in municipal bond world. Some state and local governments have unfunded liabilities that dwarf annual budgets. Reckoning could force higher taxes or spending cuts affecting bond service. MYMG does not concentrate in any single state, providing some cushion, but if multiple large states face pension stress simultaneously, returns suffer.

## Interest-rate dynamics and maturity timing

MYMG faces material interest-rate risk until 2027 arrives. If prevailing yields rise after purchase, existing bonds decline. Investor buying at 3.5 percent yield seeing yields rise to 4.5 percent will see capital losses until waiting remaining years to maturity. But this is finite risk. Unlike perpetual fund, MYMG eventually matures away sensitivity. Trade is: take rate risk for few years but know when it ends.

## Suitability and research

MYMG suits investors in higher tax brackets with known cash need around 2027 holding fund in taxable account. Couple earning 250,000 dollars annually might reasonably prefer three percent muni to 4.2 percent corporate bond, and MYMG provides diversified access without buying individual bonds. Unsuitable for lower brackets where exemption modest value, for those in IRAs or 401ks where worthless, or those needing perpetual income.

Read fact sheet and prospectus on State Street website. Note distribution yield, state allocation, average credit rating. Compare to taxable bond funds. Calculate whether tax savings exceed yield sacrifice for your situation. Watch whether composition includes issuers with known fiscal stress or large unfunded liabilities.
