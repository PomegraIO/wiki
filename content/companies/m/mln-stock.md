*The **VanEck Long Muni ETF** (MLN) tracks a portfolio of [municipal bonds](/municipal-bond/) with long maturities — typically 15 to 30 years — issued by US state and local governments. These [bonds](/bond/) are exempt from federal income tax, making them attractive to investors in high tax brackets, even when their nominal yields appear lower than taxable alternatives.*

The appeal of municipal bonds lies in their tax advantage. When you own a municipal bond directly or through a fund, the interest payments are free of federal income tax, and often state and local income tax as well, if you live in the jurisdiction that issued the bond. For someone in a high federal tax bracket, a 4% yield on a muni bond is worth more in after-tax return than a 6% yield on a [corporate bond](/corporate-bond/). That advantage is especially powerful during periods of high tax rates or in high-income states like California, New York, and Massachusetts.

MLN pursues this tax-exemption benefit by holding a broad portfolio of long-term munis across dozens of states and issuer types — school districts, water authorities, transportation agencies, hospitals, housing agencies, and general-obligation bonds backed by state and local tax revenues. The fund does not attempt to time the municipal market or pick the highest-yielding specific bonds; instead, it tracks an index of eligible long-maturity munis, making it a passive vehicle that captures the broad return of the sector.

The main risk that shapes a fund like MLN is duration and interest-rate sensitivity. Long-maturity bonds carry far more price volatility than short-maturity ones. When [interest rates](/interest-rate/) rise, the value of a 30-year bond outstanding drops sharply because new bonds now offer higher coupons, making the old lower-coupon bond less attractive. A bondholder who needs to sell before maturity faces a mark-to-market loss. Conversely, when rates fall, long-maturity bonds appreciate significantly. This is the classic duration risk: you are selling a bet on where interest rates are headed. If you hold MLN to maturity (which you can do if you own the underlying bonds outright, but not if you own the fund), you collect every coupon; mark-to-market swings do not matter. But if you are a fund investor who might sell in a rising-rate environment, the duration exposure is a real cost.

The second structural factor is [credit risk](/credit-risk/). While municipal bonds are historically very safe relative to corporate debt — defaults are rare — they are not free of risk. State and local issuers face budget pressures, [pension obligations](/pension-obligation/), revenue shortfalls, and political dysfunction. A small fraction of munis do default, particularly in sectors like housing or infrastructure that are more exposed to demand shifts or commodity prices. MLN diversifies across issuers and sectors to mitigate this, but the risk is present.

The fund's cyclicality is tied to three interlocking forces: interest rates, tax rates, and the credit quality of state and local budgets. In a booming economy with falling tax rates, municipal bonds look less attractive relative to taxable alternatives — why take the tax break if tax rates are falling anyway? In [recessions](/recession/), when budgets tighten and credit concerns rise, muni spreads can widen sharply. A rise in federal or state income-tax rates, by contrast, tightens spreads and lifts the value of the tax exemption. For decades, the municipal market was relatively quiet, with steady returns and low [default rates](/default-rate/). The 2008 financial crisis and 2020 pandemic created brief periods of stress, but the sector recovered both times. A permanent shift toward lower tax rates or a major bout of municipal defaults could undermine the entire investment case.

For investors in high tax brackets with a desire for stable income, MLN provides a way to own a diversified pool of long-maturity [tax-exempt bonds](/tax-exempt-bond/) without the trouble of selecting individual issues. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is modest, and the fund offers daily liquidity via exchange trading. Anyone considering MLN should understand that the fund's value will swing with interest-rate moves and have a genuine hold period in mind. It is a vehicle for holding tax-exempt income, not for speculating on rate direction.
