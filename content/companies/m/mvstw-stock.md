Microvast develops battery technology and energy storage solutions for transportation and stationary applications. The company designs and manufactures lithium-ion battery systems used in electric vehicles, commercial vehicles, buses, trucks, and renewable energy storage systems. The customer for Microvast is not the end consumer—the person driving an electric vehicle—but the vehicle manufacturer (the OEM) that builds the vehicle and needs reliable, cost-effective battery packs to power it.

The battery business is capital-intensive and dominated by a handful of large players. Battery performance—energy density, charging speed, cycle life, cost per kilowatt-hour—determines the viability of an electric vehicle. A bus manufacturer choosing a battery supplier is choosing a critical component that affects vehicle range, charging time, durability, and total cost. Microvast competes on technology, cost, and supply reliability.

The company's origin in China—where it was founded in 2006—positioned it early in the largest electric vehicle market globally. Chinese domestic demand for commercial vehicles, buses, and trucks created an enormous captive customer base. Microvast supplied batteries to major Chinese bus manufacturers and commercial vehicle makers, building scale and cost advantages. The company later expanded into the U.S. and European markets, where regulations increasingly mandate electrification of commercial vehicle fleets.

Revenue comes from selling battery packs and battery systems to vehicle manufacturers. The unit economics are volume-dependent: manufacturing lithium-ion batteries at scale achieves cost reductions that small-volume producers cannot match. A battery system sold to an OEM might carry a per-unit margin, but the total margin depends on how many units are sold and how efficiently they are manufactured. This creates incentive for rapid production scaling.

Microvast's technology strategy centers on fast-charging capabilities and cost reduction. The ability to charge a commercial vehicle quickly—critical for fleet operators who need fast turnarounds—is a competitive advantage. Lower cost per kilowatt-hour is essential because battery cost is a major component of electric vehicle total cost. Advances in chemistry, manufacturing efficiency, and thermal management all contribute to improvement.

The company went public in the United States in 2021 via a special purpose [acquisition](/acquisition/) company (SPAC) [merger](/merger/), gaining access to U.S. capital markets and signaling confidence in the global electrification trend. Post-SPAC, the company has focused on scaling production capacity and expanding customer relationships with major OEMs globally.

The broader context matters. Government incentives and regulations in Europe, North America, and China mandate transition to electric vehicles, creating huge demand for batteries. At the same time, battery supply chains are increasingly geopolitical—China dominates current battery manufacturing, and the U.S. and Europe are trying to build domestic capacity. Microvast, as a company with Chinese origins and current global operations, sits at the intersection of those dynamics.

Risks are substantial. Battery technology is evolving—new chemistries or designs could make Microvast's current technology less competitive. Manufacturing capacity must match demand; overbuilding capacity destroys returns, while underbuilding loses market share to competitors. Customer concentration is a risk—if one or two large OEMs account for a large share of revenue and switch to a competing battery supplier, revenue falls sharply. Supply chain risk for raw materials like lithium and cobalt affects all battery makers.

The investment thesis hinges on whether Microvast can maintain technological competitiveness, scale production cost-effectively, win large OEM customers, and capture market share in a rapidly growing but capital-intensive global battery market. The company is not yet consistently profitable and relies on continued investment in capacity and R&D to stay competitive. For potential investors, the 10-K filing (SEC CIK 0001760689) reveals production capacity, customer concentration, margin trends, and the company's path to profitability. Watch quarterly revenue from key customer segments—commercial vehicles, buses, passenger vehicles—and gross margins, which show whether the company is progressing toward cost targets.
