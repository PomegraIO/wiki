## How MAYFAIR Discloses Its Assets

The company's filings center on its Colombian concessions. Under [securities-and-exchange-commission](/securities-and-exchange-commission/) rules, mineral resources must be characterized by confidence level and mineralization type. MAYFAIR's disclosures in its [10-k](/10-k/) classify resource estimates as "inferred," "indicated," or "measured"—technical categories that inform how aggressive the development roadmap appears to investors and how much capital markets will price into share value. A reader studying MAYFAIR encounters engineering reports, NI 43-101 technical summaries (Canadian resource standard), and management's interpretation of those reports. The gap between resource tonnage and the economics of extraction—ore grade, processing costs, gold recovery—is where MAYFAIR's story lives.

Geographic concentration is MAYFAIR's primary leverage and primary risk. Colombia holds significant unmined gold and has become an increasingly important jurisdiction for junior miners seeking brownfield (known but undeveloped) and greenfield exploration plays. Regulatory and security conditions in that country shape MAYFAIR's project timeline and cost structure in ways that do not travel; a reader must evaluate Colombia-specific geology, permitting timelines, and labor and supply-chain stability when weighing the company's path to cash flow.

## Resource Estimation and Market Confidence

Mining companies trade partly on geological narrative and partly on management credibility. MAYFAIR's filings reveal how the company backs its claims: geophysical surveys, drilling programs, metallurgical assays, and third-party consultant opinions. Exploration companies routinely spend years drilling and sampling before declaring a resource; the company's disclosure cadence—how often it updates drilling results, whether it publishes third-party technical reports, how it frames resource upgrades—signals management's confidence and transparency posture.

The company must disclose:
- Property-level descriptions (claim blocks, concession agreements, terms of operation)
- Drilling density and sample coverage (how much rock has been tested vs. interpolated)
- Metal pricing assumptions (the gold price MAYFAIR used in resource modeling affects economic viability)
- Metallurgical recovery estimates (what percentage of gold in ore can actually be extracted)
- Permitting status (whether Colombia has issued or will issue development licenses)

These are not optional boilerplate; they are the substance of the investment case. A reader skeptical of MAYFAIR's resource estimate has explicit, auditable grounds in the filing to understand why.
