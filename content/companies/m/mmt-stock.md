> A closed-end fund is a static pool of capital—it raises money once, invests it, and then stops. There is no daily rush of new investors and no constant redemption pressure. That is why closed-end funds can be adventurous.

MFS Multimarket Income Trust is a closed-end fund, meaning it issues a fixed number of shares, invests the proceeds in a diversified portfolio of fixed-income securities worldwide, and then manages that portfolio for its shareholders. It is not an open-end [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/); there is no daily creation and redemption of shares. Instead, shares trade on the [New York Stock Exchange](/new-york-stock-exchange/) under the ticker MMT, and the price moves based on supply and demand for those shares relative to the underlying value of the bonds the fund holds.

The fund's mandate is straightforward: seek high current income. MFS Investment Management, the Boston-based asset manager, invests at least 80 percent of the fund's assets in fixed-income securities issued by or backed by U.S. government, foreign governments, corporations, and emerging-market entities. The portfolio includes U.S. Treasuries and government bonds, corporate debt across the credit spectrum, mortgage-backed securities, and asset-backed securitized debt from both developed and emerging markets. The average duration—the sensitivity of bond prices to interest-rate changes—sits around 5 years, meaning the portfolio is moderately interest-rate-sensitive but not as volatile as a longer-duration bond fund.

The critical feature is distribution. MFS targets an annual distribution rate of 8 percent of the fund's [net asset value](/net-asset-value/), paid monthly. This is a firm policy; the fund is engineered to generate enough income from coupons and securities sales to deliver that yield to shareholders every month. For investors seeking steady cash flow, this is the appeal. For the market, it is also the constraint: the fund cannot change its distribution or skip it without signaling trouble, and the pressure to maintain the yield regardless of [interest rates](/interest-rate/) can drive questionable portfolio decisions.

The moat of a closed-end fund is not in proprietary securities or special access to deals; it is in scale and operations. MFS is a large, established asset manager with decades of relationships across global fixed-income markets. The fund benefits from MFS's credit research, its access to underwriters and loan syndicates, and the efficiency gains of managing a large pool of capital. When a bond pays a coupon, the fund collects it; when a security matures, the fund reinvests the proceeds. There is no constant inflow of new money to deploy or outflow of redemptions to manage. The asset base is stable, letting the manager focus on returns rather than marketing.

However, [closed-end funds](/closed-end-fund/) also carry structural complications. When interest rates rise, the price of existing bonds falls, and the net asset value of the fund falls too. If the fund is trading at a discount to its net asset value—a common state for closed-end funds—shareholders suffer a double hit: their bonds have fallen in price and their shares are worth less than the fund's holdings. Conversely, if the fund is trading at a premium, new shareholders are paying more than the fund's assets are worth; the premium can collapse, leaving those investors underwater. The monthly distribution requirement can also tempt a manager to reach for yield by holding riskier securities than might otherwise be prudent.

The fund has undergone significant recent changes. In May 2026, shareholders approved a reorganization in which [MFS Charter Income](/mcr-stock/) Trust and MFS Intermediate High [Income Fund](/income-fund/) were merged into MMT. This consolidation was intended to create a larger, more efficient fund with greater scale and lower per-share expenses. At the same time, shareholders approved a change in sub-advisors from MFS to abrdn Inc., a Scottish asset manager. This shift represents a recalibration of the management approach but also a moment of risk: any change in sub-advisors introduces operational and performance uncertainty.

The global nature of the portfolio creates currency and geopolitical risk. The fund holds foreign-government and [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/) denominated in foreign currencies; when the dollar strengthens, those holdings are worth less when converted back. Emerging-market securities carry [sovereign risk](/sovereign-risk/)—the risk that a government will default or impose capital controls. Mortgage-backed securities carry [prepayment risk](/prepayment-risk/); when rates fall, homeowners refinance, the fund gets its principal back early, and it must reinvest at lower rates. The [diversification](/diversification/) across markets, credit quality, and geographies is a defense against any single catastrophe, but it does not eliminate the underlying risks.

For investors considering this fund, understand what it is: a portfolio of bonds with a monthly check. The fund is not a growth vehicle and does not aim to be. Its return comes from the yield paid on the bonds it holds, less [management fees](/management-fee/), plus or minus changes in bond prices and currency movements. Research the fund's distribution sustainability—how much of each monthly payment comes from portfolio income versus portfolio sales or return of principal. A fund that is slowly depleting its capital to pay distributions is selling you your own principal as income. Review the portfolio's [asset allocation](/asset-allocation/): what percentage is in Treasuries, corporate debt, mortgage-backed securities, and emerging-market bonds? Higher-risk allocations justify a higher yield but also justify harder scrutiny. Monitor the fund's price relative to its net asset value; a widening discount can indicate that the market is worried about distribution cuts or asset quality. Finally, watch the interest-rate environment: as rates change, the fund's ability to maintain the 8 percent distribution yield will shift, and management may need to alter the portfolio's composition or duration.
