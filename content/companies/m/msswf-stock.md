Metal Sky Star Acquisition Corp is a blank check company, or SPAC, formed with a specific capital structure designed to hunt for an operating business to acquire. The company's simple mandate is to find one or more target companies, negotiate a merger, and unite them with the capital and shell that Metal Sky Star represents. Like all SPACs, Metal Sky Star's strategic advantage lies in speed and certainty of capital — founders and sponsors can lock in a known pool of shareholder equity without the unpredictability of venture fundraising or traditional equity offerings.

The IPO in April 2022 occurred at a peak moment for SPAC enthusiasm. The market had seen a wave of successful blank check vehicles merged with fintech startups, biotech companies, and other growth-stage businesses that wanted to go public quickly. Metal Sky Star's founders positioned the company to search broadly for acquisition targets, without publicly specifying a single sector, which theoretically provided flexibility but also scattered the investment thesis.

## How the capital was structured and deployed

The $115 million raised in the IPO was divided into units, each containing one ordinary share, one warrant, and rights representing one-tenth of a future share upon business combination close. This layered structure creates multiple economic incentives: shareholders want a good deal; warrant holders want the business to succeed and the share price to appreciate; rights holders have a fractional claim on future dilution. The sponsor, meanwhile, invested a relatively small amount in founder shares and warrants that only become valuable if the merger closes — aligning the sponsor's economic interest with completing a deal.

The capital flowed into a trust account, held in escrow and earning minimal returns, segregated from the company's operating funds. Operating expenses — legal fees, accounting, regulatory compliance, director and officer salaries — were paid from the smaller general treasury. This two-bucket system is designed to protect shareholders' capital from being consumed by overhead, but it also creates pressure: as operating funds deplete over time, the sponsor's window for completing a deal narrows. With no target identified by April 2025, Metal Sky Star exhausted its three-year window and had to face the Nasdaq delisting reality.

## The delisting and its consequences

When a SPAC fails to complete a business combination within 36 months of IPO registration effectiveness, Nasdaq's listing standards mandate either a forced public offering (within a compressed timeline) or delisting. Metal Sky Star chose to accept delisting rather than pursue either option, moving to OTC trading in April 2025. This shift carries material consequences.

OTC markets lack the disclosure requirements, continuous surveillance, and institutional scrutiny of the Nasdaq. Bid-ask spreads widen significantly, meaning buyers and sellers face higher costs to transact. Institutional investors are often prohibited by their investment policies from holding OTC securities, so the shareholder base narrows. The share price typically compresses, eroding optionality for warrant and rights holders. Crucially, a delisted SPAC becomes a less attractive merger partner: if target company shareholders are to be paid in delisted SPAC shares, those shares are less liquid and therefore less valuable. This creates a vicious cycle: the longer a SPAC remains unmerged, the more it deteriorates in market value and listing status, making it harder to attract targets.

## Extensions and the timeline pressure

Metal Sky Star has secured multiple shareholder-approved extensions to its business combination deadline, pushing it back to January 5, 2027. Each extension requires a shareholder vote — a democratic check on the sponsor's efforts. The fact that shareholders have repeatedly approved extensions suggests either continued faith in the sponsor's ability to find a target, or resignation among shareholders who have already redeemed their shares and accepted the loss. The threshold for extension votes varies; some are designed to require supermajorities, forcing the sponsor to keep the support of a broad shareholder base.

Extensions are not costless. Each one incurs legal and administrative expenses, eats into the general treasury, and signals to the market that a deal remains elusive. For shareholders contemplating redemption, every extension is a moment to reconsider: is the probability of a good business combination high enough to justify waiting longer, or should they take their capital and redeploy it elsewhere?

## The financial reality as of late 2025

Metal Sky Star reported a working capital deficit of $5.3 million as of December 31, 2025. This means the company's current liabilities exceed its current assets — a precarious position. The deficit exists because operating expenses have exceeded the funds available outside the trust account. If Metal Sky Star does not complete a business combination by its deadline, it will liquidate, and trust account assets will be returned to shareholders after legal and administrative wind-down costs are paid. The general creditors — lawyers, accountants, and other service providers — face risk of not being fully paid.

This financial tightness creates urgency: the sponsor has both the incentive and the constraint to complete a deal soon, or else face a slow dissolution that may not fully recover investor capital. The irony of a SPAC's mechanics is that the very structure meant to protect shareholders' capital — the trust account — also locks capital away from the deal-making process, forcing the sponsor to operate with minimal resources once operating funds run low.

## The warrant and rights structure

MSSWF represents the warrant ticker — each warrant is a right to purchase an ordinary share at a stated exercise price, typically at a premium to the IPO price. Warrants are long-duration optionality: their value is nearly zero if no business combination occurs (since the ordinary shares they would purchase never appreciate), but can become valuable if the merger creates a successful company. The dilution they represent (hundreds of thousands of additional shares upon exercise) only matters if the underlying business is worth far more than the trust account value.

Rights, meanwhile, represent the fractional additional share holders receive upon business combination completion, providing a small sweetener for early shareholders but also deepening dilution if the merger does close.

## How to research Metal Sky Star

The company's annual 10-K filing (SEC CIK 0001882464) and quarterly 10-Q filings disclose the size of the trust account, the expense burn rate, redemption activity, and any information available on acquisition target discussions. OTC Markets filings may be less frequent or detailed than Nasdaq filings, so readers should check directly with the SEC's EDGAR database for the most current 8-K materials, which would disclose any material events such as a business combination announcement. The company's investor relations website, if active, may provide public statements on the search status. For comparative context, examining other SPACs incorporated around the same time (2021–2022) and their outcomes — whether they merged successfully, liquidated, or remain in search mode — helps frame Metal Sky Star's position in the cohort. The lesson from Metal Sky Star is that capital is only valuable if deployed promptly and wisely; a SPAC that cannot find a suitable target within its mandate does not retain or create value, merely loses time and burn away shareholder returns to advisory fees.
