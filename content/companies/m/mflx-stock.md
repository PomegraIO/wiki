*The **First Trust Flexible Municipal High Income ETF** (ticker MFLX) is an actively managed bond fund that holds municipal debt securities issued by states, cities, counties, and public authorities. It targets investors in higher tax brackets who need current income, because the interest paid by municipal bonds is typically exempt from federal income tax and often exempt from state and local tax as well.*

> "In a rising-rate environment, high-income bonds fall the hardest. Yield is not free."

That tension — between the pursuit of income and the protection of capital — defines MFLX. The fund is designed to capture above-average yields from municipal bonds while maintaining flexibility to rotate among different segments and structures as interest rates and credit conditions shift. For investors in high tax brackets seeking tax-free income, MFLX offers a professional alternative to owning individual munis directly. For investors underestimating interest-rate or credit risk, it can be a costly lesson.

## Municipal bonds and the tax advantage

A municipal bond is a debt security issued by a state, city, county, or public authority (airports, water districts, toll roads, hospitals, universities). The defining feature is tax treatment: interest paid on most municipal bonds is exempt from federal income tax and, if you live in the issuing state, often exempt from state and local income tax as well. This tax exemption is extraordinarily valuable to high-income earners. A municipal bond yielding 4 percent is economically equivalent to a taxable bond yielding 6 percent if your combined marginal federal and state tax rate is 33 percent. For the wealthy, municipal income can amount to an enormous after-tax advantage.

The bonds finance essential public infrastructure — schools, highways, water systems, airports, public transit — and essential services like hospital and university networks. They carry credit risk: an issuer with weakening finances may struggle to pay interest or principal. But municipal bonds have historically defaulted far less often than corporate bonds of comparable quality, which is why they often carry lower yields than comparably rated corporate debt.

## "High income" and "flexible" — what these words mean

MFLX's name signals two things. First, it aims for municipal bonds with above-average yield — tilting toward the higher end of the municipal spectrum. This typically means lower credit quality (higher-yielding bonds from weaker issuers with less certain revenues) and longer duration (bonds whose interest rates are locked in for longer periods). The pursuit of extra yield introduces extra volatility and extra credit risk.

Second, "flexible" means the fund does not lock itself into a fixed allocation. Managers can vary the proportion held in different types of municipal debt: general obligation bonds (backed by an issuer's full taxing power), revenue bonds (backed by cash flow from specific projects), pre-refunded bonds (whose principal has been escrowed for future payment), and other structures. They can adjust duration — the sensitivity to interest-rate changes — and shift between taxable municipal bonds and tax-exempt ones as relative value shifts.

## What the portfolio holds

A typical MFLX portfolio includes general obligation bonds from states and cities, revenue bonds from airports and water utilities, and bonds from healthcare systems and university networks. Because MFLX targets higher yields, it holds bonds rated in the BBB to BB range (still investment-grade but lower tier) and speculative-grade, rather than only AAA and AA securities. The exact holdings shift as managers adjust for credit risk and interest-rate expectations.

## Duration and interest-rate sensitivity

Higher yield comes with higher duration risk. A bond that pays 5 percent instead of 3 percent typically has a much longer maturity — perhaps 20 or 30 years instead of 5 or 10 years. The longer a bond's duration, the more its price swings when interest rates change. If rates rise sharply, MFLX will see net asset value fall significantly. If rates fall, the fund will see capital gains. This volatility is the hidden cost of chasing yield. During rising-rate periods (like 2022), high-income municipal funds experienced painful losses. During falling-rate periods, they flourished.

## Credit deterioration and spread risk

The emphasis on higher-yielding municipals means exposure to lower-credit-quality issuers — cities, counties, and authorities with weaker revenues or aging infrastructure, offering higher yields precisely because they carry higher default risk. During economic downturns, when local government revenues falter, these bonds underperform. MFLX might hold debt from financially stressed municipalities that investors gradually reprice lower.

The fund also faces "spread risk" — the risk that the yield premium demanded for lower-quality municipal debt will widen. If investor sentiment turns skeptical about municipal credit generally, non-AAA bond prices will fall across the market as buyers demand higher yields, including the lower-quality bonds MFLX holds.

## Tax efficiency and the right investor

MFLX's tax-exempt income is most valuable to high-income investors in high marginal tax brackets. A retiree in a 35 percent combined federal and state bracket gains far more from tax-exempt income than someone in the 12 percent bracket. MFLX should be held in a regular taxable account, not in a tax-deferred retirement account, because the tax exemption is wasted inside a retirement plan — all income in a retirement account is already tax-deferred.

The fund appeals to investors who need current income, who benefit meaningfully from tax-exempt interest, and who can tolerate the volatility of higher-yield, longer-duration bonds. It also suits active bond traders who want exposure to shifts in municipal credit spreads and interest-rate moves.

## Research and outlook assessment

Start with the fund's prospectus and current holdings. Look at the weighted-average credit quality (typically shown as a letter rating) and the average duration in years. Compare MFLX's yield to other high-income municipal funds and to taxable bonds of similar duration, then calculate the taxable-equivalent yield for your own tax bracket. Research the largest holdings to understand the credit outlook for those issuers: are they fiscally stable or facing revenue stress? Finally, clarify your own interest-rate outlook. If rates are likely to fall, MFLX offers both yield and potential price appreciation. If rates are likely to rise further, the duration risk is steep, and lower-quality issuers may see downgrades that hit the fund hard.
