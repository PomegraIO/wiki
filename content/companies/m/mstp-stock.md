The GraniteShares 2x Long MSTR Daily ETF (MSTP) is a recent innovation in the world of single-stock leveraged products, born from the confluence of easier retail trading access and growing investor appetite for amplified exposure to individual stocks. Its journey reflects a broader market evolution toward more accessible, exchange-traded leverage on concentrated positions.

## The emergence of single-stock leverage

For most of the 2000s and 2010s, leveraged ETFs existed almost exclusively for broad market indexes — the Direxion and ProShares families offered 2x and 3x levered versions of the S&P 500, the Nasdaq 100, and various sector benchmarks. These products appealed to traders who wanted tactical bets on market direction without the friction of margin accounts or futures contracts. But single-stock leverage remained hard to access in simple, exchange-traded form. Retail investors seeking amplified exposure to a single company had to resort to margin accounts, options strategies, or cumbersome structured products.

That began to shift in the early 2020s as retail investing participation grew, commission-free trading became ubiquitous, and derivatives markets became more liquid. Several sponsors began launching leveraged ETFs on individual stocks, particularly the most heavily traded and volatile names. MicroStrategy emerged as a natural candidate — volatile, widely held by retail traders, and attracting particular interest from investors seeking Bitcoin exposure through the company's treasury holdings and corporate strategy. GraniteShares, known for launching thematic and structured exchange-traded funds, introduced MSTP into this expanding category.

## How MSTP works mechanically

MSTP holds MicroStrategy stock paired with financial instruments — primarily equity futures and derivatives — that collectively deliver 2x the daily change in MicroStrategy's closing price. Each market close, the fund rebalances its positions so that its leverage is reset to exactly 2x for the next trading day. A 1 percent daily move in MicroStrategy aims to produce a 2 percent move in MSTP, in either direction.

The mechanism is identical in principle to Direxion's 2x levered index products, simply applied to a single stock. Because leverage resets daily, MSTP is purpose-built for traders expecting sharp moves over hours or days, not months or years. The daily reset captures and compounds gains or losses over short timeframes but incurs structural erosion over longer periods through volatility decay.

## The role in single-stock trading

MSTP arrived into a market where retail interest in MicroStrategy had intensified, especially among investors tracking the company's Bitcoin holdings as a form of leveraged cryptocurrency exposure. The fund provided a liquid, transparent, exchange-traded way to amplify that exposure without wrestling with options pricing, expirations, or margin requirements. An investor could hold MSTP in a standard brokerage account, buy or sell it at market prices throughout the day, and receive 2x MicroStrategy movement without needing margin approval.

The product also fills a gap where alternatives were limited. Other MicroStrategy leveraged products either use options mechanics (more complex and less transparent) or arrive as inverse or mixed-leverage products aimed at different trader types. MSTP offers a straightforward slot: a simple 2x bull play on one stock, wrapped in a standard ETF.

## Volatility decay and compounding risk

Like all daily-reset leveraged products, MSTP is vulnerable to volatility decay — the structural drag that accumulates as a stock bounces up and down. If MicroStrategy rises 2 percent one day and falls 2 percent the next, it closes unchanged, but MSTP, resetting daily, compounds differently and loses value despite the stock ending flat. That decay is purely mathematical and relentless, worsening as the stock becomes more volatile.

MicroStrategy is among the more volatile stocks on the market, particularly over longer timeframes that some retail holders maintain. A trader planning to hold MSTP for weeks should expect that even if MicroStrategy ends the month modestly higher, MSTP may trail because of the turbulence along the way. The volatility itself becomes a cost, invisible but real.

## Who MSTP serves

MSTP appeals to active traders and tactical investors who believe MicroStrategy is about to rise sharply and want to amplify that conviction without setting up a margin account or managing options. It also reaches investors seeking to control exposure — buying two shares' worth of MSTR exposure using only one share's worth of capital — at the cost of accepting leveraged volatility and drawdown amplification.

For buy-and-hold investors, MSTP is structurally unsuitable, regardless of conviction in MicroStrategy. The daily reset works mechanically against long-term holding, and volatility decay will erode returns silently over months. For traders holding intraday or over a few days, MSTP offers efficiency and simplicity; for anything longer, holding MicroStrategy stock directly avoids the compounding tax.

The prospectus explains the daily rebalancing mechanism and typically includes historical performance comparisons showing actual returns alongside the theoretical 2x return. That gap is volatility decay made visible.
