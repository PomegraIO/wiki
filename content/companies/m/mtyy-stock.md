*The GraniteShares YieldBOOST MSTR ETF (MTYY) is a structured exchange-traded fund that holds MicroStrategy common stock and systematically sells call options against those holdings each month to generate income distributions. It is designed for investors who want concentrated exposure to MicroStrategy but prioritize near-term yield and accept that upside will be capped.*

## Core structure and mechanics

MTYY holds actual MicroStrategy shares. Rather than distributing the income from any dividends or relying on capital appreciation, the fund overlays a covered-call writing program. Each month the fund sells out-of-the-money call options on its MicroStrategy position, collecting premiums from buyers. Those premiums are distributed to shareholders. The trade-off is simple: if MicroStrategy's stock appreciates beyond the strike price, the fund's shares get called away and shareholders forfeit gains above that level. If the stock languishes or falls, the call premium provides modest cushioning.

This is not leveraged exposure in the financial sense — the fund does not borrow to invest. It is income-oriented leverage: the fund captures upside if MicroStrategy appreciates moderately, collects premium if the stock is flat, and loses principal if the company declines, but loses less in percentage terms than an unhedged long position would due to the monthly call premium.

## Costs, concentration, and realistic returns

GraniteShares is the sponsor. MTYY trades on NASDAQ with moderate liquidity. The expense ratio is transparent in the prospectus; compare it to the fund's actual distribution yield to assess net benefit. Because the fund holds only MicroStrategy, it offers zero diversification. A downturn in the software or analytics sector, a stumble in the company's cloud business, or competitive pressure on its data analytics platform directly harms the fund without any offset from other holdings.

The call-writing program incurs transaction costs and bid-ask spreads in the options market that are embedded in the fund's net asset value. The prospectus details the strike selection methodology — whether calls are sold at fixed percentages above the stock price or using some other formula — which determines how often shares are called away and how much premium is collected.

## Risks worth understanding

The primary risk is concentration: this is a one-company bet. MicroStrategy's stock is volatile and affected by shifts in enterprise software spending, data center investment cycles, and sometimes by founder-led capital allocation decisions that surprise the market. That volatility is unhedged by other holdings.

The secondary risk is the cap on gains. If MicroStrategy appreciates sharply on strong earnings or strategic announcements, MTYY shareholders will have missed that upside. The monthly distributions, while attractive, do not compensate for multi-year opportunity cost in a bull market.

The third risk is that covered-call premiums are ordinary income, not qualified dividends, and the fund's distributions may themselves trigger capital gains if the called-away shares realize gains. Tax complexity for shareholders in taxable accounts is higher than for a simple stock purchase.

## Who this is for and how to research

MTYY suits an investor who holds a stable outlook for MicroStrategy — believes it will perform reasonably but not multiply in value — and who prioritizes consistent monthly distributions. It is not for growth-oriented investors, nor for those building a diversified portfolio. It is a tactical income vehicle for a concentrated bet.

Start with the fund prospectus and fact sheet from GraniteShares. Review the call-strike history: how often are shares called away, and what strike prices are used? Monitor MicroStrategy's quarterly earnings and 10-K filings (SEC CIK 0001050863) to understand the underlying business health. Compare MTYY's trailing yield to historical distributions; sharp swings suggest changing volatility or changes in the call-writing methodology. Track the actual options chain on MicroStrategy to understand implied volatility and option premiums, which feed into MTYY's monthly distribution. Over time, ask whether the regular income is worth the concentration risk and the foregone upside in a strong bull market.
