*A **[municipal bond](/municipal-bond/) ETF** is an exchange-traded fund that holds a portfolio of [bonds](/bond/) issued by state and local governments, offering investors a simple way to own tax-exempt debt instruments without purchasing individual bonds. The RJ Eagle Municipal [Income ETF](/income-etf/) (RJMI) tracks a broad selection of such bonds, seeking to deliver income that may be free from federal taxes and, for residents of certain states, free from state and local taxes as well.*

## The emergence of municipal ETFs and RJMI

The market for municipal bonds has existed for decades, but until the early 2000s, individual investors faced substantial friction in owning them: bond dealers charged wide spreads, minimum purchases ran into tens of thousands of dollars, and [price discovery](/price-discovery/) was opaque. Exchange-traded funds changed that equation by bundling hundreds of municipal bonds into a single tradable security. The first municipal [bond ETFs](/bond-etf/) arrived in the mid-2000s, but the space remained relatively modest for years as many advisers favoured [closed-end funds](/closed-end-fund/) or active management for municipal exposure.

RJ Eagle Municipal Income ETF entered that landscape as part of a broader wave of specialized [fixed-income ETFs](/fixed-income-etf/) designed to serve particular investor needs. The fund targets investors across the United States who use municipal bonds specifically to reduce their federal tax liability, taking advantage of the constitutional exemption that shields municipal bond interest from federal income tax. Over time, the fund has grown to encompass a substantial portfolio of investment-grade and intermediate-grade municipal bonds, offering both stability and the tax-exempt yield profile that makes municipal bonds attractive to high-income investors.

## What RJMI holds and how it generates income

RJMI holds a diversified portfolio of municipal bonds issued by state and local governments, water authorities, transit agencies, school districts, and other qualified issuers. The fund does not simply own a random collection — it targets bonds that offer a balance between yield and credit quality, typically concentrating on investment-grade or near-investment-grade securities that carry relatively low default risk.

The income RJMI distributes comes from the [coupon payments](/coupon-payment/) those underlying bonds make to the fund. Because the bonds themselves are exempt from federal taxation, the distributions to shareholders are generally also exempt from federal tax. For investors living in states that honour the federal exemption—most do — those distributions are also free from state income tax. This tax advantage is the primary reason investors hold municipal bonds. For a high-income individual in a combined federal and state tax bracket of 45 percent, a 4 percent tax-free yield from a municipal bond is economically equivalent to a 7.3 percent taxable yield. That spread is compelling enough that many wealthy investors treat municipal exposure as a core holding.

RJMI's portfolio reflects this reality. The fund manager screens for bonds that offer reasonable yield while maintaining credit quality, balancing exposure across geographies and bond types ([general obligation bonds](/general-obligation-bond/) issued by states and cities, [revenue bonds](/revenue-bond/) tied to specific projects or utilities, and various special-purpose issuers). The result is a fund that can offer monthly or quarterly distributions without exposing holders to extreme [credit risk](/credit-risk/) or concentration in any single issuer or state.

## Risks that matter for municipal bond investors

Municipal bonds are not riskless, despite their tax-advantaged status. The most obvious risk is credit risk — if a municipality or its revenue source weakens, it may struggle to pay its obligations, and the bond's price will fall. Most municipal bonds default at extremely low rates, but they do default, particularly smaller local issuers or those reliant on declining revenue streams (e.g., utilities facing reduced consumption, or cities with shrinking populations). RJMI mitigates this by tilting toward higher-quality issuers and diversifying across many jurisdictions, but investors cannot eliminate the risk entirely without owning only the safest AAA-rated bonds, which would typically mean lower yields.

A second risk is [interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/). When prevailing [interest rates](/interest-rate/) rise, existing bonds with lower coupons fall in value because investors would rather buy newly issued bonds paying higher rates. For a long-term buy-and-hold investor in individual bonds, this matters only if they need to sell before maturity. But for a fund like RJMI, which constantly manages a portfolio of bonds with varying maturity dates and marks prices to market daily, interest-rate movements can swing the fund's [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) noticeably.

A third, often overlooked risk is [reinvestment risk](/reinvestment-risk/). As the fund collects coupon payments and maturities, it must reinvest the proceeds. If interest rates have fallen since the bonds were purchased, reinvestment will occur at lower yields, reducing the income stream the fund can distribute. Conversely, if rates have risen, reinvestment at higher rates benefits holders.

Tax policy itself is a structural risk. The federal exemption for municipal bond interest is written into the tax code and has endured for over a century, but it is theoretically subject to change. Any alteration that reduced or eliminated the exemption would erase the primary tax advantage that drives demand for RJMI. This is a low-probability but high-impact risk.

## How to research RJMI

Investors interested in RJMI should start by reviewing the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, which describe the fund's objective, holdings, [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/), and distribution frequency. The prospectus includes the fund's credit-quality breakdown (what percentage of holdings are AAA, AA, A, BBB, or below-investment-grade) and its average maturity, both essential for understanding the fund's risk profile. The fact sheet typically shows recent yield, expense ratio, and performance data.

Because RJMI holds municipal bonds, understanding the broader municipal bond market is valuable. The Bond Buyer Index and the iBoxx Investment Grade Municipal Bond Index are useful reference points for tracking how the broader municipal bond market is trading. Investors should also monitor news about municipal creditworthiness — specific state or city fiscal stress can affect valuations within the fund if RJMI holds debt issued by that jurisdiction.

Finally, investors should consider their own tax situation. The tax-exempt yield advantage of RJMI is meaningful only for those in high tax brackets; for investors in lower brackets or in tax-deferred accounts, a taxable bond fund might offer better after-tax returns. RJMI's prospectus and the fund sponsor's website often include calculators that help investors assess whether municipal bonds are suitable for their circumstances.
