*Return Stacked [Bonds](/bond/) & [Managed Futures](/managed-futures/) ETF (RSBT) is an [actively managed fund](/actively-managed-fund/) that combines core fixed-income holdings with systematic managed futures positions, aiming to harvest returns from multiple asset classes and trend-following patterns simultaneously.*

The core insight behind return stacking is simple but powerful: if two asset classes move independently (or move opposite to each other), you can hold both without their volatility amplifying the overall portfolio risk as much as holding either one alone. RSBT starts with bonds as its ballast—investment-grade securities that tend to provide steady yield and cushion against equity selloffs. Then, over that foundation, it layers managed futures positions: algorithmic, trend-following trades in commodities like [crude oil](/crude-oil/) and gold, [currency pairs](/currency-pair/), and equity index futures. The idea is that bonds might decline during inflationary shocks while managed futures pick up gains from the resulting price momentum, or vice versa during [deflation](/deflation/). By holding both, the fund does not depend on just one bet being right.

Managed futures—also called systematic trend-following—works by buying assets that are moving upward and selling those moving downward, trying to ride major market moves in either direction. This is not [market timing](/market-timing/); it is pattern recognition applied across dozens of tradeable [futures contracts](/futures-contract/). When crude oil is in a clear uptrend, a trend-following system will be long oil. When volatility is spiking, it might short equities. The strategy is designed to make money in directional moves, not to predict which direction that will be. Because futures respond to global supply shocks, geopolitical events, and monetary swings that do not always move equity or bond markets in the same way, adding them to a bond core creates genuine [diversification](/diversification/).

RSBT is actively managed, which means a team oversees the fund's holdings rather than a fixed index rule. The bonds portion is typically held to maturity or near-maturity, collecting coupons as a baseline. The managed futures overlay is traded more dynamically—positions size up and down based on the strength of observed trends and volatility levels. This active approach comes with a higher [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) than a passive alternative would charge, but the trade-off is that the fund can adapt to changing market regimes and adjust its risk exposure. An entirely passive fund would lock in its allocation and stick to it even when economic conditions shift dramatically.

The real appeal of this structure is for investors uncomfortable with a pure stock-or-bond split. Equities offer growth but can fall sharply; bonds provide stability but lag during [inflation](/inflation/). Managed futures offer a third source of return that thrives when others struggle, though they are not a free lunch—they can lag in range-bound, quiet markets where trends do not develop. A portfolio holding all three avoids being left out when any one of them is strong and reduces the sting when any single asset class stumbles.

For those researching this fund, the prospectus and fact sheet explain the precise composition of the bond holdings and the managed futures methodology. Watch for updates on the allocation percentages between fixed income and futures, as the fund may adjust this balance based on yield levels and volatility expectations. The fee structure reflects active management and the ongoing cost of implementing the futures overlay. As with any actively managed fund, comparing performance not just to absolute benchmarks but to what a comparable passive fund would have delivered helps clarify whether the active return is covering its own cost.
