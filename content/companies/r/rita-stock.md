Real estate has always thrown off income—buildings rent, leases generate cash, and the owners collect it. The ETFB Green SRI REITs ETF (RITA) narrows the lens to a particular slice of that world: REITs that meet not just operational standards but environmental and social responsibility criteria. It is a fund for investors seeking real-estate exposure, regular distributions, and alignment with sustainability-focused investment principles.

## How screening filters REITs

Not all real estate is created equal in the eyes of an ESG (environmental, social, governance) investor. RITA applies explicit criteria—typically via a third-party screener like IdealRatings or similar—to identify REITs that demonstrate strong environmental practices (energy efficiency, waste reduction, renewable-energy use), sound governance (board independence, executive pay alignment), and social responsibility (tenant relations, community engagement, labour standards).

This approach excludes some REITs outright and elevates others. A REIT that has invested heavily in green-building certification, energy-efficient retrofits, or renewable-energy installations scores better than one that ignores these dimensions. The screening is measurable, data-driven, and tied to real management decisions, not marketing spin—though distinguishing genuine commitment from greenwashing remains a challenge.

The result is a portfolio that tilts toward REITs perceived to be future-proofed against environmental regulation, better-positioned to attract ESG-focused tenants and investors, and managed with more rigorous governance. Whether this translates to better financial performance is contested; some studies suggest ESG-screened REITs perform in line with the broader REIT universe over long periods, while managers claim reduced risk and better capital discipline.

## Real estate's income character

REITs are legally required to distribute at least 90% of their taxable income to shareholders as [dividends](/dividend/), making them income-producing by design. RITA inherits this structure; investors typically receive quarterly distributions from the REITs it holds. The yield is often attractive relative to broad equity funds, though it fluctuates with property values and rental markets. Rising [interest rates](/interest-rate/) can pressure REIT valuations (since real estate's discounted cash flows shrink as the [discount rate](/discount-rate/) rises), and a downturn in [commercial real estate](/commercial-real-estate/) particularly affects office and [retail REITs](/retail-reit/).

The income is also taxable at ordinary rates, not the preferential rates applied to [qualified dividends](/qualified-dividend/) from stocks, so RITA is less tax-efficient in taxable accounts than it might appear.

## Concentration and sector risk

RITA's universe is narrower than the full REIT market; screening for environmental and governance quality eliminates many smaller, niche, or lower-quality players. This concentration is both a feature and a bug. It means the fund holds genuinely high-quality REITs but also that it has higher sector concentration and is exposed to the specific risks of large-cap, well-managed properties. If the office-REIT segment falls out of favour (as it has periodically with remote-work trends), RITA's concentrated exposure to high-quality [office REITs](/office-reit/) may underperform a broader REIT index.

[Diversification](/diversification/) by asset class within the REIT universe—industrial, residential, healthcare, retail, data-centre—is typically good; however, the screening process may inadvertently tilt the fund toward certain REIT types that better meet ESG standards.

## How to research RITA

Begin with the fund's prospectus and most recent holdings list, which show the exact REITs held and the weighting. Examine the top ten holdings—this is where the bulk of the fund lives—and assess whether you understand the business: does the fund own office, industrial, residential, or a mix? Research the specific screening criteria used; IdealRatings' methodology is public, but understand what "green" and "SRI" mean operationally in the context of real estate.

Compare RITA's performance and distribution history to a broad REIT [index fund](/index-fund/) or ETF over multiple years and different market environments. In periods when office or retail property values fall, how did RITA fare? In periods of rising rates, did the higher-quality holdings protect returns? Check the fund's portfolio turnover to understand whether it is actively trading or holding a stable set of REITs; high turnover increases costs.

Finally, consider the source of the fund's income in context. REIT dividends are primarily a return of capital and taxable income, not a hidden bonus; they reflect the property's cash generation. A REIT paying a 4% yield is not free money—it is your share of the rent the building collects, minus operating costs and maintenance. RITA's role is to select the highest-quality REITs meeting your values and deliver that income stream in an efficient, diversified package.
