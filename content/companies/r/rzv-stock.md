*The **Invesco S&P SmallCap 600 Pure Value ETF**, trading as RZV, is a value-tilted tracker of small-cap U.S. stocks. Launched as the value companion to Invesco's pure-growth small-cap offering, RZV applies a quantitative screen to the S&P SmallCap 600 universe to isolate companies trading at low valuations relative to earnings, book value, and sales. It appeals to investors hunting for deep-value opportunities in the small-cap band — companies the market has priced cheaply, often with higher [dividend yields](/dividend-yield/) and stronger profitability metrics than their growth-tilted peers.*

## The origins and structure

RZV was created to serve a specific investor appetite: exposure to small-cap value without the active [management fees](/management-fee/) and unpredictable turnover of a traditional [value fund](/value-fund/). The fund tracks the S&P SmallCap 600 Pure Value Index, which uses transparent, rules-based criteria to rank companies in the S&P SmallCap 600 by value characteristics. Stocks are selected for low price-to-earnings multiples, low [price-to-book ratios](/price-to-book-ratio/), and elevated [dividend](/dividend/) yields — the classic markers of an undervalued business.

The index methodology has remained remarkably stable since the fund's inception. It does not attempt to pick market bottoms or time [sector rotations](/sector-rotation/); instead, it mechanically selects for valuation metrics at a point in time, reconstituting quarterly. This predictability is central to the fund's appeal: investors know exactly what they own and why, without the opacity or cost of an active manager's views.

## What RZV holds

RZV's portfolio typically consists of 150 to 200 small-cap stocks, each meeting the value screens. The universe includes beaten-down companies in traditional value sectors — financial services, energy, industrials, consumer discretionary — where prices have fallen enough to create attractive yields or [earnings multiples](/earnings-multiple/). Because small-cap companies have less analyst coverage and smaller shareholder bases, the market often prices them inefficiently, which is where value investors hunt.

A typical RZV holding might be a regional bank, a manufacturing firm recovering from cyclical weakness, or a mature consumer business where growth expectations have been written down. Each position is sized according to the index methodology, which means the largest holdings are still considerably smaller by [market cap](/market-capitalization/) than those in a comparable large-cap value fund. That concentration in smaller companies is both the appeal and the risk — greater opportunity set but also higher volatility.

## Value, dividends, and the small-cap advantage

One of RZV's characteristics is its dividend yield, which typically runs above that of a broad small-cap index or the S&P 500. This is not accidental: cheap valuations and high yields go hand in hand. A company that is out of favor and trading at a low multiple often uses excess cash to pay dividends, signaling that the business is mature and profitable even if growth is slow. For income-focused investors, RZV can serve as a diversified, low-cost way to capture that yield from the small-cap space.

The small-cap value angle offers something large-cap value funds cannot: smaller companies often have less efficient access to capital and more volatile demand cycles, which mean that a period of undervaluation can last longer and the eventual repricing can be more violent. A value investor in small-cap believes that mean reversion is powerful at this scale and that patience will be rewarded. RZV lets them make that bet in a liquid, indexed wrapper.

## Costs and mechanics

RZV's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is low, reflecting Invesco's scale and the mechanical nature of index tracking. Because the underlying index reconstitutes only quarterly and the value criteria do not change rapidly, the fund's portfolio turnover is moderate, keeping trading costs and tax drag to a minimum. The fund trades on a U.S. exchange and creates or redeems shares to maintain alignment between its price and the index value.

## The case for and against

RZV's case rests on the belief that the [value premium](/value-premium/) — the tendency of cheap stocks to outperform expensive ones over long periods — is real and persistent. Academic research supports this, though the premium is not guaranteed in every period. In recent years, growth stocks have dominated, and value-tilted funds have lagged. Investors in RZV must be comfortable with the possibility of long dry spells between value's cycles of outperformance.

The other advantage is simplicity and cost. Compared to an active value manager charging 0.5 to 1.5 percent annually, RZV charges far less and does not risk the idiosyncratic bet or the career-limiting decision of an individual stock picker. You get pure, transparent value exposure at a price.

## Risks specific to small-cap value

Small-cap value stocks are volatile and illiquid compared to large-cap stocks. During market stress, they can fall sharply and can be difficult to sell in size without moving the market. RZV holds dozens of companies, so no single position is catastrophic if it goes bad, but a broad market downturn in the small-cap value sector can inflict significant losses.

A second risk is value traps — companies that are cheap for a reason and remain cheap or get cheaper over time. Because the index uses mechanical criteria without fundamental judgment, it can load up on businesses in structural decline (a shrinking industry, a failing competitive position) that look cheap only because the market has correctly identified them as poor long-term bets. Active value managers attempt to avoid these; RZV cannot.

## How to evaluate RZV

To understand RZV, start with Invesco's prospectus and fact sheet, which detail the index methodology and the expense ratio. Compare RZV's performance against the broad S&P SmallCap 600 and against active small-cap value funds to assess whether the value tilt is delivering its promised premium. Look at the fund's sector and geographic composition to see whether it has drifted into concentrated bets (e.g., heavily overweighted to financials or energy). Review the dividend yield relative to its history and to competing small-cap value offerings.

As with any investment, RZV's price fluctuates, and nothing here is a recommendation. Rather, it is a framework for understanding what RZV is designed to do and where its strengths and vulnerabilities lie.
