*The **Simplify [Bond](/bond/) Bull ETF** (ticker RFIX) is a fixed-income fund that combines a core portfolio of [investment-grade bonds](/investment-grade-bond/) with options and derivative positions designed to amplify gains when [interest rates](/interest-rate/) fall — making it a directional bet on bond-market strength rather than a traditional core bond holding.*

## From bond fund to rate-directional strategy

Simplify Asset Management launched RFIX during the [Federal Reserve's](/federal-reserve/) rate-hiking campaign of 2022–2023, when bond prices had fallen sharply and market participants were debating whether rates had peaked. The fund emerged from a specific thesis: that investors should be able to take a tactical, leveraged bet on declining interest rates without holding a traditional leveraged [bond ETF](/bond-etf/) — which amplifies losses just as much as gains and resets daily, creating drag in sideways or rising-rate environments.

RFIX's answer was to pair a straightforward portfolio of investment-grade bonds — securities that would benefit directly from rate declines — with [call options](/call-option/) on bond-related instruments. Calls give the fund the right to buy bonds at a predetermined price, so they increase in value as bond prices rise (which happens when rates fall). This structure lets the fund capture outsized gains during a bond rally while avoiding the daily-reset mechanics that undermine traditional leveraged bond products.

The fund reflects Simplify's broader philosophy: to use derivatives and option strategies not as hedges but as directional tools to express specific views about market direction, providing access to tactical positions that would otherwise require sophisticated trading or significant capital.

## How the "bull" structure works

A typical holding in RFIX consists of two parts. First, the bond portfolio itself — a mix of U.S. Treasury securities, investment-grade [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/), and similar fixed-income instruments. These bonds provide steady yield and benefit directly if interest rates fall: when the Federal Reserve cuts rates, bond prices rise, and the fund's bond holdings increase in value.

Second, the fund holds call options on bonds or bond indices. A call option gives the fund the right to profit from further bond-price appreciation without having to own the bonds outright. If rates fall sharply, bond prices surge, the call options become deeply in-the-money (worth much more), and the fund's leverage amplifies the gain. If rates stay flat or rise, the call options expire worthless or lose value, but the bond portfolio itself continues to generate yield and provides some protection.

This structure is a directional bet. The fund is designed to prosper when rates fall. It is not designed to outperform in a stable or rising-rate environment. A traditional bond fund would still earn coupons and might hold its value; RFIX, with money deployed in options rather than additional bonds, would not.

## Interest-rate risk and leverage effects

The fund introduces several risks absent from a plain bond fund. The most obvious is leverage: by using options to amplify bond exposure, the fund amplifies losses if rates rise. A 2% decline in bond prices would normally cost a traditional bond fund 2% of value; RFIX, with its call overlay, might decline 5% or more, depending on the exact option structure at the time.

A second risk is timing. The fund's returns depend on when interest rates fall. A call option has an [expiration date](/expiration-date/). If rates remain stable for years, the options expire and are rolled into new ones, creating a drag ([time decay](/time-decay-theta/)) that compounds over long [holding periods](/holding-period/). The fund is built for a tactical timeframe — to capture a rally if and when it materializes — not for decades of ownership waiting for rates to drift lower.

A third risk is [credit risk](/credit-risk/), though muted. The bond portfolio holds investment-grade securities, which are relatively safe from default, but economic stress can still impair credit quality and reduce prices independent of rates.

The fund is not suitable for investors seeking stability, rising income, or bond-market returns without leverage. It is designed for investors who hold a strong conviction that interest rates will fall in a material way and are comfortable taking on leverage and path risk to benefit from that outcome.

## Active management and turnover

RFIX is actively managed. The portfolio managers decide which bonds to hold, how much duration exposure to maintain, and how to structure the option overlays. They adjust positions based on their assessment of economic conditions, Federal Reserve policy, and market pricing. This discretion introduces manager risk: if the managers misread the rate environment or construct the option strategies poorly, performance will suffer.

The fund's turnover tends to be moderate to high because the options require rolling and adjustment, and because managers may move in and out of bonds as their outlook changes. Higher turnover creates transaction costs and, in taxable accounts, can generate short-term capital gains.

## Research and appropriate use

RFIX's prospectus and fact sheet detail the current bond holdings, the duration exposure, the specific call options or call strategies in use, and recent performance. Key things to examine: What does the bond portfolio look like, and how much yield does it carry? How is the fund using derivatives — are calls the full story, or are there other instruments? How has the fund performed during the periods it was in effect? Did it amplify gains during the bond rallies that did occur, and by how much?

RFIX is a tactical bet, not a core fixed-income allocation. It should represent a small portion of a diversified portfolio, held by investors who are comfortable with leverage and who believe rates have a material chance of falling in the near to medium term. It is not appropriate for conservative investors, those nearing retirement, or anyone depending on stable portfolio value. For investors with a specific rate-decline thesis and a time horizon of months to a few years, it offers a structured way to express that view.
