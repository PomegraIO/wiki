## What RVNL does (and what it does not do)

RVNL is a leveraged ETF that aims to deliver two times the daily return of Rivian Automotive stock. If RIVN gains 1% in a day, RVNL attempts to gain 2%. If RIVN falls 1%, RVNL attempts to fall 2%. It achieves this leverage using derivatives—primarily swaps and futures contracts—rather than borrowing money directly. GraniteShares, the fund sponsor, manages the daily rebalancing needed to maintain the 2x exposure as markets move.

RVNL is explicitly not designed to track RIVN over weeks, months, or years. The "daily" in the fund's name is critical: the leverage reset happens every single market day. This makes RVNL radically different from a simple loan that let you buy twice as much Rivian stock at yesterday's price.

## The mathematics of daily reset and decay

When a fund resets leverage daily, it rebalances its derivatives each night to maintain the target 2x ratio. This creates a drag that grows larger the more volatile the underlying stock is. The phenomenon is called volatility decay or compounding loss. Here is the mechanism:

Suppose RIVN rises 20% one day, then falls 16.67% the next (a round trip that leaves it flat overall). A 1x investor buying RIVN at the start breaks even. But RVNL would have gained 40% on day one (2x the 20% move), then lost 33.33% on day two (2x the 16.67% fall), ending down roughly 7% overall despite the underlying stock being flat. The larger the swings, the worse the decay.

This decay is mathematical, not a fee the fund charges. It arises directly from resetting leverage daily in a volatile market. Over short holding periods—hours to a few days—decay is manageable. Over weeks or months, decay becomes the dominant force, and RVNL will lose money even if Rivian stock stays flat or edges slightly higher.

## Rivian as the underlying

Rivian Automotive is an electric vehicle manufacturer founded in 2009, known for its R1T and R1S vehicles aimed at the adventure segment. As an early-stage automaker, Rivian is highly capital-intensive and has not achieved profitability. The company's stock is volatile, reflecting investor sentiment about the viability of EV startups and the competitive dynamics of the auto industry.

RVNL's performance is entirely tethered to RIVN's price movement. Leverage amplifies both gains and losses on the underlying stock. If you are bullish on Rivian and want to bet 2x as much as the stock itself would move, RVNL lets you do that—for one day at a time. But the compounding mechanics mean any multi-day holding requires a different mathematical analysis than simply "I am bullish on Rivian and want 2x exposure."

## When RVNL might make sense (and when it absolutely does not)

RVNL is a tool for active traders who:
- Are making a short-term directional bet on RIVN for a single trading session.
- Want to amplify a move they expect to happen within a single day.
- Understand the daily reset mechanics and accept the volatility decay inherent in them.

RVNL is not appropriate for:
- Buy-and-hold investors. Decay will erode the position over weeks and months, independent of whether Rivian stock rises or falls.
- Investors who think Rivian is cheap and will hold for years. You would be fighting decay the entire time.
- Investors who lack access to real-time market data or the ability to monitor the position daily. Leveraged single-stock products require active management.

The fund carries significant risks: if Rivian stock drops sharply, RVNL will drop roughly twice as much, wiping out value rapidly. In extreme volatility, the daily reset mechanism itself can cause unexpected losses beyond simple 2x math.

## Costs and how it trades

RVNL trades as an ETF on NASDAQ throughout the market day. The expense ratio is moderate—higher than a standard equity ETF but lower than actively managed funds—reflecting the cost of maintaining the leverage through derivative positions. The biggest cost, however, is not the fee but the volatility decay, which is baked into the mathematics rather than charged explicitly.

RVNL trades with reasonable liquidity given its single-stock focus. You can buy and sell shares directly through any brokerage account. However, the bid-ask spread may be wider than it is for simpler, more widely held ETFs, particularly during periods of low market activity.

## Understanding this fund's true role

RVNL is a leveraged derivatives product, not a substitute for owning Rivian stock outright. It is a tactical tool for the specific bet "Rivian will move sharply in the next one to three trading days." If you are making a longer bet, the daily reset mechanics and resulting decay will work against you, and you would be better off owning RIVN directly (even on margin, if leverage is your goal) or using options that let you set an explicit time horizon.

Anyone considering RVNL should start with GraniteShares' prospectus, which lays out the daily reset mechanism and the volatility decay phenomenon explicitly. The historical performance of the fund relative to 2x RIVN is instructive—that gap is decay at work.
