*The **Invesco ESG S&P 500 Equal Weight ETF** (RSPE) applies two distinct filters to the S&P 500: it removes companies that fail to meet ESG standards, then equal-weights the remainder. The result is a portfolio that diverges from market-cap weighting on two axes — both through exclusion and through mechanical rebalancing — creating a fund that is simultaneously narrower than the broad S&P 500 and tilted toward smaller, more values-aligned constituents.*

## ESG screening and the starting universe

RSPE begins by filtering the S&P 500 through environmental, social, and governance criteria. Invesco's ESG standards exclude or underweight companies with poor records on carbon intensity, workforce practices, board diversity, governance controversies, or other dimensions that Invesco deems material to long-term risk. The resulting universe is smaller than 500 companies — typically 350–450 — and excludes certain fossil-fuel-heavy companies, some financial-services firms, and others that fall below ESG thresholds.

ESG screening is not one standard but a spectrum. Invesco applies its own proprietary screens and partnerships with ESG data providers, which means the composition is both rules-based and subjective. A company's ESG rating can change if new controversies emerge or if it improves practices. This adds a layer of active judgment to what is otherwise a passive, index-based fund.

## The double tilt: exclusion plus equal weighting

Once the ESG-screened universe is established, RSPE equal-weights it. This creates two sources of divergence from the broad market-cap-weighted S&P 500:

**From exclusion:** The fund tilts away from extractive industries, financials with poor governance, and others that fail ESG tests. This typically tilts the fund toward technology, healthcare, and consumer-focused companies while reducing fossil-fuel and old-economy exposure.

**From equal weighting:** Like other equal-weight funds, RSPE rebalances quarterly to equal weight, which means selling appreciating holdings (often the mega-cap winners) and buying laggards. This creates a contrarian tilt that has worked well in mean-reversion environments and poorly during extended mega-cap rallies.

The two tilts can reinforce or oppose each other. In periods when ESG-friendly mega-cap stocks ([Nvidia](/nvda-stock/), [Apple](/aapl-stock/)) dominate, the equal-weighting mechanism selling them works against the ESG exclusion benefit of avoiding fossil fuels. In periods when non-tech stocks rebound, equal weighting brings the smaller ESG-screened names along, amplifying returns.

## ESG drift and forward ratings

Companies' ESG scores are not static. As business models evolve, governance improves or deteriorates, or controversies emerge, ratings shift. A company can enter the fund and later face exclusion if its ESG profile worsens, or vice versa. This is not a bug — it reflects the reality of ESG investing — but it means the fund's composition is less stable than a pure passive index. Annual or quarterly ESG rebalancing can add holdings and remove them beyond the equal-weight rebalancing schedule, creating additional turnover and trading costs.

## Values alignment and trade-offs

For investors seeking values alignment — those who do not want to own fossil-fuel companies, weapons makers, or firms with governance red flags — RSPE offers explicit exclusions and a fund constructed with those preferences in mind. However, ESG screening carries a return cost in normal circumstances. Excluding the largest fossil-fuel and financial companies means missing some of the strongest performers in certain cycles, such as energy-driven [bull markets](/bull-market/) or financial-crisis recoveries. Equal weighting compounds this by underweighting even the ESG-screened mega-cap winners.

The trade-off is philosophical as much as financial. Investors should understand that optimising for values alignment typically reduces potential returns, and RSPE makes both a values choice (ESG exclusion) and a structural choice (equal weighting) that reduce [diversification](/diversification/) relative to the full S&P 500.

## Costs and tax efficiency

RSPE's [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is higher than a plain equal-weight fund because the ESG screening requires ongoing monitoring and additional rebalancing events beyond the quarterly equal-weight rebalance. The fund may also incur higher realised gains because ESG reclifications force sales that trigger [capital gains distributions](/capital-gains-distribution/). Taxable investors should expect less tax efficiency than a simple buy-and-hold broad [index fund](/index-fund/).

Trading volume is moderate; the fund has established liquidity but is smaller than broader universe funds. [Bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) are tight but not negligible.

## Cyclical behaviour with an ESG overlay

RSPE's performance depends on the interaction of three elements: ESG-sector exposure (tech and healthcare tend to score well on ESG metrics), mega-cap concentration in the ESG universe (Nvidia, Apple, and other mega-caps pass ESG screens), and equal-weighting rebalancing (which sells both to balance the fund mechanically). In periods when ESG-favourable sectors lead and equal weighting suppresses the winning mega-caps, RSPE underperforms the broad S&P 500. In recovery periods when excluded sectors like energy rebound, RSPE may outperform because it is not held back by those legacy holdings.

## Who RSPE is for

RSPE suits investors who want broad S&P 500 exposure but have a commitment to values-based investing and ESG principles. It is most appropriate for long-term investors who are comfortable accepting some return trade-off in exchange for alignment with ESG criteria. It is less appropriate for investors solely focused on return maximisation, or those who are sceptical of ESG screening effectiveness.

The equal-weighting component is a secondary consideration; it adds complexity and rebalancing costs. For many ESG-focused investors, a simpler market-cap-weighted ESG S&P 500 fund might be more efficient. RSPE combines both tilts and is thus suitable for those who believe in both ESG screening and mean-reversion mechanics.

## Researching RSPE

Invesco publishes detailed ESG criteria and methodologies. Review them carefully to understand what the fund excludes and how ratings are determined. Check the quarterly composition reports to see which companies have entered or been removed due to ESG changes. Compare RSPE's ESG profile against its returns relative to a plain [S&P 500 index](/sp-500-index/) and a market-cap-weighted ESG S&P 500 index to understand the cost of equal weighting added on top of ESG screening.

Track ESG trends more broadly: as more companies adopt strong practices, the ESG-screened universe will expand; as controversies arise, exclusions will change. RSPE's composition is living, not static, and part of understanding the fund is understanding how ESG standards themselves evolve. For impact-minded investors, also consider whether RSPE's passive screening approach aligns with your values, or whether active ESG-focused funds that engage with companies would better serve your goals.
