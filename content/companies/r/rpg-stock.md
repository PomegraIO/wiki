*The **Invesco S&P 500 Pure [Growth ETF](/growth-etf/) (RPG)** distils the growth segment of the S&P 500 into a single fund by excluding dividend-paying stocks, cyclical earners, and value-oriented companies, leaving behind a concentrated basket of businesses priced for rapid expansion of future earnings. Where a vanilla [S&P 500 index](/sp-500-index/) fund holds 500 household names in rough proportion to their market weight — [General Electric](/ge-stock/) alongside [Apple](/aapl-stock/), Ford alongside [Nvidia](/nvda-stock/) — RPG holds roughly 240 stocks screened for pure growth momentum. The fund is built for investors who want the broad market's scale and liquidity but believe the growth winners will outrun mature, dividend-heavy peers over time.*

## The stock selection filter

RPG uses the S&P 500 Pure Growth Index, a rules-based methodology that ranks the 500 stocks on several growth and value metrics. The index applies an explicit value filter first, eliminating stocks with high [dividend yields](/dividend-yield/) or low [price-to-book ratios](/price-to-book-ratio/) — this sweeps out the mature, cash-returning businesses like utilities, energy companies, and cyclical industrials. What remains is a universe of growth-oriented equities, typically dominated by technology, healthcare, and consumer-discretionary sectors. The index then selects from that filtered pool by ordering stocks on forward earnings growth, [earnings revision momentum](/earnings-revision-momentum/), and [return on equity](/return-on-equity/), aiming to capture businesses with the strongest near-term growth tailwinds.

The result is a notably concentrated fund. The top 10 holdings often represent 30% or more of the fund's assets, and the sector split is heavily tilted toward technology and healthcare — sectors where earnings growth is priced in as an assumption. Because [dividend](/dividend/) payers are excluded by design, the fund has minimal income and is purely a capital-appreciation play. Unlike a dividend-focused fund, RPG does not cushion downturns with distributions; it lives or dies by stock price movement.

## Who the fund targets

RPG appeals to two constituencies. The first is the growth investor who believes the S&P 500's largest companies will expand earnings faster than the market consensus prices in, and who wants to concentrate on the purest expression of that bet without being dragged down by utilities, regional banks, or mature industrials. The second is the investor who trusts an S&P-sanctioned rules-based system more than active managers or quant models, and who wants the psychological safety of holding a recognisable subset of a recognisable index.

The fund's liquidity and structure as a transparent ETF make it suitable for both retail investors and institutions. It trades with reasonable daily volume and tight spreads, so large positions can be built or exited without moving the market.

## Sector and style risks

A pure-growth filter inevitably concentrates risk. In the 2022 [bear market](/bear-market/) for growth stocks, when high-multiple companies fell hardest as [interest rates](/interest-rate/) rose, RPG fell roughly twice as much as the broad S&P 500. This is not a flaw in the fund — it is the inevitable result of excluding defensive dividend stocks and overweighting expensive growth names. An investor buying RPG implicitly accepts that in a rising-rate environment or a [recession](/recession/) that crushes high-flyer valuations, the fund will underperform.

The sector concentration also matters. When technology earnings disappoint or when healthcare regulation tightens, the fund's largest 30–40 holdings move together, reducing [diversification](/diversification/) benefits that the "500 stocks" label might suggest. RPG is 240 stocks on paper, but its effective diversification is sometimes closer to 50 or 60 because the top holdings dominate.

## Cost and efficiency

RPG carries a modest [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) — lower than an active growth manager but slightly higher than a pure S&P 500 [index fund](/index-fund/), reflecting the cost of the screen, the rebalancing required to maintain it, and Invesco's operational overhead. The fund is tax-efficient within its mandate; because it is a systematic rules-based strategy, it does not generate the trading activity an active manager would, and the turnover is modest. For tax-conscious investors in taxable accounts, that efficiency adds value.

## Comparison to the S&P 500

RPG consistently outperforms the broad S&P 500 during periods of strong growth-stock dominance, such as 2020 and 2021. In value and dividend-heavy periods, it lags — sometimes by a large margin. Over very long periods, the outperformance is more muted because the outsize growth in some years is offset by underperformance in others. An investor choosing between RPG and a total-market index fund is making a style bet, not a safety bet. The fund is not "better" — it is different, and it will appeal only to those who believe growth will outrun value.

## How to research RPG

Start with the fund's prospectus and the documentation of the S&P 500 Pure Growth Index methodology, which explains exactly how the screens work. Compare RPG's rolling returns — one, three, five, and ten years — against the S&P 500 directly and against other growth-focused funds like those from Vanguard or iShares to understand the competitive position. Track the sector composition — the percentage in technology, healthcare, and financials — and watch how it shifts as growth and value fortunes alternate. The fund's website publishes the top 20 holdings quarterly, so verify that the filter is working as intended and that you recognize the businesses inside. Finally, consider your own time horizon and risk tolerance: RPG is a long-term, volatile growth play that works best for investors who can hold through cycles and believe the future belongs to expanding businesses, not cash-returning incumbents.
