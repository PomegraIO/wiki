*The **Tradr 2X Long RGTI Daily ETF** (RGTU) is a leveraged exchange-traded note designed to approximate twice the daily return of the Direxion Daily Retail Bull 2X Shares (known colloquially as RTIH), magnifying both gains and losses in the retail-sector equity space through daily rebalancing.*

## What is RGTU trying to do?

RGTU uses leverage to amplify the daily swings of a concentrated retail-sector portfolio. On days when retail stocks rally, RGTU aims to capture roughly twice that gain. On days when they fall, RGTU falls twice as hard. The mechanism is mechanical: every day, the fund rebalances to maintain its 2X [leverage ratio](/leverage-ratio-forex/) relative to the underlying exposure. This daily reset is what distinguishes a leveraged fund from simply holding the underlying stock on margin; it is a feature that creates both opportunity and a specific set of risks.

## Who is RGTU built for?

This is not a buy-and-hold investment. Leveraged ETNs with daily resets exist for active traders and tactical-allocation specialists who intend to hold for days or weeks, not years. A trader might use RGTU to amplify conviction on a short-term retail rally, or to hedge a short position in the sector by going long on leverage. The daily rebalancing mechanic makes these products expensive and distortive for long-term holders because of volatility decay — a phenomenon where the fund's total return lags even twice the underlying's return over extended periods, especially in choppy markets.

## What is volatility decay and why does it matter?

Volatility decay is the mathematical cost of resetting leverage every day in a market that moves up and down. A simple example: suppose the underlying index starts at 100, falls 20 percent to 80 on day one, then rises 25 percent to 100 on day two. The underlying is back where it started. But a 2X leveraged fund would fall 40 percent on day one (to 60) and then rise 50 percent on day two (to 90). It underperforms the flat underlying by 10 points because the leverage was reset at a lower level. In volatile markets—which the retail sector can be—this drag compounds. A trader holding RGTU for months during a choppy [bull market](/bull-market/) often finds the fund has crushed the underlying's 2X return, not amplified it. This is why leveraged daily-reset products are strictly tactical instruments, not strategic holdings.

## What are the actual risks?

Beyond volatility decay, RGTU carries the leverage risk itself: a market crash that drops retail stocks 50 percent would drop RGTU 100 percent (to zero), wiping out the entire position. There is also the structural risk that retail stocks are cyclical and concentrated: a [sector rotation](/sector-rotation/) away from consumer discretionary can trigger steep drawdowns that are amplified 2X. Additionally, as an ETN (exchange-traded note) rather than an ETF, RGTU is a debt instrument of its issuer. If the issuer fails, the noteholder loses, regardless of the underlying retail sector's performance. Finally, the fund carries a meaningful [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/), which compounds as costs over the trading period.

## How should a reader research RGTU?

Start by understanding the underlying retail sector: which companies comprise it, what are their margins and capital-allocation policies, and what is the macro outlook for consumer [discretionary spending](/discretionary-spending/). Check the daily settlement prices of RGTU against the underlying retail-sector index to see if the 2X amplification is working as advertised or if decay is visible. Read the [fund prospectus](/fund-prospectus/) carefully, particularly the risk-disclosure sections, to understand leverage mechanics and the daily reset formula. Most importantly: do not hold RGTU expecting it to deliver 2X the annual return of retail stocks; that expectation will be disappointed. Instead, use it only as a tactical position for days or at most weeks, with a clear exit plan and [position sizing](/position-sizing/) that reflects the leverage risk.
