*The **Invesco S&P MidCap 400 Revenue ETF** ([NASDAQ](/nasdaq/): RWK) holds the 400 mid-cap U.S. companies in the S&P MidCap 400, but sizes each position by the company's annual revenue rather than its [market capitalization](/market-capitalization/), creating a portfolio that tilts toward companies large in sales relative to stock-market value.*

## At a Glance

**Fund**: S&P MidCap 400 Net Revenue Index
**Universe**: Mid-cap U.S. equities (roughly $3 billion to $10 billion market cap range)
**Weighting scheme**: Revenue-based, not market-cap-based
**Structure**: Passive index ETF, quarterly rebalancing
**Issuer**: Invesco

## The Revenue-Weighting Lens

RWK tracks the same 400 companies as the standard market-cap-weighted S&P MidCap 400, but flips the weighting logic. A company with $20 billion in annual revenue gets roughly twice the portfolio weight of a company with $10 billion in revenue. This approach creates a portfolio where the portfolio's profit-and-loss profile is less influenced by investor sentiment about *future growth* and more influenced by the *actual economic scale* of each business today.

Mid-cap companies tend to be established players past the startup phase but not yet mature megacaps — regional banks, specialty manufacturers, business-services firms, smaller healthcare providers. Many trade at lower valuations than large-caps and higher volatility. Revenue weighting nudges the fund toward those companies that are large by revenue relative to their stock-market value, typically a modest value tilt.

## The Trade-off: Information Loss

Revenue is a clean number — relatively hard to manipulate compared to earnings. But revenue alone tells nothing about **margin quality** or **reinvestment needs**. A company growing revenue at 2% per year with 25% net margins generates far more cash than one growing at 10% with 5% margins. Revenue weighting cannot distinguish. A fund that overweights low-margin businesses (contract manufacturing, distribution) while underweighting high-margin ones (software, consulting) will drag returns even if the low-margin businesses have higher revenues.

The other catch: revenue weighting is mechanical and backward-looking. Last year's revenues determine this year's weight. A mid-cap company facing secular decline — think retail or traditional media — might retain a high weight until the company has actually shrunk in revenue terms. Market-cap weighting, by contrast, reprices problems instantly as investors see them.

## Where RWK Fits

RWK is appropriate for investors who believe mid-cap equities are underpriced relative to their economic fundamentals and who think that revenue is a better anchor for valuations than stock price. It is useful as a diversifier against market-cap-weighted funds — the revenue tilt means it will lag in periods when growth and momentum dominate, but outperform in periods when stable, profitable companies are in favor.

The fund's annual cost is modest but meaningful — several basis points higher than market-cap-weighted mid-cap ETFs — so the fee is worth checking against the prospectus. Quarterly rebalancing creates turnover that can generate tax drag in taxable accounts, though not as much as an [actively managed fund](/actively-managed-fund/) would.

## The Real Vulnerability

The single biggest risk is that **mid-cap stocks are more volatile and less liquid than large caps**, which is true regardless of weighting scheme. Revenue weighting does not reduce that volatility; it merely changes which companies within the mid-cap universe are emphasized. In a severe market downturn, the benefit of owning large-revenue, low-sentiment companies disappears quickly — they tend to fall alongside everything else. RWK offers no hedge against equity-market risk; it is only a different slice of the same equity market.

Readers evaluating RWK should compare its 5- and 10-year returns against the standard market-cap-weighted S&P MidCap 400 to see whether the revenue-weighting tilt has added value in practice. The prospectus and fund fact sheet detail the current holdings and their revenue weights.
