RiverNorth Flexible Municipal [Income Fund](/income-fund/) II, Inc. (RFMZ) is a closed-end investment company that pools capital from shareholders to purchase and manage a portfolio of [municipal bonds](/municipal-bond/) and related securities. The fund is listed on the [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) and pays out a regular distribution to shareholders, making it a vehicle for income-seeking investors who want exposure to tax-exempt municipal debt.

## What is a closed-end municipal fund?

A [closed-end fund](/closed-end-fund/) differs from an open-ended [mutual fund](/mutual-fund/) in a fundamental way: it raises capital once through an [initial public offering](/initial-public-offering/) and then trades as a fixed set of shares, like a stock. Investors who want to buy or sell shares must do so in the [secondary market](/secondary-market/) at prices set by supply and demand — not directly through the fund itself. [RiverNorth Flexible Municipal Income](/rfm-stock/) Fund II raised its initial capital through an IPO and now trades as RFMZ on the NASDAQ, with prices fluctuating based on investor appetite.

The fund's portfolio consists primarily of municipal [bonds](/bond/) — debt issued by states, cities, and other local governments to finance infrastructure, schools, and public services. Municipal bonds carry a distinctive tax advantage: the interest income they generate is typically exempt from U.S. federal income tax, and often exempt from state and local taxes too. This tax benefit is valuable to high-income investors in high-tax jurisdictions, which is why municipal funds appeal to a specific subset of the investment universe.

## How does RiverNorth manage money and distribute it?

RiverNorth Flexible Municipal Income Fund II employs professional portfolio managers who select specific municipal bonds to buy, hold, and sell on behalf of shareholders. The manager — RiverNorth Capital Management — has discretion to move between different types of municipal bonds based on their outlook for [interest rates](/interest-rate/), credit quality, and supply. The word "flexible" in the fund's name signals this discretion: rather than tracking a fixed index, the managers actively choose which bonds to own.

The fund distributes income to shareholders regularly — typically monthly or quarterly, depending on the fund's distribution policy. This income comes from the interest ([coupon payments](/coupon-payment/)) collected on the bonds in the portfolio. Because municipal interest is tax-exempt, shareholders receive the distributions without owing federal income tax on them, a significant advantage over taxable bond funds for high-tax-bracket investors.

## What makes the capital structure work?

The fund's capital structure is simple: shareholders own shares (equity), and the fund borrows money (debt) to amplify its purchasing power. This borrowing, called leverage, lets RiverNorth buy more bonds than its equity base alone could support. If bonds return 5 percent and the fund borrows at 2 percent, the spread funds additional distributions to shareholders. However, leverage also magnifies losses if bonds decline in value or credit conditions worsen. The fund discloses its leverage in regulatory filings; investors must monitor how much debt the fund is carrying relative to its assets.

The portfolio itself is the fund's operating asset. RiverNorth buys and holds municipal bonds, collecting coupons, reinvesting (or distributing) the proceeds, and adjusting the holdings as market conditions and the managers' views shift. When bonds mature or are sold at a gain, RiverNorth redeploys that capital into new positions.

## Risk and price dynamics

The fund's share price reflects two separate forces: the underlying value of the bond portfolio, and investor sentiment toward the fund itself. If interest rates rise, the market value of existing bonds falls (because new bonds offer higher yields). If municipal [credit spreads](/credit-spread/) widen — meaning the risk premium investors demand for owning municipal debt increases — portfolio values decline further. Both effects flow through to the fund's [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) (NAV), the underlying worth of each share.

But RFMZ shares also trade at a premium or discount to NAV. When investors are eager to own municipal income, the fund trades above NAV; when fear runs high or there is an oversupply of closed-end fund shares, RFMZ may trade below NAV. This premium or discount is distinct from the underlying bond values and reflects supply and demand for the fund shares themselves in the secondary market.

## Monitoring RiverNorth Flexible Municipal Income Fund II

Investors should begin with the fund's prospectus and [annual reports](/10-k/) (SEC CIK 0001817159), which detail the bond holdings, [expense ratios](/expense-ratio/), leverage level, and distribution history. Watch for changes in the distribution rate — when a fund cuts its distribution, it signals either declining income (falling bond coupons or portfolio turnover issues) or a deliberate shift in capital allocation. Track the fund's discount or premium to NAV: a persistent discount may signal hidden risks or excessive costs; a premium suggests investor confidence, but it also means buying shares at an inflated price.

The broader context matters too: as interest rates change, municipal bond valuations shift across the board. A rising-rate environment is typically challenging for bond funds, while falling rates boost portfolio values. The fund's leverage amplifies both directions, so understanding whether RiverNorth is taking more or less leverage helps frame the outlook.
