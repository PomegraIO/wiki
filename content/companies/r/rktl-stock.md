*The **Defiance Daily Target 2X Long RKT ETF** (RKTL) is an exchange-traded fund designed to deliver twice the daily return of Rocket Companies stock — the online mortgage lender and financial services company. It accomplishes this amplification through the use of derivatives (primarily [futures contracts](/futures-contract/) and options) rather than by borrowing money to buy additional shares. For every 1 percent that RKT moves up on a given day, RKTL aims to move up 2 percent. For every 1 percent that RKT declines, RKTL aims to decline 2 percent.*

## The mechanics of daily leverage and the daily reset

Understanding RKTL requires grasping how daily-reset [leveraged ETFs](/leveraged-etf/) work, as this structure is where the fund's chief risks and opportunities hide. The fund uses index derivatives to track twice the daily return of RKT, resetting that leverage target at the close of each trading day. This means the fund is designed for very short [holding periods](/holding-period/) — ideally a single trading day — and it will behave very differently when held for longer stretches.

On a single day, if RKT rises 2 percent, RKTL should rise approximately 4 percent. If RKT falls 3 percent, RKTL should fall approximately 6 percent. The fund manager uses daily futures contracts on RKT and other derivatives to maintain this two-to-one amplification. At the end of each trading day, the fund resets its derivative positions to maintain the 2X target for the next day's trading.

This daily reset is crucial and often misunderstood. It means the fund is not tracking twice the total return of RKT over a multi-day period — it is tracking twice the daily return, renewed each day. The distinction matters enormously for investors holding the fund longer than a single day.

## Why volatility decay matters for multi-day holders

Here is where RKTL diverges from a simple 2X bet on RKT. Suppose RKT rises 5 percent on Monday and then falls 5 percent on Tuesday, ending the two-day period essentially flat (in reality, a 5 percent rise followed by a 5 percent decline on the reduced base leaves it down fractionally). RKTL, if the amplification worked perfectly in isolation, would rise 10 percent on Monday and fall 10 percent on Tuesday. But 10 percent off a higher base is larger than 10 percent off the original base, so RKTL ends up significantly negative even though RKT is essentially flat.

This is volatility decay — the enemy of leveraged ETFs held over any period longer than a few days or weeks. In markets that oscillate or meander, leveraged ETFs lose value not because the underlying stock moves down overall, but because daily gains and losses compound against a moving base. Mathematically, the longer the holding period and the more volatile the price action, the worse the decay becomes. Someone holding RKTL for a year through a volatile market will almost certainly lag both RKT and a 2X constant-leverage position, even if RKT appreciates overall.

## When RKTL makes sense

RKTL is built for traders with very short time horizons — ideally day traders or swing traders who hold the position for hours or days, not weeks. It is also useful for investors with a very strong bullish conviction about RKT who want to amplify gains in a specific near-term move they anticipate. If an investor believes RKT will rise 10 percent over the next week, buying RKTL offers a way to capture roughly 20 percent upside (minus costs) rather than 10 percent, assuming the move happens and the fund does not experience significant volatility decay.

Because RKTL is exchange-traded and trades like a stock, it also offers liquidity and flexibility that buying leveraged derivatives on your own might not. An investor can buy or sell RKTL during any trading hour without needing a derivatives [broker](/broker/) or a margin account.

The fund is not suitable for long-term investing. Holding RKTL for years, even if RKT itself rises substantially, will likely produce disappointing returns due to volatility decay, compounding costs, and the constant daily rebalancing drag. It is a tactical tool, not a strategic holding.

## Rocket Companies and the underlying business

To assess whether RKTL makes sense as a tactical position, investors need to understand what RKT is. Rocket Companies operates Rocket Mortgage, an online platform for home mortgages, along with other financial services for real estate transactions. The company was a public entity for decades, became private, and then went public again in 2020. Its business is inherently cyclical — mortgage demand swings with [interest rates](/interest-rate/), home prices, and macroeconomic conditions. In periods of low interest rates and rising home prices, RKT thrives. In periods of high rates or declining home prices, the business contracts sharply.

RKT's stock is also known for volatility. Daily moves of 3 percent or more are not uncommon, which means RKTL can experience swings of 6 percent or more in a single day. This high volatility is both the appeal and the danger for investors using RKTL. It offers the chance for amplified gains but also amplified losses, and it accelerates volatility decay for those holding beyond a day or two.

## Costs and tax considerations

RKTL charges an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) to cover the costs of managing the derivatives and rebalancing daily. This fee, while modest in absolute terms, compounds daily and represents a constant drag on returns. Over months or years, even a 0.5 percent annual fee can significantly erode performance, particularly for a fund trading to track a 2X return that already suffers from volatility decay.

There are also implicit costs associated with the daily rebalancing. The fund must buy and sell derivatives each day, and those transactions carry [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) and other transaction costs. These are not explicitly visible to investors but effectively reduce the fund's returns.

From a tax perspective, the daily rebalancing inside RKTL can generate capital gains and losses that may not align with what an investor's actual buy-and-hold gains and losses would be, particularly in leveraged ETFs tracked by derivatives rather than actual stock holdings. Investors should be aware that RKTL trades are taxable events if held in a regular brokerage account, not tax-deferred.

## The practical reality for short-term traders

For a trader who believes RKT will move sharply in one direction over days or weeks, RKTL offers amplified exposure with the convenience of an exchange-traded wrapper. But success requires discipline: selling when the anticipated move is achieved or when the thesis changes, not waiting in hope that gains will continue. The fund's natural enemy is the investor who buys RKTL on conviction, expects 2X returns, and then holds through months of sideways trading and volatility. That investor will watch the fund decay toward zero even as RKT itself holds value. RKTL is a tool for precise, time-bounded tactical bets, not a substitute for owning RKT itself if the goal is long-term appreciation.
