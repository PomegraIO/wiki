## Floriculture industry overview

Floriculture—the cultivation of flowers for sale—is a specialized agricultural sector distinct from field crops or food production. Commercial flower growers produce cut flowers, potted plants, and ornamental flowers for wholesale distribution to florists, garden centers, retailers, and landscape operations. The sector has specific characteristics including seasonal demand patterns, perishability of product, and specialized growing infrastructure.

## Growing operations

AFCG operates controlled growing environments, likely including greenhouses and nurseries, to cultivate flowering plants. Modern commercial floriculture employs controlled environment agriculture, managing temperature, humidity, light, and nutrients to optimize growing conditions and produce consistent quality. The sector requires significant capital investment in facilities, equipment, and climate control systems.

## Market channels and customer base

Flower growers distribute their products through wholesale channels to florists, garden centers, and large retailers. Demand varies seasonally, with significant peaks around holidays like Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, and Christmas. The company also serves landscape operations and commercial customers. International competition, particularly from countries with lower labor costs, is a significant factor in the industry.

## Key business factors

Profitability in floriculture depends on crop yield, quality consistency, labor efficiency, energy costs, and market prices for flowers. Growers face challenges from fluctuating demand, competition from imports, and vulnerability to crop diseases or environmental conditions. Success requires effective crop management, market timing, and distribution relationships.
