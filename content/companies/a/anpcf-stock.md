*[ANGLE plc](/wiki/public-company/) is a **British diagnostic technology company** (ticker **ANPCF**) that develops and commercializes liquid biopsy solutions for detecting and monitoring circulating tumor cells in cancer patients. The company operates at the intersection of oncology and precision medicine, providing tools used in clinical settings and research.*

## What the company does

ANGLE plc develops proprietary diagnostic technologies centered on the detection and analysis of circulating tumor cells (CTCs) — cancer cells that shed into the bloodstream. The company's flagship platform, the Parsortix system, uses a novel microfiltration approach to isolate CTCs from patient blood samples, allowing oncologists to study tumor characteristics without invasive biopsies. These captured cells can be analyzed for genomic and proteomic markers, enabling personalized treatment decisions.

The company's technology addresses a fundamental challenge in oncology: obtaining tumor material for genetic testing without repeat biopsies. CTCs offer a "liquid biopsy" alternative that can be sampled repeatedly throughout treatment to monitor disease progression and treatment response in real time.

## How it makes money

ANGLE generates revenue through multiple streams. The primary avenue is the sale and lease of the Parsortix instruments to hospitals, cancer centers, and research institutions, coupled with consumables (microfluidic cartridges and reagents) required for each test. The company also receives revenue from research collaborations and partnerships with pharmaceutical and biotech firms that use ANGLE's platform in clinical trials to monitor patient outcomes.

The business model depends on adoption by clinical laboratories and oncology centers, which drives recurring consumables revenue. ANGLE has pursued [regulatory approvals](/wiki/10-k/) in major markets to enable clinical reimbursement, a key factor in broader hospital adoption.

## Where it sits in its industry

ANGLE operates in the competitive liquid biopsy and circulating tumor cell detection space, which has attracted significant investment and development from larger diagnostics companies, academic institutions, and startups. The company's focus on CTC isolation sets it apart from competitors emphasizing cell-free DNA (cfDNA) detection.

Its market position reflects the broader diagnostic industry shift toward non-invasive, repeat-sampling approaches for cancer monitoring. Success depends on achieving clinical evidence of utility, regulatory approvals, and partnerships that embed its technology into clinical workflows. ANGLE competes for adoption alongside both established diagnostics players and well-funded liquid biopsy rivals.
