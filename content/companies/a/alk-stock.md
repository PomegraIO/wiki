*[Alaska Air Group, Inc.](stock)* (**ALK**) is a [public company](/wiki/public-company/) providing air transportation services throughout Alaska, the Pacific Northwest, and connecting service linking the US West Coast to Hawaii. The company operates from major hubs in Seattle and Anchorage, serving leisure and business travelers across its regional footprint.

## What the company does

Alaska Air Group operates scheduled air passenger service with a focus on the underserved Alaska market and connections through the Pacific Northwest. The airline network includes flights within Alaska (critical for communities lacking ground transportation), regional service across Washington, Oregon, and Northern California, and leisure routes to Hawaii. The company holds a significant market position in Alaska, where it competes against limited alternatives and provides essential air service to rural and remote areas.

## How it makes money

The airline generates revenue from [ticket sales](/wiki/revenue/) on both scheduled domestic routes and occasional charter operations. Like other carriers, Alaska Air Group derives revenue from multiple sources: passenger fares (economy and premium seating), baggage fees, seat selection charges, frequent flyer program operations, and ancillary services. The company's operating costs include fuel expenses (volatile with commodity prices), labor costs for pilots and crew, airport landing fees, aircraft maintenance, and ground operations. The razor-thin [profit margins](/wiki/profit-margin/) typical in commercial aviation make Alaska Air Group sensitive to fuel price swings and demand fluctuations.

## Competition and market position

Alaska Air Group operates in the highly competitive US airline industry, where major carriers (United, American, Delta, Southwest) dominate transcontinental routes while regional carriers focus on point-to-point service and secondary markets. Alaska Air Group's strength lies in its geographic specialization: Alaska air service and the Pacific Northwest represent a concentrated market where the company has structural advantages over carriers serving that region as a secondary focus. The company competes with Southwest Airlines on many routes and with smaller carriers in regional markets. Increased fuel costs, wage inflation, and capacity additions by larger competitors represent ongoing competitive pressures.

## Capital intensity and fleet

Airlines are fundamentally capital-intensive businesses, requiring continuous investment in [aircraft](/wiki/capital-expenditure/) replacement and maintenance. Alaska Air Group must periodically retire older, less efficient aircraft and acquire new equipment to remain competitive on operating costs and passenger comfort. Aircraft financing, depreciation, and maintenance reserves represent material components of the balance sheet. Like all carriers, the company is exposed to [supply chain](/wiki/supply-chain/) disruptions affecting parts availability and aircraft delivery timelines.
