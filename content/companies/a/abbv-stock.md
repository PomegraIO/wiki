*[AbbVie Inc.](https://www.abbvie.com/) **(ABBV)** is a publicly traded biopharmaceutical company headquartered in Chicago, Illinois. The company discovers, develops, manufactures, and markets prescription pharmaceuticals across immunology, oncology, virology, and specialty therapeutic areas, serving patients globally through a portfolio of branded medications.*

## What the company does

AbbVie researches, develops, and commercializes prescription medications across multiple therapeutic areas. Its portfolio includes immunology drugs (treating rheumatoid arthritis, Crohn's disease, and ulcerative colitis), oncology medications (cancer treatments), virology therapies (including hepatitis C treatments), and specialty care products. The company operates manufacturing facilities that produce its medications, and maintains a sales and marketing organization that promotes products to healthcare providers and patients in developed markets.

## Business model and revenue generation

AbbVie's revenue comes primarily from sales of branded prescription medications protected by patents and regulatory exclusivity periods. The company generates higher margins on branded products than generics because brand protection allows pricing power. Once patent protection expires on a key drug, manufacturers can launch generic versions at significantly lower prices, creating earnings pressure. AbbVie also generates revenue from licensing its medications to other pharmaceutical companies in certain territories and from royalties on patents and technologies it has licensed to others. The company maintains a robust pipeline of experimental drugs in clinical trials, representing future revenue potential if regulatory approval is obtained.

## Patent cliffs and product lifecycles

AbbVie's financial performance depends critically on its ability to replace revenue from medications losing patent protection. The company manages this transition through continuous drug development and strategic acquisitions of promising compounds and companies. As blockbuster medications face patent expiration, the company works to extend market exclusivity through regulatory mechanisms (such as expanded indications), acquire new revenue sources through mergers and acquisitions, and introduce next-generation medications within existing therapeutic areas. This dynamic creates cycles in earnings that investors must monitor closely.

## Market position and competitive landscape

AbbVie competes with major multinational pharmaceutical firms including Pfizer, Merck, Eli Lilly, and others in core therapeutic areas. The company benefits from strong brand recognition with healthcare providers and strong market positions in immunology, particularly in rheumatoid arthritis and gastroenterology. Competition comes from generic manufacturers, biosimilar producers, and new entrants developing novel therapies. Regulatory approval, patent strength, and clinical evidence of efficacy are key competitive levers. Pricing pressures from government healthcare programs and managed care organizations in developed markets constrain revenue growth.

## Research and development investment

Biopharmaceutical companies typically invest heavily in research and development—often 15-20 percent of revenues—to discover and develop new drugs. AbbVie's R&D efforts span basic research, clinical trials, and regulatory affairs. The company must conduct expensive multi-year clinical trials to prove safety and efficacy to regulatory authorities such as the FDA. Many experimental drugs fail in development, so the company maintains a broad pipeline to manage risk. Success rates in bringing drugs from discovery to market approval are low, making portfolio breadth and diversification essential.

## Regulatory and market access considerations

Drug prices and market access are shaped by regulatory approval processes, healthcare policy, and negotiating power of insurance companies and government programs. In the United States, the FDA determines whether medications are safe and effective. In other countries, separate regulatory authorities make approval decisions. Pricing negotiation, prior authorization requirements, and formulary inclusion on insurance plans all affect actual revenue a drug generates. International pricing differs significantly by country, with many nations imposing price controls or referencing prices in other markets.

## How to research it

Start with AbbVie's annual 10-K filing to understand the pipeline of drugs in development, approved products, patent expiration dates, and revenue by therapeutic area. The 10-Q quarterly reports track product sales trends and clinical development progress. Pharmaceutical industry publications and equity research reports assess the competitive position of specific drugs and likelihood of pipeline success. News about FDA decisions, clinical trial results, and patent expirations is critical to understanding near-term earnings catalysts. Analyzing the company's cash flow and capital allocation reveals how management prioritizes shareholder returns versus investment in research and acquisitions.
