*Anfield Dynamic Fixed [Income ETF](/income-etf/) (ticker ADFI) is a fixed-income fund that actively shifts its exposure between [investment-grade bonds](/investment-grade-bond/) and high-yield (often called [junk bonds](/junk-bond/)) depending on market conditions. Rather than holding a static allocation or tracking a fixed index, ADFI uses a rules-based dynamic process to reweight between [bond](/bond/) categories, aiming to capture gains when [credit spreads](/credit-spread/) are attractive while defending capital when risk rises.*

## Investment-grade bonds: the safety pillar

The foundation of ADFI's portfolio is investment-grade [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/) — debt issued by larger, more stable companies with [credit ratings](/credit-rating/) of BBB or higher. These bonds carry lower default risk than their riskier cousins and typically offer lower yields in return for that relative safety. A company like [Procter & Gamble](/pg-stock/) or [Johnson & Johnson](/jnj-stock/) issues investment-grade debt; their bonds trade tightly and are owned by conservative portfolios and institutions. ADFI uses these as the ballast of the fund, particularly when market volatility is rising or when credit spreads (the gap between safe and risky bond yields) are widening.

## High-yield bonds: the opportunity layer

ADFI also holds [high-yield bonds](/high-yield-bond/) issued by companies with lower credit ratings or higher financial leverage. These bonds offer substantially higher yields to compensate investors for the greater risk of default. A [leveraged buyout](/leveraged-buyout/) company, a cyclical manufacturer, or a high-growth but cash-burning biotech might issue high-yield debt. These bonds are far more sensitive to economic slowdowns and market stress — when the economy weakens, defaults among high-yield issuers rise. But when credit conditions are favourable, high-yield bonds can offer compelling return potential for the risk taken.

## The dynamic rebalancing process

Rather than fix an allocation (say, 60% investment-grade and 40% high-yield) and hold it through cycles, ADFI uses a systematic process to shift between the two categories. When credit spreads widen — a sign that the market is pricing in higher risk — the fund moves money away from high-yield bonds and into the safety of investment-grade debt. When spreads narrow and credit conditions improve, ADFI rotates toward high-yield, capturing the higher yields available. This approach aims to avoid being fully invested in risky assets when a downturn is imminent and to be positioned for higher yields when conditions are stable.

The effectiveness of this strategy depends on the accuracy of the model driving the rebalancing. If the rules correctly identify peaks and troughs in [credit risk](/credit-risk/), the fund outperforms. If the signals lag or misfire, the fund may rotate into risk just as it is peaking or miss the best parts of credit rallies. Active bond management is controversial in a field where passive [index funds](/index-fund/) have become dominant; whether ADFI's particular rules and oversight justify the active [management fee](/management-fee/) is a judgment call each investor must make.

## Costs and credit risk

ADFI carries a higher [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) than a broad bond index ETF, reflecting the cost of active management and trading needed to rebalance dynamically. The fund's true costs also include any trading spreads on the bond sales and purchases required by the rebalancing process. Because many corporate bonds trade in relatively [thin markets](/thin-market/), large transactions by ADFI can move spreads wider, eating into returns.

The fund is also exposed to credit risk — the risk that a bond issuer defaults or that spreads widen sharply in a market panic. Unlike a [treasury bond](/treasury-bond/), a corporate bond can lose significant value if the issuer's finances deteriorate. ADFI's mix of investment-grade and high-yield gives it more credit risk than a pure investment-grade fund, but less than a pure high-yield fund.

## Who ADFI serves

ADFI fits investors seeking higher yield than plain bond indices offer but who want some attempt at [systematic risk](/systematic-risk/) management. It appeals to those willing to accept the costs of active management in exchange for a rules-based process that adapts to [credit cycles](/credit-cycle/). Conservative investors who cannot tolerate meaningful credit risk should stay closer to pure investment-grade funds; aggressive investors may prefer pure high-yield exposure. ADFI sits in the middle, assuming the dynamic rebalancing adds value over a fixed allocation.
