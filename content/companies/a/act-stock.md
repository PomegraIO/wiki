*[Enact Holdings, Inc.](https://pomegra.io/wiki/companies/a/act-stock/) (ticker **ACT**) is a [public corporation](/wiki/public-company/) operating in [mortgage insurance](/wiki/mortgage-insurance/). The company provides [mortgage insurance](/wiki/mortgage-insurance/) products to mortgage lenders and homebuyers, protecting lenders against losses from borrower [default](/wiki/default/) on residential [mortgage loans](/wiki/mortgage-loan/).*

## What the company does

Enact provides [mortgage insurance](/wiki/mortgage-insurance/) protecting mortgage lenders against credit losses when borrowers default on residential loans. The company serves as an intermediary between lenders and borrowers, issuing insurance policies on residential mortgages. Mortgage insurance enables borrowers to obtain financing with [down payments](/wiki/down-payment/) below traditional 20% thresholds, expanding the borrower population available to lenders.

## How mortgage insurance works and economic model

Mortgage insurers collect [insurance premiums](/wiki/insurance-premium/) from borrowers and lenders throughout the life of the mortgage. In exchange, they compensate lenders for covered losses when borrowers default and properties are foreclosed. Profitability depends on [loss ratios](/wiki/loss-ratio/) (actual claims paid versus premiums collected), operating expenses, and [investment income](/wiki/investment-income/) on premium reserves. Lower [default rates](/wiki/default-rate/) improve profitability; higher default rates trigger claims and losses.

## Market conditions and credit cycles

Mortgage insurance demand correlates with residential mortgage origination volumes, which fluctuate with [interest rates](/wiki/interest-rate/), employment, consumer credit quality, and [home prices](/wiki/home-price/). During periods of low [interest rates](/wiki/interest-rate/) and strong housing demand, mortgage originations increase and demand for mortgage insurance rises. Economic downturns reduce originations and increase default rates, creating simultaneous revenue decline and claim pressures.

## Capital and reserve requirements

Mortgage insurers maintain [capital reserves](/wiki/capital-reserves/) and [claims-paying reserves](/wiki/claims-paying-reserves/) as dictated by regulators and rating agencies. Insurance companies face [catastrophic](/wiki/catastrophic-loss/) loss scenarios during housing downturns; reserve adequacy determines solvency. [Credit default swaps](/wiki/credit-default-swap/) and [securitizations](/wiki/securitization/) allow companies to transfer portions of mortgage insurance risk to capital markets investors.

## How to research it

Review Enact's annual [10-K](/wiki/10-k/) and quarterly [10-Q](/wiki/10-q/) filings on the SEC's EDGAR database. Study premium written by [loan-to-value ratio](/wiki/loan-to-value-ratio/) and credit score of insured borrowers. Analyze [loss ratios](/wiki/loss-ratio/), non-performing insurance contracts, and reserve adequacy. Evaluate [investment portfolio](/wiki/investment-portfolio/) yields and allocation. Compare against peer mortgage insurers and monitor housing market indicators, interest rates, employment trends, and home price movements affecting demand and default rates.
