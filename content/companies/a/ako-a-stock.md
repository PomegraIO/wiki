*[ANDINA BOTTLING CO INC](/wiki/public-company/) ([AKO-A](/wiki/stock/)) is a major [beverage bottling and distribution](/wiki/consumer-staples/) company headquartered in Bogotá, Colombia, with extensive operations throughout South America's Andean region. The company manufactures and distributes carbonated soft drinks, bottled water, juices, teas, and related beverages, serving consumers across multiple countries through both company-owned and franchised bottling operations.*

## What the company does

Andina Bottling operates as a bottling and distribution company for major international and regional beverage brands throughout the Andean region of South America, primarily serving Colombia, Venezuela, Paraguay, and Ecuador. The company manufactures, bottles, and distributes a wide portfolio of products including carbonated soft drinks, purified and flavored water, juices, ready-to-drink teas, and sports and energy beverages. Its operations span multiple bottling plants, distribution networks, and retail channels—from large supermarkets to traditional small retailers and informal outlets across its markets.

The company handles both branded products through [franchise agreements](/wiki/franchise-model/) with major global beverage manufacturers and regional or private-label brands. This dual portfolio model has historically allowed the company to maintain scale while benefiting from established brand recognition and marketing support for its portfolio franchises.

## How it makes money

Andina Bottling generates revenue primarily from the sale of packaged beverages across all distribution channels. The company earns margins through the spread between input costs (raw materials, packaging, water, energy) and wholesale and retail selling prices. Like other bottlers in the [consumer staples sector](/wiki/consumer-staples/), its profitability depends on production efficiency, supply chain management, distribution costs, and pricing power relative to raw material inflation—particularly for aluminum, plastic, sugar, and energy.

The company also benefits from high-frequency repeat purchasing behavior characteristic of beverage consumption; volumes tend to correlate with regional economic conditions, population growth, and per-capita consumption trends. Seasonality affects beverage sales, with warm-weather months typically driving higher demand in equatorial and tropical markets.

## Where it sits in its industry

The global beverage bottling sector includes a mix of large multinational bottlers, regional specialists, and smaller local producers. Andina Bottling operates in the mid-to-large tier of the regional bottler category, with strong positioning in its core Andean markets. The industry has consolidated significantly over decades, with dominant global bottlers controlling large portions of capacity; Andina maintains relevance through deep regional presence, established relationships with franchisors, and efficient local operations.

Bottlers face structural pressures from shifting consumer preferences toward healthier beverages (reduced sugar, functional drinks), pressure to reduce plastic packaging, and rising input costs. Competition comes from larger multinational bottlers, regional competitors, and direct distribution by major beverage producers themselves. Tariffs, currency fluctuations, and regulatory changes in different jurisdictions add complexity to cross-border operations.
