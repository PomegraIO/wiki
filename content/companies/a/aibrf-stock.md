## What the company does

AIB Group is one of Ireland's principal banking institutions, serving customers across retail, commercial, and corporate segments. The bank operates primarily in Ireland and the UK, providing deposit-taking, lending, and payment services. Its customer base spans personal customers, small and medium-sized enterprises, and large corporates. The group's operations extend beyond traditional branch banking to include online banking, mortgages, business loans, and treasury services.

## How it makes money

The bank generates revenue principally through net interest income—the spread between rates paid on deposits and rates charged on loans. It also earns fees and commissions from lending, payment services, investment advisory, and insurance products. Trading and investment income contribute to overall profitability. Operating costs include staff expenses, technology infrastructure, regulatory compliance, and deposit insurance contributions.

## Capital structure and regulation

As a systemically important bank in the Irish and UK markets, AIB Group operates under strict regulatory oversight. The European Central Bank (ECB) and the Central Bank of Ireland set capital and liquidity requirements. The bank maintains a tier-one capital ratio exceeding regulatory minimums, funding itself through retail and wholesale deposits, debt issuances, and equity. The balance sheet reflects exposure to Irish and UK real estate lending, particularly mortgages, which historically has driven profitability and risk in the Irish banking sector.

## Industry position and history

AIB's roots trace to a 1939 merger; the modern group emerged following major restructuring during the 2008 financial crisis and subsequent eurozone debt turmoil, when the Irish government recapitalized the bank. The bank competed with Bank of Ireland and Permanent TSB in the Irish market. European interest-rate policy, housing demand, and commercial lending cycles directly influence earnings. Competition from digital-only banks and non-traditional lenders has shaped strategy in recent years.
