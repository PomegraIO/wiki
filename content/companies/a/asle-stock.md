*AerSale Corp*, listed as **ASLE**, is a US-listed public company operating in the aerospace and aviation supply chain. The company distributes aircraft parts, provides repair and maintenance services, and offers inventory management solutions to commercial airlines, military operators, and maintenance facilities worldwide.

## What the company does

AerSale operates across three primary segments: aircraft parts distribution, component repair and maintenance services (often called MRO—maintenance, repair, and overhaul), and aviation supply chain inventory solutions. The company sources, repairs, and distributes aircraft components ranging from engines and landing gear to smaller avionics and interior components. Its customer base includes major US and international airlines, regional carriers, military operators, and independent maintenance facilities.

The company's distribution network spans multiple facilities globally, positioning it as a middleman between original equipment manufacturers, used parts suppliers, and operators that need reliable access to aircraft components. This intermediary role gives AerSale exposure to both new-build aircraft deliveries (through original-equipment channels) and the larger installed fleet maintenance and upgrade market.

## How it makes money

AerSale generates revenue through three primary channels. Component distribution is the largest revenue stream, where the company earns margins on the sale of aircraft parts to airlines and maintenance facilities. The MRO division performs repair, overhaul, and modification services on components and systems, with revenue based on labor, parts, and technical expertise. The supply chain management and inventory solutions segment serves customers seeking consignment programs or inventory optimization services, where AerSale holds and manages spare parts on behalf of operators, earning management fees and volume discounts on component sales.

Revenue is heavily influenced by fleet utilization rates across the commercial aviation sector. Higher flight hours mean more maintenance demand and parts replacement cycles. Military spending on aircraft maintenance also contributes to the revenue base. The company benefits from long-term contracts with major operators that provide visibility and stability, though it also remains exposed to cyclical downturns in air travel demand.

## Where it sits in its industry

AerSale competes in a fragmented aerospace parts distribution and MRO ecosystem. Larger, more diversified aerospace suppliers like Boeing-subsidiary companies or major independent distributors operate at greater scale. Conversely, many competitors are regional or specialized in narrow niches (specific aircraft types or component categories). AerSale positions itself as a mid-market player with scale advantages over purely regional operators but greater focus and flexibility than massive conglomerates.

The company benefits from secular trends in aviation: aging aircraft fleets require more parts and maintenance; military aircraft modernization programs create sustained demand; and supply-chain consolidation favors established players with multiple facilities and technical depth. However, the industry is highly competitive on price, and any significant downturn in commercial aviation or defense budgets can pressure margins and aircraft flight hours.
