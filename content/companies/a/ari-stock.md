*[**Apollo Commercial Real Estate Finance, Inc.**](/wiki/ari-stock/) (ticker **ARI**) is a commercial real estate finance company and [mortgage REIT](/wiki/mortgage-reit/) that originates, acquires, and manages large loans secured by income-producing commercial properties. The company focuses on senior, floating-rate loans across major U.S. metropolitan markets.*

## What the company does

Apollo Commercial Real Estate Finance originates and manages large loans secured by commercial real estate. Its portfolio typically includes senior mortgage loans on office, retail, hospitality, industrial, and multifamily properties. The company focuses on floating-rate structures, which allows it to benefit when short-term interest rates rise. Loans are generally made to experienced sponsors and sponsors with significant equity in the properties, aiming to balance yield with credit quality.

The company's lending typically targets properties with strong fundamentals or value-add potential, often in gateway and secondary markets. Loan sizes generally exceed $10 million, with many deals in the $50 million to $200 million range. This large-loan focus reduces operating costs per dollar lent and appeals to institutional borrowers seeking efficiency.

## How it makes money

Apollo Commercial Real Estate Finance generates income primarily through interest received on its loan portfolio. As a mortgage REIT, the company is required to distribute at least 90% of taxable income to shareholders annually, which supports an equity dividend. Interest income depends on portfolio yield, which is influenced by both base rates (linked to SOFR, LIBOR, or other floating benchmarks) and loan-level spreads.

The company may also generate gains from sales of loans or securitizations, and may earn fees for loan origination, servicing, and management activities. Its floating-rate focus provides some protection against erosion of net interest margin if rates remain stable or decline, though rising rates may pressure borrower profitability on some assets.

## Where it sits in its industry

Apollo Commercial Real Estate Finance operates within the commercial mortgage REIT sector, alongside competitors including Blackstone Mortgage Trust, Gramco Corp, and others. Unlike residential mortgage REITs, which hold pools of smaller mortgages, commercial mortgage REITs typically hold larger individual loans with direct relationships to borrowers.

The company's focus on senior, floating-rate loans positions it toward the less-risky end of commercial real estate credit, relative to mezzanine or subordinated lenders. Its parent, Apollo Global Management, provides platform support, access to capital, and deal flow, though the REIT operates independently as a public company.

Commercial real estate lending cycles with property values and borrower cash flows. Loan portfolios face headwinds during property downturns and benefits during property appreciation and when rents rise. The sector also responds to interest rate policy; higher rates can compress borrower margins and pressure loan values, while they expand net interest spreads for floating-rate lenders.

## How to research it

Start with the company's quarterly 10-Q and annual [10-K](/wiki/10-k/) filings with the [SEC](/wiki/sec-filing/). These documents detail the loan portfolio composition by property type, geography, and loan structure; quarterly net interest income; loan loss provisions; and any securitizations or portfolio sales. The company's investor presentations often highlight portfolio trends and underwriting discipline.

Key metrics to examine include net interest margin, average loan yield, loan-loss ratios, and interest coverage ratios of the underlying borrowers. Pay attention to prepayment rates, as lower prepayments extend duration; watch for [credit](/wiki/credit-risk/) stress signals such as modified loans or increases in non-accruing assets.
