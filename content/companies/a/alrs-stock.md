*[ALERUS FINANCIAL CORP](/wiki/alrs-stock/) (ALRS) is a bank holding company that operates as a full-service community bank primarily serving the Upper Midwest. The company provides traditional [banking](/wiki/bank/) services including deposits, lending, and wealth management to individual and commercial customers.*

## What the company does

ALERUS operates primarily through its subsidiary bank, Alerus Financial Bank, providing comprehensive banking products to consumers and businesses. The company's service offerings span traditional retail deposit products (checking and savings accounts), consumer and commercial lending, as well as wealth and investment services. Geographic footprint is concentrated in the Upper Midwest, particularly North Dakota, Minnesota, and Wisconsin, where the bank maintains multiple branches and serves both rural and urban markets.

## How it makes money

The bank generates revenue primarily through net interest income—the margin between what it earns on loans and what it pays on deposits. Additional revenue streams include fee-based services such as wealth management, investment advisory services, and loan servicing fees. Like other community banks, the company benefits from customer relationships developed over time, with recurring deposit balances and loan portfolios providing stable, predictable income.

## Where it sits in its industry

ALERUS competes in the regional bank space, a segment characterized by smaller institutions serving defined geographic areas with personalized service. The company emphasizes agricultural and small-business lending, sectors where community banks have historically maintained competitive advantages over larger national institutions. Within the broader [financial services](/wiki/financial-services/) industry, regional banks typically focus on relationship banking and local market knowledge rather than competing primarily on price or scale.

## Capital and funding

As a [bank holding company](/wiki/bank-holding-company/), ALERUS maintains [capital](/wiki/capital-structure/) to support its subsidiary bank's lending activities and comply with regulatory requirements. The company accesses deposits from customers as a primary funding source, supplemented by borrowings in capital markets as needed. Regulatory capital ratios constrain growth and dividend capacity, making capital management a key strategic consideration.
