## What the company does

ADI Global Distribution acts as an intermediary in the technology supply chain, aggregating products from manufacturers and selling them to resellers, system integrators, and corporate end-users. The company provides computer hardware, peripherals, networking equipment, storage systems, software licenses, and related IT services. Beyond simple product distribution, ADIG offers value-added services: technical training, supply-chain financing, demand planning, and integration support. The company operates globally, with presence across multiple regions and customer segments.

## Market position and customer segments

Technology distribution is a mature, competitive industry where success depends on scale, relationships, and operational efficiency. ADIG serves multiple customer tiers: large corporate accounts that purchase IT infrastructure directly; system integrators and managed service providers who build customized solutions for end customers; and smaller resellers and retailers. The distributor must maintain relationships with major hardware and software manufacturers while also building loyalty among resellers. Margins are typically narrow, requiring efficient operations and high volume to achieve profitability.

## Distribution model and supply-chain role

The company purchases products from manufacturers at wholesale prices and resells them at higher margins to customers further down the supply chain. ADIG must forecast demand, manage inventory across multiple locations, and ensure product availability and delivery speed. As manufacturing has become increasingly global and supply chains complex, distributors play a critical role in aggregating product assortment, managing logistics, and providing local presence and support. The company also extends financing and credit to resellers, adding a financing function to distribution.

## Service and solutions expansion

Distributors like ADIG increasingly emphasize services and solutions to differentiate and defend margins against direct-manufacturer sales. Services include IT consulting, implementation support, training, cloud enablement, and cybersecurity solutions. These services create stickier customer relationships and generate higher-margin revenue than simple product distribution. The company may also invest in software platforms and tools that help customers manage procurement, inventory, or operations, creating recurring revenue streams and data insights.

## Competition and market pressures

Technology distribution faces structural challenges: direct sales by manufacturers, online retailers, and changing customer buying patterns reduce distributor margins and importance. Consolidation in the distribution industry has been significant. ADIG must continuously adapt to changing technology cycles, manage relationships with both powerful manufacturers and price-sensitive resellers, and invest in technology infrastructure to maintain competitiveness. Cybersecurity and supply-chain security have become important selling points and cost centers.
