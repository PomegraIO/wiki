## Community banking business model

Community banks operate with a fundamentally different approach than megabanks. They typically maintain a local or regional geographic focus, emphasize relationship-based banking, and participate actively in decision-making within their communities. AFBI serves its customer base through branch banking, providing deposit products, lending services, and other financial services.

## Retail and commercial banking services

The bank's retail banking division serves individuals and families, offering deposit accounts, mortgages, personal loans, and investment services. The commercial banking segment serves small and medium-sized business customers with business loans, lines of credit, cash management services, and treasury products. Revenue derives primarily from net interest income (the spread between interest earned on loans and interest paid on deposits) and fee income from services.

## Competitive position

Community banks compete with large national banks, other regional banks, and increasingly, fintech and online-only banking platforms. Advantages for community banks include local market knowledge, personalized service, and faster decision-making. Challenges include scale limitations, higher cost-to-income ratios, and difficulty competing on price with larger institutions.

## Key business drivers

Bank profitability is influenced by interest rates, loan demand, credit quality, deposit stability, and operating efficiency. Rising interest rates typically improve net interest margins, while economic downturns increase credit losses. Community banks must maintain adequate capital levels to support lending and absorb potential losses, regulated by federal banking authorities.
