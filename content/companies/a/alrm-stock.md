*[Alarm.com Holdings, Inc.](/wiki/public-company/) (**ALRM**) is a cloud-based software and services provider that delivers security systems, monitoring, video surveillance, and connected home automation solutions for residential and commercial properties. The company operates in the security technology and Internet of Things space, serving both end consumers and service providers.*

## What the company does

Alarm.com provides a cloud-based platform that connects home and business security systems to monitoring centers, end users, and third-party services. The company operates a Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) ecosystem that integrates smart home devices, security cameras, and alarm systems. Its platform enables authorized service providers—such as security companies and telecommunications providers—to offer managed services to their customers, as well as direct-to-consumer offerings.

The platform supports video surveillance, access control, environmental sensors, and mobile applications that allow property owners to monitor and control their systems remotely. By serving as an intermediary platform, Alarm.com connects hardware manufacturers, service providers, and end customers in a networked ecosystem.

## How it makes money

Alarm.com generates revenue primarily through recurring subscription services. Service providers pay platform fees to use Alarm.com's cloud infrastructure and software to deliver security and smart home services to their customers. The company also earns revenue from professional monitoring services and from licensing its technology to other firms.

The subscription model creates predictable, recurring revenue streams tied to the number of active accounts on its platform. Additional revenue comes from professional installation services, equipment sales, and advanced features or add-on services.

## Where it sits in its industry

The residential and commercial security industry spans both traditional alarm companies and newer technology-focused players. Alarm.com operates in the software and connectivity layer, positioning itself as a platform provider rather than a traditional alarm monitoring company. This allows the company to work with multiple service provider partners, multiplying its reach without directly operating monitoring centers.

The company faces competition from both established security firms and newer IoT platforms, but its focus on providing cloud infrastructure and software has allowed it to maintain a platform position that benefits from scale. The connected home and smart building segments represent areas of growth as automation and remote management become more prevalent in the industry.
