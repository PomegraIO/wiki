The Abacus FCF Real Assets Leaders ETF (ticker: ABLD) is a concentrated, actively managed fund designed to capture durable cash-generating businesses in real assets — a sector definition spanning energy, utilities, metals, infrastructure, and other capital-intensive industries where physical assets are central to competitive advantage. Rather than track a passive index, ABLD's manager selects securities based on free cash flow strength and quality criteria, aiming to identify companies capable of returning cash to shareholders through dividends and buybacks while maintaining competitive positions.

## The Real Assets Opportunity

Real assets businesses occupy a distinctive economic moat. They control scarce resources — oil and gas reserves, pipeline networks, mineral deposits, railway infrastructure — that cannot be easily replicated or displaced. These companies typically operate in mature, stable markets where pricing power is limited by competition or regulation, so their competitive edge rests on cost discipline and operational efficiency rather than brand or innovation. That makes free cash flow generation the primary measure of quality and long-term value creation.

The sector includes pure-play commodity producers, integrated energy majors, midstream logistics operators, metals miners, utilities, and real estate investment trusts. All share a characteristic: they tend to be capital-intensive upfront, then generate substantial recurring cash flows once assets are constructed or discovered and brought online. During periods of commodity strength or tight supply, these companies can generate exceptional returns, though they also face secular pressures — decarbonisation policy, energy transition, and shifting end-market demand — that create genuine risk for any long-term holder.

## Fund Structure and Strategy

ABLD is an actively managed ETF, meaning the fund manager makes security selection decisions rather than replicating a published index passively. The fund typically holds 20 to 40 positions, concentrating holdings in the most conviction-weighted ideas. The selection process emphasises free cash flow yield, balance-sheet strength, and the durability of competitive advantages, with a preference for companies that have demonstrated multi-year capacity to generate cash and return it to shareholders.

The fund trades on the Nasdaq exchange like a stock, providing daily liquidity and low trading costs compared to traditional mutual funds, whilst offering active management oversight. The expense ratio, typically in the range of 0.70 per cent annually, reflects the costs of active research and trading, higher than pure index ETFs but competitive for actively managed products in the category.

## Risks Specific to the Strategy

Commodity and real assets businesses face cyclical earnings and cash flows tied to global economic conditions, energy prices, and metal markets. A prolonged downturn in energy prices or commodity demand can compress cash generation and force dividend cuts — a material risk for income-focused holders. The concentration of holdings (20–40 names) creates stock-specific risk; any single major position that faces operational failure or capital misallocation can meaningfully hurt returns.

Regulatory and energy transition risks present a longer-term pressure. Policy favouring renewable energy, carbon pricing, methane regulation, and electrification of transport all reduce the end-market demand for fossil fuels and create uncertainty around stranded assets. A company that generates strong free cash flow today may face secular headwinds that gradually erode that advantage over a decade. Finally, the fund's active management introduces the risk of manager misjudgement — poor security selection or sector timing can underperform a passive alternative.

## Who This Fund Is For

ABLD appeals to investors seeking income and capital stability rather than growth. It suits holders who believe real assets and commodity producers offer compelling value at given price levels, or those seeking diversification away from equities and bonds into alternative asset categories. The concentration and active mandate mean it is best suited to investors with time horizons of five years or longer and tolerance for volatility. It is less appropriate for those unable to tolerate periods of weak cash flows or dividend cuts tied to commodity price cycles.

## How to Research This Fund

The fund's prospectus and most recent factsheet lay out the objectives, holdings, and expense structure. The investment strategy — the criteria the manager uses to select holdings — appears in fund materials and SEC filings. Holdings changes can be monitored via the fund's website, and reviewing the top ten positions gives a sense of the portfolio's tilt toward energy, metals, or other segments within real assets. Any prospective holder should read the risk factors carefully, paying close attention to commodity price exposure and regulatory or energy-transition headwinds the largest holdings face.
