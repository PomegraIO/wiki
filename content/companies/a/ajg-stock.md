*[**Arthur J. Gallagher & Co.**](/wiki/public-company/) (**AJG**) is one of the largest insurance brokerage and risk management services providers in the United States, operating across commercial, industrial, and public sector markets. The firm bridges clients and insurers, providing comprehensive coverage solutions and advisory services.*

## What the company does

Arthur J. Gallagher operates as a full-service insurance brokerage, helping businesses, organizations, and individuals secure appropriate coverage for property, liability, workers' compensation, and specialized risks. The firm combines retail brokerage services—where agents work directly with clients—with wholesale operations that connect brokers to hard-to-place policies. Beyond placing insurance, Gallagher provides risk consulting, loss control advice, and employee benefits administration, positioning itself across the value chain of enterprise risk management.

## How it makes money

The brokerage earns commissions from insurers when placing policies on behalf of clients. These commissions typically represent a percentage of the [insurance premium](/wiki/stock-exchange/). The firm also charges fees for advisory and consulting services, particularly in benefits administration and risk management. A portion of revenue comes from what Gallagher calls "risk management and consulting" fees—direct charges to clients for customized risk assessments and mitigation strategies. This diversified revenue model reduces dependence on commission-driven business alone.

## Scale and market position

Gallagher ranks among the largest [publicly traded](/wiki/public-company/) insurance brokers globally, competing with peers like Aon and Marsh. The company operates through multiple divisions: risk management services, employee benefits, and acquisition-integrated brokerage operations. Its scale provides leverage in negotiations with insurance carriers while its geographic and vertical diversification—spanning healthcare, construction, professional services, and public entity clients—mitigates concentration risk. The firm has grown substantially through acquisitions, systematically acquiring regional and specialist brokers to expand capabilities and geographic reach.

## Industry context

Insurance brokerage is a mature, relationship-intensive business where sustained growth depends on acquiring and retaining clients, entering new markets, and offering specialized expertise. As organizations face increasingly complex risk environments—from cyber liability to supply-chain disruptions—demand for sophisticated risk consulting has risen. Consolidation in the industry continues as larger brokers acquire smaller, regional competitors to achieve scale and cross-selling opportunities. [Commercial real estate](/wiki/commercial-real-estate/) conditions and corporate risk appetites directly influence client demand for brokerage services.

## How to research it

Gallagher files annual [10-K](/wiki/10-k/) reports and quarterly 10-Q filings with the [SEC](/wiki/10-k/), detailing operations by segment, revenue composition, acquisition activity, and client concentration. These filings clarify the mix of commission versus fee-based revenue, the dollar value of organic growth versus acquisitions, and operating leverage in the business. Investor presentations and earnings calls provide management commentary on market conditions, client retention, and strategic priorities. Industry reports from insurance trade associations and competitive analyses of peer brokers help contextualize Gallagher's performance within the broader market.
