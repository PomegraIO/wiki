*An **American Century Small Cap Growth Insights ETF** (ACSG) is an exchange-traded fund that gives investors access to a portfolio of smaller U.S. companies selected for growth potential. The fund tracks an index of small-cap stocks meeting growth criteria, with the manager applying active screening to select firms positioned for above-market earnings expansion.*

The fund sits in the small-cap growth segment of the U.S. equity market — companies with market capitalisation typically between $300 million and $2 billion, chosen for characteristics suggesting future revenue and earnings growth rather than current [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/) or defensive stability. Small-cap stocks are more volatile and less liquid than large-cap peers, and single-company risk is higher, but the growth payoff can be substantial over full [market cycles](/market-cycle/).

## What the fund holds and how it works

ACSG holds a concentrated basket of small-cap growth companies across sectors — technology, industrials, healthcare, consumer discretionary, and financials. The exact holdings shift as the index it tracks is rebalanced and as constituents grow out of or decline into the small-cap range. The fund is a plain ETF, not leveraged or inverse, and settles daily on the primary U.S. [stock exchanges](/stock-exchange/) where it trades.

Like all [actively managed funds](/actively-managed-fund/), ACSG charges an [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) that covers the cost of portfolio management and screening. The fund is liquid during trading hours, but the underlying stocks are less actively traded than mega-cap names, so [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) can be wider — a consideration for large positions or investors trading frequently.

## Risk and tracking

Small-cap growth stocks carry three overlapping risks worth separating. First is volatility: smaller companies with accelerating earnings see bigger price swings on earnings surprises, competitive shocks, or macroeconomic slowdowns. Second is concentration: the portfolio is smaller than a broad [index fund](/index-fund/), so individual names can move the needle. Third is liquidity: some small-cap stocks trade in lower volume, which can make positions harder to enter or exit without slippage.

The fund's prospectus discloses how closely it is expected to track its underlying index. Tracking error — the gap between fund returns and the index — arises from fees, trading costs, and the manager's selection decisions. For a growth-focused fund with active management, modest tracking error is typical and expected.

## Who ACSG is for and how to research it

ACSG suits investors seeking concentrated exposure to U.S. small-cap growth and willing to tolerate higher volatility than a broad market index in exchange for potential long-term appreciation. It is often used as a satellite position within a larger, more diversified portfolio — not as a core holding on its own.

To evaluate the fund, start with its prospectus and fact sheet from the issuer, American Century Investments. These disclose the index it tracks, its expense ratio, redemption fees (if any), and the manager's screening criteria. Compare the fund's [price-to-earnings ratio](/price-to-earnings-ratio/), sector breakdown, and five-year return (if available) against other small-cap [growth ETFs](/growth-etf/). Monitor concentration: how much is held in the top 10 stocks, and whether the manager is leaning heavily into particular sectors. Lastly, check the fund's trading volume and bid-ask spread — important if you plan to trade in and out frequently.
