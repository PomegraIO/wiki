## Who actually runs Andersen Group?

Andersen Group operates as a global professional services firm with roots tracing back to 2002. The company built itself on the heritage of the Arthur Andersen legacy, positioning itself as a successor firm in the tax and advisory space. Today, Andersen maintains a decentralized network of offices across multiple continents, including recent expansions into Ireland, New Zealand, Nigeria, and Uruguay through strategic [acquisitions](/acquisition/). This structure reflects a commitment to delivering region-specific expertise while maintaining unified service standards across its client base.

## What services does the firm provide?

The core business centers on three interconnected pillars: tax advisory, valuation services, and financial consulting. Andersen helps its clients—ranging from individuals and family offices to mid-market enterprises and institutional investors—navigate complex tax structures, optimize capital allocation, and structure deals effectively. Their tax teams work on everything from straightforward compliance to aggressive cross-border planning. Valuation work supports M&A transactions, estate planning, and financial reporting. The firm positions tax expertise not as a cost center but as an economic tool that unlocks value for clients.

## Where do clients come from?

Andersen serves a deliberately broad market. On one end are ultra-high-net-worth individuals and family offices seeking sophisticated tax and wealth management strategies. On the other are corporate clients running deal-heavy operations who need real-time valuation and tax analysis. The institutional side includes funds, investors, and portfolio companies seeking advisory support. This diversified client mix reduces dependency on any single sector or deal type, though the firm naturally concentrates where complexity commands premium fees—private equity, cross-border transactions, and multi-jurisdictional structures.

## How does a firm like this compete?

Professional services competition in this tier comes down to access, reputation, and specialized expertise. Andersen competes against both the Big Four accounting firms and a long tail of boutique advisories by positioning itself as nimble and specialist-driven. The recent acquisitions suggest a strategy of geographic expansion—building local presence in emerging markets where demand for high-end tax and advisory services is growing. The firm leverages its Arthur Andersen heritage for credibility while shedding the baggage of that firm's collapse, instead marketing itself as a fresh institution built on proven frameworks. Client switching costs in this business are high, so retention and organic growth matter alongside business development.
