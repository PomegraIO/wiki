## What is an enhanced-market strategy?

Anfield Enhanced Market ETF applies systematic option-writing techniques to a portfolio of U.S. stocks in order to generate extra income that increases total return. Rather than holding stocks passively and collecting only dividends, the fund writes call options against its equity holdings—a practice known as "covered call writing." When the fund sells a call option, it receives a premium from the buyer, boosting current income. The trade-off is that if the underlying stock rises sharply, the fund's shares may be called away, capping appreciation. The strategy aims to enhance returns in flat to moderately rising markets while protecting some downside through the income cushion the premiums provide.

## How does the option income work?

When AEMS sells call options on the stocks it holds, it collects a premium from option buyers who are betting the stocks will rise. That premium is income the fund keeps regardless of what happens next. If the stock stays below the strike price of the call, the option expires worthless, the call is called off, and the fund retains both the stock and the premium. If the stock rises above the strike, the fund's shares get called away at the strike price—a level the sponsor chooses strategically to balance income collection against the risk of losing upside. The premium collected in each rolling cycle adds to the fund's yield above what dividends alone would provide.

The strike prices are typically set out of the money—above the stock's current trading level—so there is a cushion before shares get called away. Over time, the rolling cycles of selling, collecting premiums, and rolling to the next position are designed to capture returns from underlying stock appreciation plus option premium income. In sideways or mildly rising markets, this approach can outperform simple buy-and-hold; in sharply rising markets, capped appreciation becomes a drag.

## What are the risks of writing calls?

The core risk is opportunity cost. By selling upside potential in exchange for premium income, the fund gives up the full benefit if a held stock rallies sharply. This creates a ceiling on gains in bull markets, which is the explicit cost of the enhanced-income approach. In a prolonged, steep rally, the fund would underperform a simple broad-market fund that held the same stocks unhedged.

A second risk is that early assignment can force the sale of stocks the fund wanted to hold longer, disrupting the intended portfolio composition and potentially triggering unintended tax consequences for shareholders. The fund also faces operational complexity: managing a portfolio with rolling option positions requires active trading, monitoring strike-price selections, and handling assignments, all of which add overhead.

Option writing also introduces volatility asymmetry. In a falling market, the fund still owns the stocks and suffers the downside, while the option premiums collected provide only partial cushion. The enhanced-income strategy is designed for neutral to moderately positive markets, not as a downside hedge.

## Who should consider this fund?

AEMS appeals to investors seeking current income from their equity exposure and who are comfortable trading some upside potential for enhanced yield. It suits portfolios designed to generate cash distributions for retirees or income-focused investors who believe equity markets will move sideways or modestly upward rather than surge. It is less appropriate for growth-oriented investors with long time horizons who want to participate fully in major market rallies, or for those uncomfortable with the complexity of options.

## How to understand the fund's performance

Review the fund's fact sheet and prospectus for its rules around strike selection, how frequently it rolls positions, and what percentage of its return historically comes from option premiums versus underlying stock appreciation. Compare its yield to simple dividend-paying index funds and evaluate whether the enhanced income is worth the capped upside and added complexity. Monitor the fund's holdings to ensure they align with your view of U.S. equity valuations, and track performance relative to a broad-market benchmark such as the S&P 500 to assess whether the strategy is delivering on its promise of enhanced returns over a full market cycle.
