*Aircastle LTD (ticker **AYR**) is an aircraft lessor that owns and leases commercial aircraft to airlines worldwide. Based in the aviation finance sector, it operates as a specialized asset-based company focused on long-term equipment financing.*

## What the company does

Aircastle owns commercial aircraft and leases them to airlines on a long-term basis. The company typically acquires aircraft from manufacturers or the secondary market, then enters into leases with operators ranging from major carriers to regional airlines. This business model allows Aircastle to generate stable lease revenues while airlines avoid capital expenditure on aircraft purchases.

The company manages a diverse portfolio of narrow-body and wide-body aircraft across multiple aircraft types and manufacturers. Leases generally run for periods of 8 to 15 years, with options for renewal or purchase at the end of the lease term. Aircastle may also perform maintenance and modification work on its fleet between lessee transitions.

## How it makes money

The primary revenue stream comes from lease payments made by airline operators. These payments are structured as fixed monthly or quarterly amounts over the lease term. By leasing rather than selling, Aircastle captures the aircraft's residual value at lease expiration—either by re-leasing it to another carrier, selling it on the secondary market, or managing the end-of-life transition.

Interest income on aircraft financing arrangements and fee-based services (such as lease management and technical consulting) provide secondary revenue sources. The company's financial stability depends on its lessees' ability to pay and on the residual value of aircraft when leases terminate.

## Where it sits in its industry

Aircraft leasing is a capital-intensive, specialized sector within commercial aviation. Major competitors include larger, publicly traded lessors and private equity–backed firms. Aircastle competes on factors such as fleet quality, lease pricing, operational efficiency, and financial strength. The industry is concentrated among large players with substantial asset bases, making it a market where balance-sheet strength and access to capital markets matter significantly.

Demand for aircraft leases fluctuates with airline profitability and capacity expansion plans. The sector is also exposed to long-term trends in air travel, fuel costs, and regulatory changes affecting aviation. Aircastle's position in this market depends on maintaining a competitive fleet, managing credit risk with lessees, and optimizing returns on its asset base.
