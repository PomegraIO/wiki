**Atkore manufactures the unglamorous backbone of modern construction and electrical systems—conduit, cable trays, metal framing, and piping that contractors install inside buildings, across power grids, and through data centers.** The company holds leadership positions in most of its product categories across North America and beyond, treating its role as a mission-critical supplier rather than a glamour-stock play.

The business splits into two main operating wings. The Electrical segment produces electrical conduit, cable and cable management systems, and installation accessories—essentially the plumbing and wiring channels that make power and data travel safely through buildings and infrastructure. The Safety & Infrastructure segment designs metal framing, mechanical piping, perimeter security products, and related systems. Together, these address six core markets: non-residential construction, electric power transmission and distribution, data centers and telecommunications, water infrastructure, transportation, and solar energy installations.

Atkore's end-markets are cyclical and tied to capital spending, construction cycles, and infrastructure investment. When developers and utilities spend, demand for the company's products tends to follow. The company distributes primarily through electrical and infrastructure wholesalers and directly to contractors and engineers, meaning it operates behind the scenes rather than in consumer sight. Its earnings depend on volume, commodity steel prices, manufacturing efficiency, and the ability to pass cost [inflation](/inflation/) to customers—dynamics that swing with economic activity and raw-material cycles.

Founded in 1959, the company has evolved through [acquisitions](/acquisition/), [divestitures](/divestiture/), and [business combinations](/business-combination-purchase/) over decades, including the transition that brought it public as part of infrastructure-focused consolidation. Atkore's position as a top-two supplier in most of its product niches reflects the accumulated brand, distribution reach, and operational scale that make it difficult for smaller competitors to displace, though the business remains exposed to downturns in construction and capital-spending cycles, input-cost volatility, and price pressure from customers and substitutes.
