*[**ARB IOT Group Ltd**](/wiki/public-company/) (**ARBB**) is a technology company focused on [Internet of Things](/wiki/internet-of-things/) connectivity solutions and smart device platforms for enterprise customers and consumers. The company operates in the rapidly evolving IoT sector, competing in markets that span industrial automation, smart home systems, and connected devices across multiple vertical industries.*

## What the company does

ARB IOT Group develops hardware, firmware, and cloud-based software platforms that enable devices to connect, communicate, and exchange data over cellular, wireless, and broadband networks. The company's technology stack spans connectivity modules, edge computing devices, and cloud management systems. Its customers include original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), system integrators, and enterprise IT buyers in sectors such as manufacturing, logistics, utilities, and smart buildings. The company also operates direct-to-consumer product lines in the connected device and smart home space.

## How it makes money

The company generates revenue through multiple channels. Hardware sales represent a significant portion, as customers purchase IoT modules, gateways, and edge devices. Software and cloud services generate recurring subscription revenue from customers who rely on ARB's management platforms and analytics tools. The company also earns licensing fees from partners who integrate its connectivity technology into their own products. Volume discounts and large enterprise contracts drive scaling in its OEM channel, while direct sales support higher-margin consumer products.

## Where it sits in its industry

ARB competes in a fragmented IoT ecosystem alongside larger incumbents in semiconductor and networking (Qualcomm, Sierra Wireless, Cisco), specialized IoT platforms (Telit, Hologram), and regional players. The company's competitive advantage rests on its vertical integration—combining hardware, firmware, and cloud software—and its ability to support both short-cycle consumer products and long-cycle industrial deployments. Shifts toward edge computing, 5G adoption, and enterprise digital transformation create tailwinds; however, the sector remains competitive on price and vulnerable to disruption from cloud hyperscalers entering the IoT market.
