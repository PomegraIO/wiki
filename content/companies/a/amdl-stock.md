## What is AMDL and what does it track?

GraniteShares 2x Long AMD Daily ETF (AMDL) is a leveraged exchange-traded fund that aims to deliver twice the daily performance of [Advanced Micro Devices](/amd-stock/) Inc. stock. The fund is issued and managed by GraniteShares, a New York-based investment firm specializing in leveraged and [inverse ETFs](/inverse-etf/). AMDL trades on [NASDAQ](/nasdaq/) under ticker AMDL, allowing investors to buy and sell shares during market hours like any publicly traded fund. The fund's objective is to amplify daily moves in AMD stock by a factor of two.

## How does AMDL achieve 2X leverage?

AMDL's portfolio manager uses margin borrowing and derivatives—primarily total-return swaps and AMD share purchases on leverage—to create exposure approximately twice the fund's assets. When AMD stock rises 1%, AMDL targets a 2% gain. When AMD falls 1%, AMDL aims for a 2% loss. The fund rebalances daily, adjusting positions so leverage resets precisely to 2X. This daily reset is the defining feature and explains why it is designed exclusively for short-term tactical trading.

## What is volatility decay and why does it matter?

The daily reset mechanism creates a mathematical consequence called volatility decay. In volatile markets, AMDL's returns over periods longer than one day lag simple 2X arithmetic. If AMD bounces up 10%, then down 9%, the underlying stock is down roughly 1%. But AMDL gained roughly 20% on day one and lost roughly 18% on day two. An 18% loss applied to a base already up 20% leaves the fund down overall. This drag is automatic and baked into structure; it is unavoidable because it is a mathematical property of daily rebalancing.

## Who is GraniteShares and what is the fund's structure?

GraniteShares is a boutique firm focused on leveraged, inverse, and [thematic ETFs](/thematic-etf/), operating under SEC oversight. AMDL is registered as a diversified fund though it holds leveraged exposure to a single stock. It operates like any ETF: buying and selling on NASDAQ during market hours with [net asset value](/net-asset-value/) calculated and published daily. [Authorized participants](/authorized-participant/) create and redeem shares to keep market price aligned with underlying value.

## What does AMDL cost?

The fund charges an annual [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) covering management, administration, and maintaining leveraged positions through derivatives and margin. [Leveraged ETFs](/leveraged-etf/) typically charge 0.5% to 1.0% or more annually, significantly higher than passive index trackers, because daily rebalancing and derivative positions require continuous active management and incur financing costs.

## What are the real risks in holding AMDL?

Volatility decay is the first and most important risk. AMDL is mathematically destined to underperform simple 2X arithmetic in choppy or oscillating markets. If AMD stock trends sharply in one direction with minimal retracement, decay is less concerning. But sideways or reversing price action means AMDL bleeds value.

Leverage itself is the second risk. A 10% decline in AMD produces a 20% loss in AMDL. A 20% decline produces a 40% loss. If AMD crashes, AMDL loses half or more of its value quickly.

Liquidity and [bid-ask spreads](/bid-ask-spread/) are the third risk. AMDL is a narrow, single-stock leveraged product with smaller asset base and trading volume than broader funds. Traders buying or selling large positions encounter wider spreads, acting as hidden transaction cost.

## Who should and should not own AMDL?

AMDL is designed for professional or sophisticated traders making short-term bets on AMD over days or a few weeks. It is absolutely unsuitable for retirement accounts, long-term portfolios, or anyone intending to hold for months or years. It is equally unsuitable as hedge for longer-term positions because volatility decay erodes hedge value. Before buying, an investor needs clear entry point, exit plan, position size, and understanding of maximum loss they can sustain.

## How to evaluate and research AMDL

The fund's prospectus and fact sheet from GraniteShares and SEC's Edgar database disclose exact rebalancing methodology, fees, and performance objectives. Investors should examine AMDL's daily performance against AMD daily moves to verify tracking. They should study volatility decay examples by comparing AMDL's longer-term returns to simple 2X calculations of AMD's moves. The key question is whether you expect AMD to trend higher with minimal retracement, and whether you are comfortable risking 2X leverage over a short timeframe.
