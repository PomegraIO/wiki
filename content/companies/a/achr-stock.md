*[Archer Aviation Inc.](/wiki/public-company/) (ticker **ACHR**) develops electric vertical takeoff and landing ([eVTOL](/wiki/evtol/)) aircraft for urban air mobility. The company is designing piloted aircraft intended for short-distance, on-demand air transportation in congested metropolitan areas.*

## What the company does

Archer designs and develops electric vertical takeoff and landing aircraft, commonly known as eVTOLs. The company's primary focus is building aircraft for urban air mobility (UAM)—short-distance trips within or between cities by air. Unlike traditional helicopters, eVTOL aircraft are designed to use electric propulsion, reducing noise and operating costs. Archer is working toward certification and production of piloted passenger aircraft intended to operate from distributed vertiports (landing pads) in urban environments.

## How it makes money

Archer's revenue model depends on future aircraft production and sales. The company plans to earn revenue through selling eVTOL aircraft to operators and potentially from licensing technology. In early stages, the company generates limited revenue and operates at significant losses, typical of pre-commercial aerospace companies. Future revenue would come from aircraft sales, maintenance services, and partnerships with operators or aviation companies. The company has announced partnerships with existing aviation operators interested in acquiring its aircraft.

## Where it sits in its industry

Archer competes in the nascent but crowded eVTOL market, alongside dozens of other startups and aerospace incumbents developing similar aircraft. The company faces challenges including regulatory approval (which remains incomplete in most jurisdictions), manufacturing scale-up, pilot training infrastructure, and market demand validation. Success requires navigating Federal Aviation Administration certification, building supply chains, and creating a viable market for commercial operations. Traditional aerospace companies and aerospace-adjacent technology firms also compete for market share in urban air mobility.
