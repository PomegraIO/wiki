*[Aviat Networks, Inc.](/wiki/avnw-stock/) is a [public company](/wiki/public-company/) headquartered in San Jose, California, specializing in wireless backhaul technology and microwave transmission systems. The company provides critical connectivity infrastructure to telecommunications carriers, broadcasters, government agencies, and enterprises worldwide, serving as a core supplier in mobile and fixed wireless networks.*

## What the company does

Aviat Networks manufactures wireless transmission and backhaul solutions that carry voice, data, and video traffic across telecom networks. Its product portfolio includes point-to-point microwave radios, hybrid terrestrial-satellite systems, and software-defined networking platforms. The company's technology fills a critical gap in network infrastructure: it bridges large geographic distances where fiber deployment is impractical, uneconomical, or physically impossible. This makes it essential for cellular towers, remote broadcast centers, emergency response networks, and maritime communications.

## How it makes money

The company generates revenue through three primary channels: product sales of microwave and wireless equipment; software subscriptions and licensing; and services including installation, integration, and support. Enterprise customers typically purchase equipment upfront and maintain ongoing service contracts for monitoring and maintenance. Telecom carriers, the largest customer segment, often procure in volumes tied to network expansion cycles and technology refresh initiatives. Government and defense contracts add diversification but operate on different acquisition timelines and margin profiles.

## Where it sits in its industry

Aviat Networks competes in the specialized microwave backhaul and wireless transmission market, where customers value reliability, spectrum efficiency, and integration with existing network infrastructure. The company has deep roots in telecommunications equipment design and holds relevant [patents](/wiki/patent-claims/) in wireless transmission technology. Its customer base spans mature markets like North America and Europe as well as high-growth regions in Asia-Pacific and Africa where fiber infrastructure lags behind wireless expansion. Competition comes from specialized equipment manufacturers and occasional in-house development by the largest carriers, but the technical barriers to entry and established customer relationships provide defensibility.
