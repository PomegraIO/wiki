The Avantis Short-Term Fixed [Income ETF](/income-etf/) (ticker: AVSF) is an exchange-traded fund that holds high-quality short-term debt — primarily investment-grade [corporate bonds](/corporate-bond/) and government securities with maturities between one and four years. Like all short-duration [bond](/bond/) funds, AVSF aims to deliver the yield that bonds provide while minimizing the price swings that come from changes in [interest rates](/interest-rate/).

## How short-duration bonds protect and constrain returns

The fund's appeal lies in the mathematics of bond pricing. When interest rates rise, bond prices fall — and the longer a bond's maturity, the more its price drops. A ten-year bond might lose 10% in value if rates jump 100 basis points; a two-year bond might lose only 2%. By confining itself to bonds that mature in one to four years, AVSF trades away the higher yields that come from taking longer-term [interest-rate risk](/interest-rate-risk/). The payoff is reduced volatility and a portfolio that can more gracefully absorb the effects of rate changes.

This positioning makes AVSF useful as a core holding for bond allocations in rising-rate environments, where longer-duration funds suffer larger drawdowns. It also appeals to investors who prefer not to forecast interest rates — the shorter the maturity, the faster a fund's holdings roll over and refresh their yields without requiring a bet on where rates are headed.

The trade-off is straightforward: lower interest-rate risk comes at the cost of lower yield. In periods when longer bonds are in favour and rates are steady or falling, longer-duration funds will often outperform AVSF. The fund does not protect against [credit risk](/credit-risk/) — the chance that an issuer defaults — though its focus on [investment-grade bonds](/investment-grade-bond/) (BBB and above) keeps credit losses typically low relative to broader bond markets.

## Holdings and composition

AVSF builds its portfolio from three primary sources. It holds U.S. Treasury and agency securities (debt backed directly by the U.S. government or issued by government-sponsored enterprises like [Freddie Mac](/freddie-mac/)). It holds investment-grade corporate bonds — debt issued by stable, creditworthy companies across sectors including utilities, technology, industrials, and financials. And it includes smaller positions in mortgage-backed securities and other shorter-maturity fixed-income instruments.

The fund's weighting taps a factor-tilted approach: American Century's portfolio managers apply screening to favour bonds that exhibit superior value characteristics — in effect, selecting those trading at wider spreads or with other attributes that history suggests offer better returns relative to their risk. This factor layer is what distinguishes AVSF from a purely passive short-term bond [index fund](/index-fund/); the managers are not trying to beat the market through timing, but rather to capture a persistent value premium within the universe of short-term, high-quality bonds.

The portfolio turns over steadily as bonds mature and are replaced. This turnover is considerably lower than it would be in a longer-duration fund, since bonds spend less time in the portfolio before reaching maturity and being paid off.

## Costs and structure

AVSF is a genuine ETF — not a note or a structured product — meaning it holds actual bonds, trades on an exchange like a stock, and can be bought and sold at market prices throughout the trading day. The [expense ratio](/expense-ratio/) is modest, in the range of 0.25% annually, reflecting the fund's factor-tilting methodology and the cost of managing the bond holdings. That figure is low by active-management standards and moderately low compared to the cheapest passive short-term bond index funds.

The fund's liquidity is strong: AVSF trades millions of shares daily, and the [bid-ask spread](/bid-ask-spread/) (the cost you incur when buying or selling shares) is typically tight. For most investors, buying and selling AVSF is straightforward and cheap relative to purchasing individual bonds.

## Who AVSF is for and how to research it

AVSF suits investors seeking bond exposure with minimal interest-rate volatility — those who want a stable anchor in a portfolio, or who believe rates may rise further and wish to avoid the largest drawdowns. It is often used as a defensive holding or as a component of a broader [bond ladder](/bond-ladder/) when an investor wants short-duration bonds but prefers the flexibility and transparency of an ETF over individual bond holdings.

Anyone researching AVSF should consult the fund's prospectus and fact sheet, available from American Century's website, which detail the exact holdings, the fee structure, and the fund's tracking methodology. The fund's performance relative to short-term bond benchmarks (such as the Bloomberg 1–3 Year U.S. Aggregate Bond Index) reveals whether the factor tilt is adding value or if fees are eroding it. Monitoring changes in the fund's duration, average maturity, and credit quality indicators shows how the portfolio's risk characteristics are shifting. Because AVSF's job is to deliver steady bond income with minimal drama, watching for sudden increases in holdings of lower-grade credits or very long positions in any single issuer is prudent due diligence for long-term holders.
