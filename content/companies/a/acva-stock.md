**ACV Auctions operates a digital marketplace for wholesale used vehicles, connecting auto dealers and buyers with inventory ranging from salvage to near-new cars.** The company has built a platform that extends far beyond simple bidding—it encompasses vehicle inspection, transportation coordination, financing facilitation, and dealer-to-dealer sales networks. At its core, ACV Auctions addresses a fragmented $200+ billion wholesale automotive market where traditional physical auctions dominated for decades before digital alternatives began to reshape the landscape.

The business works by aggregating vehicle inventory from multiple sources: lease returns, off-lease programs, trade-ins from dealerships, rental car fleets, and insurance companies managing salvage vehicles. These vehicles are photographed, inspected, and listed on ACV's platform where thousands of registered dealers and other buyers bid in real-time auctions lasting minutes to hours. The company operates its own logistics subsidiary to handle transportation, which adds margin and sticky customer relationships. Beyond pure auction commissions—typically a percentage of final sale price—ACV generates recurring revenue from dealer subscriptions, transportation services, and a financing marketplace that helps dealers purchase inventory.

The company's growth trajectory reflects broader consolidation in automotive wholesale. Physical auction houses like Manheim and IAAI operated regionally and required dealers to attend in person. ACV's digital-first model eliminates that friction, allowing a dealer in rural Texas to bid on cars across the country from their office. This has enabled the platform to gain density quickly in markets where physical auction capacity was strained. The business benefited particularly during periods of tight used-car supply—when dealer inventory was lean and prices elevated—because high volumes flow through auctions.

ACV Auctions faces structural challenges that become apparent during cyclical downturns: auction volume correlates directly with dealer inventory levels and used-car price confidence, both of which compress when credit tightens or consumer demand weakens. The company also operates in an ecosystem with entrenched competitors and the ever-present threat of OEMs (manufacturer captive finance arms) and large dealer networks building proprietary exchanges. Transportation costs remain a persistent operational headwind, making logistics efficiency critical to profitability. Yet the shift to digital wholesale remains largely intact—the question for investors is whether ACV can achieve scale-driven margins while competing with both specialized horizontal platforms and vertical integration by larger players.
