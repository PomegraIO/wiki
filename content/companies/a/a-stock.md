Agilent Technologies manufactures and markets analytical instruments, systems, and services for laboratory and field applications across life sciences, chemical analysis, and diagnostics. Spun off from [Hewlett-Packard](/wiki/public-company/) in 1999, the company built its foundation on high-precision measurement technology and expanded into consumables, software, and service offerings that support research, quality control, and clinical diagnostics across pharmaceutical, food safety, petrochemical, and environmental testing.

The company operates through multiple customer segments, each with distinct revenue drivers and competitive dynamics. Laboratory customers—pharmaceutical R&D, academic research, and contract testing—drive substantial reagent and consumable sales alongside recurring service contracts. Diagnostic customers in hospitals and clinical labs purchase instruments for routine pathology and disease screening, creating steady installed-base revenue. Industrial customers in petrochemical refining, food processing, and materials science rely on Agilent for process monitoring and compliance testing. Each segment benefits from high switching costs, proprietary reagent ecosystems, and the mission-critical nature of analytical work.

## Business Segments

| Segment | Primary Customers | Revenue Model |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|
| Life Sciences & Diagnostics | Pharmaceutical firms, hospitals, research labs | Instruments, reagents, services, software |
| Chemical Analysis | Petrochemical refineries, environmental testing, food safety | Instruments, consumables, calibration |
| Diagnostics | Hospital pathology, clinical labs | Diagnostic systems, test kits, support contracts |

The business model emphasizes recurring revenue streams. Once a lab or clinic installs Agilent analytical systems, the customer becomes committed to Agilent reagents, columns, and calibration standards—often the only compatible consumables. Maintenance contracts, technical support, and software licensing extend customer lifetime value and profitability. Instrument sales fund customer acquisition; reagent and service margins drive profitability. This dual revenue structure insulates earnings from single-point cyclicality, though it exposes Agilent to competitive pressures in reagent pricing and the persistent risk of disruptive alternative chemistries or measurement platforms.

Agilent has pursued growth through selective M&A, acquiring specialty measurement businesses and diagnostic niches while divesting non-core assets to sharpen focus. The company competes globally with [Thermo Fisher](/wiki/public-company/), PerkinElmer, and Waters on instruments and reagents, and faces ongoing pricing pressure in emerging markets. Innovation in mass spectrometry, gas chromatography, liquid handling automation, and molecular diagnostics remains central to differentiation and sustained customer stickiness. The regulatory compliance burden in pharmaceuticals and diagnostics creates additional barriers to entry that protect Agilent's installed base.
