*[Amerant Bancorp Inc.](/wiki/amtb-stock/) (**AMTB**) is a [US-listed](/wiki/stock-exchange/) regional [bank](/wiki/public-company/) primarily serving South Florida and the Caribbean. The company provides commercial banking, consumer banking, and wealth management services to individuals, families, and businesses across its operating footprint.*

## What the company does

Amerant Bancorp operates as a regional [banking](/wiki/bank-of-america/) institution with a concentrated geographic footprint in South Florida and the Caribbean. The company serves a diverse customer base including small and medium-sized businesses, multinational corporations, high-net-worth individuals, and retail customers. Its service offerings span traditional commercial lending, residential mortgage origination, deposit products, investment and [wealth management](/wiki/wealth-management/) services, and treasury and payment solutions.

The regional focus on South Florida and the Caribbean provides exposure to Latin American trade flows and demographic trends. Many of the bank's commercial customers derive their business from trade and operations in Central and South America, giving Amerant a niche position in hemispheric financial services.

## How it makes money

Amerant Bancorp generates revenue primarily through net [interest income](/wiki/interest-rate/)—the spread between the rates it pays on deposits and the rates it charges on loans and other interest-bearing assets. Secondary revenue streams include fee income from advisory services, wealth management, payment processing, and other banking activities.

Like most regional banks, Amerant's earnings are sensitive to [interest rate](/wiki/interest-rate/) changes, the level and shape of the [yield curve](/wiki/bond-yield-curve-risk/), credit quality, loan growth, and deposit competition. Asset quality and loan loss provisions are significant drivers of profitability.

## Where it sits in its industry

The regional banking sector comprises hundreds of smaller [public companies](/wiki/public-company/) competing alongside megabanks for local and regional market share. Amerant's geographic niche—South Florida and Caribbean—distinguishes it from purely domestic regional banks but also concentrates its exposure to that region's economic cycles. The bank competes on relationship management, specialized knowledge of its markets, and personalized service rather than on scale.

Regional banks generally face structural headwinds from consolidation, fintech disruption, and the [interest rate](/wiki/interest-rate/) environment. Amerant, like peers, must navigate regulatory compliance, capital requirements, and credit risk while managing margin compression and deposit competition.
