*[Aspira Women's Health Inc.](https://www.aspirawh.com/) (**AWHL**) is a healthcare diagnostics company focused on women's health, particularly in the early detection and screening of ovarian cancer. The company develops and commercializes proprietary blood tests and other diagnostic tools designed to improve women's health outcomes.*

## What the company does

Aspira Women's Health develops and markets diagnostic tests aimed at improving the early detection of serious women's health conditions, with a primary focus on ovarian cancer screening. The company's core product portfolio includes proprietary blood-based tests that leverage biomarker science and machine learning to stratify risk and guide clinical decision-making. These tests are designed to be used by gynecologists, primary care physicians, and other healthcare providers in clinical practice.

The company operates in the broader context of precision medicine and in vitro diagnostics, where genomic and proteomic testing continues to expand. Aspira's approach centers on non-invasive testing methodologies that can inform earlier intervention and improve patient outcomes in women's health.

## How it makes money

Aspira generates revenue primarily through the sale of diagnostic test kits to healthcare providers and laboratories. Revenue is earned when tests are performed and results are delivered to ordering physicians. The company also pursues reimbursement coverage from insurance providers and Medicare, as broader payor acceptance directly impacts test utilization and patient access.

The business model depends on building clinical evidence, securing [reimbursement](/wiki/healthcare-reimbursement-policy/) coverage codes (CPT codes and insurance plan acceptance), and establishing adoption among healthcare providers. Cost of revenue includes manufacturing, laboratory operations, and fulfillment; the company also invests in sales, marketing, clinical validation, and regulatory compliance.

## Where it sits in its industry

Aspira competes within the in vitro diagnostics (IVD) sector, a segment that encompasses companies offering blood tests, genetic testing, and other laboratory-based diagnostic tools. Larger, established diagnostics companies with broad test portfolios and extensive payor relationships dominate this space, while smaller, specialized firms like Aspira focus on narrower indications and build clinical evidence to support adoption.

The ovarian cancer screening market is less mature than screening for other conditions (e.g., breast cancer or colorectal cancer), and clinical guidelines around screening vary. Aspira's success depends on clinical acceptance of its testing approach, payor coverage expansion, and achieving sufficient scale in test volume to reach profitability.

## How to research it

Prospective investors and observers should review [Aspira's SEC filings](/wiki/10-k/), particularly the company's 10-K annual reports and 10-Q quarterly filings. These documents detail the company's business segments, clinical validation efforts, payor coverage status, operating losses, cash burn rate, and management's strategy for achieving profitability.

Key areas to examine include:
- **Clinical evidence**: Publications, clinical trials, and analytical validation supporting the company's diagnostic claims.
- **Payor coverage**: Status of reimbursement agreements with major insurers and Medicare.
- **Test volume and utilization**: Trends in the number of tests performed and revenue per test.
- **Competitive landscape**: Other companies entering ovarian cancer diagnostics or women's health testing.
- **Regulatory status**: FDA classification of tests and any regulatory changes affecting diagnostics.
- **Cash position and burn rate**: Critical metrics for pre-profitable or early-stage diagnostics companies.

The company's investor relations website and SEC filings provide the most authoritative information on business performance and strategy.
