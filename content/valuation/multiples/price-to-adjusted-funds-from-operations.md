*The **price-to-AFFO multiple** (P/AFFO) values a [real-estate investment trust](/real-estate-investment-trust/) by dividing its market [capitalization](/market-capitalization/) by its adjusted funds from operations per share. AFFO strips away non-cash items and adds back maintenance capital expenditures, yielding a more conservative and cash-like figure than [FFO](/enterprise-value/). For REIT investors, AFFO is the true bottom line—the cash a trust can sustainably distribute to shareholders without degrading its asset base.*

REITs are required to distribute at least 90% of taxable income as [dividends](/dividend/), making cash yield a paramount concern. Traditional [net income](/income-statement/) is misleading because [depreciation](/depreciation/) on real estate is deducted (a non-cash charge), yet the property retains or appreciates in value. FFO corrects this; AFFO refines it further by accounting for the actual cash needed to maintain the portfolio.

## From FFO to AFFO

FFO begins with [net income](/income-statement/), then adds back depreciation and amortization (both non-cash), and removes [gains or losses](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) on asset sales. The result is a proxy for "earnings if depreciation didn't exist." For most REITs, FFO is two to three times higher than net income.

AFFO goes further:

1. **Starts with FFO**
2. **Subtracts recurring capital expenditures** (capex needed to keep buildings operational and competitive—roof repairs, HVAC replacement, parking lot resurfacing)
3. **Adjusts for straight-line rent** (the non-cash impact of lease contracts where rent increases or decreases predictably over the lease term)
4. **May subtract tenant-improvement reserves** (cash set aside for building out leased space)

The result is a conservative estimate of distributable cash. A REIT showing $5.00 in FFO but only $4.00 in AFFO is acknowledging that $1.00 per share must be reinvested annually to keep the portfolio in good shape. Dividends should be sourced from AFFO, not FFO.

## The valuation lens

A REIT trading at 12× AFFO is more expensive than one at 10× AFFO, all else equal. But "all else" is rarely equal. A REIT with strong [earnings growth](/earnings-per-share/) or pricing power might justify a premium P/AFFO. A mature, slow-growth REIT (say, a diversified office portfolio) may trade at 8–10× AFFO, yielding 4.5–5% AFFO yields. A growth-stage REIT focused on logistics or data centres might command 15–18× AFFO if investors expect sustained rent growth and [capital allocation](/capital-gains-tax-investor/) discipline.

P/AFFO is often paired with [dividend yield](/dividend-yield/). A REIT with a 5% AFFO yield trading at 12× AFFO is implicitly saying the market expects limited [distribution growth](/dividend-distribution/). One with a 3.5% AFFO yield at the same 12× multiple is betting on meaningful dividend escalation.

## Adjustments and interpretation

The devil in AFFO is in the adjustments. Different REITs calculate maintenance capex differently. Some conservatively set it to 40% of capex; others use a three-year average. Straight-line rent adjustments can be material for REITs with volatile lease terms (common in retail or office). Tenant-improvement reserves vary widely. Analysts comparing REITs must normalize these to ensure apples-to-apples comparison.

One nuance: AFFO can temporarily exceed actual cash distributions if a REIT is deferring capex (e.g., during a pandemic) or aggressively harvesting straight-line rent benefits. Investors should track both the AFFO metric and actual cash [free cash flow](/free-cash-flow/) to spot accounting games.

## Sector variations

AFFO's relevance varies by REIT sector. Industrial and logistics REITs—with newer, lower-maintenance buildings—may show AFFO very close to FFO. Older retail REITs, with aging strip centres and high renewal capex, see AFFO well below FFO. Office REITs in weak markets may show negative AFFO if capital needs exceed FFO, signalling unsustainable distributions.

Specialised REITs (data centres, cell towers, infrastructure) often show AFFO growth outpacing FFO, if capex intensity declines over time. This can justify premium P/AFFO multiples if investors believe the trend is durable.

## The relationship to NAV and other multiples

AFFO multiples and [price-to-NAV](/price-to-nav/) multiples tell different stories. A REIT might trade at 0.95× NAV (asset backing is intact, with room for upside) but 14× P/AFFO (income is expensive relative to peers). This can signal the market values the REIT's income stream highly, perhaps due to long-term lease security or [credit quality](/credit-rating/) of tenants. Conversely, a REIT at 1.1× NAV but 9× P/AFFO suggests the market trusts the assets but doubts near-term distribution growth.
