*The **M0** (or **monetary base**) is the most basic measure of a country's [money supply](/m1). It comprises all the physical currency in circulation (notes and coins) plus the electronic reserves that [banks](/broker) hold at the central bank. M0 is entirely under the central bank's control and serves as the foundation upon which the broader money supply is built through the [money multiplier](/money-multiplier).*

## The two components

M0 has two parts:

**Currency in circulation:** This is the physical money—paper notes and metal coins—that households and businesses hold in their wallets, tills, and safes. When you withdraw $100 from an ATM, you increase currency in circulation.

**Bank reserves at the central bank:** These are electronic balances that [banks](/broker) hold in accounts at the Federal Reserve (or equivalent central bank). These are not cash, but they are essentially equivalent to cash for [banks](/broker) because they can be drawn out instantly.

Together, these two forms constitute M0. Everything else in the [money supply](/m1)—deposits at commercial [banks](/broker), credit, etc.—is built on top of M0.

## Why "base"?

M0 is the **base** because the central bank creates all of it. The Fed cannot create currency that does not go out into circulation (via [banks](/broker) requesting notes from the Fed's currency vault). The Fed cannot create reserves without accounting for them (by making loans at the [discount window](/discount-window) or by purchasing assets via [open-market-operations](/open-market-operations)).

Everything else in the [money supply](/m1)—a [bank](/broker) deposit, a credit card balance, [money-market](/stock-market) fund shares—is a claim on base money, but not base money itself. If everyone with a [bank](/broker) deposit simultaneously demanded cash, a [bank](/broker) could run out of cash if the central bank did not provide it.

This is the fundamental reason for the [central bank's](/central-bank) existence: to ensure there is always enough base money available to keep the system solvent.

## How M0 grows: monetary expansion

When a central bank wants to expand the money supply (typically during a [recession](/recession) or when [inflation](/inflation) is below target), it increases M0. The mechanisms include:

- **Asset purchases.** The Fed buys a [bond](/bond) from a dealer. It pays by crediting the dealer's [bank](/broker) with new reserves—new M0. The [bond](/bond) is gone from the financial system, but new M0 exists.
- **Lending.** The Fed lends to a [bank](/broker) via the [discount window](/discount-window) or [reverse-repo facility](/reverse-repo-facility). The [bank](/broker) receives new reserves—new M0—in exchange for posting collateral.
- **Printing cash.** The central bank literally prints notes and coins. When a [bank](/broker) requests fresh currency from the Fed, the Fed supplies it, and M0 rises.

Each of these increases M0 and, through the [money multiplier](/money-multiplier), allows the broader [money supply](/m1) to expand.

## How M0 contracts: monetary tightening

Conversely, when the central bank wants to contract M0 (typically when [inflation](/inflation) is high), it:

- **Sells assets.** The Fed sells a [bond](/bond) it holds. The buyer pays with reserves, which the Fed removes from circulation. M0 shrinks.
- **Allows reserves to mature.** The Fed stops reinvesting proceeds from maturing securities, shrinking reserves. M0 shrinks. (See [balance-sheet-runoff](/balance-sheet-runoff).)
- **Reduces lending.** As discount-window loans are repaid, M0 shrinks.

## The surge in M0 during crises

During the 2008 financial crisis and 2020 COVID-19 panic, M0 surged dramatically. The Fed bought trillions in assets ([quantitative easing](/quantitative-easing)), injecting vast quantities of new reserves. M0 roughly tripled.

This surge led to fears of severe [inflation](/inflation), but [inflation](/inflation) remained muted for years. Why? Because much of the new M0 was held as excess reserves by [banks](/broker) and was not lent out aggressively. The [money multiplier](/money-multiplier) fell, so M0 growth did not translate into proportional growth in the broader [money supply](/m1).
